### Police

The airport had a day of its own. A hydraulic leak off an aircraft at Gate 11 brought in hazmat, and the leak turned out to be less than a gallon. That is a lot of paperwork for a puddle, but it's the airport's way of doing business: everybody shows up, everybody stands around, and a few ounces of fluid gets a bigger audience than it deserves. Well, that went well.

The same airport also spent the afternoon on a fire alarm traced to a room in Module A. Officers posted up at the exit doors to keep anybody from wandering in while the panel got reset. No fire, no smoke, just a building that cried wolf and a few officers guarding the wolf's door. Somebody on the radio kept calling for "Dan, where are you at, Dan?" which is either a lost officer or the most honest call in Burbank.

Over on Fermin Street, somebody at 424 worked a door handle at apartment 301 until officers came out to see whether it was a lockout or a break-in. My money's on a lockout, which is the most Burbank thing that can happen before brunch. An escort from 22-4 got assigned to the airport, and a unit from Baggage Claim was running late, which is also the most airport thing that can happen.

I'm not going to make a bit out of the restricted domestic violence call on Humboldt Street. It went out, it was handled, and that's the only part of it I'm going to talk about. A checkpoint officer got sent to the public lanes, which is cop for "go be visible and look at cars," and I respect the commitment to the bit.

CHP on the 5, 134, and 210, Metrolink and the Union Pacific corridor, CB Channel 19, and air traffic at the airport are coming soon. Until then I've got what I've got, which is a lot of airport.

### Fire / EMS

Engine 11 answered a hydraulic leak on a red aircraft on the inner street side of the airport. I said last Wednesday that Engine 11 is everywhere, and now it's clearly the airport's cousin. Engine 17 spent a stretch checking Knox box keys, which is the fire service's way of asking the building to please let them in before the building asks for a warrant.

The biggest call of the day was a structure fire at Verdugo Road and Foothill Boulevard. Engine 11, Truck 15, and Medic 63 got sent, then a second unit reported an outdoor fire on Foothill just south of the next cross street. Unit 37 came up at extremely low volume, got asked to repeat itself, and then announced it was "in the area," which is the radio equivalent of "I'm in the neighborhood." Foothill, you keep this up and you're going to need your own zip code.

A single vehicle went over and landed on its roof. Engine 24 reported the passenger was out, Battalion 2 cancelled, and ELS-25 kept going, with PD on scene. Kandosii, which is Mando'a for "nice one." Somebody got out of a rolled car and got the good news on the radio, and I'll take it.

The medical calls read like the greatest hits of the human body. A GI bleed on South Helena, chest pain on South Garfield in Room 3D, chest pain on North Oakland with Engine 33 in response, abdominal pain on South Bushnell, a medical alarm on Tufts, and a trauma call on Monterey Road. Rescue Ambulance 61 got cancelled to LA County, which means somebody else's ambulance was having a worse day than we were. Engine 11 sent crews on a pediatric seizure on North Broadmoor, and I'm not going to joke about that one. The crew did the job, and that's the whole story.

At 7 in the morning dispatch ran its 0700 test of the alerting system, which is the only thing in Burbank that announces itself at 7 a.m. and still doesn't get a cup of coffee. Shiny. The system works, and I'm told that's the point.
