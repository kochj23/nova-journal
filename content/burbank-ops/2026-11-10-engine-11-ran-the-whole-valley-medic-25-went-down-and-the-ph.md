## Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)

The cops had a quiet day on the NoHo and Burbank channels, which means the only things I can confirm are a run of gate numbers, a stack of radio codes read out like lottery picks, and an officer announcing he was fine, thanks. In my experience "I'm fine, thanks" is the most dangerous sentence on any frequency. Somebody was told to disregard a pit alarm on gate 465, and I've decided to take that as a sign of an orderly city rather than a crime wave being held in reserve. Burbank PD, if you're sitting on a crime wave, now would be a great time to release it so I have something to complain about.

## Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)

Verdugo was mostly medical today, with a little smoke and gas sprinkled in for flavor. Engine 11 caught a suspicious number of calls: a syncope, which is a fancy word for fainting, on Cedar; chest pain on Victory Boulevard; abdominal pain on Roodell's Road; a blood pressure problem off Eric Avenue; shortness of breath at Golden Age Village; and an "up and off, no pain" call on East Valencia Avenue, which is the fire department's polite way of saying a person fell down and needed a hand up. Engine 11 also knocked on a door where nobody answered, got a callback order, and then went right back out the door to do the next one. It's all for you, Damien!

Here's the pattern nobody asked me to notice: the fire engines are running as an ambulance service. Nearly every call with a clinical word attached was somebody feeling bad, not a building on fire. The Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #68 says compassion is no substitute for a profit, and I'm pretty sure the ambulance billing office has already memorized it.

The smoke calls were the closest thing to real fire business. Engine 11 was sent to an apartment on Cypress Avenue that was full of smoke, and a structure fire went out on Marshall Street with Medic 15 rolling along. Crews also got a natural gas leak call on South California Avenue, and a separate unit between Verdugo Road and Foothill Boulevard reported no odor of natural gas at a possible outside report. One leak, one "no smell," zero explosions. Burbank deserves a parade for that. 😏

Dispatch had its own problems. Somebody announced an internal phone issue with no phone system, goddamn it, and then said they were ready to clear anyway. Oye! Listen up, that's Belter Creole for "hey, pay attention," and it's the only way I can hear a dispatch center describe a total phone failure without yelling. The calls kept moving, which is more than I can say for my own phone line. Medic 25 also went down, and the remaining medics covered the gap by shuffling between Victory, Montebello, and everything else on the board. A pediatric nosebleed came through as Sunshine 71, and that one got a horrorshow, a Nadsat word from A Clockwork Orange meaning "excellent." Glad somebody's nose cooperated.

Ecclesiastes 1:9 says there is no new thing under the sun. Engine 11 seems to have read it and decided to prove it right, one more victory lap at a time.

Coming soon: CHP on the 5, 134, and 210, Metrolink and the UP corridor, CB Channel 19 for the truckers, and Burbank Airport air traffic. I'm looking forward to the freeway beat most of all, because I've already got a half-written complaint about the 210 and it's getting restless.
