**Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)**

Two thousand seven hundred and four transmissions in twenty-four hours. Let that number settle while my liver processes what it means: 2,704. Radio hits. Of which seven — seven (7) — were coherent sentences. The other 2,697 were the digital equivalent of everyone screaming simultaneously while recording themselves and broadcasting it back.

The seven that made sense sketched a brief narrative. A wanted white male in black shirt and brown pants covered in tattoos (the kind that whisper "I made very specific decisions"). Units echoing the description because when your radio system is eating its own voice, you repeat everything and pray somebody hears it. One actual dispatch: mental health crisis at 520 East Temple Street. Fire answered because Police was too busy existing in a state of broadcast chaos to answer their own calls.

That's the Police beat. One mental health call Fire ended up handling, and 2,697 reasons why nobody's confident about what else was happening.

**Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)**

Nine hundred and thirteen transmissions. Sixteen made sense. And in those sixteen: six structure fires before the day ended.

10th and Verdugo Avenue. 38th and the Interstate 10 interchange. Delmar near Wilson Middle School — yes, that's Little Mister's neighborhood, so the universe continues its tradition of aiming fire at everything Jordan finds remotely convenient. Union Street. Verdugo and Foothill. Plus a possible fire at 525 West Elk Avenue that dispatch was chasing down.

Underneath the structure fires: chest pain calls at Oakland and Howard (chest pain is apparently running a route). Shortness of breath on Garfield. A diabetic patient on San Gabriel. Falls. People sick in apartments. The usual October medical cascade that turns every Fire shift into triage roulette.

Engine 11 ran most of it. One engine. Six structure fires. Medical calls in between. That's not a staffing model, that's hope-based infrastructure disguised as a city service. And it held. Somehow it held. Which is the moment you realize we've stopped expecting adequacy — when the broken system doesn't catastrophically collapse, we celebrate it as working. Fire shows up. Engine 11 knows the map by heart. Nobody dies on our watch. We call that success.

**Rail (Metrolink / UP corridor)**

Nothing. Radio silence. The one beat that got to have a quiet day.

October's two weeks deep, and the pattern's hardened into something resembling the new normal: Fire is the infrastructure that functions. Police radio is white noise with jurisdiction. Engine 11 is Burbank's personification at this point. We're not halfway through fire season, so this holding pattern will keep holding until something fails. Engine 11's patient, but October isn't infinite.
