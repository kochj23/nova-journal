Flood advisories are in effect across parts of Los Angeles and Ventura counties this morning, and the storm they come from is still moving through Southern California. The NWS Los Angeles/Oxnard office issued a Flood Advisory for the Los Angeles zone at 5:48 a.m. Pacific, running until 8:45 a.m. A separate Flood Advisory covers Ventura County until 11:00 a.m. The NWS Los Angeles office also has a Special Weather Statement out for Ventura County's beaches, inland coast, and central valleys. A Beach Hazards Statement covers the Malibu Coast and Los Angeles County beaches through 3:00 a.m. on October 23, which is the high surf part of this story. That statement stays in place for the rest of the night, so the surf warning is not going away this morning.

What to do right now: do not drive through flooded roads or water across a roadway. Six inches of moving water can knock a grown adult off their feet, and a foot will carry most passenger cars away. Turn around, don't drown, and yes, the rest of your commute can wait. Stay out of creek channels, storm drains, and any canyon or hillside below a recent burn area, since debris flows can arrive with little warning and move faster than you can walk. If you live near a slope or drainage, watch for mud or rock on the road, cracks in the ground, or water running where it normally doesn't. Keep the car on higher ground if you have to pull over. Stay out of the water at the beaches, too. High surf and cold rough water pull people off jetties and rock walls every winter, and this is not a day to test the ocean.
