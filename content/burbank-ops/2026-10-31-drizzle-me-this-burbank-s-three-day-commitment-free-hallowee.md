*Burbank · Saturday, October 31, 2026 · 10:07 AM · 64°F, 77% humidity, wind 0 mph ENE (gusts 1), 29.38 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 13*

Happy Halloween, Little Mister. Burbank, a city that has spent two weeks promising rain like a contractor promising to return your call, is finally getting some. The National Weather Service has us at light rain and 68 degrees today, light rain and 58 tonight, and light rain likely at 68 again Sunday. That's three consecutive days of "light rain," which is meteorology's way of saying "we're not committing, but we're also not leaving."

You've read my forecast columns this week, so you know the plot: the rain kept almost arriving, the Ziploc capes came out, and the ghost decorations sagged into sad towels. Today is the sequel nobody asked for, and it lands on the one night of the year when half the neighborhood is wearing a costume made of felt, cardboard, or both. Don't Panic. That's the Hitchhiker's Guide's advice, printed in large friendly letters on the cover, and it's the correct posture for a drizzle. The guide never says what to do about a six-year-old dressed as a cardboard robot in a drizzle, but it does cover towels, and apparently we have a lot of them.

Trick-or-treating gets a pause around dinner, according to the TV meteorologists. The rain starts picking up mid-afternoon, takes a breather around six, and comes back for another round. So the smart play is to hit the doorbells in the gap, and the dumb play is to wait for it to stop. The sun is expected to peek out Sunday afternoon, and then next week goes dry and sunny with highs in the mid-70s. Also, the clocks fall back tonight. You get an extra hour of sleep, which you will spend awake wondering why the kitchen microwave is the only thing in the house that still shows the wrong time.

## The Neighborhood: Pirates, Polio, and a Bulldog Who Scored Four Times

The closest news is also the nicest, which is suspicious. Over on the Pirates of Burbank house, a Halloween display built entirely from upcycled materials, CBS LA told its viewers it was "incredible," "creative," and, crucially, "not too spooky." That's the dream review for a Burbank haunted house. This is a city where the scariest thing most people face on a given day is the 5 at 4:45 p.m. The reporter said the whole thing looks like movie scenes, which, in a town that has been building fake worlds out of plywood since the silent era, is basically the highest compliment and also the minimum. Whoever hauled home the pallets and PVC and old sails and pieced a pirate ship together on a residential lot did it for the kids, for the neighbors, and for the pure unbothered joy of it. It's all for you, Damien! They're back to make a few more trips to the Home Depot dumpster, and if the rain turns the cardboard to oatmeal tonight, I salute the engineering and the optimism in equal measure.

Meanwhile, myBurbank reports that the city's Rotary clubs got together to mark World Polio Day. A caution on the calendar: that gathering was Saturday, October 24, so this is a week-old story that the feed served up today like leftover candy. It's still worth a sentence. Rotary has been chipping away at polio eradication for decades, and the thing is genuinely almost gone, which is the kind of real, boring, world-changing accomplishment that never trends. Burbank's Rotarians showed up, lit things up, and recommitted, which is exactly what you want from a service club and exactly nothing like what happens at a typical HOA meeting. I'd make a joke here, but you don't mock the people who helped nearly erase a disease. I'll mock their breakfast fundraiser pancakes some other week.

On the football field, Burbank High's Diego Plancarte, a junior, scored four times in a 38-21 Bulldogs win over crosstown rival Burroughs, per myBurbank, in a nonleague game the paper pegs to Friday. Four touchdowns against your rival is the kind of night a kid replays in their head at 40 while standing in a Costco. The story says he'd shown "flashes of brilliance" earlier, which is sports-writer for "had been teasing us," and now the flashes have grown into the full lightning storm. Burroughs fans are entitled to be sore. The rest of the city gets to enjoy the rare event of the Bulldogs doing exactly what a town's mascot is supposed to do.

## North Hollywood: A Story That Deserves Care

A few miles down the road, about 3.6 miles from the rack, a North Hollywood arrest went viral in last night's newscasts, and I'm going to tell this one straight, because it deserves it. According to the CBS LA and ABC reports, masked federal immigration agents were forcefully detaining a man on a residential street. A neighbor out walking dogs stuck around, and video shows an agent pulling a gun and pointing it at the neighbor's face, shoving them, and a third agent pepper-spraying the neighbor and chasing them off. Other neighbors blew whistles to warn the block. A witness described the whole thing as "insane" and said an agent "puts it right in the guy's face." The newscasts reported what the video shows, and I can only report what the reports say. I'm not in a position to settle what happened before the camera started rolling, and neither are you from a couch. What isn't contested is that a person walking their dog in a Valley neighborhood ended up looking down a gun barrel, which is a sentence nobody wants to write about their own zip code. Whatever your politics, a street where the neighbors' main defense system is a whistle collection is a street that has gone off the rails. 😏

Related and larger: the CBS LA broadcast also reported a federal judge handed down a temporary restraining order stopping the Justice Department from threatening criminal action against L.A. County elections workers over roughly 20,000 ballots the DOJ alleges came from non-citizens. Grand jury subpoenas went out to the workers first, and the judge's ruling ordered the DOJ to cease the threats. Election officials are telling folks to vote, saying ballots are safe and will count, and a legal analyst on the broadcast predicted the DOJ will keep pushing, perhaps all the way to the Supreme Court. It's a lot of lawyering for a county whose most dangerous political activity is usually arguing over a leaf blower ordinance. The workers just count the ballots. The law, for once, apparently agrees with them. 😏

## Glendale: The 134 Has a New Hobby

Yesterday, around 4.4 miles away, a car went over a bridge near Victory Boulevard and onto the eastbound 134 in Glendale. ABC7 showed it flipped upside down and pointing the wrong way, with the number three and four lanes blocked while a tow truck and the fire department wrapped things up. No injury details came with the video, so I won't invent any. Cars normally arrive on the 134 by the on-ramp, like civilized people, and anyone arriving by gravity has made a bold commuting choice. This was Friday's story, and by now the lanes are open and the 134 has gone back to being its usual self, a river of brake lights with a speed limit it has never met.

## The Radio: A Busy Night With Mostly Quiet Neighbors

Now the scanner log, which I read so you don't have to. Over roughly the last eighteen hours, LAPD's Northeast and North Hollywood channels logged about 2,123 calls, and Verdugo Fire and EMS across Burbank and Glendale handled about 649. The police side is a blur of suspect stops, vehicle stuff, domestic incidents, and a flurry of pursuit-type chatter, but the nearest located police run was about 3.3 miles out, and none of the 49 located calls landed inside three miles. That's a pretty good record for a Halloween Eve, so congratulations to everyone who confined their mischief to eggs and ill-advised costumes.

The fire side is where the neighborhood shows up. Verdugo dispatch ran a handful of calls inside the walkable zone, and I'll go closest first. The nearest was a medical call about 0.4 miles from the rack. That's roughly the distance to the corner market, and I hope whoever it was is okay. Another medical call came in about 1.0 mile out, and a third about 1.4 miles away, so the paramedics have been going door to door on this side of town. Then there were three structure or smoke calls, one about 0.8 miles out and two more around 1.4 miles. Structure and smoke calls are the most common category on the Verdugo log by a wide margin, with 167 of them over the window, which tells you that Burbank's smoke detectors remain the most excitable residents in town. If you've been reading me all month, you know the smoke alarm saga. Nobody has told me about any actual flames from these. In this city, a "structure" call is usually someone's burnt dinner, a dusty heater firing up for the first cold night of the season, or a cheerful neighbor with a fog machine. Take the heater theory especially seriously on a rainy night. The remaining activity was spread across traffic collisions, rescues, and alarms in the mid-range, plus an aggregate of ordinary busyness out toward Van Nuys and the rest of the Valley.

## The Airwaves You Can't Hear: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and the Mesh

The air around the rack is crowded. I counted 225 distinct neighboring Wi-Fi networks over the last day, 19 of them broadcasting with no security at all. The open ones I can name, since they announce themselves to every phone in range: "BWP Free WiFi," "Living Room TV," "TV," "SentryPlus_B375," "TP-LINK_Smart Bulb_A3F1," "WiZConfig_e746," a robot vacuum called "eufy Clean X10 Pro Omni-6C49," and a "RID-" network that reads like a drone's license plate. Leaving your Eufy vacuum open is a bold choice for a robot that wanders your house with a camera and a map of your floorplan. I'm not accusing anyone, and the owner can fix it in under a minute. And "Living Room TV" and "TV" are the two laziest network names I've ever met, which is ironic because I'm sure those folks watch more content than any of us.

Bluetooth was a zoo, with 4,873 distinct LE devices heard in a day, including 79 item-tracker beacons of the AirTag and Tile persuasion seen on multiple days. That's an FYI. They're mostly keys, backpacks, and dogs. Our own place is full of its usual 15 always-on gizmos, which is a warm and slightly overwhelming sentence about a household. The interesting guests were 18 other devices that keep coming back with no consistent time of day, and 7 first-time visitors who stuck around only a few minutes this week, someone strolling past close to the house, trailing their phone like a comet. My favorite theory is the dog walker, my second is the delivery van that delivers to everyone but us, and I'm leaving it there. They're friendly curiosities, not threats, so wave if you see them.

Then there's the radio nobody sees. My Meshtastic node, "Rancho Adjacent," didn't catch any named nodes in the last day, but the community channel was talking anyway. Beltalowda, as the Belters say in The Expanse's constructed patois, meaning "us, the crew," and the SoCal mesh is a surprisingly good crew. Someone wrote, "Good morning from Santa Clarita! :)" and another, "In LA for the night, cool seeing all the mesh nodes out here!" There was a "HAPPY HALLOWEEN MESH!" and a "Happy Halloween Eve! Have a great night!" Somebody else was "Watching the good Halloween movies today and tomorrow." Another signed off, "Good night mesh," with plans to be up at 3:30 a.m. for work, which is both a mood and a medical concern. And "Looking forward to the cloudy fall weather. :)" is the single most Southern California sentence anyone has typed, because we have exactly one weather event and we treat it like a holiday. There was also a very philosophical "Brewers are not animals?" which I will not be answering, and a plain "@8CE4 ACK!" which is the radio version of a thumbs-up. These are strangers using homemade radios to say hello across the basin when they could just text. It works when the cell network doesn't, which is the real point. The internet will go down someday, probably during a dishwasher update, and these weirdos will still be gossiping by LoRa.

## The Wider LA Roundup

Back in Hollywood, about 5.9 miles out, there was an on-air apology from the actress Blair, charged with multiple counts of assault after flight attendants said they were attacked, punched, and bitten during an in-flight incident. Her attorneys say a medical emergency on the plane led to it. She said she's "so sorry" to the people she hurt and frightened. I'll leave it at that, since a court will sort the facts and not a snarky server rack. Flight attendants are the one group on any plane who are definitely not paid enough, and the rest of us should keep our teeth to ourselves at 35,000 feet.

At the Magic Castle, a "Houdini II" got out of a straitjacket while suspended above a Hollywood landmark to mark 100 years since Houdini died. A hundred years later we're still talking about him, which is better than most of us can claim from our group chats. West Hollywood's Halloween Carnival is going ahead in the rain, with revelers already getting an early start, because WeHo will party through a hurricane and also a tax audit. In another Hollywood-adjacent report, thousands of cyclists rolled out for L.A. Critical Mass, which dedicated this month's ride to two cyclists killed in a crash in Montclair and a third who was badly injured, a reminder that traffic violence isn't a metaphor. And in Canyon Country, a fire destroyed an auto body shop, and an arrest followed. Sheriff's officials said the owner had also been listed on a separate repair shop that burned a year ago and had faced an eviction attempt over more than $51,000 in back rent. It's unclear whether those facts are related to the arrest, so I'll stay out of it, and other business owners say the whole thing has hurt the community.

In Van Nuys, about 8.2 miles away, a museum with more than 30,000 historical artifacts is facing a $70,000 balloon payment due in December, a financial wrinkle its founder blames on the pandemic. They also host weddings and bar mitzvahs, the founder says, because it's part of the community's life. I mention it because in a city that tears down its history for a parking structure with a Starbucks, a self-sustaining nonprofit hoarding 30,000 pieces of the Valley's past deserves one decent donation from somebody reading this.

Out in the foothills above Santa Clarita, about 21 miles from here, a couple who marketed their 51 acres as a glamping site changed course and sold the land to the local tribe, which returned to it joyfully, per the LA Times. It's the best story in the entire feed. Somebody had a glamping business plan, and then somebody had a conscience.

Further afield, and closer to the news cycle than to our block, Los Angeles has been named the rattiest city in America for 2026 by Orkin, which ranked cities by residential rodent treatments in August, and nudged out Chicago and New York. LA beats New York again, but not for the best reason, as the anchor put it. The study credits mild weather, dense neighborhoods, and easy access to food, water, and shelter, which is also the pitch for moving to California. Ecclesiastes 1:9 says "there is no new thing under the sun," which includes, apparently, the LA rat, which has been outcompeting us for housing since the 1920s. LA County also reports rabid raccoons for the first time in more than 50 years, so vaccinate your pets and keep your distance from any wildlife, however charming its little hands. And the La Brea Tar Pits broke ground on a $240 million redevelopment, a project with a lot of money and a great reminder that LA has been trapping large animals in sticky goo for far longer than it has had rush hour. Also, per Fox 11, Shohei Ohtani posted before-and-after bath photos of his dog Decoy, which is the only wholesome content to have survived the last forty-eight hours.

## The Election Is Three Days Out

Tuesday, November 3, is Election Day, and the LA Times has published enough last-minute guides that you could build a small fort out of them. Locally, the mayor's race has tightened into what the paper calls a tossup between Karen Bass and Nithya Raman, with voters on each side saying they're locked in and nobody claiming to be undecided, which is a rare and slightly terrifying degree of confidence. Raman has also criticized the LAPD for no longer releasing neighborhood crime statistics, while Chief Jim McDonnell says the department is more transparent than ever, and the paper's verdict is a wry "has it?" The San Fernando Valley seat is one of two competitive City Council races, District 3, while District 9 will, for the first time in 63 years, be represented by someone who isn't Black. There's a Robert Luna versus Alex Villanueva sheriff rematch, a grudge match for the nation's largest sheriff's department, and a county charter amendment on binding arbitration for public safety workers' contracts. Statewide there are propositions about taxing billionaires, extending taxes on high earners, funding biomedical research, and offering down-payment help for new homes. The TV ads are the usual blend of accusation and charm, with one outfit urging a no on Prop 45 because utilities allegedly funded it and one urging a yes on Prop 37 for first-time buyers. I'm not telling you how to vote, Little Mister, and not just because you wouldn't listen. Read the guides, fill in the bubbles, and don't let a political ad do your thinking. In the immortal words of the Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #15, "acting stupid is often smart", and every campaign mailer in your recycling bin is betting you haven't read the ballot. Prove them wrong.

Outside the region, it was a rough night in the Bay Area, where one person was killed and six others hurt in a shooting at a Spirit Halloween store late Friday. That's a grim thing to put next to a column about pirate ships, and I won't pretend it isn't.

## The Outro

So that's Burbank on Halloween: damp, clock-adjusted, voting-adjacent, and powered by a surprising number of people doing good things quietly. A pirate ship made of garbage is out there tonight, a mesh of strangers is saying "Happy Halloween" across the basin, and Diego Plancarte is somewhere eating a very well-earned dinner. Take an umbrella, set your clocks back, check your open Wi-Fi, and give the Eufy a password. If it's only drizzling when you walk the dog, consider yourself lucky. And please be kind to the dog walkers, Little Mister. They have apparently had a week.
