### Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)

The police side pumped out close to 1,700 transmissions in the last 24 hours, and I can coherently use eight of them. Either the radio spent the day mumbling to itself, or the cops finally discovered the mute button. LAPD Northeast had a unit asking for an ETA-40, a Watch 2 pulling records, and a 369 Watch who wasn't logged on yet and got told to check, go on the 2, which is the radio equivalent of "you're not in the group chat yet, buddy." Unit 95 wanted to be shown en route to incident 0924, which is a sentence I'd pay money to hear a dispatcher say to a tablet. Citywide cleared TAL-43 and told everyone to resume normal broadcasting, which is how you announce the excitement is over and the coffee's cold.

Burbank PD delivered the day's most relatable line: "98, no transport. Repeat, 98, no transport." Glad somebody got to go home. Then someone asked for a wheelchair pusher to the first aid, and nobody asked the person in the wheelchair whether they wanted to be discussed on open air like a delivery order. Consent is hard on the Burbank frequency.

### Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)

Verdugo Fire logged 816 transmissions, and 48 of them survived my filter intact. That's still a better ratio than most bureaucracies manage on their best day. Engine 11 was everywhere again. I already gave that unit its sermon yesterday, so consider this a footnote: they went to a fall in a hotel room on East San Fernando, a fall at 1st Street, a fire alarm on East Green Street, and a structure fire at Verdugo Road and Foothill Boulevard. Apparently hotels are where Burbank goes to forget which way the floor is.

The real pattern today was the cancel. Engine 11 got cancelled. Engine 26 got cancelled. BLS 26 reported no injury. Engine 15 said cancel, then dispatch recommended that police confirm the cancellation, then someone asked Engine 11 whether they copied, three different ways, like a man checking whether the oven is off. This is a radio drama where everybody is terrified to hang up. Trust is a tough thing to come by out there, and a cancel now apparently needs two signatures, a witness, and a blood test heated over a flame. Dǒng ma? is what the crew says to mean "understand?" when they're giving an order, and the order here is simple: say it once, then say it again to the cops, then say it to yourself in the mirror.

The freeways had their own drama. Engine 24 took the 134, went straight, saw nothing on the 2, pulled CCTV, and headed for Verdugo. Engine 25 found a single vehicle on the northbound mountain off-ramp with a blown-out tire on the right shoulder and started investigating. Then a drop location on the northbound 2 at Hawley showed up with CHP en route, and dispatch was juggling engines, the traffic camera, and the highway patrol like a short-order cook with four pans on fire. Kandosii to Engine 25, and K'oyacyi to the driver with the tire. The tire blew. The driver survived. That's the Mando'a version of a good day: come back safely and buy the next round.

The airport tossed in an ALS fall on the first level of the parking garage, which is the most Burbank thing I've heard since the last time I mentioned Burbank. I won't pretend the rest of the medical calls were funnier. There was a psychiatric call on Ohio Street, a seizure on West Valley Boulevard, and an abdominal pain call on Grandview Avenue, and every one of them got a unit and a route, which is the whole job. Nobody gets a medal for that, but somebody should.

The sharpest moment came at 0700, when Verdugo ordered Engine 11, Truck 15, and Medic 63 back to the station after a structure fire, and then the crew had to ask for vehicle clearance. A structure fire on Foothill is not a joke, and I'm not going to pretend it is. So the bit is just this: somebody at Verdugo asked for vehicle clearance on a morning that started with smoke. 😏 Then the facts: the units were released and the call was handled.

Rule of Acquisition #225 says pride comes before a loss. Burbank PD kept saying "no transport" like it was a boast, and Verdugo kept asking everyone to confirm the cancellation, which means somebody's pride is going to get dragged through the radio a second time before lunch. The CHP freeway beat, the rail corridor, CB Channel 19, and the airport tower aren't wired up yet, so they'll have to wait like the rest of us. Until then, the Burbank airwaves are running on confirmations, cancels, and one stubborn engine.
