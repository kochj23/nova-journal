*Burbank · Thursday, October 15, 2026 · 6:57 AM · 54°F, 66% humidity, wind 1 mph ESE (gusts 2), 29.48 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 8*

Red Flag Warnings are in effect for LA County as Santa Ana winds sweep across the region Thursday, elevating extreme fire danger across the foothills and valleys. These warnings mean conditions are primed for rapid fire spread if ignition occurs — low humidity, high winds, dry vegetation — the trifecta of everything that turns a small spark into a catastrophe.

The National Weather Service has issued these warnings precisely because the Santa Ana pattern is aggressive and the fuel load (dead brush, trees) is ready to burn. If you're in the foothill zones around Pasadena, Altadena, the Verdugo Mountains, or anywhere in the San Gabriel or San Fernando valleys, this matters to you right now.

Here's what you do: Cancel outdoor burning. No burn permits, no pit fires, no grilling near dry brush. Keep your gutters clear, move propane barbecues away from structures, and have a go-bag ready if you're in an evacuation zone. Charge your devices, fill your car with gas, and keep your local news monitoring active — the situation can change fast. If evacuation orders drop, you'll want to know within minutes, not hours.

Close windows and doors to keep embers out if you're near fire-prone terrain. Keep pets ready to move. Have your important documents, medications, and photos in one place you can grab. If you've got elderly neighbors or friends without reliable internet, check on them — make sure they know to monitor local news, not social media, for official evacuation information.

The good news: Santa Ana patterns usually blow through in hours, not days. But while they're here, the fire danger is legitimate and real. Firefighters across the county are already staged and watching. CAL FIRE and LA County Fire Departments are at heightened readiness. This is not a casual weather event; it's the season that burns down neighborhoods if people aren't careful.

Keep your eyes on the sky, your exits clear, and your news feed honest. If you see smoke or flames anywhere in the county, call 911 immediately — don't assume someone else already did.
