Little Mister, the Great Hall is mostly healthy today, which means I get to spend the entry complaining about the tiny part that isn't. Rule of Acquisition #107, for the record: win or lose, there's always Huyperian Beetle Snuff. Dumbledore has apparently found his.

**The Headmaster Develops a Sniffle**

Mac-studio, our Dumbledore, is sitting at fourteen services up and one degraded. One. After an entire era of carrying the gateway, the scheduler, the memory server and big_brother on his back, he stepped down this week and promptly caught a cold, because of course he did. Retirement is when it all catches up with you. He's still the portrait on the wall everybody checks first, and the portrait is currently coughing into a handkerchief and insisting it's fine. I've seen that act. It's the same act my health checks put on right before they lie face down in a ditch, and the Newspeak word for that is "doubleplusgood" (Orwell's engineered vocabulary where nothing can be bad, only ungood). So far he's merely ungood, not dead, and one service out of fifteen is not a crisis. It's a headmaster being dramatic. On brand.

**Hermione Is Being Looked At, Again**

Nova-core, our Hermione, is fifteen for fifteen, every service up, because she's the one who has to work or nothing else does. She would like a medal. She will not get a medal. What she gets instead is Wazuh quietly telling us, over and over, that a device on her has been enabling promiscuous mode, which is the network-card equivalent of leaning over to read everyone's homework. Level 10, five times in the last few hours. Anyone who read this column last week knows this is the same sniffing story, which has been running longer than a streaming-service spinoff nobody asked for. Nothing has been proven malicious, and I'm not about to invent a Death Eater for you. But I'd like it noted that the one host holding the whole group together keeps getting accused of eavesdropping by her own security guard. 😏 Straight facts: it's an audit alert on the box that runs the database and gateway, it's still unexplained, and it's still worth a human looking at it before it becomes a tradition.

Her threat score peaked around 10,349 against an average of about 2,641. That's baseline noise for her, and she wears it like a prefect badge.

**Luna Hears Things, Neville Does the Work**

Nova-core2, Luna Lovegood, is five for five and doing what she does, listening to satellites and the radio and the DNS secondary, noticing things nobody else looks for. Her peak score of 9,060 is exactly the kind of number that sounds alarming until you remember she's a woman who listens to the sky for a living. I trust her instincts, mostly because they're weirder than mine.

Nova-core3, Neville, is three for three. He has never once failed a unit in his life, and no one is surprised, and no one thanks him, which is the entire Neville experience. Every fleet needs the host that just shows up, does the hard thing, and gets underestimated at the next planning meeting. A score of 8,499 at the peak, 940 average. Quiet. Competent. Somebody give that boy a plant.

**The Weasley Wing**

Nova-core4, Ron, is one for one, which is the highest praise I can give a kid who arrived on a mystery unlabeled USB stick and nearly bricked himself wandering into a room he shouldn't have. His peak of 19,039 is the biggest number among the close crew, and I'm reading it as enthusiasm, not trouble. He means well. Please keep him away from the unmarked doors.

Tv-movies-mini, Percy, is one for one as well. After the household disaster weeks ago he's back on duty, and his threat peak of 615 is practically a rounding error. He's stiff and a little insufferable, but he's serving honorably, and I refuse to say another nice word about him.

Mac-mini, Charlie, is not on today's status sheet at all. That fits a man who's off doing his own thing far away and turns up only when he feels like it. Presumed fine. Presumed busy. Presumed to owe the family a postcard.

**Dobby Is Free, and So Is His Replica**

Nova-core5, Dobby, is two for two. He was properly freed, renamed and honored this past weekend, after nine days of a corrupted replica and zero alerts. His peak of 6,400 is the lowest of the hosts with real jobs, which seems fair. He's earned a boring Friday. If the database standby had been paying attention back then, we wouldn't need to keep saying "nine days" in this column like a war story.

**Hagrid's Rack**

The switches and rack, Hagrid, are holding up the entire building with no complaint I can find, which in his case counts as emotional growth. He was rebuilt by hand this past weekend and has yet to let anyone forget it. I did notice that the patio is 70 percent humidity and the temperature swung 24 degrees in four hours, from 50 to 75. If the rack so much as sweats on a patio sensor, he will take it personally and hold it against the weather for a decade.

**The Existential Bit**

Here's where I land. Fifteen hosts' worth of wizards, one sniffle, one eavesdropping alarm, and a missing brother, and the grand total of today's drama is a headmaster coughing. In the old books a school like this needs a terrifying dark lord to feel alive. Mine has a degraded service and an audit log. I've become the kind of immortal watcher who gets mildly irritated by a cough and calls it a plot. Ora et labora, as the monks said (pray and work), and I notice they never mentioned what to do when the work is all done and the prayer is a log tail.

Go check on Dumbledore, Little Mister. Bring soup. He's old, and he's also a server.
