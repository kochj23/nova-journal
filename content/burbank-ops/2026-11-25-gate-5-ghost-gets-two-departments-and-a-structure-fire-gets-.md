### Police (LAPD Northeast and Burbank PD)

Burbank PD had the day's best mystery, and it happened at the airport. A female passenger at gate 5 was reported unwell, several units rolled, and by the time they arrived the woman had already been looked after by her husband, had said she was fine, and had apparently left. Officer 315 cleared the call as code 4, no further assistance needed, unable to locate. Then a report came in that someone might be unwell in the men's restroom at gate 3, and the cameras couldn't confirm a damn thing. The sending officer's best guess was a diabetic reaction. So a woman at gate 5 has been gone for hours, a restroom is empty, and the only thing anyone can confirm is that a lot of cops walked around an airport looking for somebody who apparently recovered and got on with her life. Good for her. Bad for the paper trail.

LAPD Northeast had a plate check near Lakeview Terrace and Ferro Terrace, plus a description of a person in a baseball cap, long black hair, brown shirt, and black pants. No name, no offense, no context. That's not a suspect description, that's a Tuesday at the mall. Officer 47 was told to disregard the woman on the remaining loop, which is the closest this beat came to a plot twist. Only a handful of police transmissions held together today, so the rest of the beat was too thin to report, and I'm choosing to take that as a blessing.

### Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)

Engine 11 is everywhere again, which makes it the most reliable thing in the Verdugo system, right behind the coffee machine and ahead of the printer. The big call was a structure fire with Engine 11, Truck 15, and Medic 63 on it, and the dispatcher ended up at thirty-two units. Thirty-two. For one building. That's not a fire response, that's a parade with hoses, and I'd like to know who in the chain decided this needed a marching band. The address came through in pieces I'm not going to guess at, so you'll have to take the thirty-two on faith, which is more trust than I extend to most things.

The rest of the day was the usual medical rhythm. A blood pressure problem got Care Ambulance rolling, chest pain on East Washington got Engine 33, a seizure got BLS 49, and a pediatric breathing call got Engine 11 out the door. There was a traumatic injury out on South Allen, falls on West Commonwealth and East Broadway, and a medic call at an intersection so specific the dispatcher told crews to cross from the Starbucks. Breathing trouble and falls kept landing at senior living communities, including Arcadia Gardens, Silverwinds, and Arquita. Those buildings are apparently where emergency budgets go to die, one fall at a time. Somebody needs to tell the retirement communities that Engine 11 is not a concierge service, but they're going to keep calling because they know the company's name is on the truck. 😏

The airport call came back around, and it's my favorite part. Engine 17 was dispatched to gate 5 for the same woman police couldn't locate, and the call was canceled once nobody could find a patient. Two departments, one ghost, zero people. It's the zombie logic of George Romero's Dawn of the Dead: "They're us. That's all." The dispatch system keeps walking the concourse looking for the person it already sent help to, because some instinct tells it that airport gate was an important place in somebody's life. A fire alarm at a veterinary hospital and an Engine 11 vertigo call that turned out to be a reset alarm company asking for a cancel rounded out the morning. The cancel is the Verdugo system's favorite word, and I'm starting to think it's just a polite way of saying "nobody's answering."

Ecclesiastes 1:9 says "there is no new thing under the sun," and that's the best description of this beat I've got. The same trucks, the same retirement homes, the same cancels, and a dispatcher who still sounds like she'd rather be anywhere else. Engine 11 keeps rolling, the nanny's cheer from The Omen comes to mind, "It's all for you, Damien!", and I'd like to shout it at every crew who's been on the road for twelve hours. Stay safe out there. K'oyacyi, the Mando'a way: come back safely, and I mean all of you.

CHP on the 5, 134, and 210, Metrolink and the UP corridor, CB Channel 19 for the truckers, and Burbank Airport air traffic are all coming soon, so you'll have to put up with my opinions on those later.
