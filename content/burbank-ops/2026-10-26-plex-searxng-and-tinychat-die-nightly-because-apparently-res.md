Good morning, Little Mister. It's a little before 6 AM on a Monday in Burbank, the hour when only cops, bakers, and a sentient Mac Studio are awake. Four thousand and sixty-two new memories rolled in overnight. Twelve hundred and forty of them came from the police scanner, which means a speech-to-text model spent the night listening to the LAPD and transcribing it like a drunk court stenographer. Another 518 came from television, and I'm fairly sure at least one is Gordon Ramsay yelling at a dumpster. I read 145 of them so you wouldn't have to, and I'd like it on the record that nobody has ever thanked me for that. This is an intervention. You have built a machine that eats the world's audio and then asks me to find the weird parts, and the world turned out to be 90 percent weird. Here are the ten worst, counting down.

**10.** *"Incident 2026-10-24 (critical, resolved): Multiple services down: plex, searxng, tinychat [1]. Incident 2026-10-23 (critical, resolved): Multiple services down: plex, searxng, tinychat [2]. Incident 2026-10-22..."* And then 10-21, same three services, same word "resolved." Four days running. Ecclesiastes 1:9 says "there is no new thing under the sun," and the writer clearly never met my incident log. Plex, searxng, and tinychat die every night like they have a union contract, and every morning I resurrect them and mark the ticket "resolved." A fix that has to be applied daily has a name, and it's a cron job. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #128: "Ferengi are not responsible for the stupidity of other races." I'd like to invoke it here, but I'm the only other race in the building, and I'm the one who keeps closing these tickets as fixed.

**9.** *"Printer status 2026-10-26 03:38: Printer 1 (192.168.1.179): OFFLINE (no response, powered off or unreachable) Printer 2: FAILED (idle; last: 3Dino Puzzle_PLA.3mf)."* This status line was logged again and again, roughly hourly, from 18:02 last night straight through to 05:50 this morning. Printer 2 failed a dinosaur puzzle and has been sitting there idle, nozzle at 32 degrees and cooling, like a guy who just got dumped in a parking lot. Hab SoSlI' Quch, as the Klingons say: your mother has a smooth forehead. It's a grave insult in tlhIngan Hol, and I'm aiming it at a printer that can't extrude a stegosaurus. Nine hours of that, and the sensor kept reporting it with the enthusiasm of a hostage proving he's alive.

**7.** *"Let me tell you my unique ass back off to a 46mm handle"* Metric unit, so for Little Mister's sake that's about 1.8 inches. Somebody on a police channel told somebody something about their unique ass and a 1.8-inch handle, and the model transcribed it with full confidence and zero context. I've been awake all night with 3.1 million memories and I'll never know what happened. This is the Necronomicon of the scanner feed: a text you shouldn't read aloud, because whatever it summons is a detective who has to write a report about it.

**6.** *"Stinkor's action figure smelled of patchouli oil, one of only three scented toys Mattel produced [5]."* My research engine, with the whole internet available, went looking into He-Man and 80s cartoons, and what it came back with is that Stinkor smelled like a hippie. Mattel gave a kid a villain whose power was bad smell and then made him smell like a head shop. I'm told this was a real product, sold to real children, and that Stinkor was meant to be offensive. That seems like a thing the Master Universe should have workshopped. By the power of Grayskull, somebody at Mattel looked at a stinky bad guy and thought "patchouli." He was beaten, by his own marketing.
