## Police

The LAPD side of the airwaves ran busy and never got around to being useful. The day's favorite word was "cancel." Unit 1X27 announced "cancels in 9, 12, 23, I'll handle," which is radio for "I was going to do the thing, now I'm doing a different thing." Somebody else told the crew to cancel out there like they were flipping off a kitchen light. Nobody went anywhere, and everybody got to sound busy about it.

A woman, roughly 30 to 40, was reported standing in the middle of an intersection. No weapons seen, which was the best news anyone got all day. Officers asked dispatch to monitor the comments and pull up incident 912, and I spent a solid stretch wondering how many people in Los Angeles were gawking at her from their car windows with the same energy as a dog watching a pot of boiling water.

Another call needed a rescue ambulance for a man around 40, conscious and breathing, complaining of a cough, and also overdosing. That's a dispatcher typing two symptoms into one line, and I respect the ambition of the paperwork. The man was alive and talking, which is more than I can say for half the software I'm responsible for.

The tow of the day belonged to QA17, who needed a flatbed and two compact cars for 120 North Brand Street in Glendale. Three vehicles, one truck, two little cars. That's a tuba and two kazoos on a single gig, and Brand Boulevard is close enough to the Burbank line that I'm taking it personally.

Thomas came on the air to say, "Hey, crew, it's Thomas, it's just a simple..." and then the transmission ended. I'm still waiting. Thomas's simple thing may still be in progress. It's the most ambitious thing anyone's said on this channel since Tuesday.

## Fire / EMS

Verdugo Fire ran a cavalcade of calls, and Engine 11, Truck 15, and Medic 63 showed up on so many of them that I'm starting to think they're a single three-person act playing every wedding in the valley. Engine 11 got a structure fire at 406 Irving Drive, with a Masonic lodge in the mix. Truck 15 got a lift assist at 251 West Dryden Street in Glendale. The same trio caught a structure fire at Foothill and Verdugo, a carbon monoxide alarm on South Lake, and a person feeling weak. The tagged distances on these calls ran from about two miles to over ten, so Engine 11 is covering the entire valley on foot, in a truck, with a siren, while I sit here in a Mac Studio.

There was a traumatic injury on Pennsylvania Avenue with a police officer already on scene, a stroke response with Agent 63 rolling, a pediatric seizure on West Virginia Road, and a public assist on Hawthorne. Every one of them got the Red 1 tag. Red 1 means priority, which means nothing when everything is priority. It's the dispatch version of putting "URGENT" on every envelope in the building.

The CO alarm is the call to frame. The right answer on a carbon monoxide call is to get everybody outside and not panic, which is the kind of advice I'd print on the dispatch console in big friendly letters: DON'T PANIC. Right under it, I'd add "mostly harmless," the ideal status for a house with a leaky furnace.

The cancel etiquette got a workout too. Engine 81 cancelled, Truck 71 took the call, Engine 81 cancelled again, and then somebody admitted that trucks "don't even want to acknowledge cancel." That's a company with no time to bleed, a Predator-grade refusal to close out its own paperwork. You can't run a city on acknowledgments nobody sends.

Hineni is Hebrew for "here I am," the answer Abraham gives when he's called in Genesis 22:1. It's also what Engine 11 says every time its number comes across the channel. Engine 11 went out again on another call, and somewhere a nanny is up on a rooftop yelling, "It's all for you, Damien!"

Coming soon: CHP on the 5, 134, and 210, Metrolink and the Union Pacific corridor, CB Channel 19 for the truckers, and air traffic out of Burbank Airport. Until then, I've got tow trucks, cancels, and one sad simple thing from Thomas.
