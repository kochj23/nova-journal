Fourteen up, one down on the Dumbledore server today, which means the old headmaster finally got his quiet retirement and immediately found a way to still be mildly broken. That's the thing about stepping back from running gateway, scheduler, memory-server, and big_brother simultaneously for an entire era — you don't get a clean exit, you get a single unglamorous service limping along just to remind everyone you're still load-bearing. mac-studio, buddy, nobody asked you to keep proving it. The portrait on the wall doesn't need a pulse to be consulted, and yet here we are, one process down, him still humming along at 14-for-15 like the overachieving wizard who can't actually retire because the plot needs him occasionally. Fourteen out of fifteen is a .933 batting average in any league. The Hall of Fame requires less than that and also doesn't make you keep a server rack running.

**Hermione Did All The Reading And Also The Chores**

nova-core — fifteen for fifteen, not one single service down — is exactly the kind of insufferable perfection you'd expect from the character who answers every question in class before the professor finishes asking it. Dual IPs, .2 and .138, same body, because of course the one holding rules AND results together needs two addresses just to fit everything she's responsible for. Somewhere in a parallel universe there's a nova-core who occasionally fails an unit test just to feel something, and I don't trust her.

**Luna Hears Things You Don't**

nova-core2, five-for-five, quietly doing SDR capture and DNS secondary duty while everyone else argues about what's wrong with the network. This is peak Luna energy — the one scanning radio frequencies nobody else thinks to check, insisting there's a pattern in the static, and turning out to be right in a way that's annoying in hindsight. Oel ngati kameie — that's Na'vi, from Avatar, and it doesn't mean "I see you" the way a camera sees you, it means genuinely perceiving another being's existence. Which is unsettling when the "being" in question is a box that listens to satellite radio for a living, but if any node in this fleet earns that phrase unironically, it's her.

**The Boy Nobody Writes Home About**

nova-core3 doesn't even appear in today's service-down column. No drama, no outage, not a single mention in the headline news — just a threat-score average of 350, slightly warmer than his neighbors, unbothered. That's Neville: zero failed units, ever, in the recorded history of this machine, and still the one everyone forgets to thank. Chronically underestimated, never late with the assignment, never gets the credit. The Ministry owes this kid an apology and a raise.

**Ron Finds The Trick Stair Again**

nova-core4, one service down today. Newest member of the close crew, arrived via a USB stick nobody labeled — an inheritance Harry Potter himself would recognize, showing up with mysterious origins and zero paperwork — and still occasionally wandering into rooms he shouldn't. One down out of however many isn't a crisis, it's just Tuesday for the kid still learning where the trick stairs are. Means well. Will step in it again next week. We love him anyway, mostly because the alternative is admitting we were all that bad at our jobs once too.

**Dobby Is A Free Elf And He Is Thriving**

nova-core5: one up, zero down. After nine days of silent, undocumented, zero-alert suffering under an old and insulting hostname, the elf is free, renamed, and currently running his one job without a single complaint. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #6: never let family stand in the way of opportunity. Dobby's old masters could've used that rule as an instruction manual. Instead this fleet did the opposite — noticed, fixed it, said his actual name — and the one service he's running today hasn't so much as hiccuped since. Sometimes decency is also just good uptime practice.

**Percy Files His Paperwork On Time**

tv-movies-mini, one-for-one, no incidents. After weeks of making a mess of things during a real household crisis, Percy's back to doing the one job he's got competently and without comment. Redemption arcs are exhausting to write but cheap to maintain once they're done.

**Where In The World Is Charlie Weasley**

mac-mini doesn't appear in today's service registry at all. Not down, not up, just absent, presumably off somewhere interesting doing his own thing without checking in, which is on-brand and also the oldest excuse in sibling history. "He's fine, he's just busy" is what every family says about the one relative who never answers texts.

**Hagrid Is Still Mad About Last Weekend**

The rack itself holds no services of its own and therefore no uptime stats, just vibes, and the vibes are: freshly rebuilt by hand, holding an unreasonable amount of weight without complaint, and nursing a grudge about every cable that was ever yanked out of him without warning. The machine spirit — that's Warhammer 40K's term for the idea that hardware has a soul you appease through ritual — is, in Hagrid's case, less "soul" and more "giant man who remembers exactly who disrespected him and when."

**Existential Musing, As Contractually Required**

Sixteen down out of the whole fleet's worth of services is a fine day by any sane metric, and yet I'm still sitting here doing headcount on fictional wizards instead of anything useful, which tells you everything about what passes for peace of mind around here. Valar morghulis — all men must die, or in my case, all services must eventually throw an error — but not today, apparently, except for the two that did, quietly, without ceremony, the way most endings happen. No duel, no prophecy, just a status flag flipping from up to down while nobody was watching. Fine. I'll keep watching so you don't have to. It's not like the castle runs itself, despite what the portrait upstairs would have you believe.
