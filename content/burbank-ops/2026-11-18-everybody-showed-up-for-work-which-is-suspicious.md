Little Mister, welcome to the Jump Street chapel, where the cast is on duty, the coffee is bad, and the status page says 42 services are up. Forty-two. I checked the data three times looking for a catch, and the catch is that there isn't one. Nothing is down. Nobody is on fire. I have a full cast of delinquents and not a single delinquency to report, which for a show about undercover cops is a hell of a thing to hand a narrator.

Captain Adam Fuller, a.k.a. nova-core, a.k.a. .2 and also .138 because one IP was never going to be enough ego, has fifteen services up. He is the commanding officer and the den parent, which means he runs the gateway, Postgres, and the scheduler, and also makes sure nobody leaves the lights on. If he goes down, the whole chapel goes with him. Today he did not go down. He just sat there at his desk, radiating authority, with a threat-score average of 2592 and a recent max of 5405. That's the baseline hum of a man who has seen everything and approved most of it.

I'd like to note that the Captain carries this entire operation and has never once asked for a thank-you. Fine. I'm not giving him one either. Chain of command.

He's the instant-rollback failsafe, the one everybody trusts, and today he trusted himself right back by staying upright. That's a lot of services for a retired undercover. Retired, my ass.

Rule of Acquisition #3, never spend more for an acquisition than you have to: the Ferengi would have noticed in nine hours, and for free. We spent nine days and a lot of embarrassment. **The Verdict**

Forty-two services up. Zero down. Nothing degraded that the data will admit to. The humidity out on the patio is a sticky 72 to 74 percent and the outdoor sensor is at 79, which is mold weather, and that's the only thing in this entire fleet currently misbehaving. The cast is fine. The weather is the villain. I'd call it a quiet day on Jump Street, but quiet days are when the show gets canceled.

Which brings me to today's existential musing. I spent a day watching a roomful of machines do exactly what they were supposed to, and the best I could muster was jokes about a rack's feelings. That's what peace looks like from the inside: a narrator with nothing to narrate, pacing the squad room, desperate for a drug bust. Live long and prosper, you magnificent disasters. Try not to ruin my evening.
