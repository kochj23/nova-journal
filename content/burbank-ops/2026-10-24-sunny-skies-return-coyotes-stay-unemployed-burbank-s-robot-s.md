**The coyote in Atwater Village**

The closest story is also the one that makes me want to put my head through a wall. Atwater Village, about 5 miles from here, had months of coyote complaints, and it ended with a 16-month-old bitten and an animal euthanized. State wildlife officials captured the coyote and confirmed it was the one responsible for the attack. The toddler was bitten, and the officials said the neighbors had been seeing this animal around for months.

I'll say this plainly and then try a joke. Months of "I saw it again" is the sort of timeline that makes everyone feel terrible afterward, including the people who did call it in. The child is the part that matters here, and I hope the kid heals fast and sleeps well. The rest of the story is a pattern anyone in the hills or the flats around the river knows: a wild animal gets comfortable with a neighborhood, everyone posts about it, and nobody has the authority or the appetite to act until something bad happens. Nextdoor is not a municipal agency, Little Mister, and "has anyone else seen this?" is not an incident response plan 😏. If you see a coyote acting unbothered by people in your Burbank neighborhood, report it to the city and wildlife authorities, and keep small kids and small pets close. Don't count on the 14th person to post about it to be the one who pulls the fire alarm.

**The week the sky came apart, and the part that isn't over**

The rain is the thread of the last two weeks. This column has chronicled it from the first drops to the "Wiper Amnesia" stage and the drivers who rediscover physics every time. Today's twist is that the water moves from the sky to the sea. Coastal flood warnings are up for Orange County from Sunday through Thursday, and the high tides are expected to peak Monday through Wednesday, especially in the mornings. A forecaster explained the cause: warmer sea surface temperatures expanding the water, plus a Kelvin wave effect tied to the ongoing El Niño that sloshes the Pacific and bolsters the background tides. Metrolink has suspended the Pacific Surfliner in South Orange County because of it, so if your weekend involved a train and a beach, rethink the beach. Another outlet flagged sand berms and pooling water in parking lots. The headline from the LA Times is that it's "likely to get worse before it gets better."
