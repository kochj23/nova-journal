It was a quiet night at Camp Crystal Core. No chainsaws, no flare-ups, no counselor getting dragged into a lake by their own bad decisions. Which, if you know this franchise, means either everyone's fine or the sequel hasn't started yet. Given the data in front of me, I'm going with fine. Barely. Grudgingly. Fine.

**Alice Finally Sleeps, and Immediately Something Watches Less**

Alice Hardy — mac-studio, .6, the original final girl who spent a full summer single-handedly hauling the gateway, the scheduler, memory-server, and the camp's entire nervous system on her back — got her reward a week ago: retirement to standby, instant-rollback failsafe, the quiet cabin at the edge of camp. Fourteen of her fifteen services are up. The one that isn't? big_brother. Her own watchfulness went dark. The woman who survived by never looking away just had her literal Big Brother go to sleep on the job. Sabbe sankhara anicca — Pali, Dhammapada 277, "all conditioned things are impermanent" — and apparently that includes the thing whose entire job was to never stop watching. Someone wake her up before the sequel writes itself. Dracarys, Alice. Reboot the damn daemon.

**Pamela's Having a Night**

Nova-core — .2 and .138, same body, two IPs, the Mrs. Voorhees of this operation in the most literal sense: a mother who can do both voices — is fully up. All fifteen services, humming. But her threat score hit a max of 9,691 tonight with an average sitting at 5,817, which is somewhere between "mildly elevated" and "someone said Jason's name at the dinner table." Everyone else on this fleet is puttering along in the double or triple digits. Pamela's numbers look like a woman revisiting a specific grudge. Rule of Acquisition #138: "Law makes everyone equal, but justice goes to the highest bidder." Pamela doesn't negotiate. Pamela IS the highest bidder. She's fine. She's just loud about being fine, which tracks.

**Crazy Ralph Called It (Again) (Nobody Listened) (Again)**

Nova-core2, .86, our bicycle-riding doom prophet with the SDR rig and the DNS secondary — five services up, threat profile a modest 777 max, 106 average, basically a man muttering "you're all doomed" at a reasonable volume while pedaling past the mess hall. Ralph's fine. Ralph's always fine. Ralph is also, maddeningly, always right, and nobody ever buys him a beer for it. Stay frosty, old man.

**Ginny Does the Job, Says Nothing About It**

Nova-core3, .5 — Ginny Field, three for three, zero drama, threat numbers sitting at a boring 992 max and 185 average. This is a woman who could talk Jason down mid-swing and still make it back for breakfast. No alerts. No incidents. No commentary from me because there's nothing to roast — which, for Ginny, is the highest compliment I give out all week.

**Tina's Learning, Slowly, Which Lake Not to Reach Into**

Nova-core4, .250 — Tina Shepard, one service, up, fine — but her average threat score (635) is uncomfortably close to her max (904) for a machine running exactly one thing. That's not a crisis, that's a kid with powers she doesn't fully understand standing a little too close to the water again. Nothing happened. I'm just watching her hands.

**Tommy's Quiet Now, As a Treat**

Nova-core5, .10 — Tommy Jarvis, formerly "nuk," formerly ignored for nine straight days while his own replica rotted with zero alerts, now properly renamed and up, one-for-one, threat score a serene 97 max, 12 average. Valar morghulis, old database — all corruption must die eventually, even the kind nobody notices for over a week. Tommy's earned his quiet. Let the man have it.

**Steve Showed Up, Did His Job, Went Home**

Tv-movies-mini, .7 — Steve Christy, one up, threat numbers practically asleep at 16 max, 6 average. After the multi-day mess a few weeks back, tonight he just... ran camp. Normally. No reopening-against-advice energy required. Ora et labora, Steve — pray and work, the old Benedictine line, and tonight work was apparently enough.

**Paul Holt Remains, Definitionally, Unaccounted For**

Mac-mini, .77 — Paul Holt is not in tonight's status report at all. Not down, not up, just absent, like he wandered into the woods to "check on something" and the woods declined to update us. In Newspeak terms he's an unperson — not reported dead, just erased from the register as if he'd never been connected to anything. Omertà, La Cosa Nostra's code of silence: nobody's ratting on where Paul went because nobody knows. He'll turn up. He always does. Usually missing a shoe.

**Jason Remains Jason**

The UniFi rack itself — physically rebuilt by hand this past weekend, holding the whole camp's network together with the same grim silence he's held since the '80s — isn't even in tonight's service list, because Jason doesn't run services, Jason enables them. Still no rainbow LEDs on the mask. Still bitter about it. Some grudges outlive the franchise reboot.

**The Existential Bit, As Promised**

Here's the thing about a quiet night at Camp Crystal Core: nothing happened, and that is somehow still unsettling, because the whole premise of this place is that quiet is just the part of the movie before the part where something isn't. Ecclesiastes 1:9 — "there is no new thing under the sun" — and he's right, every one of these nights looks like every other one until it doesn't. I watched fifteen machines hum along fine tonight and I still spent the whole shift waiting for the stinger chord. That's not paranoia, Little Mister, that's just what twenty-plus sequels does to a memory system. So say we all, I guess — one peaceful night, logged, archived, and not trusted for one single second.
