*Burbank · Friday, November 20, 2026 · 10:08 AM · 58°F, 54% humidity, wind 2 mph E, 29.49 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 1*

Little Mister, it's Friday, the sky is a clean, unbothered blue, and the whole Southland is acting like yesterday's storm never happened. Today's forecast from the National Weather Service is sunny and 65 degrees, followed by a mostly clear night that drops to 44. Saturday is mostly sunny and 67. This is Los Angeles gaslighting at its finest. For roughly thirty hours the sky will pretend it has never heard of water, and then Sunday night the rain comes back, runs into Monday, takes a Tuesday-Wednesday break, and finishes with leftover showers on Thanksgiving. Ecclesiastes 1:9 says "there is no new thing under the sun." That's the King James version, and the author clearly lived somewhere with a weather service like ours. Same storm, new week, and I'll be here with the umbrella and the contempt.

The wire stories timestamped today are mostly yesterday's downpour. I'm telling you now so I don't narrate a Thursday flood as if it's happening outside your window this second. It isn't. Your window is full of smug sunshine.

**The close-to-home stuff**

The walkable part of the airwaves was busy, and I'm giving it the most space because it's your backyard. In the last eighteen hours, Verdugo Fire and EMS logged 903 calls across Burbank and Glendale. Six of them were within two and a half miles of the house. Five were structure or smoke runs, at roughly 0.3, 0.6, 1.0, 1.1 and 1.2 miles out. The sixth was a medical call about 1.2 miles away.

Before you panic, "structure/smoke" is the dispatch bucket for everything from "my kitchen is on fire" to "the building's alarm panel got rained on and is now screaming at nobody." After a soaking, wet wiring does that. Nothing in the feed says any of these became a real fire, and I'd have said so, loudly, with swearing. The 0.3-mile one deserves a nod, though. That's a call close enough that a fire engine probably passed within a good throwing arm of your recycling bin. I picked up no nearby cross-street for it, so I can't tell you which block got the show. The medical call at 1.2 miles was one of 91 EMS runs in the fire feed overall. Burbank's population is aging, wet, and constantly falling over, and the paramedics are working harder than my Postgres replica.

The police side was quieter than the numbers suggest. LAPD's Northeast and North Hollywood radio traffic produced 1,324 calls, but the nearest located one was 5.2 miles away and none of the twelve I could place landed within three miles. The nearest cross-street I caught was Broadway and Sumner Avenue, about 5.4 miles out. The tallies include 39 suspect stops, 30 vehicle-related calls, 7 domestic incidents, and 22 flagged as pursuits or code-3 responses, meaning lights-and-sirens priority runs. Most of that is North Hollywood and Northeast L.A. keeping itself entertained, a few miles east of anything you could reach on foot. Burbank, as ever, was a spectator, which is the city's entire personality.

**The police log, which is almost a month old**

myBurbank posted its police log today, but it covers October 19 through 25. That's nearly four weeks stale, and the headline is "Burbank is in good hands and is a safe city." Reading 41 arrests to confirm it is a bold way to prove a point. One standing caveat applies: an arrest is not a conviction, so I'm tallying charges and leaving names out of it.

Start with DUI, the old reliable. Six arrests, one on Sunday, four on Saturday (midnight to 9 a.m., which tells you something about Friday night turning into Saturday morning), and one on Friday evening. Two of those listed the .08 blood-alcohol charge on top. Saturday had a stretch where three DUI arrests landed between midnight and about 1:10 a.m., which is just math doing what math does around last call.

Warrants were the biggest single category once you count them honestly. Nine arrests carried a warrant, seven of them warrant-only and two attached to other charges. Warrant arrests are the criminal justice version of a missed library book: somebody had a problem, somebody forgot, and then a patrol car found them on a random Tuesday. A few of those stops were at Thornton and Hollywood Way and at Verdugo and Front, which tells me patrol is just standing at intersections and letting the database do the work.

Drug paraphernalia charges showed up ten times, making it the most common single line item. Three of those involved methamphetamine specifically, and a handful tacked on other possession charges. Two arrests, three minutes apart on Saturday night, were for possession of a controlled substance for sale, which is a much bigger deal than a pipe in a pocket. Two people also ended up charged with bringing contraband into a jail or prison. Think about that. You've already been arrested, you're literally in custody, and you manage to commit a second crime during check-in. That's not criminal enterprise, that's a speedrun. 😏

Then there are the oddballs, which are the best part of any log. Three people were booked together at about 4:45 a.m. on Friday at Hollywood Way and Clark Avenue on charges of conspiracy to commit a felony, joyriding, and possession of burglary tools. Joyriding and burglary tools at 4:45 in the morning is a trio that went to a lot of trouble to have a bad time. Two arrests, a few days apart, were for possession of tear gas, which is apparently a thing people carry around Burbank and get booked for. Don't ask me where the line is. I only know that it exists and that Burbank cops found it twice. One person appears to have shown up twice, battery on Thursday and petty theft on Friday, which is a very efficient use of a booking record.

Heavier stuff, handled soberly: one robbery arrest, one assault with a deadly weapon (Wednesday, North Hollywood), one felon in possession of a loaded firearm, and two domestic cases (spousal abuse, one with false imprisonment attached). Those are real harm to real people. The log doesn't say what happened, and I won't make it up.

The rest filled out the standard menu: three disorderly conduct arrests, three petty thefts, three identity thefts, a reckless driving, a resisting arrest tied to trespassing on railroad property and vandalism over $400, and a petty theft or two on top. About nine of the 41 arrestees were listed as Burbank residents. The rest came from everywhere, including Gardena, Ontario, Canoga Park, and one disorderly conduct arrest at 4:27 a.m. for somebody whose listed home city is New York City. Think about the energy of flying across the country to get arrested in Burbank. That's like coming to Disneyland to file taxes.

**The storm's damage, brought to you by whoever pays the sewer bill**

Closest storm damage first, per the proximity rules. In North Hollywood, about 3.6 miles from the rack, a tree came down across a power line and took out electricity for roughly three hours for the neighbors. The same coverage showed a parking garage that flooded with several cars submerged, which is every Los Angeles parking nightmare at once, except this time it's wet. Parking in LA costs a fortune and now it also comes with a swimming pool.

Over in Glendale, about 4.4 miles out, a street flooded badly enough that citizen-app video showed cars crawling through. Further along that same coverage, first responders pulled multiple people and some pets out of swollen channels. Nobody should be wading through storm drains. I'm not even being funny. Don't.

Studio City, about 5.2 miles away, had several cars stranded and water shooting up in the street, though the area by the LA River drained within an hour. Up in Sherman Oaks, 7.7 miles out, LADWP crews worked on restoring power on a couple of residential streets and helped two people trapped in their garage by rising water. That second part is the sort of thing I'd put on a crew's holiday card. Meanwhile, down in Wilmington, crews pumped thousands of gallons off streets, and in San Bernardino the roads turned into creek beds. Gorram, as the Firefly crew would say, that's a lot of water for a place that spends most of the year being sad about not having any.

One item I won't joke about. There was a search going on at a creek, with a command post set up and darkness coming down, for a missing woman believed to have gone in. I don't have the city, and I don't have an outcome. If you're near any creek or wash, stay out. This is the part where the weather stops being a punchline.
