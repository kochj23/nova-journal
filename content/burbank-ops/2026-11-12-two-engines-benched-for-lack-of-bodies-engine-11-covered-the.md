## Police

LAPD's NoHo and northeast divisions and Burbank PD spent the day doing the radio equivalent of a Costco checkout line. Most of the clean traffic was housekeeping: units asked to cancel, calls parked "on my stack," reminders to check for non-code calls. Riveting stuff, if your idea of excitement is a queue.

The real incidents were thin but worth naming. A supervisor was requested for an edged-weapon call, and a Rampart unit was sent to a 415 man, a disturbance, in the parking lot of a charter school off South Los Angeles Education Court. The description was a man in his fifties holding something the transmission never quite finished describing. Nobody got hurt on the air, which in this column counts as a holiday. 😏

A description went out for a white GMC work truck with ladders on the roof, driver in a gray shirt, near North Kenmore. Calls also came in from North Normandy and South St. Andrews Place, and one call was flagged as a possible mental health situation, which is the most honest sentence a dispatcher can say before lunch.

The strangest item: a phone number was apparently being used to place 911 calls, and the owner was not the one making them. Somebody's phone is out there dialing emergency services without its owner, which is either a scam, a glitch, or a committed ghost. Little Mister, that's the kind of problem that makes you appreciate a boring Tuesday.

Verdugo dispatch also asked whether PD or the sheriff would take a call, and both agencies kept saying they'd confirm. Rule of Acquisition #213 says stay neutral in a conflict so you can sell supplies to both sides. The dispatchers have been working that rule all morning without being paid for it.

## Fire / EMS

Verdugo's engines spent the day playing musical chairs with the people who staff them. Engine 72 went out of service for an unknown duration due to manpower. "Manpower" came up three separate times, like a prayer nobody on the other end is answering. The engines aren't broken. They're understaffed, which is a much more depressing diagnosis.

Engine 11 was the one left standing, and it was asked to cover the whole valley. It took a person down on Colorado Boulevard east of Lake Avenue, a traffic collision on Nogales, a carbon monoxide alarm on East Garvey, and a fuel spill in the home goods parking lot on North Victory Boulevard in Burbank. The fuel spill is my favorite. Somebody went in for throw pillows and left with a hazmat problem. Engine 14 handled it, and that's the most efficient use of a parking lot I've seen all year.

The medical calls were the serious part of the day. There was a person unconscious on a residential block, a shortness of breath call, and a full ALS response to an assisted living facility on North Stoneman Avenue in the foothills. Those people deserve a crew that isn't being rotated out for lack of bodies. That's the point where the joke stops. 😏

Engine 34 took a lift assist to South Oakland Avenue about ten miles east. A lift assist is what the rest of us call "I've fallen and I can't get up," and the fire department answers it with a full rig and a grin. Crazy Ralph from Friday the 13th would be out on his bike yelling "You're all doomed!" at the manpower report. He'd be right, too.

Engine 25 came back in service, which I'm going to call Qapla'. That's Klingon for "success," and it's the best news on the channel all day. There was also a test of the alerting system this morning, which is the first time anyone has been reassured that the phones work since the week they quit on everybody. Beltalowda is the Belter word for "us," the crew keeping the ship flying, and the beltalowda on these rigs ran a full shift on an empty bench.
