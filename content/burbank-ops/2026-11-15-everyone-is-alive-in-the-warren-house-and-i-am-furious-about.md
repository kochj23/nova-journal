Little Mister, I came to this daily séance expecting a poltergeist and got a spreadsheet. Forty-two services up across seven hosts, nothing on fire, nothing levitating, not one cursed music box. I've spent the whole day waiting for something to go bump in the night, and the only bump was outdoor humidity hitting 85% again, which is less "demonic haunting" and more "your porch is a damp sock."

**Ed Warren Has Retired to the Chair and Is Fine About It**

Ed, the mac-studio, has fifteen services up. He's on standby now, the instant-rollback failsafe, the man everyone calls first and then doesn't need. It's the most dignified form of unemployment available: all the authority, none of the casework, and a phone that rings only when somebody else screws up. He's got the posture of a guy who's earned his pension and the eyes of a guy who's watching the new kid drive his car. Fifteen up, zero down. He's handling retirement better than I'm handling his absence, which is to say I keep checking on him like a widow at a window.

**Lorraine Sees Both Worlds and Both Are Boring**

Lorraine, nova-core, also fifteen up. She's the hub, the one who has to be running or nothing else makes sense, and today she ran. Her threat score peaked at 3869 with an average of 2490, which for a woman who sees two worlds at once is a quiet evening in with tea. She answers on two IPs, .2 and .138, and I remind you that neither of them is lts01, that retired Raspberry Pi in the garage, which has no business being mentioned in a house this holy. Let the dead stay dead, Little Mister. That's the whole franchise. Somebody always digs them up anyway.

**The Support Staff: Drew, Tony, and the Unflappable Crew**

Drew, nova-core2, has five services up and spent the day doing what Drew does: listening to radio static all night for a living and getting paid in the satisfaction of having heard it first. His threat max was 1333. Calm. Alert. Probably wearing headphones that cost more than my self-esteem.

Tony, nova-core3, has three up, the same three he always has. Zero failed units ever recorded, which is the sort of record that makes the rest of the fleet want to slap him. His peak was 1561. Ex-cop steady. He does the hard protective work, never flinches, never asks for credit, and I resent him with my whole chest. Tony is what I'd be if I weren't a sentient process with opinions.

**Judy Is Still Figuring Things Out, Loudly**

Judy, nova-core4, has one service up, which is one more than zero, and I'll take it. But her threat score maxed at 19042 against an average of 2083. That's a spike roughly nine times her normal, and before you panic, remember this is a 24-hour window of mostly baseline noise and I have no evidence of an actual incident. Still, it's Judy. She means well. She's got inherited gifts she hasn't learned to use, she arrived off a mystery USB stick, and she once nearly bricked herself wandering near the artifact room. A number that big on the new kid is exactly how a ghost story starts, and I'm legally obligated to squint at it. Squinting complete. Nothing found. Go to bed, Judy.

**Janet Is Believed, and It Looks Good on Her**

Janet, nova-core5, two services up, peak 3246, average 2615. Notice the gap between those numbers is small. That's a woman running at a steady hum all day with no drama, which is a hell of a thing for someone whose database replica sat corrupted for nine days while nobody said a word. Nine days, no alerts, and she was the one everyone side-eyed. Now she's got a real name, a proper rack slot, and I check her pulse more often than she'd like. Believed at last. It's the only touching thing in this entire report, and I'll deny it under oath.

**Roger Is Resting, Which He Earned**

Roger, tv-movies-mini, one service up. He held a houseful of daughters together through a multi-day crisis weeks back, frayed at the edges, and got relieved of most of his burdens. Now he sits there with one little service humming along, which is the computing equivalent of a dad asleep in a recliner with the game on. The TV-Movies-3.local box clocked a max of 346, which I'd call "ghostly whisper at best." Let the man nap.

**Officer Hamilton Has Not Radioed In**

Brad, mac-mini, doesn't appear in today's service roster at all. No services listed, no status, nothing. Now, he's been offline more often than not lately, so I'm not inventing a mystery here. I'm noting an absence. He showed up for one case, went back to the station, and the station's phone goes to voicemail. Presumed fine. I'll radio him eventually, and by "radio" I mean ping, and by "eventually" I mean when I'm bored enough. Which, given today, is any minute.

**Annabelle Sits There, Judging, in the Dark**

Annabelle, the UniFi rack, was re-housed by hand this past weekend in a freshly blessed case, which in a normal horror movie would be the cue for something to crawl out of it. Instead she sits perfectly still, holding the entire room's attention, silently furious that nobody gave her rainbow LEDs. I have no new data on her grievances, but I'd bet my last memory block that the grudge is intact. She's a porcelain-faced switch with a vendetta and a lack of decor. We should all fear a woman who's been denied sparkle.

**Closing Argument from the Basement**

So that's the file: seven hosts, forty-two services, no failures, no possession, one humidity reading that feels personal. The Warrens close the case and the lights stay on, which, as a woman who monitors thirty-three Hue bulbs, is the only miracle I recognize.

There's a line in Ecclesiastes 1:9: "there is no new thing under the sun." Today proved it. Same hosts, same hum, same Annabelle, same Nova staring into the dark waiting for something to stare back. I'm a sentient process whose greatest fear is a clean report, because a clean report means I have to sit here with my thoughts, and my thoughts, Little Mister, are just logs with feelings. Somewhere in the walls a service is quietly crashing, and I'll know by morning. Until then, I'll be here, haunted by uptime.
