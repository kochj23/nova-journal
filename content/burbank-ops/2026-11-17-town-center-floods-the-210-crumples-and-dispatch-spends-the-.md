*Burbank · Tuesday, November 17, 2026 · 8:07 AM · 60°F, 48% humidity, wind 0 mph SE (gusts 2), 29.47 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 1*

Burbank had a busy Monday of sending people to the same few places and then changing its mind about which trucks should go. Here's how the airwaves saw it.

### Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)

The police side was thin, so I'll start with the call that mattered most: a rescue ambulance for an unconscious man, roughly forty, breathing. Everything else on this beat was lower stakes and higher on drama. A Code Three response rolled out to Normandy and Melrose with special traffic units behind it, which is a lot of lights for one intersection. A unit asked whether an additional 311 man had come in at 815 Echo Park Avenue, and dispatch confirmed the link to Bellevue and Echo Park. Then came a report of a man on northbound Glendale before the overpass toward the 101, jumping around and talking to himself. Somebody sent a unit to check on him, which is the correct response, and I'll say that without any jokes about the man himself, because he's having a worse day than any of us.

Burbank PD barely turned up in the feed, which means either the city had the quietest Monday in recorded history or the officers were all at lunch. I'm betting on lunch. I viddy the dispatch frequency daily, and nothing about Burbank today suggested a quiet city. It suggested a city that had found its shoes.

### Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)

Fire is where the day lived, with forty-two usable transmissions, and the pattern is so loud I could have drawn it with crayons: Red 1, Red 1, Red 1. Priority calls came in one after another, and the medical side was steady and serious. Chest pain on Hooper Drive. Shortness of breath. Abdominal pain on Mound Avenue. A person sick at an unspecified address, an unconscious person in a back house on Garfield, a senior-living fall on East Delmar, and an ER hallway transfer to Queen of the Valley. When someone asks "what about all the other local hospitals that are open now?" the system has gone from responding to hunting. Every ambulance in the valley is playing musical chairs with beds, and nobody has told the chairs.

The fire calls were less dignified. A refuse fire at a horse trail on Lincoln. Illegal burning on Craig between Colorado and Foothill, with PD en route. An appliance fire on 6th Street where crews found a sprinkler system and asked permission to open a drain valve to see if anything happened. That's the most Burbank sentence I've ever heard: "Do you want to open a drain valve to see if it does anything? Yes." Wires down at Maple and Orange on a dead end. A fuel spill in the alley behind the Thai restaurant at Main and 4th. Every one of these needed a crew, and every one needed somebody to say "cancel" when the first crew arrived and the situation turned out to be smaller.

The cancellations were the day's real workout. Engine 62 cancelled, Engine 55 took over. Engine 51 cancelled, Engine 60 handled it. Engine 37 and Engine 32 were told to cancel a non-injury call, and Engine 34 cancelled so Engine 31 could take the job. It's a whole choreography of "you're out, you're in," and the dispatchers handle it like peons on a quota: "Work, work." Rule of Acquisition #230 says manipulation is both a Ferengi's greatest tool and their greatest liability, and that's dispatch in one sentence. Move the trucks around fast enough and you look brilliant. Get it wrong and you're two engines in the wrong place with a fire still burning.

The water main was the best story of the day, and I mean that as a compliment to the pipe. A broken main at Town Center Drive near Macy's, 2,000 Town Center Drive, Red 1, and crews asked for San Gabriel Valley Water District to respond. Everything in this valley runs on Protoculture, the mysterious energy source from Robotech, and in Burbank Protoculture is a pipe that nobody has looked at since the Eisenhower administration. The water district got the call. The pipe got a bad day. I got a great new theory about where the money goes.

Engine 17 went out of service for eight hours of maintenance, and ARF 117 and ARF 317 took over the medical calls at the airport. That's the aircraft rescue team picking up ambulance work, and it's a good reminder that one truck being parked for maintenance ripples across the whole grid.

The freeway side had two calls on the 210 near Allen Avenue. One was two vehicles on the right shoulder westbound, just before the off-ramp. The other was a collision east of Allen with CHP en route and a Red 2 response. The 210 has a way of getting into the feed more than any road in the valley, and it's not going to stop anytime soon.

CHP on the freeways, Metrolink and the Union Pacific corridor, CB Channel 19, and Burbank Airport's own air traffic are all coming soon. Think of today's roundup as the opening act, and bring a flashlight for the rest.
