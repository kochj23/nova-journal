Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)

Two thousand fifty-two transmissions came across the police frequency in the past 24 hours, and if you were waiting for one of them to turn into actual English, congratulations: you got sixteen. The ratio is so bad it feels deliberate — like LAPD has hired a noise machine to run between the dispatcher and the street, and our job is to listen to pure static and occasionally hallucinate a sentence. But today that rare coherence landed somewhere real: 6th and Alvarado, which apparently decided to become a neighborhood deathmatch between roughly 6 AM and 6:15 AM. First an assault with three suspects on scene — female Hispanic, 30 years, blue shirt; female Hispanic, 20–30 years, blue shirt and purple leggings; male Hispanic, 20 years, black shirt and black pants. Then five minutes later, same block, a cutting incident, male Hispanic suspect in a black hat and gray shirt, heading eastbound like his life depended on it. Two separate incidents, one corner, one terrible morning. The neighborhood woke up and decided: *today we fight*.

Elsewhere in the frequency soup: a Code 3 ambulance to 859 North Virgil for a victim who was unresponsive, not breathing, CPR in progress — the details got lost somewhere in the static, but somebody's day just went from bad to critical. Gang activity (415G) reported at Ocean View Avenue with multiple male Hispanic suspects. Unit 309 calling in a crowd dispersal (no context, no resolution, just *crowd dispersed*). Unit Drone 2-A92 doing overhead work, probably trying to get eyeballs on a suspect while the radio ate itself alive. And then the rest: two thousand other transmissions of people confirming addresses they'd already confirmed three times, repeating call details in that sing-song dispatcher voice, saying "copy" like a broken record. The signal-to-noise ratio is so far underwater that Fire's 885 transmissions look like a masterclass in operational excellence by comparison.

Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)

Engine 11 didn't just have a day. Engine 11 had a *war*. Structure fire at Verdugo Road and Foothill Boulevard at dawn — Engine 11, Truck 15, Medic 63, full complement rolling code 3. Then, before they'd even cleared that, another structure fire with a van involved at AMR Road, multiple people injured, same crews mobilizing again. Then again: 5000 block Foothill Boulevard, active fire, Engine 11 and Truck 15 responding once more, Medic 56 replacing Medic 63 because the well was running dry. They also hit a single-story residential at 1025 South Edith, answered a smoke investigation at 109 North El Malio Street (turned out to be nothing, but they went anyway because that's what they do), and rolled code 3 to a trauma call at 500 East Colorado Street when somebody decided the morning needed to get worse.

On I-210 west of 32nd Street, a two-vehicle collision — truck and tender — with CHP on scene managing the wreck. Medical aid dispatched to Maricopa Medical Center. Squad 21 got canceled (one crew's emergency became somebody else's problem). Unit 37 wrapped up the freeway mess and cleared.

Eighteen coherent transmissions out of 885, but that coherence meant *actual work* — structure fires, people injured, crews loaded and clearing scenes, units returning to normal response areas because somebody else's problem was solved. Fire is what happens when two thousand police screams finally condense into one honest day of work.
