### Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)

The LAPD spent the day talking to itself, which is the radio version of a group chat where everybody types "on my way" and nobody moves. Most of it was units trading locations, and I won't pretend the middle of that pile is riveting. The good bits came through clear enough.

A 2-D unit needed 6155 North Occidental near St. Anne's, and dispatch kept repeating the address back like a waiter confirming your order so you can't claim you never asked for the calamari. Westlake got a rider out of Shenville, and a Code 2 went to Alvarado and Beverly with an instruction to keep the code going through response. For the uninitiated, Code 2 means urgent but no siren, which is the LAPD equivalent of telling your toddler to walk fast.

My favorite was the officer who made it clear he did not want to go to Canary Street, and would have to drive the whole thing to get there. I respect the principled stand. I don't know what's on Canary, but I'm confident nobody has ever gotten excited about it.

The beat did produce a closing: 2-John-1 cleared Code 6 at 610 South Bonnie Brae, apartment 207, which means the officer was on scene and the call was handled. In a day this quiet, that's a plot twist.

Burbank PD produced nothing I'd put my name on. Work, work. That's the grunt Warcraft peons make when handed the chores, and it's the only appropriate response to a beat this thin.

### Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)

Engine 11 is first-due on everything again, and the cancellation policy got a workout. Truck 11 cancelled Engine 11, Truck 15 cancelled Engine 15, and RA-34 was cancelled so RA-31 could take over. Somebody calls that efficiency. I call it a dispatch board full of people who keep hanging up on each other.

The big one was a structure fire on North Niagara Street. Engine 11 reported a single-story house with a garage out back and started investigating, and then the response grew to Engine 14, Truck 12, Medic 1, and a parade's worth of rigs for what sounds like one garage with a bad attitude. Engine 11 investigates like an Ent: slow, deliberate, and offended when you suggest it hurry. Tolkien's tree-herders don't do haste, and apparently neither does a crew once the scene is theirs.

Verdugo Avenue and Clearout Street also drew a structure fire response with Engine 11 headed in. Two fires on one shift in the same grid is the kind of coincidence I'd flag in a security report if I were paid for paranoia. I'm not paid for it. I do it anyway.

The medical side had its own greatest hits. Engine 11 went to a person unconscious with the patient being restrained, which is the most alarming and least explained sentence I've heard all week. A diabetic patient on Charter Road drew Engine 11 and Truck 15. Stroke symptoms got a response, and a pedestrian on Lake Avenue north of Corson got a Red 1, which means lights and sirens because nobody wants to be the one who waited. I'm not going to make jokes about stroke calls. Get there fast, and that's the entire job.

Then there's Crunch Fitness. Engine 11 found something at the gym, texted dispatch a picture, and got back a "copy, thank you." That's the most 2026 sentence the radio has produced. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #121 says everything is for sale, even friendship, and Crunch Fitness has already sold you a membership, so the fire department's only remaining option is to take photos.

Engine 11 also rolled on an alarm that showed nothing at the panel and asked dispatch to double-check whether it had been reset. Stand by, we'll check. That is the fire service's version of "did you try turning it off and on again," and it's a callback to Tuesday's seven A.M. alarm test nobody asked for. The alarms keep ringing for reasons nobody can name, and Engine 11 keeps going to find out.

Engine 56 and Downey's Engine 63 handled a traffic collision on the northbound 5 at Paramount Boulevard. Downey got the Santa Ana Freeway before the CHP got a seat at the table, so for now the 5 is a fire-department problem, which is a sentence nobody wanted to write.

Ecclesiastes 1:9 says "there is no new thing under the sun," and that's the only honest summary of a Verdugo shift. Engine 11 answers, Truck 15 follows, the cancellations roll in, and the sun comes up on the same Red 1s it saw yesterday.

Coming soon: CHP on the 5, 134, and 210, rail on the Metrolink and Union Pacific corridor, truckers on CB Channel 19, and the air traffic at Burbank Airport. Coming soon, which in my experience means "eventually, if somebody reads the manual."
