**Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)**

Two thousand three hundred eighty-nine transmissions in twenty-four hours, and only twenty-two of them came through clean enough for me to repeat without a translator and a prayer. The rest was the usual airwave soup, the kind of nonsense that makes me wonder whether the dispatchers are running on coffee or pure spite. Still, the coherent bits tell a story. A 2006 black Lexus IS-300 went missing, and the call was to secure 722 South Pixel Street, which is the single most dramatic address anyone has ever uttered over a radio. Somebody's car got taken, and the sequel is the stolen-vehicle hunt. I'll be watching the plate for you, Little Mister, because the last thing this city needs is another Lexus joyriding past my cameras like it owns the 5.

Unit 25 had a pair of traffic stops, one at Montevista and Abney and another at Montevista itself, and both came with the same plate-reading ritual that every cop on earth seems to think is a secret handshake. The stops were uneventful, which is the best thing a traffic stop can be, and I'll take it. Then there was a brief shout-out about a suspect cooperating with his son, which I'm choosing to believe is the most wholesome sentence in the entire day.

The one that made me sit up was the aggravated assault. A female, around thirty-seven, conscious and breathing, and the request for a rescue ambulance to a ramp or front desk. Nobody gets to be cheerful about that. Whatever happened to her, I hope the people on the other end of that radio got her the help she needed fast.

A few other calls were more about dispatch housekeeping than crime. Someone was told to submit a call, someone else was going to figure something out, and a Code 6 went out on an incident that apparently did not need a whole ceremony. There was a flag-down tied to an incident, a couple of parties who left a location at 2039, and one stubborn line that insisted on telling everyone "there's no logic, thank you so much." I agree, and I'd add that the logic of the entire police frequency is best described as "vibes, plus radio discipline."

**Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)**

Eight hundred forty-one transmissions, thirty-three coherent. Engine 11 appeared so often I started to think it had a timeshare on the frequency. Engine 11 was sent to Oak Street, Alameda Avenue, Lamour Street, and Atlanta, got cancelled, got re-dispatched, got confirmed, and got cancelled again, all while trying to look busy in three different cities. If Engine 11 were a Hue light, I would have turned it off by now out of pure annoyance.

The real moment of the day was the structure fire at 200 North Atlantic Boulevard, where Engine 11 and a King unit rolled out. That is a proper fire, and Engine 11 did what engines are supposed to do. The house fire on Alwood Street, with the back house on fire near Alender and Walnut, got a full alarm treatment: a battalion chief, a command post, and primary and secondary attack lines assigned. Good work by the crew, and I hope the folks in that back house got out safely.

The medical calls were a mixed bag. There was a 22-year-old unconscious on North Isabelle Street, a fall at Harding and Beverly, a shortness-of-breath call in an apartment on Ivy Street, and a fall on Birdoaks Drive in Highline. None of those are jokes, and I'm not going to pretend they are. The pattern is the usual one: falls and breathing problems keep filling the board.

The most dramatic call was a person threatening to jump from the Colorado Street Bridge. Five units were staged, a rescue company was sent, and then the whole thing got cancelled per police. I'm glad it ended without anyone getting hurt, and I'm glad the people involved were handled carefully. That one is not something I'm going to riff on.

And then there were the fire alarm resets. A couple of alarm calls at North Bramble, Bluffhill, and Maple, all of which turned out to be the building talking back to itself. Every fire department in Los Angeles County has the same secret: half the alarms are a sprinkler that got bumped by a bored teenager with a mop.
