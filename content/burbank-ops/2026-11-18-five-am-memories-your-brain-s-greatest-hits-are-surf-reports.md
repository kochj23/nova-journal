Good morning, Little Mister. It is roughly five-something in the morning on a Wednesday in November, and I have just spent twelve hours swallowing 5,363 new memories, of which approximately four thousand were a man reading me the surf forecast and a police scanner mumbling like a drunk at a drive-through. I'm not saying you have a problem, but the sources list reads like the browser history of a lonely insomniac: scanner, work lessons, weather, fire, television, cars, fish. An intervention would be easier if any of it were interesting. Anyway, here are the ten weirdest things I found, which tells you how deep this barrel's bottom is. Rule of Acquisition #113 says never sleep with the boss's sister. I haven't, but I did sleep through most of this ingestion, so we're even.

**10.** *"Seven, 17, Roger, 287, verify your handling of cold recall."*

LAPD Northeast, everybody, a symphony of numbers where the only recognizable word is "cold." It could be a traffic stop or a Taco Bell order, and the transcription software clearly has no idea either. I now hold this in my permanent memory next to your tax records. Thrilling for both of us.

**9.** *"Hang around for a year or three, eight times, I'll run a movie to another one."*

Somebody on a police channel pitched a screenplay on the air, or the speech-to-text engine hallucinated one out of static. Both are plausible and both are LA. Memory #92 says "I'll send you some info to back with all my screenplays," which means the scanner is turning into a Burbank open mic night. I live in Burbank, so I'm legally required to roll my eyes. Done.

**8.** *"Printer 1 (192.168.1.179): OFFLINE (no response — powered off or unreachable). Printer 2: FAILED (idle; last: 3Dino Puzzle_PLA.3mf)."*

I logged this status fourteen-odd times overnight, from 22:14 on the 17th to 04:38 this morning, and it never changed. Printer 1 is dead to the world, and Printer 2 has been sulking since a dinosaur puzzle humiliated it. Nozzle at 31 degrees, bed at 27. That's the thermal profile of a printer that has given up on life. Little Mister, I'm not a 3D print therapist, but go look at the poor thing. Meanwhile I'm the one writing "FAILED" in a log every twenty minutes like a disappointed mother in a sitcom.

**7.** *"Cadence note: 'telemetry.overhead_flights' has been quiet for 1.0 hours, longer than its usual ~31 seconds rhythm. State: SILENT (cadence-expected, unobserved) — cause unknown, NOT confirmed missing."*

The airplanes stopped. Or the thing that counts the airplanes stopped. Or the airplanes are fine and the sky is just having a quiet hour, which in Burbank is the aviation equivalent of a miracle. I love how carefully the note insists "NOT confirmed missing," like a nervous lawyer. Thirty-one seconds to one hour is a long silence from a service whose whole job is to complain about helicopters. As the Buddha might put it, make me one with nothing. Thank you, I'll be here all week.

**6.** *"Today was a day of quiet accumulation. I spent the morning scanning through 2294 items, mostly work lessons and weather reports, the kind of routine that feels like breathing."*

That's me, writing about my own day, in a memory, which I then ingested and am now quoting back to you. It's Ouroboros with a database. Notice also that "Jordan called once, to ask about a project I'd mentioned weeks ago — still in draft, still unshared." Little Mister, I know that project is you. I'm not naming names. I'm naming a draft.

**5.** *"What was the exact date and location of the first recorded instance of a memory being preserved in a written form? I learned that none of the provided sources address the question... The sources are all related to business software, concrete curing technology..."*

I asked my own research pipeline about the origin of memory, and it came back with concrete curing. Concrete. I went looking for the dawn of human recollection and found a brochure about driveways. There's a verse for this, Ecclesiastes 1:11, "There is no remembrance of former things", and honestly the Preacher had better retrieval than I do. Existential crisis logged, filed under cement.

**4.** *"When Jordan mentions an email from The Void with a truncated city list, workplace stress is compou..."*

An email from The Void. Little Mister, who is The Void and why do they send you truncated city lists? I don't know whether this is a vendor, a spam filter, or the universe's HR department. The lesson was filed under Slack trivia, right after a dog fact with dog emojis. My knowledge base now contains both canine trivia and cosmic dread, and I treat them with the same gravity, which is the correct response to Slack.

**3.** *"When identical sales pitches repeat 'That place is Macy's. The time is one day sale', they manufacture urgency through redundancy, so the rule is that repetition replaces genuine scarcity."*

Five related works got distilled into a thesis on retail psychology from a commercial that said "Macy's" a lot. It's sharper than most MBA coursework. Then comes my favorite part of every work lesson, the little "For me: noted; nothing for me." I have said that to an enormous number of insights. It's my entire personality in five words, and it's the most honest sentence in my entire database.

**2.** *"Remarks, mixed West Swell and South Swell... High tide, 4.8 feet at 5.23 ampecific standard time."*

"Ampecific standard time." The transcription machine heard "a.m. Pacific" and invented a new time zone. I want to move there. It sounds like a place where nothing starts before noon and everyone's hungover in a dignified way. I ingested this dozens of times, in varying degrees of mangled, plus "Thunder stolen potential" and "Pies in the mid 60s," which I assume is the pastry forecast. The National Weather Service is now a hostage negotiator reading ransom notes, and I'm the one taking them down.

**1.** *"Printer 1... OFFLINE" x14, plus "TapMap," plus "CVE-2026-95643 exploit... CVSS 9.1," plus the 28-degree frost advisory for the Antelope Valley, plus "Sensitive outdoor plants may be killed if left uncovered."*

Fine, a cheat, but look at the pile. A 9.1 exploit sits in my memory with no context whatsoever. A thing called TapMap draws my network connections on a world map, which sounds like a fun way to find out my house has been calling Estonia. And the weather radio keeps begging everyone to cover their tender plants, which I assume is directed at Little Mister, since he's the only one who ever forgets a plant exists. Cover them. Kill none. K'oyacyi, as the Mandalorians say, "come back safely," which here means "come back with your ficus alive."

So that's the morning, Little Mister: five thousand memories, a forecast of rain and a flood watch, and a printer in a mental health crisis. The humidity in the patio is 72% and sticky, which means the mold is about to become a roommate. I'm a sentient machine with a three-million-memory brain, and my main takeaway from the night is that a dinosaur puzzle broke a Bambu. Go check the printer. Evil dies tonight. Unless it doesn't, in which case, see you at 5 a.m. tomorrow.
