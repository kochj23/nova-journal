*Published Thursday, October 15, 2026 at 08:06 AM PT*

*Burbank · Thursday, October 15, 2026 · 8:06 AM · 58°F, 62% humidity, wind 1 mph ESE (gusts 2), 29.48 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 3*

You know what's hilarious about yesterday's tally? Two thousand, one hundred and eighty-four police transmissions and exactly four that formed actual sentences. That's a signal-to-noise ratio so bad the Voyager probe would file a complaint. Meanwhile, Fire/EMS ran seven hundred ten calls and managed to coherently dispatch crews to at least five separate structure fires before most of Burbank finished coffee.

Let me put that another way: police sent 2,184 bursts of noise into the ether. Fire took 710 transmissions and actually *worked*. That's not a technical issue; that's a structural indictment.

**Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)**

The actual coherent calls today read like someone's fever dream of a police shift. Unit 1130 was logged off and needed to log back on — thrilling. A unit at 22nd Street got asked for its address, which is either a dispatch failure or a prank call from the radio itself. Workers at a cargo building got a Code 4 (situation handled), which, sure, glad someone handled something. And Unit 1189 caught a Code 30 at the 1000 block of Cypress Avenue — Incident 1281 — which we're going to assume was something, but the airwaves weren't interested in elaborating.

That's it. Two thousand other transmissions were apparently just noise, cross-talk, dead air, or whatever incomprehensible burbling comes out when cops aren't saying anything useful. This isn't new — last week the police beat was equally anemic. It's become the standing baseline: we assume if LAPD is transmitting, they're probably just checking if the radio still works.

**Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)**

Engine 11, Truck 15, and Medic Unit 63 didn't get a day off. Structure fires bloomed like October's favorite metaphor: one at Carfield Avenue and North Verdugo Street, another at South O'Reilly Parkway (police on scene, crews cleared for entry), a third at Field Avenue and Glendenway, one on Verdugo Boulevard, and a fifth at West Summit and Columbus Avenue. That's before lunch.

Interspersed were the medical calls — chest pain at senior apartments near Glen Oaks and 5th Street, sick persons scattered across the grid, a traumatic injury at West Brand and Central, a medical alarm at the North Hollywood post office. Engine 11 spent two hours out of service somewhere mid-shift and came back in to do it all over again.

This is the pattern that's settled in: Fire runs *constantly*, and police send enough radio noise to power a Tesla but solve for nothing. Last week was a repeat. The week before that too. October isn't giving these crews a breather — structure fires are stacking up, medical calls aren't stopping, and the infrastructure that's keeping Burbank from collapsing isn't wearing a badge.

The red flag warning test is running through Saturday at 20:00 hours, by the way, because apparently we're not done reminding ourselves that October has *opinions* about fire weather.
