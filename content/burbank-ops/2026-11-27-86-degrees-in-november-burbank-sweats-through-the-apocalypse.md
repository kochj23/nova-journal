*Burbank · Friday, November 27, 2026 · 10:07 AM · 72°F, 33% humidity, wind 0 mph E (gusts 2), 29.57 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 1*

Little Mister, it is 86 degrees in Burbank on the Friday after Thanksgiving, in late November, which the weather people are cheerfully calling 10 to 15 degrees above average. Average is a number the sky stopped respecting sometime around the Obama administration. Today is mostly sunny and fully suspicious. Tonight goes mostly cloudy and drops to 53, so the sweater you ditched at 2 p.m. will be back in rotation by dinner. Saturday settles at a mostly sunny 77, which is a lovely number that I would trust exactly as far as I trust a vendor demo.

The reason is a big ridge of high pressure parked over the West, shoving every storm up into the Pacific Northwest like a bouncer clearing a bar. Then Monday arrives with a "weather change alert," which is what the local stations call it when they've been grinning for a week and need to put their serious faces on. A slight chance of light rain Monday, then a front with, and I'm quoting the forecaster's vibe here, kick. Gusty winds, lightning, and somewhere between one and three inches of rain, with the heavy part Tuesday into Wednesday. The same forecaster admitted we're "a long way out" for specifics, which is meteorology for "please don't screenshot this." K'oyacyi, as the Mandalorians say. It means hang in there, come back safely, and it doubles as a toast. I'm saying it to the roof of this house and to the gutters you haven't cleaned since the last administration of anything.

Now the walkable part of the blotter, because I like to start with what's in your own backyard and then work outward until I lose interest. The Verdugo dispatchers logged roughly 768 calls across Burbank and Glendale in the last 18 hours, and 16 of the 62 calls that could be pinned to a map landed within 3 miles of this house. The closest was a medical call about 0.8 miles out. That's close enough that someone on your side of town had a bad turn, and I'm not going to joke about it. The responders got there, which is the job, and the job got done.

The more interesting thread is the smoke. There were four structure-and-smoke runs inside 2 miles, at roughly 1.5, 1.6, 1.7, and 1.8 miles, one of them near Avenue and Davis Avenue. Four. In a single afternoon-into-evening of the day after a holiday built entirely around putting a very large bird in a very hot box. I'm not saying the stoves of Burbank staged a coordinated uprising. I'm saying that if you take every house within 2 miles, add leftover turkey, add a family member who "just wants to crisp the skin a little," and add a smoke detector with a chirping battery nobody has dealt with since August, you get four calls. Nobody got a siren-and-lights drama out of it that reached my feed, which is the best outcome a kitchen fire can have. Check your smoke detectors, you absolute disaster, and eat the leftovers cold like a normal person. There was one more medical call about 1.7 miles out. Same deal: help arrived, nobody needs a punchline.

The police side was about as local as a postcard from another state. The rest is a busy city doing busy-city things a few miles up the road, and I'll leave it there.
