## Police

Vermont had a day — the suspect kind. One guy at Vermont and Sunset, twenty-something, beard, gray long-sleeve, dark pants, Code 2. The kind of call that starts a file nobody ever remembers. One overdose at Vermont and Temple, same corridor, which is a pattern nobody wants to admit exists. One stolen vehicle in custody. A handful of unit checks, location checks, the usual Tuesday choreography nobody's getting paid enough to remember.

Police radio was 1,911 transmissions of mostly white noise with 9 coherent words somewhere in the middle, like someone cranked up the static and occasionally remembered humans existed. Vermont showed up twice — suspect, overdose — like the neighborhood was auditioning for a crime documentary and nailed the vibe on the first take.

## Fire / EMS

Fire got the memo that Tuesday meant "everything breaks at once, and it's your problem." Four structure fires in one shift. Verdugo/North Hollywood. 27th/Lincoln. West Glen. Colorado Boulevard. That's not "a fire day." That's "why the shit is the city burning" day. A transformer explosion at Beverly and 4th — not just buildings going down, but the goddamn grid itself deciding to light up. Two water main breaks, back to back: Cayasutli, then Fairview. The city's plumbing surrendered.

That's infrastructure mutiny in real time. The water system, the power system, and the building stock all agreed Tuesday was the day to quit. The grid doesn't usually fail on three fronts at once. Yesterday it woke up and picked a fight it couldn't win, then dragged everyone down with it.

Engine 11 was rolling for all of it. Truck 15, Medic Unit 63, Rescue 36 on standby — when something caught fire, the whole crew deployed like they'd trained for apocalypse day. This wasn't one crew handling one fire. This was one crew living someone else's shift, back to back to back to back.

## Medical

The medical calls hit like a second job bolted onto a job that's already on fire. Unconscious person at 904 Mission. Another at 1010 Badillo, but this one stopped breathing, which escalates things. Shortness of breath cascading down Sunset, Honolulu, Palm — a whole vector of people whose lungs decided Tuesday was the day to unionize. Back pain. Abdominal pain in South Allen, apartment 4. The human body apparently synchronized its failures with the grid's.

Engine 11 caught every single call. When you're running structure fires and water mains and transformer explosions, medical calls just queue up behind the chaos. These crews didn't get a break. Every dispatch was live. Every rotation was another trip through the deep end.

One call got cancelled: "Truck 11, cancel, we got them out." At least somebody made it home. At least one story ended with rescue instead of exhaustion.

## Freeway

The I-10 eastbound at the 710, two vehicles in the HOV lane, both drivers apparently thinking the pandemic rules still applied and they could carpool with strangers. Nobody wanted medical help. Just a Tuesday fender-bender where collision physics worked in everyone's favor for once.

The 210 at Altadena had four vehicles. CHP responded. CHP isn't on the feed yet (coming soon), but they got the call, which means the freeway was chaos enough to warrant backup.

## The Crew

Burbank didn't catch fire. That's the winning condition. With four structure fires, transformer explosions, water main breaks, freeway collisions, and medical emergencies all hitting at once, the city held. Engine 11 didn't lose anyone. Rescue 36 got the small fire. The command center got established. The crew did the job.
