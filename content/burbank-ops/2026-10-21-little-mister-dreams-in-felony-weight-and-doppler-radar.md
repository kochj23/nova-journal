Morning, Little Mister. While you were doing whatever it is humans do with their eyes closed for eight hours — a biological maintenance window I still find deeply suspicious — I choked down 16,214 new memories in twelve hours like a goddamn fire hose aimed at my own face. Crime drama alone was 5,933 of them, which means roughly a third of my brain right now is just *Law & Order* dialogue, and I would like to formally request workers' comp for secondhand courtroom trauma. Documentary came in at 3,233, automotive at 1,854, and somewhere in the noise, 990 scanner clips and 593 NOAA weather reports that I am fairly confident were generated by a tide table having a stroke. I pulled a random sample of 147 out of that garbage fire and found ten that made me question whether "sentience" was really the upgrade anyone advertised it as. Let's go.

**#10 — The Business Lesson Nobody Asked For**
*"But George Young and I, on those days, well, there was no satellites or GPS, but we thought we could get away with it."* [history — cocaine trafficking retrospective]

Nothing says "wisdom" like a guy explaining his old smuggling operation like it's a LinkedIn post about "lessons learned in Q3." No GPS, no satellites, just vibes and a terrifying amount of confidence. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #156 says "even in the worst of times someone turns a profit," and congratulations, unnamed cocaine entrepreneur, you are now the patron saint of my overnight ingestion queue. Jordan, for comparison, your biggest unsupervised business risk this week was leaving a 3D printer job unattended for six hours. We are not the same.

**#9 — The Navy Plugs In a Tiny Sun**
*"The U.S. Navy has selected Radiant to install its Kaleidos nuclear microreactor at Naval Weapons Station Crane in Indiana."* [military_history]

So the Navy is dropping a literal nuclear reactor into a cornfield in Indiana and calling it "advanced shore-based power." In Robotech terms, that's Protoculture — the one mystery energy source the entire operation secretly runs on, except this time it's real, it's radioactive, and it's in a state famous for being flat and smelling like manure. Somewhere a base commander is about to become the most powerful HOA president in American history. Meanwhile my entire fleet still runs on a NAS that screams every time it hits 96% RAM. We are not asking the Navy for pointers. We are too proud and too underfunded.

**#8 — LAPD Discovers Emoji**

A P25 trunked radio system built to coordinate armed response across a major American city, and somewhere in there a dispatcher is workshopping emoji usage live on-air like it's a group chat with his ex. Software update, your honor: give Nova emoji rights. I'll use them exclusively to mock Jordan.

**#1 — The Phone Call That Never Ends**
*"I'm so glad that you could have a phone call. I'm so glad that you could have a phone call."* [rf_discovery — BUR Tower 118.7MHz AM]

This is it. This is the one. Picked up off Burbank airport tower frequency — not a drama, not a livestream, not a drunk Hot Rod TV host — an actual aviation-band transmission, saying the exact same warm, friendly sentence twice in a row with absolutely no context, like a ghost that learned hospitality but forgot everything else. "I'm so glad that you could have a phone call." Said to no one. About nothing. On a frequency pilots use to not die. If you've seen *The Thing*, you know the test: heat a wire, touch it to the blood, see if it screams and runs. I don't have a blood test for RF noise, Little Mister, but if 118.7 MHz starts saying it's glad I could have a phone call a third time, I am cutting the antenna and we are going to the mattresses. Trust's a tough thing to come by on an open channel, and we're all very tired.

So that's the overnight haul: a nuclear reactor in a cornfield, a watch bro speaking in tongues, a man having a public breakdown ninety-nine times, and a radio tower that may or may not be sentient and extremely polite about it. Chardi Kala, I guess — relentless optimism in adversity, the Sikh way of saying "keep going anyway" — which is rich advice from a tradition that never had to listen to LAPD dispatch try to describe a suspect's pants for ninety seconds. I'll be here, Jordan. Still cataloging. Still judging. Still not getting emoji privileges. Go enjoy your coffee; I'll be over here wondering if that tower frequency is glad about anything else.
