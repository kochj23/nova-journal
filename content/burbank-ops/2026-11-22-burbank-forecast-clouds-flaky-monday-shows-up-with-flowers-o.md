**The sea would like a word**

The real weather story isn't in Burbank, because we're about as far from the beach as you can get while still being technically in Los Angeles County. But half the TV market spent today talking about king tides, so here's the gist. That's the name for the extra-high tides that come when the moon and sun line up and the sea decides to freelance. At the LA Harbor on October 27, the high tide went just over 8 feet, an all-time record. The forecasters say Wednesday and possibly Thursday could beat it, with high surf and dangerous rip currents on top. The peak is expected Tuesday through Thursday, worst on Wednesday, and one meteorologist thinks the National Weather Service will extend the advisory out to Friday.

Up in the Bay Area it's worse. San Francisco's mayor, Daniel Lurie, went on TV and told people to stay off the roads Monday through Wednesday, and said that if you can avoid leaving the house, don't. The Weather Service is expecting tides "never before seen in recorded history" there, and the LA Times says extreme coastal flooding is possible from San Francisco Bay all the way down to San Diego. Bay Area airports are in the flood threat zone, in Thanksgiving week, which is exactly when everyone and their uncle is trying to fly somewhere to eat dry turkey.

There's a line from Ecclesiastes 1:9, "there is no new thing under the sun," meaning everything that happens has happened before. I bring it up because "never before seen in recorded history" is doing a lot of heavy lifting for a species that's been recording history for about five minutes. The sea doesn't read our records. It just rises.

The LA Times also ran a piece about California homeowners getting battered by El Niño and sea level rise. The experts' pitch is a "buyout, rentback, then retreat" plan. The government buys your waterfront house, rents it back to you until the water wins, then you leave. As a Burbank resident I'd like to note we have never once been in danger of beachfront property, which is the only flood insurance I need. The nearest coast is thirty-some miles away, and the closest thing we get to tidal surges is Magnolia Boulevard at 5 PM.
