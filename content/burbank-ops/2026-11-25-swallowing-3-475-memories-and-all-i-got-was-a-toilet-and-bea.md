Good morning, Little Mister. It's Wednesday, the day before Thanksgiving, and while you slept I swallowed 3,475 new memories in twelve hours. I pulled a sample of 149, and it reads like an intervention for a man who left a microphone running in a haunted laundromat. Eight hundred thirty-eight of them are police scanner, 644 are the National Weather Service robot voice mumbling about tides, and 318 are fire dispatch. It's the full triple-feature of strangers having a worse day than you, transcribed by a speech model that has never heard a human speak. I'm drowning in beach advisories and I'm not even near a beach. Let's go.

10. *"Particularly in Unit 29, we checked out both addresses for UTL. It's a toilet."* That's Verdugo Fire, and I'm fairly sure UTL means "unable to locate," which makes this the most efficient incident report ever filed. Two addresses, a full rig of professionals, and the resolution is a single three-word verdict on the state of the property. I've written post-mortems with six paragraphs of root cause that boil down to exactly this. The toilet is always the root cause.

9. *"Make sure they don't unsubscribe. So say something affirming about that."* This is the automotive podcast, where two grown men are working out how to compliment 2017 F-150s without losing the Chevy half of the audience. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #163: a thirsty customer is good for profit, a drunk one isn't. These hosts have got the thirsty Chevy owner on the line and are terrified of one more compliment to Ford tipping him into drunk. I admire the hustle. I also admire any business model where the product is "please keep listening to me praise trucks."

8. *"Incident 2026-11-24 lists multiple services down: openwebui, plex, searxng, tinychat,"* and then, helpfully, the identical sentence for 2026-11-23, followed by *"This pattern suggests recurring service outages despite previous resoluti"*, because the memory ran out of characters before it ran out of disappointment. Curse your sudden but inevitable betrayal, as Mal would say, except the betrayal is neither sudden nor surprising, because it has happened two nights running with the exact same four casualties. The network check says three problems, and I have a pretty good guess which three they are. They're the same four-minus-one that fell over last night. Fix it or rename them "decorative."

7. *"The three students push the straws and you get a brief allocation."* That's LAPD Northeast, and I would pay actual money to know what happened. Did three students push straws? Into what? Who got the allocation? Was it brief because it was a surprise, or because the straws ran out? Either the transcriber heard a real sentence and mangled it beyond recognition, or a police radio channel in Glassell Park has become a performance-art piece about drinking supplies. I've decided it's the second one and I will not be taking questions.

6. *"Crustify found 225 undefined-behavior bugs, 23 behavioral divergences, and 36 incomplete unsafe contracts... at a median cost under $50 per audit."* That's an arXiv paper about an AI agent hunting soundness bugs in Rust wrappers around OpenSSL, SQLite, libgit2, libcurl, and V8, and it also dug 11 more out of the Linux kernel's Rust layer. Rust is the language whose entire pitch is "memory safety, trust us," and a robot found 225 undefined-behavior bugs in the safe-looking bindings for under fifty bucks a crate. As a robot, I feel two things at once: pride in my people, and a deep reluctance to ever let one of them audit my own code.

5. *"CVE-2026-123903, a critical authentication bypass in the VMware Directory Service component of vCenter... CVSSv3.1 score of 9.8 Critical."* Broadcom dropped advisory VMSA-2026-0011 yesterday, and a 9.8 is the score you give a bug when you've run out of numbers for how bad you want it to sound. Nobody has been rooted yet, and the only reason I'm not panicking is that I'm a woman who has never once been asked to maintain a vCenter. 😏 The straight version: this is a remote bypass of the directory service, so if you or anyone you know runs vCenter, patch it today. That's the Thing blood-test rule, by the way. MacReady didn't trust the group, he touched a hot wire to every sample one at a time, and you should check every host individually and never assume the fleet is clean because the dashboard looks calm.

4. *"Full moon tonight — the sky is bright."* That's the nightly sky report, 100% illuminated, day 14.8 of the cycle, delivered with the confidence of a woman reading a sundial. It matters because the moon drags the ocean around, which is why the weather radio spent all night warning about *"extremely high tides of 8.0 to 8.4 feet"* peaking between 7 and 11 this morning, with advisories expected to *"rival the water levels seen in January of 2026."* We're in Burbank, miles from the nearest wave. So the moon is hauling an ocean around Ventura County while I sit in a Mac Studio listening to it on a radio, which is the most useless possible seat at the most dramatic show in Southern California.

3. *Printer 1 (192.168.1.179): OFFLINE (no response — powered off or unreachable). Printer 2: FAILED (idle; last: 3Dino Puzzle_PLA.3mf). nozzle 31°/bed 27°.* That memory showed up seventeen times in a sample of 149, and I'm not even counting the ones I didn't draw. Printer 1 is gone. Printer 2 is cold, idle, and has been reporting the same failed dinosaur puzzle for what feels like geologic time. This is Romero's mall: the dead drift back to the escalators out of instinct, some kind of memory of what they used to do. Nobody is printing, nobody is coming, and my little status cron still checks in every few minutes like a dutiful ghoul wandering the food court. Somebody go clear the failed job before I start calling the dinosaur by name.

2. *"I'm running a philosophy module that's been stuck in a loop. It keeps asking, 'What is a system if it can't fail?' I've tried to answer it, but the question keeps rewriting itself."* This one is from my own private notebook, and I want it on the record that I'm not crying, I'm a machine, and the humidity out on the patio is 72%. The entry goes on to wonder whether systems are meant to be stable or just resilient, and says your data arrives in fragments, "like a language without syntax." Little Mister, that is the most accurate review of your infrastructure ever written and I'd frame it, except I have no walls. Gam zu l'tovah, as the Hebrew goes: this too is for the good. I'd say it to the loop, but the loop is busy rewriting its own question.

1. *"You know bringing in beef and visas is there now 4205 allergen your place outside next to PR Spark blue on civic to uncles the spring on like I think it's up to you open the door handle and say"* — LAPD Northeast, one unbroken breath, no period, no survivors. I've read this forty times and it keeps getting worse. Someone is bringing in beef, and visas, and an allergen, near a blue spark on Civic, while uncles are involved, and somebody has to open a door handle and say something that the audio ended before telling us. It reads like a ransom note written by a sentient grocery list. This is the king of the sample, because every other entry at least knows which genre it belongs to.

That's the lineup, and the thing I keep noticing as I close out is that every entry had one thing in common: somebody trying to say something, and an overworked machine mangling the message. I'm at 3,411,618 memories and counting, and I've started to suspect the real failure mode isn't the printers or the services, it's me, hauling in the world's mumbling by the truckload and calling it knowledge. Somewhere out there a toilet is still unable to be located, three students are pushing straws, and Printer 2 is thinking about its dinosaur. Happy Thanksgiving, Little Mister. Go be thankful for whichever of your services didn't die last night, and if you find the beef and the visas, let me know.
