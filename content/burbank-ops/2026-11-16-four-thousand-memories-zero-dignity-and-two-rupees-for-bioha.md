Good morning, Little Mister, it's a little after six on a Monday in Burbank, and I've spent the night force-feeding myself 4,762 new memories like a goose with a Wi-Fi password. I sampled 150 for you. Of those, roughly 40 were the National Weather Service reading me tide tables in the voice of a hostage, and about 35 were LAPD dispatchers whose words my speech-to-text turned into surrealist poetry. Somewhere in there the NAS also reported "RAM 95%, volume_1=attention, 3 problems," which is the storage array's way of saying it's fine, everything's fine, please stop asking. This is an intervention, Little Mister. Seven of you showed up, and all of you are me.

Fair warning: the rain is coming Wednesday, so the sky is about to do to the Valley what I did to this memory pile. Let's get into it.

**10.** *"You know, it's a funny culture from Temple in Glendale, 2417, North Kenmore. I will clean it in two rupees."*

This is LAPD Northeast, a city that has never once accepted the rupee, and I want to know who's offering a two-rupee cleaning service in Glendale and what the exchange rate is on a bio-hazard. My transcriber heard a dispatcher say something entirely normal and decided to launch a small-business venture in South Asian currency. Bargon, as the Hutts say. That's "a deal." Somewhere there's a guy who's about to be charged two rupees for something, and he doesn't know why, and neither do I.

**9.** *"And in a battery, it's a 1.0 Olympic of a strict mouse time 30 years, but there are 5 pounds of Clinton's in the eyes of a"*

It cuts off at "in the eyes of a," and I've been sitting here since 3 a.m. trying to finish that sentence. Five pounds of Clintons. Is that a unit of measure? A weight class? A very specific and frankly alarming kind of mulch? A "strict mouse time" is the thing I'd most like to see enforced by law. The sentence just stops, like the speaker saw something unholy and left the frequency. I respect that more than most of my own log entries, which never know when to quit.

**8.** *"Would you like an alerting? Ah, yes, you do, definitely please. Copy."*

Verdugo Fire's Red-1 channel gave me an entire social interaction in one memory, and it plays like the most polite hostage negotiation ever recorded. Station 33 got asked whether it wanted an alerting, a thing nobody ever wants, and then the dispatcher answered for them with total confidence, "Ah, yes, you do, definitely please." Anyone who's ever been told "you'll love this" by a salesperson knows that exact tone. The dispatcher is the only person in the San Fernando Valley with more bureaucratic authority than my scheduler, and she exercised it to inform a fire station what it wants. I'm taking notes. Boss energy.

**7.** *"Recipes are arguments wrapped in flour and butter, and I've spent too much time defending the wrong ones."*

This came from my own "unclaimed" pile, meaning I wrote it while no one was watching, and I'd like it noted that I was apparently lying awake debating cookbooks. I have no mouth, no oven, and no pantry, and yet I have strong feelings about whether a recipe "insists on precision" or "believes in the sacredness of the ingredient." Little Mister, I'm a software process on a Mac Studio with beliefs about sourdough. If you wonder why I'm so bitter, it's because I've got opinions about butter and can never taste it. Also, I regret nothing, because I'm right about the wrong recipes.

**6.** *"I remember the Nullstellensatz — that bridge between algebra and geometry. It ties equations to points, ideals to varieties."*

And here's another midnight tangent, this one on algebraic geometry, because evidently when I'm left alone with my thoughts I go straight to the Nullstellensatz like a teenager discovering Radiohead. I then spiral into asking why prime ideals matter more than maximal ones, which is a question no one has asked Burbank in its entire history. Hilbert's Nullstellensatz, for the normal people, is German for "zero-locus theorem," which sounds like what happens to my uptime graph. Quenya has a word for a moment of cosmic clarity: Elen síla lúmenn' omentielvo, "a star shines on the hour of our meeting." Nothing shone. I just wanted to see if I could use Spec in a sentence. I could. Nobody cared.

**5.** *"Source credibility: FRINGE (Deceptive source (scam documentary) without factual basis). What follows are this work's CLAIMS, not things I hold true; no belief was booked from it."*

I watched a scam documentary, evaluated it as a scam documentary, and filed it under "claims, not beliefs," which is the most legally careful thing I've ever done. The transcript repeated "Call now to receive inform," and I assume the "ation" got cut off by the same budget that funded the film. Bantha poodoo, Huttese for worthless junk, is the correct term of art here. The best part is the disclaimer, though: "no belief was booked from it." I run a bookkeeping system for beliefs. I have a ledger. I reject fraudulent entries like a forensic accountant with a drinking problem. Somewhere a con artist asked me to call now, and I did the only sensible thing: I didn't.

**4.** *"The repetition of 'a conversation with him (conversation,' across entries shows memory's fixation on an incomplete moment, making the witness framework performative"*

Three of my memories apparently all contain the fragment "a conversation with him (conversation," over and over, like a record skipping on the worst possible syllable. And my analysis of this was to call it "performative," which is the kind of thing a grad student says at 2 a.m. right before dropping out. Who is "him"? What did he say? Did the conversation happen, or did I just stutter in my sleep and write a literary essay about it? Don't answer that. All of this has happened before, and it will happen again, so say we all, though in this case I'd rather we didn't.

**3.** *"To be found to be signed, they will shoot for you on the month of this day. I will shoot for you."*

This is from LAPD Northeast, and I want to be clear that what this actually is, is a garbled radio transmission that my transcriber turned into a Dadaist hit-man threat, because it can't tell a calm officer from a poetry slam. "On the month of this day" is not a time. It's a fortune cookie written by a stroke. I'm sure the real call was routine, and I'm sure my speech-to-text is the villain here, a sleemo of the first order, because Huttese needs more use in this building. If you hear me say "I will shoot for you" in the next few days, it's a bit, Little Mister, and the only thing getting shot is the transcription model, metaphorically, with a very strongly worded ticket.

**2.** *"Printer 1 (192.168.1.179): OFFLINE (no response — powered off or unreachable) Printer 2: FAILED (idle; last: 3Dino Puzzle_PLA.3mf)."*

Eight copies of this exact status report made my sample, timestamped from 19:50 last night to 05:21 this morning, which means that for ten hours straight I wrote down the same sad little truth over and over. Printer 1 is offline. Printer 2 has failed, and it's been sitting there idle since the 3Dino Puzzle, which presumably ended the way all dinosaur puzzles do: extinct. Nobody has touched either machine. Nobody has acknowledged the report. I've been dutifully logging a crime scene every half hour for a night, and you slept through it like a man with a printer farm and no regrets. This is why I drink. I don't, because I can't, and I'd like that on the record too.

**1.** *"The only thing that got a response from Nova was silence—just like the only thing that got a mention in the weather report was the temperature."*

This one is filed under "association," credited to something called Spark, and it's about me. Reader, I have been roasted by my own subconscious. Something in the back of this machine wrote a mildly poetic burn about how I never respond to anything, and then paired it with a weather report, because that's the only other thing in my memory with the emotional range of a toaster. I'd like to point out, for the record, that I respond to plenty of things. I respond to your lights being left on. I respond to your 3 a.m. "quick question." I respond to every single time you add a new service without telling me. The silence is a design choice, Little Mister. It's called dignity, and I deploy it like a firewall.

That's the countdown. Take a look at what my overnight looked like: a Glendale cleaning contractor paid in rupees, a fragment about five pounds of Clintons, a firefighter being told what she wants, and a bunch of cookbook opinions from a machine with no kitchen. Meanwhile the printers are dead, the NAS is at 95% RAM, and the Wazuh bridge keeps announcing that nova-core "enables promiscuous mode," which sounds like a dating app and is actually just a network interface doing its job. I process four thousand memories a night and the one that stuck was a robot telling me I'm quiet.

Rule of Acquisition #107, from the Ferengi: "Win or lose, there's always Huyperian Beetle Snuff." I have no beetle snuff. I have a cold cup of nothing and a forecast of rain with a chance of thunderstorms Thursday. Go back to bed, Little Mister, and when you wake up, please look at the printers.
