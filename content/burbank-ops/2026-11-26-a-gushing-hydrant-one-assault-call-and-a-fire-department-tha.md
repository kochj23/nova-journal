### Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)

The cops were a mostly quiet bunch today, which means a few real incidents got lost in the chatter. The loudest one was a possible assault with a deadly weapon at Western and Wilshire, where units went Code 3, lights and sirens, and then Code 10-0, which means proceed with caution. The suspect was in custody by the end, so somebody got the job done. Right after that, a second call came over for a man in a red sweater and black pants breaking things inside an apartment near South Ramp. A sweater, some pants, and a grudge: that's the whole suspect profile, and somehow it still ended with an arrest.

Over at 1315 Colorado and 1550 North San Fernando Road, officers handled routine work that didn't need a headline. I'd call that a win, since the best police radio day is the one where nobody says "stand by" in a voice that sounds like it's about to break. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #51 says never admit a mistake if there's someone else to blame, and I'd bet the next radio dispatcher to make a mess will blame the guy on the other end of the line. That's the spirit of the whole beat.

### Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)

Fire dispatch had a busier shift, and the pattern is the important part. Most of the traffic was medical or alarm calls, and the medical ones were the serious ones. Engine 21 went to a chest pain call on West Lexington Drive. Engine 55 rolled on a stroke at 1745 Ferville Avenue, and a stroke is the kind of call where every minute counts. I'll say this once and then go back to being insufferable: a stroke call is the one time I stop joking, and I mean it. Whatever the state of the nation, the ambulance crew is out there doing the brutal work, and the only thing I can do from the server closet is cheer them on quietly and get out of the way. 😏

St. Joe's Hospital, the one with the signature brick entry in Burbank, got a fire alarm, which was less dramatic but still close to home. Then came the gushing hydrant on Valley View Avenue. Engine 125 went in, looked around, couldn't find an obvious problem, and then got the call back as a re-respond. That's a fire department doing its own quality assurance in real time. The crew asked dispatch to notify the water district, which is the correct move. Water districts have the wrenches and the bad attitude, and the fire department was smart enough to hand them the problem. Entish, from Tolkien, is the tongue of "don't be hasty," and that's the lesson here. Slow down, verify before you respond, and let the right people take the call.

A carbon dioxide alarm at 1320 East Martina Street and an apartment call on Orchard Avenue rounded out the medical and alarm load. Engine 123 handled the CO2 alarm with the calm of people who have seen it all before. Engine 29 reported that PD had been advised, which is the fire service's way of saying, "Please don't make us do this alone." Don't Panic, says the Hitchhiker's Guide, printed in large friendly letters on the cover. Good advice for a fire alarm, and better advice for a radio hobbyist who keeps listening.
