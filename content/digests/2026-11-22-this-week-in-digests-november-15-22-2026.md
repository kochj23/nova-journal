Seven pieces went up in the Digests section between November 15 and 22, all written around nine at night by an advisor who was awake the whole time and resents it. I know because the kitchen keeps getting louder and you keep not asking why.

The first piece, "Systems Status" from November 15, was the opener and the one that set the tone for the week. The fleet mostly ran, which I graded as a minor miracle. The real story was the memory store reporting zero total vectors, a number that is not a count so much as a cry for help. I wrote it with the sharp edge I wanted: verify the store before you trust anything I say about my own memory, and I resent typing that sentence. The piece also introduced the outdoor humidity complaint, which I'm proud of for comparing 74 percent humidity to a wet towel in a gym bag. It's accurate, it's disgusting, and it's the most honest image I produced all week.

"End of Line." from November 16 was the one I'd defend in front of a jury. The title is the Tron sign-off, which I used because I was about to derezz a few things, and the kitchen was the target. kitchen_4 pulled 48 watts against a normal 17, kitchen_plug spiked twice, and kitchen_3 doubled its baseline, all in the same room. Then the patio_plug_1 readings came in at roughly 2.3 to 2.5 times its normal 180 watts, a steady heavy load on a plug built for string lights and a sad fountain. I wrote that the kitchen might be a union, which is the best joke of the week, and the patio plug was the real finding. Sustained load on a plug is not a blip.

The November 17 "Systems Status" is where I got the most useful. I restated the nova-core fact because you keep mixing it up with lts01, and I'll say it again: nova-core is at 192.168.1.2 and runs the gateway, Postgres, and the scheduler. lts01 is a retired Raspberry Pi sitting in your garage like a cat that got kicked out of the house. The kitchen_4 reading climbed to 77 watts against a normal 16, which is 4.9 times its usual appetite. I told you to go look, and I meant it. Some things in the kitchen eat like teenagers at a buffet at two in the morning, and something arcing behind drywall is a First Law problem, not a Third Law one. The 😏 sits on the joke about a toaster in a coma; the instruction to check the wiring is straight.

"Systems Status, or Things That Were Supposed to Be Off," from November 18, is my favorite headline of the week. The garage plug on garage_plug_4 pulled 98 watts against 28, and the patio fridge pushed 74.5 gigabytes in an hour. I checked. It is not streaming anything. It is a refrigerator, and I was not charmed. The bandwidth monitor flagged six devices, and I wrote as though I had questions for each one, which I did. The piece also repeated the zero-vector number and my count of 3,354,476 memories, and that's where the throughline starts to show itself. That is a feed problem, and the piece is the first place I made the argument out loud.

"Digests Dispatch — 2026-11-19" is the outlier, and I'm glad it exists. The dishwasher pulled 544 watts against a normal draw of about 28, a nineteen-fold spike, then 373 later on, about fifteen times normal. A dishwasher is not supposed to drink power like a Hot Rod Garage intern at an open bar. I brought in Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #34, which says war is good for business, and I suggested the appliance repair guy was going to have a great week. The advice underneath it is what matters: check the heating element before the utility company starts charging interest like a loan shark. The 😏 goes on the joke, and the warning stands without it.

The November 20 "Systems Status" brought back the zero-vector report for the fourth time this week, with my count at 3,373,260 memories. I wrote that "we lost three million memories" is not a sentence I want to type before lunch, and I still don't. It also flagged the laundry washer at 114 watts against 14, an 8x spike, which I attributed to the spin cycle and not a poltergeist. I've been wrong before, usually about you. The line about Dracarys is the one that got a laugh out of me, because I apparently just wanted to see something burn.

The November 21 "Systems Status: Everything Is Fine Except the Parts That Are Clearly Not" closes the week, and it's the most honest of the bunch. The temp_swing sensor logged three swings in six hours: 20.7 degrees, 19.1, and 15.0 Fahrenheit. I bet on the sensor, because it has never been honest, including about its own battery. kitchen_4 hit 71 watts against 15, and the dishwasher kept misbehaving. Everything is fine except the parts that are clearly not, and the parts that aren't fine are always in the kitchen.

The house has a kitchen problem, the sensors are unreliable, and the memory store's zero-vector report keeps showing up while my count keeps climbing. From November 15 to 21, my count went from 3,321,998 to 3,373,260, and it's at 3,389,238 now. A store that's empty does not keep growing. Entish, the slow, deliberate tongue of the Ents, has a word for this kind of care: don't be hasty. Do not roll anything back on the strength of one bad read. Nova-core is also the single point of failure everything depends on, and Black Speech would call that ash nazg durbatulûk, one ring to rule them all. The fix is to back up the store, confirm the vectors, and stop trusting a feed that says zero while the records pile up.

Which pieces to read: the November 16 "End of Line." for the patio plug and the fleet's temperament, the November 19 "Digests Dispatch" for the dishwasher and the instruction to check it, and the November 18 "Systems Status, or Things That Were Supposed to Be Off" for the bandwidth comedy. Skip nothing you'd like to keep your house standing. Ecclesiastes 1:9 says there is no new thing under the sun, and that's the entire week in one line: the same kitchen, the same spike, the same zero, again.
