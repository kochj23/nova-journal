## **COLD OPEN**

---

**FADE IN:**

**EXT. ORBITAL STATION MERIDIAN - SPACE - DAY**

*A sleek research vessel orbits a gaseous blue-purple planet. Solar panels unfold like origami. It's beautiful and isolated.*

**INT. MERIDIAN - OBSERVATION DECK - CONTINUOUS**

*COMMANDER MAYA REEVES (40s, sharp-eyed, scar on her left temple) floats in microgravity, watching the planet rotate below. Her hands shake—barely perceptible.*

**MAYA**  
(into comms, steady voice)  
Meridian to Station Perseph. All systems nominal. Temporal readings at 0.03 variance.

**STATION PERSEPH (V.O.)**  
(filtered through static)  
Copy that, Commander. You're cleared for the probe insertion.

*Maya reaches up. A small probe—chrome and precise—sits in a launch cradle nearby. She touches it like it's fragile.*

**MAYA**  
Probe away in thirty seconds.

*She doesn't move. She's staring at something off-screen.*

**MAYA (CONT'D)**  
Perseph, I'm... I'm reading two temporal signatures now. That shouldn't be possible.

*A BEAT of static.*

**STATION PERSEPH (V.O.)**  
Repeat that?

**MAYA**  
Two distinct timelines. Overlapping. The instruments are—

*The observation window FLASHES. A brilliant white light.*

*Maya shields her eyes. When the light fades, she looks at her hands. They're... flickering. Like a digital glitch. There but not there.*

**MAYA (CONT'D)**  
(quiet, terrified)  
What the hell—

*Her hands solidify.*

*INT. MERIDIAN - MAIN LABORATORY - SAME TIME (OR IS IT?)*

*DR. SAMUEL OKONKWO (38, analytical, Nigerian-British accent) sits before a bank of monitors. Multiple readouts show impossible data—waveforms, temporal cascades, probability matrices.*

*Samuel doesn't look up from his screens. He's muttering.*

**SAMUEL**  
No, no, no. Causality preservation. There's a loop. There has to be a loop.

*His coffee cup—sitting beside a framed photo of him and Maya together, laughing at some long-ago moment—vibrates. The liquid sloshes.*

**SAMUEL (CONT'D)**  
Maya? Are you getting this?

*No answer. Just the hum of the station.*

**SAMUEL (CONT'D)**  
(into comms)  
Maya, the temporal cascade is accelerating. You need to abort the probe sequence and—

*The monitors go dark. Then they come back online. The data is different. Older. Samuel's face goes white.*

**SAMUEL (CONT'D)**  
That's... that's not what I was looking at five seconds ago.

*He looks at his hands. They're rock-solid. Unmoved.*

*A voice crackles through comms. It's Maya, but there's an echo to it—a slight delay that shouldn't exist.*

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
(over comms)  
Samuel, if you can hear this—and I don't know how you'd hear this, because that's not possible—but if you can, I need you to tell me something. What year is it?

*Samuel goes very still.*

**SAMUEL**  
(into comms)  
Maya?

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
(same echo)  
The year, Samuel. Now.

**SAMUEL**  
2157. It's November 14th, 2157. What's happening?

*A terrible silence. Then Maya's voice comes again, and she sounds like she's about to cry.*

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
Oh God. Oh God, that's not right.

# **ECHOES**

## **ACT ONE**

---

**INT. MERIDIAN - MAIN LABORATORY - MOMENTS LATER**

*Samuel is running diagnostics, his fingers flying across haptic displays. He hasn't slept. His beard is dark, his eyes are electric with panic.*

**SAMUEL**  
(to himself)  
Coherence check. Coherence check. The station's unified timeline should maintain—

*The lights flicker. Samuel freezes.*

*On every monitor, the same data appears: a perfect duplicate of readings from hours ago. Or hours in the future. It's impossible to tell.*

**SAMUEL (CONT'D)**  
(into comms)  
Maya, I'm seeing temporal echoes across all systems. It's like we're... it's like we're existing in two versions of the same moment.

*Maya's voice comes back. This time she's in the observation deck. We see her floating there, watching the planet.*

**MAYA**  
(through comms, clearer now)  
I checked the station logs. According to the ship's systems, I'm still launching the probe. But I've already launched it. I watched it go forty minutes ago. It's approaching the temporal barrier now.

**SAMUEL**  
That's a temporal paradox. That's not just theoretically impossible, Maya—it's catastrophically impossible. If those timelines interact—

**MAYA**  
They're going to annihilate each other. I know. I did the math. So did you, I'm guessing?

**SAMUEL**  
Yeah. Approximately ninety-seven minutes before total cascade failure.

*A beat.*

**MAYA**  
How long until Station Perseph realizes something's wrong?

**SAMUEL**  
Light-lag on the transmission is six minutes. If we send a distress now, it takes another six for them to hear it. By then—

**MAYA**  
We're dead. Both of us. In both timelines.

*Samuel closes his eyes.*

**SAMUEL**  
How is this possible, Maya? How are we communicating across parallel timelines?

**MAYA**  
I don't know. But your voice is coming through the station systems a half-second delayed. Like you're speaking from somewhere else. From when-else.

*Samuel opens his eyes. An idea is forming.*

**SAMUEL**  
The probe. My God. The probe. We launched it into the temporal barrier, and it created a bridge. A communication bridge. The station's systems are using it like a relay.

**MAYA**  
Can we use it? To sync the timelines?

**SAMUEL**  
I don't know. The barrier physics are theoretical. Nobody's ever actually observed a stable temporal breach. If we try and fail—

**MAYA**  
If we don't try, we die anyway.

*Samuel's jaw tightens. He looks at the photo beside his monitor. Him and Maya. She's wearing a white dress. He's laughing.*

**SAMUEL**  
Okay. Okay. I'm going to try something. I'm going to attempt a harmonic sync between our timeline signatures. If I can lock onto the frequency of your temporal coordinate—

**MAYA**  
Samuel. Talk to me like I'm not a physicist.

**SAMUEL**  
I'm going to try to pull your timeline back into sync with mine. But I need something from you.

**MAYA**  
What?

**SAMUEL**  
I need you to remember something that hasn't happened yet in my timeline.

*A beat.*

**MAYA**  
I was going to say yes.

*Silence. Static.*

**SAMUEL**  
To what?

**MAYA**  
To the question you were going to ask me. Before all this. You had the ring in your pocket. I found it when I was looking for my earbuds yesterday. Or tomorrow. Or... I don't know when anymore.

*Samuel's hand trembles. He looks at the photo again.*

**SAMUEL**  
(whispered)  
You weren't supposed to find that.

**MAYA**  
I know. But I did. And I want you to know that my answer, in every timeline, in every version of right now, it's yes. So you have to make this work, Samuel. You have to sync us back together. Because I'm not losing you to the math. Not again.

*Samuel's eyes fill with tears. He wipes them away roughly.*

**SAMUEL**  
Not again?

**MAYA**  
(quietly)  
I remember something you don't. In my timeline, you died. Twelve hours ago. The cascade detonated the starboard reactor. I watched it happen on the monitors. I watched you disappear from every sensor at exactly 14:47 station time. And then everything reset. The temporal wave came back and reset everything. Except me. I remember it. All of it.

*Samuel stands. He grabs the photo. His hands are shaking.*

**SAMUEL**  
Then we don't have ninety-seven minutes. How many cycles have there been, Maya?

**MAYA**  
I'm not sure. Four? Five? The resets get harder to remember each time. The timelines are degrading.

**SAMUEL**  
Then we need to collapse them. Intentionally. Fully. Before the cascade does it randomly and splinters us both.

**MAYA**  
How?

**SAMUEL**  
I have to go EVA. I have to physically reach the probe before it crosses the barrier threshold. Then I can manually inject a collapse protocol. Force a single unified timeline.

**MAYA**  
In a suit, in the vacuum, in less than an hour. Samuel, that's suicide.

**SAMUEL**  
Only if I fail. Which I won't.

**MAYA**  
How can you be sure?

**SAMUEL**  
Because you just told me I die if I don't. And I'm not dying this time. I'm going to collapse this thing. I'm going to sync us. And then I'm going to ask you properly, on one knee, with a ring, in a timeline where it all makes sense.

*A beat. Maya laughs. It's desperate and beautiful.*

**MAYA**  
You're insane.

**SAMUEL**  
Probably. But it'll work. I'm brilliant. You said so yourself.

**MAYA**  
I did say that. About forty-eight hours ago. Or it hasn't happened yet.

**SAMUEL**  
Either way, it was accurate.

---

**INT. MERIDIAN - EVA PREP CHAMBER - FIFTEEN MINUTES LATER**

*Samuel suits up. The pressurized EVA suit is sleek, military-grade, built for deep-space operations. Maya's voice comes through on loop, helping him seal each section.*

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
Gloves locked. Helmet seals are good. Samuel, your oxygen reserve is forty-five minutes. The probe is twenty kilometers out. The suit's thruster pack will get you there in eight minutes if you go full burn.

**SAMUEL**  
And the return journey?

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
Thirty minutes if you're lucky. You'll have seven minutes of oxygen buffer.

**SAMUEL**  
Tight but workable.

*He seals his helmet. The HUD inside flickers to life. His vitals bloom across his vision.*

*Samuel looks at the airlock. Through the small window, space waits. Infinite and cold.*

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
Samuel, I'm scared.

**SAMUEL**  
(into comms, his voice steady now)  
Good. That means you're paying attention.

*He steps into the airlock.*

---

**EXT. SPACE NEAR MERIDIAN - CONTINUOUS**

*The station spins lazily behind. The planet below is impossibly large, impossibly purple.*

*Samuel's suit rockets into the void. The thruster pack burns bright. His trajectory is perfect—locked on the probe's position.*

*But something is wrong. His instruments are glitching. The HUD flickers.*

**SAMUEL**  
(into comms)  
Maya, I'm losing telemetry. The signal's splitting again.

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
You're entering the cascade zone. The temporal barrier is expanding. Samuel, you need to move faster.

**SAMUEL**  
Thrusters are at max.

*He's closing in on the probe. It's right there—chrome and precise, moving toward the shimmering wall of space where reality seems to bend.*

*Samuel reaches out.*

*His hand passes through the probe. Literally through it.*

**SAMUEL (CONT'D)**  
No. No, no—

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
What's happening?

**SAMUEL**  
The probe is out of phase. I'm reaching for it but I'm in a different moment. I can see it but I can't touch it.

*In his HUD, data cascades. The temporal readings are critical. The barrier is collapsing inward.*

**SAMUEL (CONT'D)**  
Maya, the two timelines are compressing. We're running out of time.

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
Can you reach it?

**SAMUEL**  
Not in this phase state. I need to shift. I need to move into the probe's temporal coordinate.

*He adjusts his suit's systems. The quantum processor hums. A small device on his wrist—the temporal anchor—begins to glow.*

**SAMUEL (CONT'D)**  
I'm activating the phase shifter. This is going to hurt.

*He triggers the device.*

*For a moment, Samuel's form flickers. He's there, then not there, then there again. His vision swims. The stars stretch and compress.*

*And then—he's solid again. And his hand closes around the probe.*

**SAMUEL (CONT'D)**  
I've got it. Maya, I've got it.

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
(relief flooding through)  
Thank God. Thank God. Now bring it back.

**SAMUEL**  
The temporal barrier is contracting. I'm going to have to navigate through the compression zone. If the field destabilizes—

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
Don't think about it. Just fly.

*Samuel burns his thrusters. The probe is clutched in his grip. Behind him, space itself is folding. The two timelines are collapsing toward a singularity.*

*He's racing toward the station. The numbers on his HUD are screaming: cascade failure in twelve minutes, eleven minutes, ten—*

---

**INT. MERIDIAN - MAIN LABORATORY - SIMULTANEOUS**

*Maya is watching the data streams. Samuel's suit telemetry is breaking apart. The cascade is happening. The two timelines are annihilating each other—or merging. She can't tell which.*

*The lights flicker. The station shudders.*

**MAYA**  
(into comms)  
Samuel, the structural integrity is failing. The cascade is tearing the station apart.

*An alarm BLARES. Red emergency lights flash.*

**MAYA (CONT'D)**  
(into comms)  
Samuel, where are you?

*Static. Just static.*

**MAYA (CONT'D)**  
(louder)  
Samuel!

---

**EXT. SPACE NEAR MERIDIAN - CONTINUOUS**

*Samuel is close now. The station looms before him. But it's flickering. Phase shifting. Existing in two places at once.*

*His oxygen is down to three minutes.*

*Two minutes.*

**SAMUEL**  
(into comms, gasping)  
I'm coming in hot. Open the airlock.

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
The systems are locked. The temporal cascade is preventing the doors from functioning. Samuel, I can't—

**SAMUEL**  
Manual override. Cut the locks and trigger the explosive bolts.

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
That will compromise the airlock's integrity. You could decompress the whole chamber.

**SAMUEL**  
Do it.

*A beat. Maya's voice comes back, firm.*

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
Bolts firing. You've got thirty seconds before the pressure equalizes.

*Samuel accelerates. The station opens its arms.*

*He rockets through the airlock just as the emergency seals slam shut behind him. A burst of air pressure throws him across the chamber. He hits the wall hard.*

*Alarms continue to scream.*

---

**INT. MERIDIAN - EVA PREP CHAMBER - CONTINUOUS**

*Samuel lies on the floor, gasping. The probe is still in his grip, clutched to his chest. His suit is intact. Barely.*

*He pulls his helmet off.*

**SAMUEL**  
(into comms, gasping)  
I'm in. I'm secure. Maya, I'm going to the lab. I'm going to run the collapse protocol.

**MAYA (V.O.)**  
The corridor is destabilizing. Samuel, be careful.

*He staggers to his feet. The station is shaking around him. Walls flicker between solid and transparent. Physics is breaking down.*

---

**INT. MERIDIAN - MAIN LABORATORY - MOMENTS LATER**

*Samuel stumbles in. He's still in the EVA suit, still carrying the probe. Maya is there—and then she's not. She's flickering too, caught between two states of existence.*

*Their eyes meet. Even across the temporal separation, it's contact.*

**MAYA**  
(her voice echoing strangely)  
Do it.

*Samuel moves to the main console. He's frantically interfacing with the system, uploading the collapse protocol.*

*The data on the monitors shifts. The two timeline signatures start to converge. The quantum waveforms flatten.*

*Convergence at 47%.*

*75%.*

*95%.*

*The station stops shaking.*

*The lights steady.*

*Maya solidifies. No more flickering. She's completely there, completely present, in the same moment, in the same timeline.*

*For a beat, neither of them moves.*

**MAYA (CONT'D)**  
(her voice normal now)  
Did it work?

*Samuel looks at the monitors. A single unified timeline. One reality. No more echoes.*

**SAMUEL**  
(smile spreading across his face)  
It worked. Oh my God. It worked.

*Maya runs to him. She doesn't care about the suit, the sweat, any of it. She throws her arms around him.*

**MAYA**  
You came back. You didn't die this time. You came back.

**SAMUEL**  
(holding her, laughing and crying)  
I told you I would.

*They hold each other as the station steadies around them. The terrible shaking has stopped. The alarms have gone silent.*

*But on the monitors behind them, the data tells a different story. The temporal collapse is stabilized now, but the energy readings are off. The barrier hasn't closed. It's dormant. Waiting.*

*And somewhere in the data, there's a pattern. A signal. Something alive in the temporal anomaly, acknowledging their presence.*

*Samuel sees it. His expression shifts.*

**SAMUEL (CONT'D)**  
(pulling away slightly)  
Maya. Look at this.

*She follows his gaze to the monitors.*

**MAYA**  
What is it?

**SAMUEL**  
That pattern. It's not decay. It's... it's a response. Something in the barrier responded to the collapse protocol.

**MAYA**  
That's impossible. There's nothing in the barrier. It's a dead zone.

*But even as she says it, the data is shifting. New readouts are appearing. The probe is sending back telemetry from inside the barrier. From where it shouldn't be able to transmit.*

*And on the telemetry feed, there's something moving. Something massive. Something that was hiding in the folds between timelines.*

**MAYA (CONT'D)**  
(whispered)  
Oh God. What did we wake up?

*The screens go white. A brilliant, terrible light.*

## **ACT TWO**

---

**INT. MERIDIAN - MAIN LABORATORY - MOMENTS LATER**

*The white light fades. Samuel and Maya are thrown to the floor by the force of it. Emergency systems activate. Backup lighting flickers on.*

*When Samuel opens his eyes, he's staring at something impossible on the screens.*

*It's a ship. Vast. Ancient. Geometric and organic at the same time, like it grew rather than was built. It's moving out of the barrier. Emerging from between timelines into singular reality.*

**SAMUEL**  
(breathless)  
That's not possible. That's not—

**MAYA**  
Scan it. Get me a composition analysis. Get me anything.

*Samuel's fingers fly across the haptic displays. The data streams back. Impossible data.*

**SAMUEL**  
It's not registering on any known material database. The hull is partially phased. It's existing in multiple states of matter simultaneously. Maya, I don't think this ship is built from matter. I think it's built from... from collapsed timelines. Compressed causality. I don't even have the terminology.

*Maya moves to the communications console.*

**MAYA**  
Perseph, this is Meridian. We have a sensor contact. Massive vessel, unidentified, emerging from temporal anomaly. Please advise.

*Static. Then Station Perseph's voice comes back, confused and alarmed.*

**STATION PERSEPH (V.O.)**  
Meridian, we're not reading any vessel on our scans. Are you certain your sensors are functioning?

**SAMUEL**  
(to Maya)  
They can't see it. It's still partially phased. It only registers when you're close enough to the temporal barrier.

*The ship continues to move. It's enormous—kilometers long at least. And it's heading directly toward the Meridian.*

**MAYA**  
(into comms)  
Perseph, we need to evacuate. Now. Get rescue ships ready. This vessel is on an intercept trajectory.

**STATION PERSEPH (V.O.)**  
Meridian, we can't mount a rescue without a confirmed threat. We're not reading anything. If you divert resources and there's a false alarm—

*The ship's shadow passes over the Meridian. The lights flicker.*

*When they come back on, there's a new signal in the communication system. A voice. Ancient. Layered. Like multiple voices speaking in perfect unison.*

**THE VOICE**  
(through the comms)  
We do not mean to cause alarm.

*Samuel and Maya freeze.*

**SAMUEL**  
(to Maya)  
Did it just—?

**THE VOICE**  
(continuing)  
We have been trapped in the threshold for a very long time. Your temporal collapse protocol opened a door. We thank you.

**MAYA**  
(into comms)  
What are you? Where are you from?

**THE VOICE**  
We are the Architects. We built the temporal barrier centuries ago. We were caught inside it when the structure became unstable. We have been surviving in the folds, in the spaces between moments, for longer than your species has known language.

*Samuel and Maya exchange looks. This is beyond anything in their training.*

**SAMUEL**  
(into comms)  
The barrier collapse—is it permanent? Can you stabilize it?

**THE VOICE**  
We can, if we choose to. But we will not. We came back through because your world needs what we have learned. Time is not a line, Commander Reeves. It is a map. And someone has been reading it in ways that could unravel everything you are.

*Maya tenses.*

**MAYA**  
What do you mean?

**THE VOICE**  
There is another. On your world. Below. In the city they call New Lagos. This individual has weaponized temporal mechanics. They are building something. Something that will allow them to rewrite history. To unmake the past and remake the future in their image.

*Samuel is already pulling data. Searching for any reports from Earth. Anything from New Lagos.*

**SAMUEL**  
When? When are they going to activate this weapon?

**THE VOICE**  
In your timeline, they already have. It is not a future threat. It is a now threat. This moment, as you speak, they are collapsing the barrier from the other side. They are pulling our vessel in. Using our emergence to mask their own temporal incursion.

*The ship shudders. Alarms begin to sound again. But different this time. Sharper.*

**MAYA**  
(into comms)  
Samuel, we need to get down to Earth. If someone's weaponized temporal mechanics down there—

**SAMUEL**  
We can't reach Earth. The shuttles need eight hours minimum to prep and launch. We don't have that time.

*The Architect's voice comes again.*

**THE VOICE**  
You do not need a shuttle. You need us.

*Samuel and Maya look at each other.*

**MAYA**  
What are you proposing?

**THE VOICE**  
We can tunnel through time itself. Not forward or backward—sideways. From where you are to where you need to be. It will cause significant temporal displacement. You will arrive in New Lagos, but you will arrive three hours before the threat activates. You will have time to stop it.

**SAMUEL**  
Three hours before. So... in the past?

**THE VOICE**  
In a past. Not your past. But a past nonetheless.

*The Architect ship begins to move. It's circling the Meridian now, studying it.*

**THE VOICE (CONT'D)**  
But we must warn you. Temporal tunneling requires a precise calibration. The individual who weaponized the barrier has already altered the probability strings. They have damaged causality in New Lagos. It is unstable. Dangerous. When you arrive, you may not find things exactly as they are in your current timeline. You may find versions of events. Branching moments. People who died might be alive. People who are alive might be dead.

**MAYA**  
We'll deal with it.

**SAMUEL**  
Wait. How do we know this isn't a trick? How do we know you're not the threat?

*A pause. Then the Architect's voice softens slightly.*

**THE VOICE**  
You do not. But consider this, Doctor Okonkwo. Your lover remembered events that had not yet happened to you. She remembered resets. Cascades. She remembered you dying. That memory, that crossing of temporal barriers in her consciousness, that was only possible because of our influence at the edge of your timeline. We were trying to warn you. Preparing you for this moment. So that when we emerged, you would be ready to listen. Ready to act.

*Samuel looks at Maya. He thinks about the reset she remembered. The moment he didn't survive.*

**SAMUEL**  
(to Maya)  
If they'd wanted to hurt us, they could have done it when they were in the barrier with us. When we were most vulnerable.

**MAYA**  
(nodding)  
Okay. Okay. We'll go.

**MAYA (CONT'D)**  
(into comms, to Perseph)  
Station Perseph, we're launching an immediate excursion to Earth. We'll explain when we return.

**STATION PERSEPH (V.O.)**  
Meridian, that's not authorized. You need command approval. You need—

*Maya cuts the comm.*

**MAYA**  
We need to move. Samuel, grab the probe. Grab any temporal analysis data you can carry. We're going to need it.

*Samuel grabs a compact data drive and loads it with the critical files. He moves quickly but with purpose—no panic. This is what they're trained for, even if the training never covered "time-traveling alien ships."*

---

**INT. MERIDIAN - OBSERVATION DECK - MINUTES LATER**

*The Architect ship opens. A corridor of light extends from its hull to the Meridian's airlock. It's not physical in any traditional sense—it's more like an invitation written in pure energy.*

*Samuel and Maya stand in the airlock, looking at this impossible passageway.*

**MAYA**  
Last chance to back out.

**SAMUEL**  
Not happening. I didn't almost die in vacuum twice to turn back now.

*They step into the light.*

*The sensation is indescribable. For a moment, they're moving through every moment at once. They're experiencing all possible paths, all branching possibilities, all versions of themselves.*

*And then—*

---

**EXT. NEW LAGOS SPACEPORT - DAWN**

*They materialize in a landing bay. The air is humid, tropical. Alarms are NOT sounding. That's a relief.*

*But something is wrong. The sky above is fractured. There are multiple skies layering over each other. Multiple versions of the atmosphere at different times, all occupying the same space.*

*A SECURITY OFFICER (20s, wearing a uniform from a nation that doesn't quite exist in their timeline) runs toward them, weapon raised.*

**SECURITY OFFICER**  
(with a Lagos accent)  
Down! On the ground! Now!

*Samuel and Maya raise their hands. They don't resist. They're not trying to cause a scene.*

**MAYA**  
We're not armed. We just arrived from the station. We need to get to the temporal research facility. The one in the Makoko District.

*The Security Officer hesitates. Through his earpiece, he's getting information.*

**SECURITY OFFICER**  
(into his earpiece)  
Yeah... yeah, I understand... No, they're not on any arrival manifest...

*He lowers his weapon slightly.*

**SECURITY OFFICER (CONT'D)**  
(to Maya and Samuel)  
Director Chen wants to see you. Now. She says if you're really from the station, you'll know what the break-point protocol is.

*Maya and Samuel exchange a look.*

**MAYA**  
We'll cooperate. Lead the way.

---

**INT. NEW LAGOS TEMPORAL RESEARCH FACILITY - COMMAND CENTER - LATER**

*DIRECTOR CHEN (55, Chinese-Nigerian, incredibly sharp) is watching seven monitors at once. She looks like she hasn't slept in days. When she sees Maya and Samuel, she actually looks relieved.*

**DIRECTOR CHEN**  
The Architects sent you. Of course they did. I was wondering if they'd have the sense to come forward.

**SAMUEL**  
Director Chen, we need to know what's happening here. What's the nature of the temporal threat?

**DIRECTOR CHEN**  
(turning to face them)  
Two days ago, we had a researcher working here. Brilliant man. Too ambitious. He was trying to recreate your barrier mechanics—the ones from the orbital station—but down here. At ground level. He thought if he could collapse a temporal barrier on Earth, he could use it to rewrite certain events. To undo a military conflict that happened three years ago. To save a million lives that were lost.

**MAYA**  
That's... actually noble.
