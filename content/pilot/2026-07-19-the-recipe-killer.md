# THE RECIPE KILLER

## COLD OPEN

FADE IN:

INT. RIVERSIDE COUNTRY CLUB - KITCHEN - NIGHT

The stainless steel surfaces gleam under surgical lighting. Copper pots hang in perfect rows. Everything is immaculate. Almost sterile.

MARGARET CHEN (68, sharp eyes, flour perpetually under her fingernails) stands at the counter, consulting a leather-bound notebook. Her handwriting fills every margin—annotations, corrections, amendments.

She measures out ingredients with the precision of a chemist: saffron threads, counted individually. Cardamom pods, crushed by hand. Salt, weighed to the gram.

The notebook sits open before her like scripture.

She begins to work. Her movements are muscle memory incarnate—she's made this dish ten thousand times. But her eyes never leave the notebook.

The kitchen door OPENS. JAMES MORRISON (70, country club manager, all polo shirts and practiced smiles) enters.

JAMES
Margaret, the Hendersons are asking if their appetizers—

He stops. Sees her face.

MARGARET
(not looking up)
Tell them five minutes.

JAMES
You okay? You look—

MARGARET
(sharp)
I'm fine.

She returns to her work. James lingers, uncertain, then backs out.

Margaret's hands begin to shake. She sets down her knife. Breathes.

She picks up her phone. Scrolls. Stops on a photo: herself, twenty years younger, standing next to an older woman. Both are smiling. Both are holding wooden spoons.

She closes her eyes.

When she opens them, her jaw is set. She returns to her work.

The camera PULLS BACK as she plates the food. Beautiful. Perfect. Deadly-looking.

FADE TO BLACK.

## ACT ONE

FADE IN:

INT. RIVERSIDE COUNTRY CLUB - DINING ROOM - NIGHT

The Henderson dinner party is in full swing. Expensive people in expensive clothes. Margaret's dishes move from kitchen to table. Each course receives small applause.

At table, DEREK CHEN (38, Margaret's son, sharp suit, sharper phone) sits next to his mother's longtime rival, VIVIAN HARTWELL (65, diamond-sharp, diamond-covered).

VIVIAN
(tasting)
Your mother's still got it, I suppose. Though I did read that new place in the city just got a Michelin star. Have you tried it?

Derek smiles without warmth.

DEREK
I'm sure it's lovely.

VIVIAN
Of course, Margaret invented half their techniques. Didn't you, Margaret?

Margaret appears at the table, clearing plates. She doesn't answer. Vivian laughs—a sound like ice cubes in a glass.

VIVIAN (CONT'D)
Still modest. I always admired that about you.

Margaret moves away. Derek watches his mother's jaw tighten.

INT. RIVERSIDE COUNTRY CLUB - KITCHEN - LATER

Margaret moves through the space like a conductor leading an orchestra. Her sous chef, MARCUS (32, talented, eager), works at her side.

MARCUS
That sauce is incredible. What's in it?

Margaret doesn't answer. She's already moved on.

JAMES enters, tablet in hand.

JAMES
Margaret, the Hendersons want to know if you could do the recipe for their Christmas party. They want to tell their friends—

MARGARET
No.

JAMES
They're offering to pay premium—

MARGARET
I said no.

James retreats. Marcus exchanges a look with the other kitchen staff.

MARCUS
(carefully)
Everything okay?

MARGARET
(without looking at him)
When people ask for recipes, they don't want to cook. They want to steal.

MARCUS
It's just a recipe.

MARGARET
(finally looking at him)
Nothing is just anything, Marcus. Every dish tells a story. Every ingredient is a choice. You give someone your recipe, you give them your story. You give them your power.

She turns back to her work.

MARCUS
(to himself)
It's just food.

MARGARET
(sharp, without turning)
I heard that.

INT. DEREK'S CAR - PARKING LOT - NIGHT

Derek sits in his BMW, phone to his ear. The country club glows behind him.

DEREK (V.O.)
(into phone)
No, I don't think she'll do it. She's being stubborn... Because she's always stubborn. That's the whole problem.

He listens.

DEREK (V.O.) (CONT'D)
Look, I understand the deadline, but if you push her, she'll dig in harder. Trust me on this... Yes, I'll talk to her. Again.

He hangs up. Leans back in his seat. Frustrated.

Through the windshield, we see Margaret emerge from the country club, walking to her car. She moves slowly, like someone carrying weight.

Derek watches her. Something like pity crosses his face. Then his phone BUZZES. He looks away.

INT. MARGARET'S KITCHEN - APARTMENT - NIGHT

Margaret's apartment is modest, comfortable, lived-in. The kitchen is the only room that feels sacred—walls covered with framed recipes, handwritten notes, photographs spanning decades.

She sits at the kitchen table with a cup of tea. The leather notebook lies open before her.

She turns pages slowly. Each one a different recipe. Each one annotated, dated, revised. A life in handwriting.

She stops on one page. Stares at it for a long time.

There's a KNOCK at the door.

She doesn't move. Another KNOCK, harder.

DEREK (O.S.)
Mom? I know you're home.

Margaret closes the notebook. Hides it under a kitchen towel.

She opens the door. Derek stands there, holding expensive takeout.

DEREK (CONT'D)
Thought we could talk.

MARGARET
I'm tired.

DEREK
It'll take ten minutes.

She steps aside. He enters, moving through the apartment with the familiarity of someone who grew up here.

They sit at the kitchen table. Derek opens the containers—Thai food, expensive sushi.

DEREK (CONT'D)
I talked to the publisher again. They want to move forward with the cookbook. They're offering—

MARGARET
I told you. No cookbook.

DEREK
Mom, you're sitting on forty years of recipes. Forty years of—

MARGARET
I'm sitting on forty years of privacy.

DEREK
It's not about privacy. It's about legacy. It's about making sure that when you're gone, people remember—

MARGARET
(cutting him off)
I know what I'm leaving behind.

Derek sets down his chopsticks. Looks at his mother.

DEREK
The publisher also wants to do a documentary. They want to follow you in the kitchen, interview you about your process, your philosophy—

MARGARET
Absolutely not.

DEREK
You won't even consider it?

MARGARET
There's nothing to consider.

Derek stands, frustrated, and walks to the kitchen window. The city sprawls below them.

DEREK
Vivian Hartwell just published her memoir. Did you know that? "A Life of Flavor." It's already in the bestseller lists.

MARGARET
Good for Vivian.

DEREK
She stole half her recipes from you.

MARGARET
(quietly)
I know.

Derek turns to face her.

DEREK
And you're just... okay with that?

MARGARET
I'm okay with not caring what Vivian Hartwell does.

DEREK
You cared enough to leave the Culinary Institute when she won the competition.

MARGARET
That was different.

DEREK
How? How was it different?

Margaret doesn't answer. Derek waits, but she's closed herself off.

DEREK (CONT'D)
I have to go back to the city. But I'm coming back this weekend. We're going to have this conversation. Really have it.

He gathers his things. At the door, he turns back.

DEREK (CONT'D)
I love you, Mom. But you can't hide forever.

He leaves.

Margaret sits alone in her kitchen. She pulls the notebook back out from under the towel. Opens to the page she was studying.

It's a recipe for a dark chocolate tart. Nothing unusual. Except beneath the ingredients, in Margaret's careful handwriting, is a single line:

"The night everything changed."

She closes the notebook. Her hands are shaking.

FADE TO BLACK.

END OF ACT ONE

## ACT TWO

FADE IN:

INT. RIVERSIDE POLICE STATION - BULLPEN - DAY

The station is small-town comfortable, all beige walls and humming fluorescent lights.

DETECTIVE SARAH WINTERS (42, sharp mind, tired eyes, coffee cup permanently attached to her hand) sits at her desk, reviewing case files.

Her partner, DETECTIVE MARCUS REEVES (45, methodical, by-the-book), approaches with two fresh coffees.

MARCUS
Got your favorite. The burnt stuff from the corner place.

SARAH
(taking it)
You know me too well.

MARCUS
I know you too well. Which is why I'm concerned about how excited you got about that cold case file.

SARAH
Vivian Hartwell collapsed last night. Hemorrhagic shock. They're saying it was a perforated ulcer, but—

MARCUS
But what?

SARAH
She was fine. Perfectly fine. I saw her at the country club two weeks ago. Complained about her digestion, sure, but nothing serious.

MARCUS
Sarah, people get sick.

SARAH
(standing, pacing)
Not like this. Not suddenly. Not completely.

MARCUS
Have you been drinking enough water? You get like this when you're dehydrated.

Sarah ignores him. She's focused on the files.

SARAH
Before Vivian, there was Catherine Blaine. Two years ago. Also a sudden collapse. Also blamed on a medical condition. Also dead within forty-eight hours.

MARCUS
(reading over her shoulder)
Catherine Blaine was sixty-three. Had a history of heart problems.

SARAH
That she never told anyone about. Because she didn't have a history of heart problems. I remember her. She was at the club all the time. Seemed healthy.

MARCUS
Seemed healthy and was healthy are two different things.

SARAH
And before Catherine? There was—

She flips through files.

SARAH (CONT'D)
—Victoria Marsh. Aneurysm. Except she was forty-seven and had no risk factors. And before that, James Hendricks. Acute kidney failure. Healthy as a horse one day, dead the next.

She looks at Marcus.

SARAH (CONT'D)
Four people. All connected to the Riverside Country Club. All dead in three years. All from sudden medical crises that came out of nowhere.

MARCUS
Four people who were all over seventy, all had access to the country club's alcohol, all probably had underlying conditions they didn't disclose.

SARAH
Or all were poisoned.

Marcus sets down his coffee. Looks at his partner seriously.

MARCUS
You have proof?

SARAH
Not yet.

MARCUS
Then we're not doing this. You know the captain hates when we chase ghosts.

Sarah sits back down. Deflates slightly.

SARAH
What if I'm right?

MARCUS
Then you gather evidence and we open an investigation. But until you have something concrete, this is just... intuition.

SARAH
Intuition is how we solve cases.

MARCUS
Intuition is how we waste time. Come on. We've got actual cases to work.

He walks away. Sarah stares at the files. Her jaw is set.

She picks up her phone. Dials.

SARAH
(into phone)
Hi, this is Detective Winters. I need to request an exhumation order for Catherine Blaine. I want her body retested for toxins.

She listens to the response.

SARAH (CONT'D)
I know it's unusual. I have a reason to believe there may have been foul play... No, I don't have evidence yet, but I'm working on it.

She hangs up. Looks back at the files. At the faces of the dead.

INT. MARGARET'S KITCHEN - APARTMENT - DAY

Margaret stands at her counter, preparing lunch. Her movements are automatic, meditative.

She's making a salad. Simple. Just greens, vegetables, olive oil, vinegar.

But she's measuring the vinegar with the same precision she used on the saffron threads. Counting drops.

There's a KNOCK at the door.

She doesn't answer.

Another KNOCK. Harder.

JAMES (O.S.)
Margaret? It's James. From the club.

She sets down her knife. Opens the door.

James stands there, looking uncomfortable. He's holding a casserole dish.

JAMES
I know you're probably busy, but I wanted to check on you. Make sure you're okay after last night.

MARGARET
I'm fine.

JAMES
You seemed upset when you left. Derek seemed upset too.

MARGARET
(taking the casserole)
Thank you for this. That was kind.

JAMES
(not leaving)
Margaret, I've known you for twenty years. I know when something's wrong.

MARGARET
(meeting his eyes)
Some things are meant to be wrong, James.

He doesn't understand. She closes the door gently.

She sets the casserole on the counter. Doesn't open it. Just stares at it.

After a moment, she throws it away. Unopened.

INT. RIVERSIDE POLICE STATION - SARAH'S CAR - DAY

Sarah sits in her unmarked sedan, parked outside the Riverside Country Club. She watches the entrance.

She's got a thermos of coffee, a notebook, and a stack of photographs—the four dead people, their faces young and alive.

Her phone BUZZES. A text from Marcus: "Captain wants to see you. Now."

Sarah sighs. Starts the car.

INT. RIVERSIDE POLICE STATION - CAPTAIN'S OFFICE - DAY

CAPTAIN RODRIGUEZ (55, tired, political) sits behind his desk. Sarah stands before him like a student called to the principal's office.

CAPTAIN RODRIGUEZ
You requested an exhumation order for Catherine Blaine.

SARAH
Yes, sir.

CAPTAIN RODRIGUEZ
On what grounds?

SARAH
I have reason to believe her death may not have been natural.

CAPTAIN RODRIGUEZ
Based on what evidence?

Sarah hesitates.

SARAH
Based on a pattern I've noticed.

CAPTAIN RODRIGUEZ
A pattern. Not evidence. A pattern.

SARAH
There have been four deaths connected to the country club in three years. All sudden. All unexpected.

CAPTAIN RODRIGUEZ
(leaning back)
Are you aware that the Riverside Country Club is one of the largest employers in this town? Are you aware that many prominent citizens are members?

SARAH
Yes, sir.

CAPTAIN RODRIGUEZ
Then you should also be aware that I'm not going to authorize exhumations and investigations based on hunches. That's how we end up in the newspaper. That's how we end up with lawsuits.

SARAH
What if I'm right?

CAPTAIN RODRIGUEZ
And what if you're wrong? What if you dig up a dead woman based on nothing, upset her family, waste departmental resources, and find absolutely nothing?

He stands. Walks to the window.

CAPTAIN RODRIGUEZ (CONT'D)
I'm denying the exhumation request. And I'm reassigning you to traffic duty until you can focus on cases with actual evidence.

SARAH
Sir—

CAPTAIN RODRIGUEZ
(not turning around)
That's final.

Sarah stands there, anger radiating off her. Then she turns and walks out.

INT. RIVERSIDE POLICE STATION - BULLPEN - CONTINUOUS

Sarah walks past Marcus, who looks at her with sympathy.

MARCUS
(following her)
I'm sorry. I tried to—

SARAH
(not stopping)
Traffic duty. Apparently I'm not focused enough for real police work.

MARCUS
Sarah, the captain just needs time. If you can find something concrete—

SARAH
(turning to face him)
People are dying, Marcus. And nobody cares because they're old and rich and it's easier to believe it's natural causes.

MARCUS
(quietly)
I care. But I also care about not getting fired. And right now, the fastest way to get fired is to keep pushing this.

He walks back to his desk. Sarah stands alone in the bullpen, surrounded by busy officers who don't look at her.

She grabs her jacket and leaves.

INT. MARGARET'S KITCHEN - APARTMENT - NIGHT

Margaret sits at her kitchen table with the leather notebook open. She's reading through it like someone reading their own obituary.

She stops on a page. A recipe for beef bourguignon. Dated twenty years ago.

Beneath the ingredients, in Margaret's handwriting:

"The recipe that changed everything. The recipe that took from me what I could never get back."

She touches the page gently. Like it might disappear if she's not careful.

There's a KNOCK at the door. Urgent. Repeated.

SARAH (O.S.)
Ms. Chen? My name is Detective Sarah Winters. I need to talk to you about Vivian Hartwell.

Margaret's entire body goes rigid. She stares at the notebook.

Another KNOCK.

SARAH (O.S.) (CONT'D)
Ms. Chen? I know you're home. I just need to ask you a few questions.

Margaret slowly closes the notebook. Hides it. Takes a breath.

She opens the door.

SARAH stands there, badge visible, eyes sharp and observant.

SARAH (CONT'D)
I'm investigating the death of Vivian Hartwell. I understand you worked with her at the country club?

MARGARET
(carefully)
I knew of her.

SARAH
(noticing the careful word choice)
Just knew of her? You didn't work with her?

MARGARET
We worked at the same place. That doesn't mean we worked together.

SARAH
I see. Can I ask you a few questions about her?

MARGARET
(not opening the door wider)
What kind of questions?

SARAH
The kind about whether her death was natural or not.

Margaret's expression doesn't change. But something shifts behind her eyes.

MARGARET
She had a medical condition, didn't she?

SARAH
That's what the doctors said. But I'm not convinced. I think someone poisoned her.

The words hang in the air between them.

MARGARET
(very quietly)
Do you have any proof of that?

SARAH
Not yet. But I'm working on it. And I'm wondering if you might be able to help me.

MARGARET
(closing the door slightly)
I'm sorry, Detective. I can't help you.

SARAH
(putting her hand on the door)
Ms. Chen, if someone murdered Vivian Hartwell, they might do it again. They might have already done it. If you know something—

MARGARET
(meeting her eyes)
I said I can't help you.

She closes the door.

Sarah stands outside, looking at the closed door. She pulls out her notebook and writes:

"Margaret Chen. Chef at Riverside Country Club. Connected to all four victims. Knew Vivian Hartwell. Knew Catherine Blaine. Knew Victoria Marsh. Knew James Hendricks."

She underlines Margaret's name.

SARAH (V.O.)
(into her phone)
I know what you're going to say. But I'm telling you, something's wrong. I can feel it.

She listens to the response.

SARAH (V.O.) (CONT'D)
I don't care if it's traffic duty. I'm going to figure this out.

She hangs up. Looks back at Margaret's door.

INT. MARGARET'S APARTMENT - CONTINUOUS

Margaret leans against the closed door. Her heart is pounding. Her hands are shaking.

She walks to the kitchen. Opens a cabinet. Takes out a bottle of sleeping pills.

She looks at them for a long moment.

Then she puts them back.

She picks up her phone. Dials.

MARGARET
(into phone)
Derek? I need you to come back. Tonight... Because I'm going to tell you everything.

She hangs up. Sits down at the kitchen table.

The leather notebook sits open before her, waiting.

FADE TO BLACK.

END OF ACT TWO

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**SERIES SETUP:**

The pilot establishes a mystery-box premise: What is in Margaret Chen's notebook? What recipes has she been perfecting? And most importantly—has she been using her culinary knowledge to commit the perfect murders?

The tension between the three storylines (Margaret's secrets, Derek's pressure, Sarah's investigation) creates the engine for the series:

- **Season Arc**: Sarah slowly pieces together evidence that Margaret has been poisoning people at the country club, but discovers that each victim deserved it for reasons only Margaret knows. The show becomes a moral mystery: Is Margaret a killer or a vigilante?

- **Character Arc**: Derek must confront whether his mother is a criminal and decide whether to turn her in or protect her.

- **Thematic Core**: The show explores how intimacy and secrecy work in families, how recipes are passed down and kept hidden, and whether justice can exist outside the law.

**KEY MYSTERIES FOR SEASON ONE:**

1. Who are the four victims, really? What did they do to Margaret?
2. What is the notebook? What recipes does it contain?
3. Will Sarah solve the case? And if she does, what will she choose to do with the information?
4. Can Derek save his mother, or is she beyond saving?
5. **The Twist**: The final episode of Season One reveals that Derek himself is involved—he's been helping Margaret, and he knows exactly what's in the notebook.

**TONE:**

The show balances dark mystery with intimate family drama. It's *Ozark* meets *The Bear*, with the emotional specificity of *Fleabag*. The recipes aren't just plot devices—they're windows into character, history, and motive.
