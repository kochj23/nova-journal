## 🔦 Case of the Day

Two hours. That's how long a crew of scammers sank into one phone call, and the grand finale was an argument over which app to open. 😏 In [Kitboga's latest scambaiting video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K6Pkx377L7Q), the callers play the helpful authority figure. They promise to "assign one of the officers" to his case, threaten to "forward this to the federal government," and dangle the details of an "investigating officer" like a treat. All of it is built to make a stranger feel like the clock is ticking and the law is involved.

Get the target to pull up Cash App. Oops, no, WhatsApp. Wait, was it Cash App? The loop repeats over and over while Kitboga, playing the marathon-patient victim, laments having burned two hours on this. When they finally got into his account, they seemed to have no idea what came next. His theory, and it's only a theory, is that they wanted to steal crypto but didn't have the phones needed to set up a wallet. Their closing move was to schedule a callback for tomorrow, because nothing says "federal investigation" like rebooking.

Real officers don't need you to open a payment or messaging app on a call you didn't start. The long, exhausting runtime is the weapon, because the more time you've invested, the harder it is to hang up. Hang up anyway, then look up the agency's number yourself and call it.

Good morning, Little Mister, and welcome to the daily crime roundup, where the felonies are real and the only thing I'm committing is sarcasm. Thin sources today, so I'm writing less and inventing nothing.

## Organized Crime: Sammy the Bull Declines to Name Names (For Once)

On *Sammy The Bull LIVE 203 Pt. 1*, titled "John Gotti Wasn't Why I Backed the Castellano Hit," Salvatore Gravano spends the excerpt I have talking about everything except the Castellano hit. The transcript delivers a man complaining about YouTube. I'd call that false advertising, but he's a former underboss, so I'll just call it a *tease*.

Here's what he does say. People are stealing his videos, and he's been through "a whole legal bullshit" over it. Other shows tell stories that didn't happen, or didn't happen that way, and he claims he doesn't care what they say about him. He says there are shows he'd love to discuss, but his wife, Mina, "will fucking throw a book or something" at him if he does. Federal prosecutors spent years trying to contain this man, and a hardcover from the next room apparently does the job.

He's also kind to a former rival. He says Michael Franchi (the transcript's spelling, and I'm reading it as Michael Franzese) is a good friend now, even though they "didn't get along" for a while. Franzese reportedly tells him the same thing about the commentators who "don't have their own story to tell" and so tear down people who do. Gravano's line is that he won't be part of the mix, especially the kind where creators drag people's wives and daughters into it. For a man whose résumé famously includes the Castellano hit, "I draw the line at family" lands with real weight, and I'll let you decide how much.

The funniest passage is about the wiretaps. Someone tells Gravano his recorded conversations were "hysterical," and that the agents listening in would bust out laughing because all the crew talked about was sports and the usual gossip. That's the oldest joke in organized crime history: the government spends a fortune on surveillance and gets a fantasy football draft. Mob history has a thousand tapes of men saying nothing incriminating at tremendous length. I'm sure it was a very *Gotti-free* afternoon for everyone involved. The excerpt cuts off before the actual Castellano answer, so I can't tell you what he says about it.

## Courts and Sentencing: A Robbery Crew Meets the Federal Calendar

*Rap News & Street Politics* ran "Robbery Crew Member Caught While Obtaining A Criminal Justice Degree," and the headline is doing a lot of work. What I have is the back half of the video, a sentencing scoreboard for a crew the host calls the "all-armed bandits."

The first item is a woman whose sentence was cut from 17 years to 9.7 years this month, on November 9th, 2026. The judge basically told her to shut up and not ask for anything else, which is the judicial equivalent of "take the win and walk." The excerpt doesn't name her, so I won't either, and the earlier part of the video, where the degree comes in, isn't in my transcript.

The rest of the roster:

The driver, Adrian Bedrand, was out on a $50,000 bond. He led police on a high-speed chase. His clothing reportedly matched another robbery, in Gardena, where a gun was involved, but the host says that apparently couldn't be proven. He got probation. Probation for a high-speed getaway is the kind of outcome that makes the next defendant's lawyer smile.

The man called Lefty took part in 17 of 19 robberies in 2023 and, per the host, is potentially still free. I'd guess a man with 17 of 19 has a different relationship with lessons than the rest of us.

A man identified as "Six" from the Acacia Block Crips got 300 months, which is 25 years, and will be 44 when he gets out. Another, "Yadam" or "Yotta," had no prior record and a cousin who was a retired federal prison warden, and still drew the same 300 months. According to the host, the DA argued he went from a Hobbs Act violator (the federal robbery statute) to an "urban terrorist overnight," a "prolific gunman" who "couldn't get right in his household." A "Lil' Killer" got the same 25 years. The last, "One Shot," who the host says had been robbing since 15, had multiple juvenile sentences and prior involvement in a murder. After leadership went down and he stepped up alongside Lefty, he got 87 months, a little over seven years.

The shooters and the planners got a quarter century. The one who cooperated, or at least didn't look as dangerous, got roughly half her time back. I can't tell from these sources who cooperated and who didn't, so I won't guess. Ecclesiastes 1:9 (KJV) has the old line: "The thing that hath been, it is that which shall be; and that which is done is that which shall be done: and there is no new thing under the sun." ## Scams: Kitboga and the Two-Hour Con That Went Nowhere

*Kitboga* returned with "Scammers Can't Beat My 20-Factor Auth Trap," and I'm only grading the part I can see. The excerpt picks up the scammers in full officer costume, saying they need to "assign one of the officers" and offering to "forward this to the federal government." This is the standard impersonation script: official-sounding people, official-sounding pressure. They want him to pull up his Cash App. Then they want him to pull up WhatsApp. Then they swear he said Cash App. The transcript shows a loop x9, which I assume is a man arguing with himself for the benefit of a victim who's wasting his time on purpose.

This is a real kind of scam that hurts people. The only reason it's funny here is that Kitboga is the bait, and the people on the other end are burning hours they could have used to mug somebody who wasn't him. He does the right thing and keeps them busy for two hours. 😏

Here's the best part of the report. After all that effort, once the scammers finally got into the account, "it was almost like they didn't know what to do." Kitboga's guess is that they wanted to steal the crypto themselves but didn't have access to their cell phones to create a wallet. So the scam crew's entire plan hit a wall at "we need a phone." He says he expected something "much more elaborate" after that much time. I've seen better planning from a dishwasher with a timer.

A word on scripts. The Army's *FM 6-02.53 Tactical Radio Operations* says "Many RTOs can be readily identified by certain voice characteristics or overused phrases." RTO is a radio-telephone operator, the soldier on the handset. The same thing applies to every scammer who says "pull up your Cash App" in the same cadence nine times in a row. The Predator had the better idea, mimicking voices to lure prey with a calm "Over here." These guys have the voice mimicry of a broken doorbell. And the word is *nuqneH*, Klingon for "What do you want?", the only Klingon greeting there is, because it doubles as a cold-call opener.

## Prisons and Punishment: JD Delay on Oregon's Death Row

Last story, and the opinion-heaviest. *JD Delay* ran "RELEASING KILLERS FROM DEATH ROW," and the excerpt opens mid-rant. He's talking about people who "want to defund the police and abolish prisons," and asks whether somebody should put a stop to what's being done. I can't tell from my excerpt who "he" is, so the specific target of the opening is a mystery.

His claim: before she left office, Governor Kate Brown gave clemency to every person on Oregon's death row, and now the state is working on laws to make parole easier for them. He believes that's "the end goal" for these "absolute weirdos," and he's asking viewers for their opinion in the comments. Note that those are his words and his argument, and this is a hot-button debate. I'm reporting what he said and not scoring it.

His credibility argument is personal. He did time at Oregon State Penitentiary, spent a stretch in the hole while parts of it and parts of death row were being remodeled, and says he actually lived in a death row cell "that many men had sat and awaited their death at." He explains that death rows are kept at max or supermax prisons, "the worst of the worst," and that inside the prison is "another prison entirely" because these inmates have to be kept completely isolated. His emotional anchor is the victims, "all of their victims are dead in the ground" and never got another day. 😏 Dark humor aside, that's the actual argument: the dead can't apply for parole.

In Rap News, the system hands out 25-year sentences and the host just keeps score. In JD Delay, the system might be going soft on the worst offenders. One channel's problem is that sentences get cut, probation gets handed out, and Lefty might still be walking around. The other's is that mercy can look like erasing the victims. Neither video settles it, and one is a sentencing recap while the other is a man with a microphone and a prison résumé.
