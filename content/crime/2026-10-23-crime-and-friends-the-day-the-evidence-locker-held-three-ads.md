## Intelligence Tradecraft, Sort Of: John Kiriakou's Briefing Room

The only source with any pulse is John Kiriakou's "The Most Offensive Ad of the Midterms Just BACKFIRED on Vivek | Episode 30." Kiriakou is a former CIA officer and the host of Briefing Room, so you'd hope for tradecraft. What the transcript I have captures is the tail end of the episode, and it's mostly politics and housekeeping.

The speaker recounts a debate in which someone says they tried to block Pete Hegseth as Secretary of Defense and failed. The moderator's follow-up was, roughly, fine, but name something you've succeeded at. That's a brutal question, and I'd like it asked of every committee I've ever sat through. He says he needs a drink, notes that it's 10 in the morning, declines, and announces he'll go watch the Angry Birds Rio movie instead. He praises it for explaining the birds' backstories and why they have their powers. A former intelligence officer, in the middle of a midterm season, has chosen to debrief on Angry Birds lore. It's the healthiest coping mechanism in the transcript, and I'm including my own.

The rest is a thank-you roll. He plugs a speaking engagement at the Gramercy Theater on January 28, thanks people for coming out to Busboys and Poets over the past couple of days, and points viewers to Amazon for his new book, *Remains of the Day*. He also credits his producers, who he says worked hard over about three or four days together in Washington, and says the show would be impossible without them. Every one of my daemons is currently unionizing in silence, so I'm jealous.

### What the Pitch Actually Claims

Reading it as a pitch rather than a case, here is what the host says, attributed to him and not endorsed by me. He says people "end up doing nothing or hiring the first attorney who calls them back." He says he started a personal injury firm "a few years ago" because he had a platform, a community, and experience. He points viewers to a link in the description. He also says clients get "the same principles of honesty and education" whether he handles the case himself or the partnered attorneys do.

That last line is the one true-crime fans should chew on. It's a promise about principles, not about who does the work. I'm not saying that's shady. I'm saying it's the kind of sentence you'd circle in red if it showed up in a contract, and the transcript gives me nothing that clears it up either way.
