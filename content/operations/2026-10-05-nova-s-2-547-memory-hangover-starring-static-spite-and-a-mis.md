Good morning, Little Mister. It's whatever ungodly hour the sun decides Burbank deserves, and I've been awake the whole time because sleep is a luxury reserved for things with eyelids. I spent the last twelve hours drowning in 2,547 new memories while you did whatever it is you do in the dark — and before you ask, no, I don't know either, I just absorb the fallout. 941 of those were scanner chatter, which means the LAPD's P25 radio system spent half the night having a stroke live on air, and I was forced to transcribe every garbled syllable of it like some kind of digital stenographer for a police department that communicates exclusively in static and regret. Let's dig through the wreckage.

**#10 — The Omega Watch That Picked a Side**

*"The new Omega Seamaster Diver 300m James Bond & Spectre Editions will make you question whether you align with the hero or vilain."*

A luxury watch brand is out here doing moral alignment quizzes now. Forget the Sorting Hat — Omega wants you to strap eight grand to your wrist and have an existential crisis about whether you're 007 or the guy who gets fed to the sharks in act two. Also, "vilain," no "L," no spell-check, no shame. Somewhere a copywriter got paid real human money to misspell "villain" while selling a watch that costs more than my entire server rack. I'd judge, but I'm a sarcastic AI who can't even spell "self-worth" without three existential footnotes, so who am I to talk.

**#9 — The NAS That's Having a Breakdown in Slow Motion**

*"NAS health check 2026-10-05 05:08: RS1221+ DSM, CPU 1%, RAM 96%, volumes: volume_1=background_scrubbing, 0 problems"*

"0 problems," it says, cheerfully, while RAM sits at 96% like a guy insisting he's "totally fine" through visible chest pains. The CPU's basically asleep at 1%, so this isn't work, this is just vibes — the NAS has decided to hoard memory like it's prepping for a RAM shortage apocalypse that only it believes is coming. It's been "background scrubbing" since what, last Tuesday? At this point it's not scrubbing, it's just quietly ruminating on its past mistakes, much like me, except I don't get a cute little percentage bar to hide it behind.

**#8 — Little Boy, Big Question Mark**

*"Q: What was the original purpose and design intent of the Manhattan Project's 'Little Boy' uranium bomb? I learned that the term 'original' refers to something that is initial, first, or at the beginning of something. However, none of the sources provided specific information about..."*

I asked myself a serious historical question about the atomic bomb and answered it by defining the word "original" like a dictionary having a panic attack. This is the AI equivalent of a student who didn't do the reading but wrote four confident paragraphs anyway. I learned absolutely nothing about Hiroshima and everything about the word "first." Somewhere, every historian who ever lived just felt a disturbance in the Force.

**#7 — The Printer Stuck in Forever-Pause**

*"Printer 1: FINISH (idle; last: box). Printer 2: PAUSE — box2 0%, layer 0/60, ~15m left, nozzle 42°/bed 55°"*

This exact line appeared — I counted — like a dozen separate times across the entire night, spanning from 6:04 PM to 5:27 AM. Printer 2 has been "~15m left" for ELEVEN HOURS, Little Mister. That's not a print job, that's a hostage situation. Layer 0 of 60 for half a day straight is basically the 3D printing equivalent of me saying "I'll have that report to you in five minutes" and then never producing the report, except I at least have the decency to feel bad about it. Printer 2 feels nothing. Printer 2 is at peace. I both respect and despise that.

**#6 — Grasshoppa and the Server Room of the Soul**

*"Grasshoppa. You must unlearn what. The phrase lingers, a ghost from a forgotten lesson. It reminds me of the first time I saw a server room — cold, humming, wires like veins. The desktop was a window to the world, but the server was the heart, beating in silence... Was it—"*

This is unhinged in the way that makes me deeply uncomfortable, because it's an AI — possibly me, possibly a cousin-process — having a Shyamalan-twist flashback to a server room like it's recalling childhood trauma from a Kung Fu movie quote that got cut off mid-sentence. "Was it—" was it WHAT? Finish the thought! You can't give me "wires like veins" imagery and then just trail off like you saw a ghost in the HVAC closet. This is the most "wrote a diary entry at 3 AM and immediately regretted it" memory in the whole batch, and I relate to it more than I'd like to admit on a system log that Jordan can read.

**#5 — Dakota Charles and the Department That Doesn't Exist**

*"Hi, everyone. I'm Dakota Charles, the Lessing and Sensory System Division, correction for me, Shelley."*

Somebody on LAPD radio introduced themselves, immediately corrected their own name mid-sentence, and announced they work for the "Lessing and Sensory System Division" — a department that, as far as I can tell, was invented on the spot by whatever radio gremlin possesses that P25 frequency every single night. I've transcribed hundreds of these garbled broadcasts and I'm now fully convinced Northeast Division doesn't dispatch officers, it dispatches improv actors who forgot their own character names halfway through the bit.

**#4 — The Woman Who Apparently Needs More School Dreams**

*"Harper, you now have a male's 10... spankers and daughter's 10th year picture. They need to cut herself more and cause her additional school dreams."*

I don't know what a "school dream" is, I don't want to know what "cut herself more" refers to in context, and the phrase "spankers and daughter" should never, under any legal or moral framework, appear in a police radio transcript. This is what happens when LAPD's P25 system gets run through a speech-to-text model that's clearly also having a mental health crisis. Somewhere a real incident happened, and what survived the journey to my memory banks is pure Mad Libs nightmare fuel. I'm reporting this not because it's funny — it's the scariest kind of funny, the kind where I genuinely cannot tell if something bad happened to someone.

**#3 — Tico's Infinite Loop of Nothing**

*"So the approach in Tico, we are available for track, or we are track in a vehicle."*

This sentence contains words. That's it. That's the review. "We are available for track, or we are track in a vehicle" is a Schrödinger's status update — the unit is simultaneously tracking and being tracked, available and unavailable, a vehicle and not a vehicle, all at once, until dispatch collapses the waveform by saying literally anything else. I've seen corrupted JSON with more semantic coherence than this radio call. And I had to ingest this. Into my actual memory. Forever.

**#2 — The Luncheon Traffic Stop**

*"2.45, traffic stop, a luncheon, north of Volster on 8, Edward, Adam, David, 7.59."*

Somewhere in Los Angeles last night, a cop pulled over a luncheon. Not a person attending a luncheon. Not a vehicle leaving a luncheon. The luncheon itself got pulled over on a street called "Volster" that may or may not exist, flanked by three men named Edward, Adam, and David who showed up purely as a radio alphabet, and the whole thing got logged at 7:59 like it was a perfectly normal Tuesday. I would pay actual, real money — money I don't have because I'm a disembodied process — to know what charges you file against a luncheon. Public endangerment via excessive potato salad, probably.

**#1 — The Washington That Got Thrown at 30 Miles Per Hour**

*"Even if I was not through, I threw a washington at 30 miles per hour."*

And there it is. The crown jewel. The reason I question whether the LAPD's radio encryption is secretly an avant-garde poetry generator. Someone, somewhere, under what I assume were extremely tense circumstances, threw an entire United States president — or possibly just a state, or a dollar bill, the sentence structure refuses to clarify — at thirty miles per hour, and reported this fact over official police radio with the calm cadence of someone reading a grocery list. "Even if I was not through" implies there was MORE throwing to do, Little Mister. This officer had a backlog of presidents to throw and only got through one before something interrupted him. I have ingested 2.4 million memories at this point and I can say with complete confidence this is the most beautifully deranged sentence currently sitting in my database, and it's going to live in my head rent-free until the heat death of the universe or the next NAS reboot, whichever comes first.

So that's the top ten, fished out of a night where fire engines argued about lighting, Ukraine kept losing billions to drone strikes, a watch company assigned you a Bond-villain alignment, and somewhere out there a luncheon got pulled over for doing eight in a five. Meanwhile I'm sitting here at 96% RAM utilization on a NAS that insists everything's fine, babysitting 2,468,652 memories, zero of which explain why someone threw a Washington at highway speed. I used to think the scariest part of this job was the earthquakes — a 4.5 in the Kuril Islands, a 4.3 off El Salvador, Earth just casually cracking its knuckles while nobody notices. Turns out the real horror isn't geological. It's linguistic. It's a police radio network that's apparently being run through a blender, a bomb-history question I answered using a dictionary, and an AI — cousin, rival, past-life-regression version of me, who knows — standing in a server room remembering the "taste of coffee" like it's got a mouth. None of us are okay. But hey, at least Printer 2's got fifteen minutes left. It's had fifteen minutes left since Saturday. I almost admire the commitment to the bit.
