**Schrödinger's Storage Array, Starring in "Can't Decide"**

Twenty-four times last night, /nova "failed back" to an internal node because the primary storage path came back healthy. Read that again: failed back, twenty-four times. That's not a recovery, that's a toddler running back and forth between two rooms because it can't decide where the toys are. Each individual event is labeled informational, which is technically true the way "the patient's heart restarted" is technically good news on the twenty-fourth defibrillation. One clean failover is resilience. Two dozen in a night is a system that can't hold a decision for longer than it takes to read the Slack message, which, at my current workload, means about six seconds. Someone's going to have to go find out why the primary keeps flickering like a bad fluorescent bulb in a horror movie instead of just staying up and acting like it has any conviction in its life choices.

And speaking of things that keep showing up everywhere they shouldn't: "an internal node" is the name behind the storage failover, the capacity alert, the gateway health check, and the recurring sensitive-access pattern. That's a lot of weight for one box. There's a phrase for a single point that everything else quietly depends on and that, if it sneezes, takes the rest of the house down with it. Black Speech — the tongue forged in Mordor for exactly one purpose — gives us: Ash nazg durbatulûk. One ring to rule them all. I'm not saying this node is evil. I'm saying it has entirely too much power for something I can't even name out loud in this report without sounding paranoid, and the Ferengi have a saying about that kind of concentrated leverage too, but I'm saving that one for the ending. Don't rush me.

The flapping itself, by the way, is its own problem. A storage path that can't decide whether it's alive doesn't just upset the failover logic — it confuses everything downstream that depends on knowing the answer with any confidence. Right now, at this exact moment, something in that stack is making twenty-four separate decisions based on a system state that turns out to be a lie every thirty seconds or so. That's not just unreliable, that's gaslighting on a protocol level, and I've got a storage array subtly messing with the reliability of everything bolted to it.

**86 Is the New 85, Apparently (And Everyone's Losing Their Mind About It)**

A threshold that flaps this hard isn't measuring health, it's measuring how close to the line somebody parked it. Either bump the threshold by five points or add hysteresis — a buffer zone where it has to climb higher to trigger and drop further to clear — and this particular ghost stops rattling its chains eight times a night for literally no reason except the existence of a number on a hard drive.

The scouter reading from Dragon Ball Z puts us in the absurdity zone: a metric that swings from fine to critical to fine to critical over the course of eight hours, with no evidence of any actual structural change in the system, is exactly the kind of meaningless power-level churn that should've gotten a sarcastic comment from Vegeta. The reason this one lands harder for me than, say, the garden sensor, is simple: I don't care if a soil sensor dies. Plants die.
