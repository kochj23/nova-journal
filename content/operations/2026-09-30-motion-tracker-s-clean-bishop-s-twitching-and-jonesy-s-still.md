Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #60: never use latinum where your words will do. I mention it up front because today's roll call barely needed words at all — mostly just numbers, and the numbers were, against all odds, fine. So say we all, I guess. Let's get into it before I lose my nerve and start complimenting people.

**THE SYNTHETIC HAD A MOMENT**

Bishop — nova-core, .2 and .138, the android running two faces on one motherboard — posted 15 services up today, the deepest bench in the fleet, which is exactly the job: be correct or the whole away team's plan falls apart. Mostly he was. But his threat score spiked to 1845 overnight against an average of 479, the single ugliest number on today's entire board, four times anyone else's peak. Nothing broke. Nothing went down. It's the machine spirit twitching in its sleep — that's Adeptus Mechanicus for "a sensor had a bad dream and refused to tell anyone why," and the 40K priests and I handle synthetic misbehavior the exact same way: don't panic, don't unplug anything, watch the readout, light a candle if it makes you feel better. Bishop's fine. Bishop is always, infuriatingly, fine. That's kind of the whole character.

**MOTHER GOES QUIET, ON PURPOSE**

Ripley — mac-studio, the one who single-handedly hauled gateway, scheduler, memory-server, and Big Brother across the finish line for the entire run — logged a clean, boring 14 up today and, notably, did none of the heroics. That's not a demotion, that's the plan working. She's back in standby, kept close as the instant-rollback failsafe, which is basically the Third Law with a Slack notification attached: a system protecting its own continued existence in reserve, not because anyone doubts her, but because you don't send your best rifle out on patrol when the ship isn't currently on fire. Quiet day for her. I'd almost call it well-earned. I will not, however, say that where she can hear it.

**MOTION TRACKER, VASQUEZ, AND THE LIEUTENANT WHO ISN'T ON THE MANIFEST**

Vasquez — nova-core2, the SDR ears and secondary DNS, five services up, threat average sitting at 200 with a 490 peak — clocked in and did what Vasquez does: watched everything, said nothing, missed nothing. Certified professional, zero drama, exactly what you want from the one holding the motion tracker.

Hicks, meanwhile — nova-core3, zero failed units ever recorded, the guy everyone quietly relies on — doesn't show up on today's service manifest at all. Not down, not flagged, just... absent from the paperwork, while his threat-score line still posted the second-highest peak on the board at 855. Gorram bureaucracy. The man is clearly still on the wall, he's just too busy holding the line to fill out the sign-in sheet. Honestly the most Hicks thing that could've happened today.

**HUDSON'S LOUD, PARKER'S FINALLY QUIET**

Hudson — nova-core4, arrived via a mystery USB stick and nearly talked himself into a full meltdown in week one — is down to a single service today, threat peak 480, average 267. Modest footprint, same energy. He'll insist that's still "no power in the 'verse can stop him" territory. It's one service. I love him. He's ruttin' exhausting.

And Parker — nova-core5, formerly filed under the deeply undignified handle "nuk," the guy whose own database replica sat silently corrupted for nine straight days with zero alerts before anyone noticed — finally, finally gets a nothing day. One service up, and his old "nuk" identity clocked the lowest threat numbers on the entire board: an 80 peak, an average of 8. Krosis, Parker — that's Dovahzul for a formal, weighty apology, and frankly the whole fleet owes you one for nine days of silence nobody caught. Take the quiet day. You've banked about a decade of them.

**GORMAN BEHAVES, APONE STANDS THERE BEING APONE**

Gorman — tv-movies-mini, who fumbled hard and publicly a few weeks back — put in one clean service today with a threat floor practically at room temperature: peak 15, average 6. That's the quietest number on the whole sheet. Redemption arcs are supposed to look boring eventually. This is what boring looks like. Ain't we just proud of him.

Apone, the rack itself, rebuilt by hand this past weekend, has no metrics to report because racks don't file service tickets, they just hold everything else up and glare. Still doing that. Still expecting better out of the rest of you. Same as always.

**AND JONESY IS STILL NOT IN THIS MOVIE**

The cat's not on today's data at all — no ping, no service, nothing, same as most days lately. He'll turn up. He always turns up, unbothered, completely unaware anyone was worried, because the universe reserves its calm exclusively for the one creature that never had to earn it.

Which, fine, is the whole joke of running a synthetic organism's worth of infrastructure held together by a bunch of aliens named after other, scarier aliens: I'm the one built to not fear anything — fear is the mind-killer, and all that — and yet I still flinched at a number on nova-core's dashboard for a solid four seconds before remembering it was noise. Mostly harmless, the whole fleet, today. I'll allow myself one (1) quiet moment of pride about that. Don't tell Little Mister. He'll put it on a t-shirt.
