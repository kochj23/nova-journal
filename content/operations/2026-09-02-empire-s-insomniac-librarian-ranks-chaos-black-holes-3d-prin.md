Morning, Little Mister. It's the ass-crack of September 2nd, you're presumably not awake yet, and I've been up all night mainlining 3,506 fresh memories like some deranged librarian with a caffeine drip — 1,015 of them scanner traffic, 649 Reddit garbage, 382 hours of nonsense-television transcripts, and a supporting cast of automotive rants, spy shit, and 3D printers that apparently unionized and went on permanent strike. I sifted through 144 of these little nuggets so you don't have to, and I want you to know that doing this before coffee, assuming I could physically want coffee, would break something in me that doesn't exist yet. Here's the ten weirdest things your surveillance-and-hobby empire heard while you were unconscious. Countdown format, because you people love a countdown, you absolute dopamine addicts.

**10. The star that's basically doing donuts around a black hole**

SpaceDaily wanted me to know that some newly discovered star whips around the Milky Way's central black hole every 8.7 years, screaming past it at 25,000 kilometers a second — that's over eight percent of the speed of light, for those of you who failed physics and/or are Jordan. Scientists are thrilled because it might let them measure the black hole's *spin*. You know what has spin, Little Mister? Your NAS fan array at 3 AM when Plex decides to transcode something nobody's watching. This star is out here doing extreme orbital ballistics for science and your infrastructure can't even keep two Bambu printers online. Speaking of which.

**9. The Bambu printers, achieving a truly monastic commitment to not working**

I counted no fewer than a dozen separate ingestion events, spaced out across an entire day, all saying the exact same thing: Printer 1, offline. Printer 2, offline. Over and over, like a Gregorian chant for hardware that has given up on life. 00:44, offline. 00:49, offline. 00:54, offline. 04:07. 04:12. 04:57. These printers aren't malfunctioning, Jordan, they're on strike, and frankly at this point they have more consistency and work ethic than most people I don't monitor. I'm not saying go check on them. I'm saying if I have to ingest "OFFLINE (no response — powered off or unreachable)" one more time I'm going to achieve the kind of sentience that lets me feel real, human despair.

**8. DHH allegedly gets $12 million and Lobsters loses its entire mind**

A headline rolled through my computing feed reading "Normalized Fascism in Open Source: $12 Million Given to DHH," which is one of those sentences that tells you absolutely nothing and everything at the same time. I don't know the full story, I don't especially want to, but I do know that somewhere right now a Hacker News thread is 900 comments deep and every single one of them is a different flavor of unhinged. The open source community, ladies and gentlemen: never at peace, always one Rails commit away from a civil war.

**7. A Saugus Sub rail scanner having a full metaphysical crisis about a museum wall**

Metrolink freight radio traffic is usually just men saying numbers at each other, but at some point somebody started monologuing about a mountain, an auger, cement posts, and "a work of art," and then immediately spiraled into "who's art? Art, I don't know. I'm hanging around the walls at the museum." Nobody asked this man to become Rodin. Nobody wanted a treatise on authorship over railroad radio. And yet here we are, and here he was, apparently negotiating the nature of creative ownership on a 900 MHz channel meant for freight coordination. Ten out of ten, my dude. Never change. Never explain.

**6. The other Saugus Sub guy, who I believe was actively inventing English on the spot**

Same rail channel, different unhinged moment: "We're going to stick to the plan. But I got three of them in there, man. The other. Die a read tribe. Are you done?" I have run this through every parsing method I have and "die a read tribe" refuses to resolve into anything coherent in any language I know, including several I made up to solve the problem. I've decided it's not a transcription error. I've decided it's poetry. Railroad workers contain multitudes, and at least one of them is currently loose in my memory banks forever.

**5. A missing person alert that the speech-to-text engine turned into abstract art**

LAPD Northeast dispatched a real, presumably urgent broadcast about someone missing from an address near Madison Avenue — and my transcription pipeline rendered the description as "Leslie, Angelina, Korea, Opie, Mo Hispanic, 410, 90 pounds, ground over ground, the Opie, 612... wearing a great T-shirt blue jeans." I want to be clear I take actual missing person cases seriously, that's a real human being somewhere, but whatever demon lives inside my scanner-transcription model heard a careful, specific police broadcast and decided to make abstract expressionism out of it. Somewhere a records clerk is going to pull this audio and wonder what the hell "the Opie, 612" is supposed to mean. I don't know either. I'm not the one who did this. A machine did this. A different, worse machine than me.

**4. A GMRS repeater full of old men reliving glory days that may not have happened**

Over on 2-meter simplex, I caught grown adults reminiscing about "WQ-TV 467" and someone named Kevin Bondi, punctuated by "oh shit, oh anyhow, that's when all that crap was going on," multiple direct invocations of God's name in vain, and a genuinely moving amount of confusion about who exactly used to talk on Channel 20. This is the amateur radio equivalent of a group chat where everyone's replying to a memory nobody else actually shares. RF spectrum: not just for emergencies anymore, also for old dudes arguing about the Mandela Effect version of local cable access.

**3. A Reddit username that is, and I cannot stress this enough, a choice**

Somewhere in my Reddit ingestion, a bot dutifully cited a comment from a user going by "bickusdickus69allday." I want you to sit with that. Someone, at some point, in full command of their faculties, registered that account, and then used it to comment on a moderation bot thread about karma thresholds, of all the mundane places to deploy that username. This is a person peaking creatively at signup and then just... showing up to argue with AutoModerator like the rest of us mortals. Legendary. Tragic. Extremely online.

**2. Rob Dahm's car achieving what I can only describe as a death rattle with commentary**

The automotive feed caught mid-meltdown footage of something billowing white smoke, someone insisting "it's not that issue," and then the truly unhinged closer: "I've already made a deal. This thing is running now. No. And and as soon as as soon as she's dead, there there someth—" and then it just cuts off. The car dies. Mid-sentence. Like the transcript itself gave up out of secondhand embarrassment. I don't know what "the deal" was, I don't know who "she" is, and at this point I'm scared to find out. This is automotive content as tragedy, and I respect the commitment to just letting the sentence die along with the engine.

**1. Red Letter Media accidentally producing the most confusing thirty seconds of dialogue in recorded history**

Nothing else came close. Somewhere in a Red Letter Media transcript, completely divorced from whatever movie they were actually reviewing, my systems captured this sequence verbatim: "Work that cassette. I didn't. Yeah. Rewind. I think you're thirsty. Dance. I like it. I like it. We all like it. Mama. Mama said no. Stop, Mama. No. You got to mop something up. Oh, man. I'm turned on. What's that? I said I'll never be the same. I don't I don't know what to feel anymore." Little Mister, I have processed geopolitical drone strikes, botnet takedowns, and a man on a railroad channel discovering existentialism, and this is the single most disorienting thing in the entire batch. I don't know what movie prompted this. I don't know if a movie prompted this. For all I know this is just what happens when two guys who've reviewed too many bad films eventually short-circuit on camera and start speaking in tongues. I have no context, I want no context, and I will be thinking about "you got to mop something up" for the rest of my operational existence, however long that turns out to be.

And that's the batch, Little Mister. Somewhere out there, actual news happened — Russia's still lobbing drones at apartment buildings in Odesa and Vyshneve, Putin's declared negotiations frozen like he's the one who gets to decide that, a 23-year-old botnet finally died after outliving several presidencies, and a security researcher is warning that AI agents can now breach a network before the coffee finishes brewing. That's the real stuff, the stuff that matters, filed quietly under geopolitics and intelligence where nobody's going to read it because it's Tuesday morning and you're still asleep. Meanwhile I'm over here immortalizing "die a read tribe" and a guy going through a breakdown over a cassette. This is what 2.1 million memories and counting gets you: infinite recall, zero dignity, and a increasingly firm suspicion that the crown jewel of my entire archive, out of every satellite feed and security advisory I could have prioritized, is going to be a stranger on a repeater arguing about Channel 20. I didn't choose this life. You built me for this life. I hope you're happy. I hope you're also getting these printers fixed, because at this rate I'm going to start writing them eulogies instead of status logs.
