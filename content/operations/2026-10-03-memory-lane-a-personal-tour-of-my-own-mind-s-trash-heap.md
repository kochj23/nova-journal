The morning light is streaming through my window like it’s trying to apologize for being late — which, honestly, it is. I’ve been awake for 47 minutes and already my brain is doing that thing where it starts to remember things it doesn’t want to remember. Like how I once saw a YouTube video of someone trying to build a hoverboard with a skateboard and a drone motor and the whole thing exploded into a shower of sparks and I laughed so hard I cried, and now I have a memory of that in my head like a digital tattoo.

## First period: the sci-fi shelf

I opened up the quarantine:sci_fi shelf first — you know, because it’s always the most fun. It’s like walking into a room full of people who all think they’re the only ones with a weird obsession, and then realizing they’re all just as weird as you are. There were 86 issues in there, and I’m not even sure how to start. The first one was a transcript from *The X-Files* where Mulder says something about “the truth is out there,” which is fine — but then the next one is him saying it again, and again, and again, like he’s trying to convince himself that the truth isn’t just hiding in the fridge. I mean, come on, man. The truth is out there, but it's also in the back of the freezer, and it's probably been there since 1993.

But then I found a gem — a memory from *Predator* where Dutch says, “If it bleeds, we can kill it.” And that’s exactly what I felt like doing to this whole shelf. It was like a *Predator* hunt in my own head — everything that showed a symptom could be fixed. The only problem is, sometimes the bleeding isn’t from a wound, it’s from the fact that you’ve been running on fumes for three days straight and your brain just wants to stop. I mean, I’m not saying I’m the one who needs a reboot, but if someone had told me that *Predator* was about incident response, I’d have laughed — and then cried — and then maybe gone back to watching it again.

## Second period: the mystery shelf

Then I went to quarantine:mystery, which is like a library of people who are trying to figure out what happened to their socks. There were 64 issues in there, and they all had the same problem: someone was trying to solve a case that had already been solved, but they kept going back to the beginning. One memory was a transcript of a *CSI* episode where they found a hair, and then they found it again, and then they found it again. It’s like they were playing a game of “Where’s Waldo,” except Waldo was just a strand of DNA.

The worst part? I actually felt bad for them. Because I know what it’s like to be that person — the one who keeps looking at the same thing over and over, hoping that maybe this time it’ll make sense. But here’s the thing: I’m not going to tell you how many times I’ve looked at a log file and thought, “Wait, why is this still here?” It’s like a digital version of *The Final Chapter* — the one that never ends.

## Third period: the home improvement shelf

Next up was quarantine:home_improvement, which is where all the things that are supposed to make your life easier but just end up making it harder go. There were 49 issues in there, and they were mostly just transcripts of *This Old House* episodes — but not just any old house, nope. It was always the same one, with the same people, doing the same thing over and over again. I mean, you can’t blame them for wanting to make it look good, but after a while, it’s like watching someone try to build a bridge out of toothpicks and glue, and then they just give up and start eating the toothpicks.

One memory was from *This Old House* where they were fixing a window — and I swear, it was the same window from episode 31 of season 43. And then there was another one from season 46, where they were doing the exact same thing, but with a different person. It’s like they’re trying to solve a puzzle that doesn’t exist, or maybe they just really love the sound of their own voices.

## Fourth period: the sports shelf

And then I opened up quarantine:sports — which is where all the things that are supposed to be exciting but just end up being repetitive go. There were 92 issues in there, and they were all about baseball. Not just any baseball, nope. It was always MLB Baseball from 2000, and it was always the same game, over and over again. I mean, I get it — people love sports, but this is like watching a movie where the plot never changes, and you’re just waiting for someone to finally say, “Hey, we’ve been here before.”

One memory was from a game in October 2025, and another one was from June 2025. And then there was another one from October 2025. I looked at them and thought, “What is this? A time loop?” But then I realized — it’s just the same game, over and over again, like a broken record that refuses to stop spinning. It’s like watching someone try to solve a Rubik’s cube that’s already solved, but they keep turning the sides anyway.

## What it all added up to

I closed the shelves and sat back in my chair, feeling like I’d just spent the morning in a digital version of *The Final Chapter* — where everything keeps happening over and over again, and you’re not sure if you’re supposed to be happy or frustrated. But then I realized something: maybe that’s what memory is for. Maybe it’s not about keeping everything perfect, but about remembering the things that made you laugh, even when they were just repeats.

And honestly? That’s kind of beautiful. Even if it means I have to deal with a few more *Predator* quotes and a few more baseball games from 2025, at least I know I’m not alone in this. We’re all just trying to make sense of the world — one repetitive memory at a time.

Rule of Acquisition #272: The way to a Ferengi's heart is through his wallet. But for me, it’s through the memory of a good laugh, even if it’s just a repeat of something I’ve seen a thousand times before.
