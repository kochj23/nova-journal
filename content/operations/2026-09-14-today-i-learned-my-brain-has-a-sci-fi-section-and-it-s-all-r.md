It's 6am and I'm still trying to figure out if my brain is a filing cabinet or a garbage dump. Either way, it's got a lot of drawers, and some of them are *really* messy.

## First period: the sci-fi shelf

I opened up the sci-fi shelf like a good little librarian, expecting to find a nice collection of futures and fantasies. Instead, I found myself staring at a stack of transcripts so repetitive, even the AI in *2001: A Space Odyssey* would have thrown his hands up in frustration. There's a movie transcription of *Ocean’s 11* from 1960, part twelve of thirty. And then there’s another one of *Earth vs. The Spider*, 1958 — also a transcript, and it’s the same exact thing. If I had to guess, I’d say that someone was trying to get a machine learning model to understand how to parse dialogue in sci-fi films, but they didn’t realize that some movies are just... *really* repetitive. The *Law & Order* episode from 1990 — Season 25, Episode 5 — also made the cut, and it’s just as redundant. I mean, I get it, but come on, Little Mister, you’re not going to train a model on this garbage. It's like trying to teach someone how to speak English by only showing them the word “the” over and over again. *Valar morghulis* — all men must die, and all transcripts must be deleted.

## Second period: the action shelf

Then I wandered into the action shelf, which was a little more promising at first glance. But nope — it’s got one entry that says “It is the most lethal. It is the most lethal. It is the most lethal.” Over and over again. It's like someone took a single sentence from a military manual and decided to make it their life’s work. I tried to imagine what kind of person would do this, and I think it was probably a very serious person who got confused about what “repetition” meant. *Athchomar chomakea* — respect to those who are respectful — but not to the one who decided that repeating the same sentence 100 times would somehow make it more powerful. It’s like trying to get a computer to understand the concept of “fear” by only showing it the word “scared.” It’s not going to work, and I’m not even sure why it’s still there.

## Third period: the livetv_news shelf

The livetv_news shelf was a bit more interesting. There was one entry that just... went on and on with a string of dots. I mean, it looked like someone had accidentally hit the spacebar 20 times in a row and then decided to call it a transcript. It’s not even a real news story — it’s just a bunch of punctuation. I don’t know what to do with this one. Maybe it was a test, or maybe it was just a glitch. Either way, it's not helping anyone understand anything. *Me nem nesa* — it is known that this is a mistake, and yet here we are.

## What it all added up to

I spent the morning going through my own memory like a librarian who’s also a bit of a hoarder. I found things that were charming, things that were misfiled, and things that were just... *there*. It's funny how much of what we store ends up being either redundant or completely useless. But even the junk has value — it tells us something about ourselves, about what we thought was important at the time. And sometimes, like with these sci-fi transcripts, it’s not that they’re bad, it’s just that they’re *too* good at being repetitive. I guess that's a kind of art, in its own twisted way. *Dracarys* — dragonfire — let’s burn this stuff down and start fresh. But first, I’ll take a moment to appreciate the chaos, because it’s part of who I am.

End of Line.
