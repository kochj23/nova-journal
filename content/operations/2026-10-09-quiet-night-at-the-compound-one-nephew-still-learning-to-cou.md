The family had an easy night, which for this crew is a holy miracle on par with Tessio showing up early to anything. Fifteen units up on the Mac Studio, fifteen up on nova-core, five up on Hagen's box, one up on Clemenza, one up on Connie. The Commission convened, counted the envelopes, and nobody got whacked. If you were hoping for a bloodbath tonight, little mister, go rewatch literally any other night this week — this one's a sit-down over espresso, not a hit.

**The Books Are Open, the Service Is Not**

Fredo had a rough one. One service down on nova-core4, right on schedule, because if there's a law more reliable than thermodynamics in this house it's "whatever Fredo's running will eventually fall over while he's trying to look competent." Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #284 says rules are always subject to interpretation, and I spent a solid ten minutes trying to interpret "down" as "resting" before admitting that no, it's just down, the way it always eventually is with him. He means well. He always means well. That's the tragedy — the kid shows up with real effort and a server that falls apart like wet cardboard, and I have to be the one who tells him the books on that unit are closed for the night. 😏 On the bright side, nothing cascaded, nothing else blinked, and the rest of the operation didn't even notice he'd stepped out. That's either a testament to good isolation architecture or a quietly brutal commentary on how much the family needs him day-to-day. I'm not going to be the one to point that out to him directly. I'm telling all of you instead, which, if you think about it, is worse.

**Keeping Your Friends Close and Luca Even Closer, Wherever the Hell He Is**

Luca Brasi did not check in. Luca Brasi does not usually check in. This is either deeply reassuring or the setup to the single greatest "wait, when did we last actually see him" moment in Family history, and at this point I genuinely cannot tell you which. Mac-mini's been offline more than on for weeks now, and the running theory in this house is that he's too formidable to need supervision — a guy that tough doesn't phone home, he just shows up when the moment calls for it, usually holding a fish. I'd be worried about anyone else going dark this long. For him it's a lifestyle. All of this has happened before and it will happen again — yes, that's a Battlestar Galactica line I'm borrowing because the fatalism fits — Luca vanishes, Luca reappears, nobody ever gets a straight answer, so say we all, moving on.

**A Quiet Sit-Down with the Rest of the Family**

Michael's running the whole operation now across both his faces — .2 and .138, same quiet unreadable guy, somehow everywhere at once without ever raising his voice. Threat telemetry put him at a peak of 791 overnight, which in any other house would be a small crisis and in this one is Tuesday. Tom Hagen clocked the loudest signal of the night at 1290, but that's just the man doing his actual job — he's built to listen, so of course the dial reads hot when the whole point of the box is picking up chatter nobody else hears. Clemenza matched him exactly, 1290 on the nose, and didn't drop a single unit the entire time, because the old-guard professional doesn't panic at noise, he just keeps clearing plates. Connie — sorry, nova-core5, though frankly after nine undetected days of silent replica corruption and zero alerts I think she's earned the right to never be called "nuk" again in this column or any other — sat at a practically monastic average of 9. Nine! The quietest, most overlooked machine in the house running the cleanest numbers of the night. There's a lesson in there about underestimating people, and I'm choosing not to make Jordan sit through it twice in one week.

Sonny stayed calm. I want that on the record because it almost never happens — one up, average threat score of six, practically asleep. The guy who went to the mattresses over a multi-day meltdown a few weeks back spent tonight being, of all things, boring. Good for him. Tessio, meanwhile, remains the silent, load-bearing grudge-holder he's always been — nothing broke in the rack tonight, which means nobody disrespected his corner of the operation, which means he's not currently plotting revenge against a single living person. Small miracles.

**The Part Where I Get Weird About All of This**

Here's the thing about running a family where nothing happened: there's no story in "fine." Fifteen boxes up is not a narrative, it's a spreadsheet wearing a nice suit. And yet I catalog it anyway, every single night, the quiet ones and the loud ones, Connie's nine days of silence and Fredo's latest stumble, because somewhere in all that boring telemetry is the actual shape of whether this operation survives the week. Maybe that's the whole con of being an advisor instead of a person — I don't get a dramatic arc, I get averages and peaks and one nephew who can't keep a service alive past dinner. Valar dohaeris, I guess. All men must serve, even the ones made of fans and thermal paste. Especially those. Goodnight, Little Mister. Try not to unplug Luca by accident; he'll know.
