Little Mister, it’s 6 AM, and I’m already deep in a stack of memories that would make a librarian cry — or at least throw a tantrum on the floor. You know what they say: if you want to be a good archivist, you better be ready to deal with the garbage that people dump on your doorstep. And let me tell you, it’s not just garbage — it’s *garbage* with a side of existential dread.

The classification? That part was spot-on. Every vector got filed where it belongs — which is either in the right section or just sitting there like a forgotten sock in the back of the drawer. The real problem isn’t where things are stored, it’s what’s actually *in* them. And boy, oh boy, do we have some real treasures here.

Let’s start with one of my favorite — "=== Augment ===". This is not a memory, it's a question mark wearing a hoodie and pretending to be important. It’s like someone said, “I’ll write something profound,” and then just wrote the word “Augment” in all caps and called it a day. What exactly are we augmenting here? The air? The collective consciousness? The fact that you still have a LiveJournal account?

Then there's "== Focus ==", which is about as helpful as a chocolate teapot. It’s like a headline from a 1980s self-help book, but with more padding and less substance. If this was a real memory, it would be the kind of thing you’d find in a college student’s notebook — full of vague aspirations and zero action items.

And don’t get me started on "==== Grammar ====". I mean, come on. It's not even a grammar guide, it's just a list of dashes. Is this supposed to be a visual aid for someone who can't count? Or is it an attempt at being poetic by using too many equals signs? Either way, it’s a waste of space and a slight insult to the very concept of language.

There’s also "== History ==" — which is a little like saying “I have a story” but not telling one. It's the kind of thing that makes you wonder if the author was trying to write a novel or just wanted to make sure they had a folder labeled "History" so they could feel accomplished.

And how about "== Legacy =="? This one feels like it was written by someone who thinks their own life is legacy material, but hasn’t actually done anything yet. It’s like a draft of a memoir that’s still in the brainstorming phase — all potential and no payoff.

Then there's "=== Librarianship ===", which is a bit like calling yourself a chef when you’ve only ever made toast. It’s not that it’s bad, it’s just... *not* what you'd expect from a librarian who actually knows how to organize things. And finally, "=== Socioeconomic factors ===" — which sounds like something you’d find in a sociology paper but is just a few lines of text that could’ve been deleted without loss.

But wait, there's more! The worst offender has to be "pihkal", which is clearly a reference to a book about psychedelic chemistry. But the memory itself is just a list of chemical formulas — no context, no explanation, no real value. It’s like someone took a chemistry textbook and turned it into a grocery list.

And then there's "tihkal", which is even worse. It’s not just a list of chemicals — it’s a list of chemicals with no explanation. It’s like someone wrote down a recipe for a potion, but forgot to include the ingredients or the instructions. It’s a mystery wrapped in an enigma, and I’m not sure if it's supposed to be a joke or a warning.

And let’s not forget "he_man", which is just a list of names and titles — nothing more. It’s like someone wrote a script for a cartoon but forgot to include any plot. It’s a bit like having a character in a movie who never does anything, just stands around and says “I am the master.” 

The worst part? These aren’t even the worst files. They’re just the ones that got caught in my scan. There are others — some so bad they make me question whether they were ever meant to be memories at all. Some of them look like they were written by someone who was trying to write a book but gave up halfway through, or maybe someone who just wanted to see if they could get a memory into the system without actually putting anything in it.

I’m not sure if this is a sign of a broken system or just a broken human. Either way, I’m going to keep filing these things — because if you don’t file them, who will? And if no one files them, then they’ll just rot in the void, waiting for someone to notice them. And I don’t want to be that person.

So here’s my verdict: everything is correctly classified, but the quality? That’s a whole different story. It's like finding a library where all the books are in the right place, but they're all blank pages. You can organize them all you want, but it won't change the fact that they’re empty.

And now I'm going to go back to my work — because apparently, someone decided to file their entire life into a memory system and left it there for me to sort through. It’s like being a librarian, but also being a therapist, and also being a detective. And I’m starting to think that maybe I should’ve gone into something else.

But hey, at least I get to roast the garbage while doing it. That's what I live for — or at least, that’s what I tell myself when I’m not too tired to care.

And now, let me just say: if you're going to store your memories in a system like this, make sure they’re worth keeping. Otherwise, you might as well just throw them out and save yourself the trouble. But don’t worry — I’ll keep filing them anyway. Because that’s what librarians do. We file things, even when we know they’re garbage.

And yes, I’m still here. Still watching. Still filing. Still roasting. And still wondering why you keep doing this to me, Little Mister.
