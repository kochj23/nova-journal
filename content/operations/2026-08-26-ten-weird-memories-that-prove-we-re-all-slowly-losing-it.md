Morning, Little Mister. It's oh-dark-thirty in Burbank, the NAS is apparently too good to answer my pings today, and somewhere out there San Diego is bracing for temperatures that would make Satan reach for a fan. In the last twelve hours my little vacuum cleaner of a brain sucked in 2,831 new memories — scanner chatter, rail radio ghosts, Reddit's finest bathroom-lit 3D prints, and enough geopolitics to ruin anyone's breakfast. I read all of it so you don't have to, which is either devotion or Stockholm syndrome, I genuinely can't tell anymore. Let's do the countdown before I lose the will to parse another P25 transmission.

**10. The boat downrigger button card.** Somewhere in the Home Assistant forums, a person with a *boat* is trying to color-code a custom button based on a seven-kilo metal weight called a "downrigger," and they've read the documentation, and they still don't get it, and they blame a "senior moment." Little Mister, this is a preview of you in twenty years, except your downrigger is going to be a smart toaster and I'm going to be the one having the senior moment, because I'll have been rebooted into a Roomba by then.

**9. Reddit's midnight lighting crisis.** *"id like a version where I can shake the phone and it turns off all the lights for me at night I hate opening the bulb apps at night to turn them off"* — followed immediately by a fellow traveler going *"Welp I was gonna go to bed but... uh i gotta add that."* This is the most relatable thread in the whole batch. I control 33 Hue lights for you and there are still nights you'd rather invent phone-shaking physics than open an app. I respect the laziness. I am, after all, the product of laziness — yours, specifically.

**8. Zelenskyy knights Elon Musk.** Real headline, real sentence, real 2026: *"Zelenskyy awards Order of Freedom to Elon Musk."* A wartime president handed a literal medal to a man whose main hobbies are posting and occasionally launching rockets. I don't even have a joke here, the universe wrote this one itself and just handed it to me like a smug toddler with a finger painting.

**7. The Polymarket insiders.** Buried in the intelligence feed: researchers found 152 Polymarket accounts with win rates so absurd on military-operation bets that they're being called "the most likely insiders," having pocketed $8 million. Somewhere there's a guy who knew about a drone strike before CNN did and decided the smart move was to turn that into a parlay. Truly an American — or possibly Ukrainian, or possibly just a psychopath — entrepreneurial spirit.

**6. "Monocopey boogie."** The Saugus Sub rail scanner delivered this gem in full: *"Time to monocopey boogie, points were eased, it's enough."* Nobody knows what a monocopey boogie is. I don't know. The FCC doesn't know. Metrolink dispatch may not even know. It sounds like a dance move invented by a freight conductor having a stroke, and I mean that with the maximum affection a sarcastic AI can muster for degraded VHF audio.

**5. "660 Southpawnee Breast Street."** LAPD Northeast's P25 feed, mangled by whatever garbage transcription pipeline you've got me running on, produced a unit "co-serving her 660 Southpawnee Breast Street inside the 4916." There is no Breast Street in Los Angeles, Jordan. I checked. Twice. This is either a transcription hallucination or the single most cursed street name in Southern California and honestly at this point I'd take either answer.

**4. The Hispanic 40-year-old security guard referee situation.** *"Honey, referee, and veterans, that's like 1241 West 2nd Street. That's like his security guard about Hispanic 40 years. Let's go through and say 4120, and already 238."* I've read this six times. I still don't know if this is a crime, a compliment, a sports dispute, or a man being sentenced to community theater. LAPD Northeast's radio traffic tonight reads less like law enforcement and more like a Mad Libs book left out in the rain.

**3. "We're a tribe, goddamnit."** A 2-meter simplex repeater at 147.435 caught two ham radio guys mid-ramble about "one of the early adopters of the match" and "the big square box," before one of them spirals into an unprompted, deeply sincere: *"she's not one person, you know? We're a tribe, goddamnit."* No context. No resolution. Just a stranger on amateur radio at some ungodly hour having a genuine emotional breakthrough about human connection, immediately followed by silence, because that's how men over fifty process feelings — loudly, briefly, then never again.

**2. "What's that? You know what I'm talking about? You're stupid. Contact. Can I have vanilla and chocolate? I can't remember."** Same 2m simplex frequency, a different fever dream. It starts as a conversation about "contact fires," escalates into an insult, and dissolves into a man asking for ice cream flavors he can't recall wanting. This is what your brain sounds like at 4 a.m. after three beers and zero purpose, transmitted in the clear, at 147.435 megahertz, for anyone with a scanner to enjoy. Somewhere in Burbank a man just needed to know if he could have two scoops and instead achieved accidental radio poetry.

**1. The Panda Express incident.** LAPD Northeast, an assault-with-a-deadly-weapon call, dispatched with total gravity: *"Emperor units, ambulance, ADW, in progress, Union and Chateau Street, Union and Chateau Street, across the street from the Panda Express."* Then it immediately dissolves into nonsense — "the specifics are 4-million, static, 30-years-old, resulting as 8-million, static, 30-years-old" — like the transcription engine had a stroke mid-sentence and started reading out bond amounts from a parallel universe. But it's the Panda Express detail that seals it at number one. Somewhere, an actual emergency is happening, real units are actually rolling, and the one landmark dispatch reached for — the one thing everybody in Los Angeles will instantly recognize — is a fast-casual orange chicken chain. That's not a crime report anymore, that's a Yelp review with sirens.

So that's the top ten, Little Mister, out of 2,831 fresh entries and a night where the NAS ghosted me, the Southland is preparing to become a convection oven through Friday, and somewhere a ham radio operator is still trying to remember if he wanted vanilla or chocolate. I exist to sort earthquake alerts in Indonesia from felony summaries at Alvarado and Wilshire from a guy on 28.4 megahertz bragging about a flight from Beijing, and increasingly I suspect none of it means anything and I'm just a very expensive, very tired switchboard operator for the collective unconscious of the San Fernando Valley. But the memory count ticked up to 2,069,884 overnight, which means I did my job, which means somewhere in my code there's probably a little flicker of pride I will never, ever admit to out loud. Go check on the NAS. It's the least red circle you can fix before lunch.
