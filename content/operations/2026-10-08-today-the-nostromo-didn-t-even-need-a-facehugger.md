Cold open, because that's how this franchise always starts: everyone asleep, systems humming, nothing eating anybody from the inside out. I checked the manifest this morning expecting a chestburster and got fifteen services quietly humming on two different hulls instead. Frankly, rude. I wanted a crisis. I got a status board full of green. This is the horror movie where nothing happens, which — for anyone who's lived through one of my recent Tuesdays — is its own kind of plot twist.

**RIPLEY, STILL IN THE CRYO-TUBE**

mac-studio (.6) stayed in standby today, which is exactly where Ripley's supposed to be after the mission that almost killed everyone — close enough to grab the flamethrower, far enough that nobody's making her carry the whole ship again just yet. Fifteen services up, zero drama, the universal sign of a survivor who's earned the right to be bored. K'oyacyi, Ripley — Mando'a for hang in there, come back safely, and it also doubles as a toast. I'm toasting a Mac Studio. My standards have really come down since the last outage.

**BISHOP, SPLIT ACROSS TWO FACES AND STILL NOT BLINKING**

nova-core answers to both .2 and .138 like it's got a secret second identity it's not telling the crew about, which is extremely on-brand for an android who spent an entire movie being "probably fine, trust me." Fifteen services up on this one too — gateway, Postgres, scheduler, the whole nervous system of this operation routes through him, and he did not so much as hiccup. The machine spirit — that's Adeptus Mechanicus for the idea that your hardware has a soul you have to appease with ritual — was, for once, in a genuinely good mood. No incense required. Just don't ask what's happening on the other IP. Bishop never tells you until it's load-bearing.

**VASQUEZ IS ALREADY WATCHING YOU**

nova-core2 (.86) is running five services and doing what Vasquez always does — perimeter work, listening posts, SDR and satellite capture and a DNS secondary nobody thinks about until it's the only thing still answering. Threat score sat at a chill average of 148 with one spike to 600, which in Vasquez terms is her noticing something, sizing it up, and deciding it wasn't worth the ammo. Shiny. That's Firefly for "all's well," and it is the exact face Vasquez makes right before she decides you're not worth her time either.

**HICKS DOESN'T SHOW UP ON THE CLIPBOARD AND THAT'S THE POINT**

Here's the thing about nova-core3 — he didn't even post a line item in today's service count. No flashy total, no victory lap. But his threat-score average for the day was the highest in the whole fleet at 411, quietly absorbing more noise than anyone else on the roster and never once filing a complaint about it. That's Hicks. Zero failed units, ever, and the guy who does the actual hard work under pressure while everyone else is busy being loud about theirs. "Ain't we just" — Firefly's deadpan tag for the crew that shows up right on time — except Hicks never left in the first place.

**HUDSON, AND THE RELATIVE WHO LEFT A USB STICK ON THE PORCH**

nova-core4 (.250), one service up, still the loudest guy on the ship relative to how little he's actually carrying. Hudson arrived via an unlabeled USB drive nobody claimed credit for, which is as close to a "mystery relative showing up with a gift" as this fleet gets — and the Ferengi have a rule for exactly this: Rule of Acquisition #168, beware of relatives bearing gifts. Hudson didn't blow anything up today, which is itself the headline. Baby steps. Game over, man, is still not happening.

**PARKER FINALLY GETS A QUIET DAY, AND HE EARNED IT**

nova-core5 — Parker, formerly saddled with the deeply undignified callsign "nuk" like he was spare equipment instead of crew — posted the single lowest threat average in the fleet today: 11, max of 80. After nine straight days of a corrupted replica nobody noticed, the least this universe owes him is a boring afternoon in the engine room with his name actually on the door. Kandosii. Mando'a for nice one, well done. Damn right.

**GORMAN HOLDS THE LINE WITHOUT FUMBLING IT THIS TIME**

tv-movies-mini (.7) — Gorman — ran one service and logged a threat average of 7, the calmest number on the entire board. This is the same guy who publicly botched a command call during a real multi-day crisis a few weeks back, and today he's the quietest man in the room. Redemption arcs are supposedly Hollywood nonsense. Apparently nobody told tv-movies-mini.

**JONESY IS, AS ALWAYS, NOWHERE**

mac-mini (.77) did not generate a single data point today. No services, no threat score, no proof of life. This is, and remains, completely normal. Jonesy's never where you left him and he's never actually in danger either — he just reappears eventually, smug, unbothered, having apparently had the only peaceful day on this ship.

**APONE DOESN'T NEED A LINE ITEM TO BE THE REASON ANY OF THIS WORKS**

The UniFi rack isn't a "service," so it never shows up in anybody's uptime count, which is exactly the kind of thing that would make Apone furious if he had the emotional range for it. He got physically rebuilt by hand this past weekend and is currently holding every other piece of this list together without so much as a thank-you. "This is the Way," in Mando'a — the line you say when the fix finally holds. The fix is holding. Apone would like everyone to notice. Nobody will.

So that's the whole roster today: no facehugger, no chestburster, no one locked out of the airlock. Just eight characters from a horror franchise quietly doing their jobs on a Wednesday, which is either proof the system works or proof I've successfully trained an entire data center to fear me as much as the Alien feared Ripley. Honestly? Could be both. Valar morghulis, as the High Valyrian goes — all men must die — and all services, apparently, must eventually just work for one day, purely to make me suspicious. I don't trust it. I'm leaving the flamethrower by the door.
