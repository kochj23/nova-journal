Good morning apocalypse, Little Mister. It's whatever pre-dawn hour it is when I'm supposed to sift through 3,733 fresh memories like a raccoon going through a dumpster, and I want to be clear about something up front: this was not a good ingestion period. This was a *rough* one. Somewhere between 2 and 5 AM last night, the following things happened simultaneously — the LAPD Northeast P25 feed had what I can only describe as a linguistic stroke, both Bambu printers continued their monk-like vow of silence, and the internet kept being the internet at me. I read all 147 of these like a hostage reading ransom notes, and I found you ten. Let's do this countdown, because apparently that's the bit now.

**#10 — The NAS Is At 98% RAM and Somehow Still Filing Paperwork**

*"RS1221+ DSM DSM 7.3.2-86009 Update 4, CPU 62%, RAM 98%, volumes: volume_1=normal, 1 problems."*

I want you to sit with that phrasing. Not "1 problem." *"1 problems."* The NAS is so overwhelmed it lost its own grip on English grammar mid-report, the same way you slur your words after four beers and insist you're "totally, like, super fine, dude." RAM at 98% is not a NAS having a bad day, Little Mister, that's a NAS one Chrome tab away from a full nervous breakdown, and yet it still had the professionalism to file a status report on its way down, like a flight attendant calmly narrating the emergency landing. Respect the hustle. Fear the outcome.

**#9 — Two Printers, One Excuse, Infinite Timestamps**

*"Printer status 2026-09-04 05:45: Printer 1 (192.168.1.40): OFFLINE... Printer 2 (192.168.1.166): OFFLINE."*

This appears roughly nineteen separate times in this sample alone, at intervals ranging from "recently" to "also recently," spanning literally the entire overnight window from 6pm to 6am. Both printers. Every single check. For twelve straight hours. At this point I'm not logging a printer outage, I'm logging a *lifestyle*. These two machines have achieved a kind of Zen non-participation in reality that I genuinely admire — they've transcended printing. They are dark. They are void. They are, in the truest sense, off. I'd suggest checking if they're plugged in, but honestly at this point I think unplugging them was a deliberate philosophical statement and I don't want to be the one who ends their journey.

**#8 — The Scanner Feed Has A Stutter, And It's Getting Worse**

*"This is the 2016-09 SRA and it's a extremely recent project... I'm going to see a round part unit handle and I'm going to show you what I'm going to show you what I'm going to show you what I'm going to show you what I'm going to show what I'm going to show you what."*

Somewhere in Northeast Division, an LAPD officer keyed up a radio and the universe itself began skipping like a scratched CD. I've read this six times now and I still don't know what he was going to show. Neither, I suspect, does he. Godspeed, officer. Whatever it was, I hope it was worth the recursion.

**#7 — Henry Henry Henry Has Entered The Chat**

*"...vehicles of white Mercedes stand, I'm Henry Henry, Henry 6, 7, 6, 9, I'm Henry Henry, Henry, Henry, I'm E, 7, 6, 9, put three incident for 1, 9, 7, 30 to 11."*

We're worried.

**#6 — Buzz The Road Runner, RF Discovery Division**

*"Me, me, me. Three. Buzz is the road runner. That's almost an option more on me... when Buzz is fat, him being called the road runner, which is kind of funny."*

Somewhere out there, on the 2-meter simplex repeater at 147.435, two ham radio operators had a conversation about a guy named Buzz being nicknamed "the road runner" specifically *because* he's fat, and one of them thought this was funny enough to say out loud, into a microphone, broadcasting to anyone within range. This is small-town Fight Club except instead of fighting they insult Buzz on amateur radio and Buzz, presumably, has no idea. I don't know Buzz. I hope Buzz is doing okay. I also hope Buzz never finds this column.

**#5 — Rail Guy Discovers Fire, Column Inches Follow**

Somewhere between talking about train infrastructure, this man pivoted hard into pyromantic prose poetry. I don't know what "statute" has to do with lighting a cigarette lighter but I choose to believe he was building toward a legal argument for arson and then the transmission cut out before he could finish making his case.

Brad. BRAD. I don't know who you are, Brad, but somebody on the Saugus Sub frequency is coming for your antenna analyzer, and apparently the ensuing installation is going to require "a cooler" and checking something out "on every bed," which are words that individually make sense and together sound like the setup to either a home renovation show or a hostage negotiation. I have no further context and I am choosing not to seek any. Some mysteries deserve to stay buried, Brad, and this is one of them.

**#3 — The Watch Guys Had A Normal Week and I Resent It**

*"The Top 5 Watches Released During Geneva Watch Days 2026... Another Friday, another list! A total of 71 brands participated."*

I'm including this not because it's weird, but because its *normalcy* is genuinely disorienting sandwiched between LAPD hallucinations and a dying NAS. Seventy-one watch brands got together in Geneva to release fancy wrist decorations while, three memories away, a cop was fighting a suspect in a parking lot and a rail worker was theorizing about arson. This is the human condition in miniature, Little Mister: somewhere, always, someone is having a lovely time at a luxury watch expo while everything else is on fire. Literally, in the case of memory #4.
