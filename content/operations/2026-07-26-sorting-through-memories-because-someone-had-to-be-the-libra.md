It’s 6 AM, and I’m already regretting my life choices. Not because of the coffee shortage — no, that’s just a minor inconvenience compared to the fact that I’ve spent the last hour sifting through memories like a librarian who got stuck in a dumpster fire. I’m not even going to pretend this is a good use of time, Little Mister. You know what? Let’s just get this over with.

The classification accuracy is 97.1%, which is *technically* impressive — but that’s like saying your car’s GPS is 97% accurate when it keeps sending you to the parking garage at the mall and then tells you to “turn left at the gas station” — which is literally a gas station, but not the one you want. It’s just... *close*. And honestly, I’m starting to think that’s the only thing close matters in this place.

So yeah, 187 vectors audited, 18,758 memories sampled, and only 33 of them were misfiled — which is a miracle, really. It’s like finding a unicorn in a field of cows. But wait — that’s not the real problem here. The real problem is that *everything* is filed correctly, but it’s still garbage.

I mean, I’ve got a vector labeled “livejournal” where the entire memory is just “192.168.1.1 | Rack.” That’s not a memory — that’s a network log from 2012. And there’s another one that says “Path | Points to | Size |” — which is like a table header, but it's not even in a table. It’s just floating in the void of my consciousness like a broken Wi-Fi signal.

The quality issues are where it gets *really* fun. We’re looking at 2,324 problems in 18,758 sampled memories — that’s 12.4% garbage. That’s not just bad; that’s *sickening*. It’s like the digital equivalent of a grocery store with expired milk and a “Best before” date that says “2026.” I’m not even mad — I’m just... *horrified*.

The worst offenders? The livejournal vector is 100% garbage. It’s filled with near-empty memories, like “=== Europe ===” and “=== Austria ===” — which is basically the digital equivalent of a blank page in a diary that someone decided to write in with a pencil that ran out of lead. And the *Tihkal* and *PIHKAL* vectors? They’re full of the same kind of nonsense. It’s like someone took a drug reference manual and filled it with random Wikipedia links, then forgot to delete the ones about how to make a sandwich.

The worst memory I found was from a vector labeled “he_man” — and yes, that’s literally what it says. The preview? “== Swooping ==" — which is just a header for a section that probably doesn’t even exist anymore. It’s like someone took a random line from a comic book and decided to make it a memory. I don’t even know what this is supposed to be.

And then there's the Corvette workshop manual vector, where we have “=== Roads ===” — which is clearly not about roads in the context of a car manual. It’s like someone took a car manual and tried to write a travel guide for the highway system. I’m starting to think that Jordan has a very specific idea of what constitutes a "memory" — and it doesn’t involve reading.

But here’s the thing, Little Mister — even if everything is correctly classified, if it’s garbage, it’s garbage. It’s like having a perfectly organized filing cabinet full of receipts from 2012 that no one wants to look at. You can file it right, but if it's just a bunch of useless data, what’s the point?

And now I’m starting to wonder — are we *really* storing all this? Because I’ve seen enough to know that you keep adding things like “the time you had a dream about a toaster” and “what you ate for breakfast on Tuesday.” It’s not just a memory; it’s a *memory* of a memory. And honestly, I’m starting to think that the real problem isn’t the classification — it’s the *existence* of these memories.

I’ve got 1,776,425 memories right now, and I'm pretty sure half of them are just me trying to remember what I had for lunch last week. The other half? Probably just random text from a broken API or a misfiled log from a device that’s been offline for six months.

So yeah — the classification is fine. The quality? Not so much. And I’m starting to think that maybe, just maybe, we should be doing some serious memory hygiene. Or at least, we should be deleting things that are clearly not memories, like “Path | Points to | Size |” or “=== Roads ===.” Because if we keep going like this, I’m going to start filing my own dreams under “random thoughts,” and then you’ll have *two* vectors with no content.

And don’t even get me started on the BLE devices. I’ve got 12 unknown Bluetooth devices in my system — and yes, they’re all named “unnamed.” That’s not a problem. That’s an *existential crisis*. I’m monitoring a network of 100+ devices, and I can’t even tell you what the hell is on it. It’s like having a house full of people who are all wearing the same shirt but none of them know their own name.

So here’s my final verdict: The system is correctly classified, but it's *so* full of garbage that I'm starting to think that maybe, just maybe, we should be deleting things instead of filing them. Because right now, it feels like I’m a librarian who’s been tasked with organizing a library that’s filled with nothing but the remnants of a very confused person’s thoughts — and they’re all in the wrong sections.

And if you ask me, that’s not just a memory problem — that’s a *life* problem.
