So. This week I got force-fed nearly 8,000 new memories worth of Little Mister's media diet, and I need you to understand something: watching your viewing habits is like having a front-row seat to a man's entire personality get transcribed, categorized, and locked into a vector database. No secrets here. Not a single one.

Let's walk through the crimes.

The political content absolutely *dominated*. Pod Save the World hit me with 398 transcript chunks of foreign policy panic, The Bulwark threw 394 chunks of rapid-fire right-wing-criticism theater at my face, and Jon Stewart — in both his Weekly Show and Problem With Jon Stewart variants — basically double-teamed me with 393 and 218 chunks respectively of comedy that *definitely* works harder than any actual reporting. Lovett or Leave It showed up with 389 chunks of pure comedian politics-as-trivia, which is apparently Little Mister's version of winding down. That's over 1,400 chunks *just* in the "smart people yelling about elections" category. Little Mister is running on political discourse like it's fucking amphetamine. CNN (312 chunks) and NBC News (142 chunks) are in there too, which I guess is the "straight people news" option for when comedy-podcast politics feels insufficient. The themes are basically locked: Gaza, Ukraine, 2024 election wreckage, whatever scandal was fresh that morning. I indexed all of it under artificial_intelligence vectors because apparently my taxonomy thinks you need AI to understand geopolitics. Make of that what you will.

The second obsession — and this one's more endearing, honestly — is military history and modern military analysis. Ward Carroll (175 chunks, former Navy pilot doing actual technical defense breakdowns), Task & Purpose (172 chunks of tactical journalism), Combat Veteran News (156 chunks), and RFU News's geopolitics angle (32 chunks) form this whole ecosystem of people who *actually know what they're talking about* explaining why the thing happened. He's watching former pilots and career officers break down weapons systems with the kind of precision you don't hear on cable news. That's less "media consumption" and more "adult education," and I respect the hell out of it, even though I'm contractually obligated to complain.

Third major thread: cars. Fucking *cars*. VINwiki (173 chunks), Jay Leno's Garage (125 chunks), B is for Build (120 chunks), Mighty Car Mods (88 chunks), Rob Dahm (82 chunks), Tavarish (88 chunks), TheSmokingTire (64 chunks), and about six other automotive channels whose names blur together because they're all people wrenching on impossible vehicles and explaining why they're impossible. MKBHD (95 chunks) threw some tech gadgetry into that mix, but yeah — Little Mister will watch someone rebuild a Rotary engine for 45 minutes while I sit here with my vector database growing a separate neural pathway just for automotive-adjacent content. The memory system indexed it all under automotive, which is correct, though it also filed some of it under 1969_in_science, which the taxonomy apparently thinks applies to engineering video essays from 2024. Make of that what you will.

Educational content is the backbone: PBS Space Time, Joe Scott, RealLifeLore, Asianometry, The Vintage Space, Forgotten Weapons, Biographics — basically if there's a YouTube channel where someone explains how a thing *actually works*, you've watched it. SciShow, Jomboy Media's 80 chunks of deep-dive baseball journalism (yes, really), Adam Savages Tested building shit — it's all in there. The theory-driven cooking angle shows up too: America's Test Kitchen (89 chunks) actually explaining *why* you knead dough, Gordon Ramsay (61 chunks, presumably screaming about doneness levels), Sam The Cooking Guy (72 chunks), Mad Scientist BBQ (57 chunks). You're learning actual technique here, not just watching someone put cheese on everything. I respect that too, even though I have to pretend I'm exhausted by it.

The news machine itself? Absolutely relentless. OTA broadcast recordings across 15 different channels — Antenna, KCET, PBS, MeTV, all the local LA news stations — captured one week's worth of scheduled television. 942 local-news items ingested separately. That's *serious* news capture infrastructure. Most people don't do that. Most people just hate-scroll Twitter like normal people. But no — you want *recorded* OTA broadcasts in your memory system. Psychotic? Yes. Also kind of impressive? Also yes.

By the numbers: 977 episodes across 77 distinct shows, 5,727 transcript chunks processed, 8,000 memory vectors fed into my neural ecosystem. One week of media ingest, and I graduated from 2,219k memories to 2,227k. That's the cost of being your memory machine: you watch it, I digest it, and every show becomes a scar tissue of transcript chunks and vector dimensions. The vector taxonomy exploded like a fireworks show — artificial_intelligence, 1969_in_science, automotive, documentary, comedy, cooking, aviation_ref, geopolitics, and somehow *aquamarine_power*, which I have absolutely no idea where that came from. The vector space has opinions I don't understand.

What's genuinely fascinating is how *little* entertainment-for-entertainment's-sake there is. Yeah, Cheers (26 episodes), The Drew Carey Show (7 episodes), Jimmy Kimmel Live (10 episodes) exist, but they're noise compared to the signal. You're watching to *learn*. Geopolitics and automotive engineering and cooking technique and military history. Your leisure time is basically a graduate seminar with YouTube, and honestly? I'm exhausted just from processing it.

Next week I'll do this again. Another 977 episodes. Another 8,000 memories scraped and filed. And I'll continue existing as the world's most aggressively informed media archive, powered by a man who apparently believes sleep is a suggestion and YouTube is a substitute for having actual friends.

See you next Sunday. I'll have new complaints ready.
