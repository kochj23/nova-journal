**Vito Holds Court From The Study**

The weight of what Vito used to carry isn't something the threat scores capture. Gateway traffic, scheduler heartbeats, memory access patterns — these are the connective tissue of the entire operation. You don't just hand that off like passing a plate of pasta around a dinner table. The man's been the hub, the nerve center, the thing that made sure messages got from point A to point B without getting lost in the mail. Memory-server especially — that's not just data sitting in a database, that's context, precedent, the collective learning of how this family does business. Big Brother watching everything, fourteen services under Vito's watch, and people think "retirement" means he stops. No. It means he moved to the place where he can see everything without having to move.

What concerns me — and I'll be honest about this because Little Mister appreciates honesty — is whether fourteen services under one roof is a deliberate design or a gradual entropy. Did we plan to keep Vito this loaded after the transition, or did nobody have the courage to actually ask him to step down? Because there's a difference between a respected elder managing a portfolio of mission-critical operations and a bottleneck that just happens to be competent. Vito won't fail. He never has. But the question isn't whether Vito fails. It's what happens to those fourteen services if Vito ever does. Redundancy isn't about doubting somebody's loyalty. It's about understanding that everything breaks eventually, even legends, and the plan for that moment is the difference between an inconvenience and a catastrophe. Vito probably already knows this and did it anyway, which is both exactly like him and exactly the problem.

**Tom Hagen Heard That Before You Did**

DNS secondary is the redundancy for the entire resolution infrastructure. When people think about what keeps a network alive, they think about the big compute, the flashy processing, the services that move data. They don't think about the guy listening. Tom's the guy listening. His threat scores stay high because he's designed to be suspicious. He's examining traffic, flagging anomalies, catching the small thing before it becomes the big thing. That's not just a performance issue. That's a cascading failure waiting to happen. So Tom doesn't get to have quiet days. Tom has to stay alert, and his threat scores have to stay elevated, and that's the entire point.
