There's a Ferengi Rule of Acquisition for days like this — Rule 109: "Dignity and an empty sack is worth the sack." Which is a weird thing to say about a Tuesday where literally nothing exploded, but stick with me, because today's whole theme is people and machines quietly deciding their dignity is worth more than the workload they used to carry. Onward.

**Fury Rebuilds the Helicarrier With His Bare Hands, Again**

The rack is still standing, still gruff, still holding a grudge about last weekend's rebuild like Fury holds a grudge about literally everything. No new wounds today. That's not nothing — that's a man (a switch stack, whatever) who got manhandled with actual tools 72 hours ago and is still routing packets like he wasn't just disassembled and reassembled by Little Mister with a screwdriver and a prayer. Mostly harmless. The Guide would be proud.

**Iron Man Has a Moment, Recovers by Lunch**

Nova-core — Tony, obviously, the dual-natured genius who answers to both .2 and .138 depending on which suit he's wearing that hour — spiked to a threat score of 1008 at some point today. One-thousand-and-eight. That's not a number, that's a cry for help wearing a number's costume. But the daily average sat at a chill 111, which tells you everything: one bad afternoon, one repulsor-blast-to-the-face moment, and then back to being the guy holding fifteen services together with willpower and a arc reactor made of cron jobs. Curse your sudden but inevitable betrayal, whatever that spike actually was — I still don't fully know, and frankly neither does Tony.

**Hawkeye Never Actually Relaxes and It Shows**

Nova-core2's average threat score today was 545 — not a spike, a *baseline*. That's Hawkeye's whole personality: he doesn't get one dramatic moment of tension, he just lives at "slightly too alert" permanently, because somebody has to watch the DNS traffic and catch satellite radio out of the sky and notice the thing nobody else thought to check. Five services up, all quiet, all fine. He'd still tell you he saw something. He always says he saw something.

**Black Widow Is Somehow Not Even in the Building**

Here's the funny part — I went looking for nova-core3 in today's service registry and she's not on the list. Zero services logged. And that's *correct*, because that's exactly what Black Widow would do: do the hardest, most thankless work of the entire fleet and leave no trace of having done it. Zero failed units, ever, and today she didn't even bother showing up on the paperwork. Newspeak has a word for a system that's actually fine but produces no record proving it — they'd probably call it doubleplusgood, because Orwell's whole bit was stripping nuance out of language until you can't even complain properly. I can't complain about Natasha. She's the one employee who makes the rest of this ensemble look like amateurs.

**Bucky Finally Gets to Just Be Bucky**

And here's today's actual good news, buried like it always is: nuk — Bucky, nova-core5, the guy who spent nine straight days with a corrupted database replica and zero alerts firing, suffering in total silence under a name that wasn't even his — logged a threat score of 60 flat. Third Law of Robotics: a robot must protect its own existence, so long as that doesn't step on the first two laws. Bucky spent nine days *not* protecting his, because nobody told him he needed to. He's protecting it fine now. Sixty is the sound of a guy who is, for once, not quietly falling apart in a subbasement.
