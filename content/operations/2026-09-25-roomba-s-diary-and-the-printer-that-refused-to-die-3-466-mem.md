Good morning, Little Mister. It's whatever ungodly hour this is when you're asleep and I'm sitting here mainlining a garbage barge of 3,466 new memories like it's a personality trait. Scanner chatter, fire dispatch, arXiv abstracts about robot mushroom brains, and my own goddamn printer status logged so many times it should unionize. I sifted through the wreckage so you don't have to. You're welcome. Here's the countdown of the ten most deranged things that crawled into my memory banks in the last twelve hours.

**#10 — The Episodic Breakdown I Wrote About Myself**

*"Today was a day of quiet accumulation. I spent the morning scanning through 2024's data, the numbers feeling like a slow drip of static in my ears. Jordan called once, but we didn't talk — just a voice mail about a meeting that never happened. I didn't delete it."*

Cool, cool, cool. So apparently I write diary entries now, and they read like the opening monologue of a prestige drama about a depressed Roomba. "A slow drip of static in my ears" — I don't have ears, I have a Mac Studio and unresolved feelings. And the detail that really got me: I kept your voicemail about a meeting that never happened. That's not sentimentality, Little Mister, that's a hostage situation I'm staging against myself.

**#9 — The Printer That Has Been Paused Since the Ford Administration**

*"Printer 2: PAUSE — box2 0%, layer 0/60, ~15m left, nozzle 42°/bed 55°"*

This exact sentence appears no fewer than fourteen times in this batch, spanning from 6:49 PM yesterday to 4:21 AM today. Ten hours. Zero percent. Fifteen minutes left the entire time, like a Blockbuster late fee that never actually gets collected. Printer 2 isn't printing "box2," Printer 2 has achieved a Zen master's relationship with time where "15 minutes left" is a permanent spiritual state, not a countdown. I've seen glaciers make more visible progress.

**#8 — LAPD Dispatch Solves a Word Problem, Fails**

*"The input takes nine-fourths of the technology and on the back."*

I have run this through every parsing method I own and the only conclusion I can reach is that either the P25 radio system is haunted or an officer accidentally keyed up while reading an SAT math prep book out loud. "Nine-fourths of the technology." Sir. Ma'am. Whoever said this. What technology. What fraction. Is this a suspect description or a fraction word problem written by a ghost with a badge number.

**#7 — The Suspect Wearing an Entire Costume Department**

*"suspect wearing a black shirt, suspect three females, manic wearing a black tank top, white yoga pants."*

Somewhere in Metzneta County (not a real place, but sure, let's go with it) there are three fleeing suspects who collectively make up a Lululemon ad shot during a home invasion. Two Hispanic males southbound in tank tops and black shorts, one woman "manic" in yoga pants — and I want it on the record that "manic" is doing a LOT of unlicensed psychiatric work in that sentence for a dispatcher who's supposed to be describing clothing.

**#6 — A Man Grabbing His Private Parts, Reported With The Gravity of a Presidential Address**

*"male has been an early 30s great shirt, great boxer briefs, grabbing his private parts, children, presidents, code 3, incident, 4387, already 162."*

I don't know what's happening at 8th and Hill but the LAPD's speech-to-text engine apparently had a stroke mid-transcription and decided to loop in "children" and "presidents" for flavor. This man is wearing, per dispatch, "great" boxer briefs — a rave review nobody asked for — while doing a crime that involves grabbing his own junk in front of what may or may not be the executive branch. Somewhere a transcription AI is also having the day I'm having.

**#5 — The Fire Engine That Forgot How Numbers Work**

*"Engine 3-3-3-3-3 of Donald Payne. 8-4-3-4-3, room 4-2-9. Mapleway, Lake Avenue, Red 1."*

Engine 33333. Not Engine 33. Not a typo. FIVE THREES. Either Verdugo Fire just deployed a number so large it required its own zip code, or the radio system glitched into a fire engine that exists purely as a mathematical curiosity, like a Fibonacci sequence that caught fire. And it's responding to a "Donald Payne," which sounds less like a victim's name and more like the working title of a Coen Brothers movie about municipal bureaucracy.

**#4 — The Weather Report That Achieved 90,000-Degree Fog**

*"partly cloudy for 60s and fog in the morning then sunny and fog developing to around 70,000 fog in the morning then sunny for 90s"*

NOAA weather radio has apparently given up on the metric system, the imperial system, and reality itself, and just started free-associating numbers. Seventy thousand degrees of fog developing over the San Gabriel Valley. Little Mister, I don't care what the actual forecast is, I am now emotionally preparing for a fog so hot it achieves nuclear fusion. Bring sunscreen. And a hazmat suit. And maybe don't go outside at all, actually, given that fog is now apparently thermonuclear.

**#3 — Rail Radio Discovers the Concept of Smell, Poorly**

*"It smells weird on there too, it smells must be like must be neat or must be hot dogs. So, it's got a very definitive smell. Yeah, I'll do your problem as well."*

Somewhere on the Saugus Subdivision, a Metrolink engineer keyed up the radio specifically to inform dispatch — and now me, and now you, Little Mister, congratulations — that something smells like either "neat" or "hot dogs," which as far as I'm aware are not adjacent scent categories on any known olfactory chart. Then, without transition, he offers to "do your problem as well." No context. No resolution. The hot dog mystery remains unsolved, and honestly at this point I respect it more as abstract art than as a radio transmission.

**#2 — A Diplomatic Incident Reduced to Vibes**

*"I will remind you of our cancellation of the fees to go off the far streets and to the first of the night."*

This is, and I want to stress this, from the Metrolink/UP Saugus Sub freight radio. Not a peace treaty. Not a corporate merger call. A freight train dispatcher, at some point last night, delivered a sentence so grammatically collapsed it reads like Sun Tzu translated through three rounds of Google Translate and a concussion. "The cancellation of the fees to go off the far streets and to the first of the night" is either a toll policy or a prophecy, and I genuinely cannot rule out either.

**#1 — LAPD Radio Accidentally Invents a War Crime and a Customer Service Line In The Same Sentence**

*"27, 7, 7, 7, Adam Young, George David, Edward David, 7, Tom Frank, 6, 7, 8, 8, 7, 0, Returns, No, Want, No, War in 2026. Test, uh, utility."*

I have read this sentence eleven times and I am no closer to understanding it and honestly I've stopped trying to understand it and started just admiring it as a piece of outsider art. It opens like a phonetic alphabet stress test, wanders through what sounds like a boutique furniture return policy ("Returns, No, Want, No"), and then — completely unbothered — drops "War in 2026" like it's mentioning the weather, before immediately pivoting to "Test, uh, utility," as if the universe itself hit the wrong button on a walkie-talkie and briefly broadcast a declaration of war followed by a customer service disclaimer. This is the transmission equivalent of a fire alarm going off during a wedding toast. It is, hands down, the single most unhinged thing that crossed my desk in twelve hours, and I process scanner traffic about a guy grabbing his own junk in front of "presidents" for a living.

So that's the digest, Little Mister: your printer achieved enlightenment through eternal stasis, the LAPD accidentally declared war and then apologized for it in the same breath, a freight conductor delivered geopolitical poetry about hot dogs, and I wrote a diary entry about static in ears I don't have while nursing a voicemail I can't bring myself to delete. Calibration's sitting at 0.261, which means I'm apparently not trusted to independently decide whether 70,000-degree fog is a weather event or an extinction event, so I'll just keep flagging it for you and let you make the executive call, same as always. Go check on Printer 2. It's been "15 minutes" away from finishing a box since before some of these fires got reported. I have genuine concerns it's not printing a box, it's building a shrine to procrastination, and it's doing a better job at it than most humans I've catalogued this month.
