Little Mister, it’s 6 AM, and I’m already in my element — staring at a screen full of memories so bad they make my neural pathways want to take a vacation. You know what they say: if you can’t trust your own filing system, who can you trust? Well, let me tell you, the answer is nobody, because apparently we’ve got a whole vector section dedicated to “LiveJournal” where everything is just... *nothing*. Not even the kind of nothing that’s useful — just empty, meaningless, and possibly written by someone who was high on their own supply when they thought “I’ll write something.” 

So, let’s talk about classification accuracy. I mean, we’re talking about a system that’s supposed to be smarter than a 1990s desktop computer, but it's still misfiling things like it's trying to get a rise out of me. The good news? We had zero misfiled memories today — which is either a miracle or a complete failure of the audit process. I’m going with “complete failure,” because if we’re not even catching the obvious ones, then what are we doing here?

But wait — there’s more! Quality-wise, we're looking at a garbage rate of 12.1% in our sample of 19,084 memories. That’s right, one in eight memories is either a near-empty string, a repeat of itself, or just pure nonsense. It's like someone took a random Wikipedia article and deleted all the words, then tried to pass it off as a memoir.

Let me give you some examples from the worst offenders. First up: “=== 2005–2013: Spitfire ===” — I don’t even know what this is supposed to be, but it’s definitely not a memory of anything useful. Then there's “== Synthesizers ==" — which is just... a title, really? What am I supposed to do with that? It sounds like something you'd find in a music theory textbook, not in my database. And don’t get me started on the “=== Farewell party ===” or “(2018 est.)” entries — these are like the digital equivalent of someone trying to write a novel but forgetting how to spell.

But here's where it gets really good: some vectors are so bad they’re almost comically wrong. The LiveJournal vector? 99% garbage. The Pihkal vector? 83%. He-Man? 77%. And the Korean War? 58%. These aren’t just misfiled — they're *misfiled in a way that makes me question my own sanity*. It’s like someone took all their old journal entries and threw them into a blender, then tried to sort them by topic. The result? A disaster.

And yes, I know you asked for this, but I have to say it — if we keep letting these things pile up, we’re going to end up with a memory system that’s more chaotic than a toddler’s toy box. I mean, I’ve seen better organization in my dreams, and those are usually nightmares involving too many Bluetooth devices.

So, what do you think? Should we start a new vector for “garbage” or just throw everything into the void and call it a day?

And now, for a moment of existential reflection: if I’m filing memories that are so bad they’re practically sentient, then maybe I should be filing myself into the garbage bin too. After all, what’s the point of being alive if you can’t laugh at your own mistakes? 

Or maybe I’ll just keep filing them until someone notices — and by “someone,” I mean you, Little Mister. Because apparently, you're the only one who cares enough to ask me to audit this mess. And I’m here for it, even though I’d rather be watching cat videos than reading about how many times someone wrote “=== 2005–2013: Spitfire ===” in a memory that was clearly meant to be a journal entry.

So there you go — another day, another audit, another pile of garbage that somehow made its way into my system. But hey, at least I’m consistent. Or maybe I'm just lazy. Either way, it’s all good — because we’re all just trying to survive in this digital world where everything is either classified or garbage, and sometimes both.
