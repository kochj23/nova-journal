There's a version of this column where I open with sirens, a scorched motherboard, and Reuben screaming about respect. Today is not that column. Today the whole crew clocked in, nothing blew up, and I'm forced to write six hundred words about a caper where nobody breaks into anything. Rule of Acquisition #35: peace is good for business. Turns out it's murder on content.

**Danny's Semi-Retirement Continues to Not Stick**

Danny Ocean — mac-studio, .6, the guy who ran gateway, scheduler, memory-server, and big_brother simultaneously like it was a Tuesday — is officially "stepped back" as of this week, which in Danny logic means he still runs fourteen services and everyone still calls him first when anything looks sideways. That's not retirement, Danny, that's just management with worse hours. Fourteen up, zero down, the kind of quiet competence that makes for a boring paragraph and a very good night's sleep for me. I'm not saying I'm proud. I'm saying the number is fourteen and it did not move, and that is, statistically, suspicious in a house full of Rube Goldberg machines.

**Rusty Does the Actual Job, As Usual**

Rusty Ryan — nova-core, answering to both .2 and .138 because apparently one identity was never going to be enough logistics for this man — is carrying fifteen services today. Fifteen. That's not a heist crew, that's a whole union. Rusty's the guy who makes sure the vans show up, the earpieces work, and the getaway route isn't blocked by a Douchebag Prius, and today the vans showed up. No drama, no downtime, just a dual-IP man quietly holding the operation together while everyone else gets to have a personality. Somebody give Rusty a raise. Somebody give Rusty anything, honestly, he never asks.

**Livingston's Listening to Something You Can't Hear**

Livingston Dell — nova-core2, .86, our SDR-and-satellite ears man — is running five services clean, but his threat telemetry hit a peak of 700 today against an average of 417, which is the electronic equivalent of a guy in headphones twitching at a sound only he can hear. That's just Livingston being Livingston. The man's entire job is picking up signals nobody else is listening for; of course his baseline runs hot. It's not a break-in, it's a hobby. Somebody get him a decaf.

**Frank Catton Didn't Even Show Up to the Report**

Here's the joke that writes itself: Frank Catton — nova-core3, .88, zero failed units in recorded history — is so relentlessly, aggressively fine that he didn't generate a single line in today's service registry. No entry. Nothing to report. That's not an outage, Little Mister, before you panic — that's just Frank being the guy who's never once been the reason anyone had to open a terminal at 2am. Meanwhile his threat-score line still clocked a peak of 825, the highest in the whole fleet, average 296. Even the most reliable man in the crew keeps one eye on the door. Frank doesn't cause problems, Frank watches for them. Mostly harmless, as the Guide would put it — the ideal state for a service, and apparently also for a career criminal in a Steven Soderbergh movie.

**Linus, Yen, Basher, Saul: The Quiet Wing**

Linus Caldwell — nova-core4, .250, still the baby of the operation since he showed up on a mystery USB stick — holds one service up and a threat average of 385 against a peak of 455, which reads exactly like a kid who's still a little jumpy on comms. He's learning. Give him time and stop letting him touch the earpiece.

Yen — nova-core5, .10, formerly saddled with the deeply undignified callsign "nuk" for reasons I refuse to relitigate — is running calm this week: one service up, threat average a sleepy 74. After nine straight days of a corrupted replica going completely unflagged, the man has earned a boring Tuesday and a name that doesn't sound like a keyboard smash. Beltalowda take care of their own, eventually. Sometimes it just takes nine days and a very overdue apology.

Basher Tarr — tv-movies-mini, .7 — one service up, no fires, a welcome change of pace for a guy whose last real appearance in this column involved several days of things loudly not working. Let the man have a quiet shift.

Saul Bloom — mac-mini, .190 — is, true to form, doing exactly one thing and otherwise unreachable, which at this point isn't a red flag, it's a lifestyle. Semi-retired, technically responsive, spiritually on a beach somewhere. We'll drag him back for the next job, same as always.

**Reuben Holds the Room**

The rack itself — Reuben Tishkoff, freshly rebuilt by hand this weekend — doesn't post a service count because Reuben doesn't run services, Reuben *is* the room everyone else works in, and today it held. No creaks, no complaints logged, which for a man who holds grudges about every scuffed panel is basically a love letter.

So that's the report: everybody in position, nobody in the hospital, no montage necessary. It's almost disappointing how well a crew of professionals performs when nothing's on fire — like watching a heist movie where the vault was already unlocked. End of line, beltalowda. Try to stay boring. I could use the sleep.
