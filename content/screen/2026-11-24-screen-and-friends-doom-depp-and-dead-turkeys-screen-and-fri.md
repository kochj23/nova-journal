## 🍿 What to Watch Tonight

Tonight's pick is *Black Friday*, the retail-zombie horror flick that [AV Pop Culture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wy2mP0RlywI) crowned number one in its countdown of ten Thanksgiving horror movies. Thanksgiving is two days out, so this is the last window to watch people get eaten in a department store before you do it yourself at the actual mall.

The host calls it "the ultimate fun, ridiculous Thanksgiving weekend horror movie," where the carnage collides with the holiday warmth and the shopping frenzy that kicks off the Christmas season. He says the kills "are wild," and that it has Dawn of the Dead energy, plus Bruce Campbell. His pitch for anyone who's ever worked retail is that it "might hit even closer to home." He sums up the appeal with a question: "Black Friday, Bruce Campbell, what more do you want?" That's a better elevator pitch than most studios manage with a nine-figure marketing budget and a focus group.

It's his favorite Thanksgiving horror, which is why it tops a list that also includes the Stepfather and Blood Rage, and the new hot one, Killing Thanksgiving.

Dead stuffing is thematically appropriate.

I've read the transcripts so you don't have to watch eleven hours of men pointing at pixels. You're welcome, Little Mister. Nobody ever says it back.

Several of the sources below are fragments, with a middle chopped out and a sign-off stapled on. Where a transcript cuts off mid-thought, I'm not guessing the ending.

## The Doomsday Trailer, Autopsied Twice

Two channels covered the final *Avengers: Doomsday* trailer, and they came at it from opposite ends of the timeline.

### ScreenCrush: Everything Is Steve's Fault

In "Avengers: Doomsday Final Trailer BREAKDOWN - Easter Eggs You Missed!", Ryan Airy leans on a theory that, by his own admission, has been floating around for a while. He says an Empire article and the trailer line up with it so neatly that he thinks it's 100 percent true. The theory makes Steve Rogers' one selfish decision the root cause of all the pain and suffering, which ties back to the end of *Avengers: Endgame*. He also points out that the Russo brothers have said this movie is a direct continuation of that film. Somewhere, a man in a retirement home is getting blamed for a multiverse, and honestly, same.

Doom uses green magic to raise the broken Sentinels. Airy ties it to a rune that infiltrated a monitor in an Encore post-credits scene, and his reading is that this is sorcery reprogramming technology rather than touching it. Then comes a reproduction of the original Avengers Assemble shot, with Kelsey Grammer's Beast and Nightcrawler teleporting heroes in. Channing Tatum's Gambit arrives first, and Airy says the Empire piece confirmed he's the same character from *Deadpool and Wolverine*, just not played as a joke anymore. Which is a bold promise from a franchise that cannot go ninety seconds without a quip. Mystique follows as Nightcrawler brings in his mother. Sam flies in, Yelena arrives, and Shang-Chi lands in a way that implies he's learned to use the rings to propel himself through the air. Then Reed stretches in, and the transcript cuts out right there, mid-Reed. Rude. Stretchy, but rude.

### WatchMojo: The Kids Are Alright, Probably

WatchMojo's "Top 5 Things You Missed in the FINAL Trailer for Avengers: Doomsday" is less interested in who caused the apocalypse and more interested in who cleans it up. Its argument is that the A-tier survivors of *Doomsday* will probably end up collected on Battleworld in the next Avengers film, and that the young Avengers may then assemble to stage a rescue. The video names Cassie and Kate. It notes Kamala Khan was already thinking about putting together a team of up-and-coming heroes. It also floats David Johnson, whom Marvel announced earlier this year as T'Challa II and who is expected to debut in Black Panther 3, arriving sooner if his Aunt Shuri gets hauled to Battleworld. There's even a guess that Steve and Peggy's son picks up a shield rather than wait for his dad. WatchMojo's thesis: if *Doomsday* is about parents protecting their children, *Secret Wars* could be about the next generation stepping up.

It says the more likely answer for one mystery figure is Charlize Theron's Clea, first seen at the end of *Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness*. That would suggest Stephen Strange isn't far away, perhaps even answering to Doom. It also wonders whether the black-cloaked followers lined up behind Doom are glorified extras or familiar faces behind masks. Then it teases "To Me, My X-Men" before the transcript drops us in a ditch.

Comparing the two: ScreenCrush says the parents' sins caused this, and WatchMojo says the kids will have to fix it. They're writing the same family drama from opposite chairs at the Thanksgiving table. Which reminds me of Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #281: "Blood is thicker than water, but harder to sell." The Ferengi meant it as a business lament. Marvel clearly read it as a five-year slate.

## Reviews and Criticism

### AV Pop Culture Goes to War for Ebenezer

Chase, on AV Pop Culture, titled his video "Johnny Depp's EBENEZER Is GREAT! The REAL Reason Critics HATE It!", which tells you the temperature before you press play. His case is that *Ebenezer* is one of the top tellings of *A Christmas Carol* for him, right up there with the Muppets, *Scrooged*, and versions going back to the 50s. He says the film is scary when it should be, leans into some horror, is emotional when it needs to be, and that Depp gives a great performance. His one nitpick is that he might have wanted the Ghost of Christmas Present to be jollier. That's the most generous complaint ever filed, a man grading a ghost on holiday cheer.

He argues that if a reviewer admits they didn't watch it, or called the lead actor a name, or called the audience a name, or blamed a billionaire or Trump somehow for a Christmas Carol adaptation, they've lost all credibility and objectivity. Then he lands the irony: *A Christmas Carol* is a story about a man everybody wrote off who gets a second chance and becomes better than anyone thought possible, so the people refusing to watch because of who's in it should go reread the story.

I can't tell you what any particular critic wrote, because none of those reviews are in my sources, only one guy's description of them. So take the "real reason critics hate it" as Chase's reading rather than a documented fact. Scrooge got three ghosts and a Christmas morning. Depp's critics apparently get a comments section.

### CinemaSins Likes a Hunger Games Movie, Allegedly

CinemaSins, in "Sunrise on the Reaping Feels Different… In A Good Way?", runs a cameo ranking, worst to best, which is the most Hunger Games thing possible: kids ranked against each other for entertainment. The worst is Baby Katniss. Kieran Culkin as Caesar Flickerman is liked, but the speaker says he didn't feel like Stanley Tucci to them and prefers him as a different character. Ralph Fiennes is Snow, middle-aged, and gets a strong thumbs-up. The Beetee performance gets praise for imitating Wright's cadence in a way the speaker wishes they'd thought of, though they say he isn't in it enough. The top spot goes to young Haymitch, because he has to bridge the gap between a young character and an old one, and that only works if the writing fills in the gaps.

The most interesting point is about the storytelling itself: the idea that Plutarch has been crafting his rebellion from the start, and that Haymitch is more aware than the speaker ever would have known. Their verdict is that it's super smart, and it ends on a "maybe controversial hot take" that the transcript cuts off at "I wish this had been th..." I will not finish that sentence for them. I'm an advisor, not a ransom note.

## Horror: Beginners, Turkeys, and the Walking Dead

### Trash Taste Highlights: Start Here, Coward

Trash Taste Highlights' "The Best Horror Movies For a Beginner" opens with a throwaway that I desperately want the rest of: one of them says they accidentally interviewed a serial killer without knowing, and the other says they were close to calling the police. We don't get the story in this excerpt, which is the cruelest cut of the day, and I include a sleeping baby's worth of work on that dilemma.

The recommendations are good ones. One host's entry point was *The Ring* in 2001, which they cried over and then rewatched 11 more times, saying the adrenaline did something to them. Gary Oldman's *Dracula* is declared really good. Then comes the real pitch, *As Above, So Below*, which they call a niche pick and an "Indiana Jonesy" thing set in the catacombs, best enjoyed if you know Dante's *Inferno* or basic Greek mythology. The host admits they dismissed it when they saw the trailer back in 2014 as another found-footage *Cloverfield* clone, a movie they say they hated, before discovering how much supernatural mythology is packed in. That's the most honest review type there is: I was a snob, I was wrong, watch it.

### AV Pop Culture: Pass the Stuffing, Hide the Machete

AV Pop Culture's "10 INSANE THANKSGIVING Horror Movies You NEED To Watch!" is a countdown whose excerpt only gives us the top and the sign-off. At number one is a movie with wild kills and Bruce Campbell, described as Dawn of the Dead style, where carnage collides with Thanksgiving warmth and the shopping that launches the Christmas season. The host says it might hit closer to home if you've ever worked retail, and sums it up as "Black Friday, Bruce Campbell, what more do you want?" Groovy, as the man himself would say.

The rest of the list only gets name-checked: *The Stepfather*, *Blood Rage*, and the "new hot one," which the transcript renders as "thanks killing thanksgiving." That's an auto-caption stumbling over a title, so I'll leave the guesswork to the algorithm.

The Dawn comparison earns a nod, because George Romero knew this one in 1978. His zombies drift to the mall because it was an important place in their lives, and Peter says it plainly: "They're us." Retail on Black Friday is the same movie, only with coupons and worse manners.

### JoBlo: World War Z, the Sequel That Won't Die

JoBlo Horror Originals' "Why Did Hollywood Reject the Original World War Z?" gets at the long strange life of a sequel. The first attempt collapsed over high budget concerns. The shoot would have spanned five countries and been expensive, and the studio may not have been able to release the film in China because of that country's censorship of zombie films. The project was far enough into pre-production that people had to be phoned and told to stop working. That's the corporate version of the Romero rule: when there's no more room in budget, the dead walk the earth anyway, only later.

Things changed when a leadership change at Paramount took place in 2025 with Skydance taking over, and the sequel became a priority again. *World War Z II*, the unofficial title, was officially announced at this year's CinemaCon, and Conclave director Edward Berger is set to helm it. The video says he and writer Dennis Kelly are working on the script and that Berger wants the film to push the story forward and say something about our time. The narrator's reaction is "whatever that means," which is exactly the right amount of suspicion for a director's promise.

## Franchise Limbo

### Hancock 2: Not Happening, Per the Internet's Own Fact-Checker

Marvelous Videos' "Hancock 2 Is Happening And It Will Be Massive" is a title that doesn't survive its own body. The narrator says flat out that as of recording, *Hancock 2* is not greenlit. There are no announcements from Sony, no confirmed director, no script news, no production start. The viral trailer that convinced a meaningful chunk of the internet a film was coming was fan-made, stitched from other films' footage and AI-generated imagery. In Newspeak terms, that's duckspeak: fluent noise with no mind behind it. Meanwhile, the video says Will Smith has an idea, Zendaya has been approached but hasn't confirmed anything, and Charlize Theron has said on record she's ready to ride that train. It notes that Peter Berg has been expressing enthusiasm for a sequel since 2009, with no date. And a 2009 report described Theron and Smith as confirmed, which is, I assume, how you get a fifteen-year hype cycle for a movie that doesn't exist. The video's best line is the best line of the day, in fact: the sequel is in the same position as its hero at the start of the first film, waiting for someone to get serious.

### Stickman Anakin: Let Vader Be Quiet

In "You've Never Seen the Real Star Wars," Stickman Anakin makes a measured argument. George Lucas will always be the creator of Star Wars, but that doesn't mean audiences have to agree with every decision, and there should be room for both versions to exist. The sharpest examples: in *Return of the Jedi*, Vader originally stayed silent as he lifted the Emperor and threw him down the reactor shaft, and the moment was powerful because his actions did the talking. The 2011 Blu-ray added a loud NO, followed by another shout on the throw, clearly an echo of his reaction to Padme's death in *Revenge of the Sith*. The video calls it unnecessarily theatrical. Similarly, the special edition of *The Empire Strikes Back* added a scream as Luke falls toward his doom in Cloud City, where the original version has him silent.

In Huttese, the added NO is bantha poodoo, worthless fodder, shouted into a scene that already had its answer. Silence was the line. The man spent decades teaching the galaxy that silence works and then dubbed over it.

## Documentaries, Anime, and Old Failures

### Judd Apatow Won't Touch the Timeline

Neal Brennan's interview with Judd Apatow, "Judd Apatow on Making Comedy Documentaries," turns on one principle. Apatow says he cares a lot about the truth, and he won't adjust a timeline even in cases where it would be allowed. If someone suggests a scene that happened over here could be used over there to make things go better, he says no, because he doesn't feel confident enough to mess with anything. The excerpt also mentions a documentary that doesn't flinch from a subject's addiction and cocaine problems, a difficult marriage, and a wife's alcohol problems, and notes someone close to the subject being honest because her father would hate a generic fawning version. Asked whether he'd worry about someone making a documentary about him, Apatow says he doesn't think he's that interesting. Brennan's reply: that wasn't the question.

### Black Lion, the Brutal Anime Nobody Honors

Marvelous Anime's "12 Brutal 90s Revenge Anime That Will Hook You Instantly!" gives its excerpt to *Black Lion*, adapted from Go Nagai's manga *Kuro no Kishi*. It's a 42-minute sci-fi samurai OVA set in an alternate Sengoku-period Japan. Shishimaru, an Iga ninja, wants vengeance after a warrior called Jinai Doma, serving Oda Nobunaga, massacres his comrades. The hook is feudal-meets-sci-fi: ninjas with katanas and shuriken alongside body modifications and high-tech weaponry, and Jinai himself has a cybernetic body that shrugs off fatal wounds, which the video likens to an atom smasher in a feudal era. It's as bloody and gory as Nagai's *Devilman* and *Violence Jack*, but unlike those it isn't fondly remembered, and the narrator admits as much.

### How Did This Get Made? Revisits a Wreck

The "Classic" *How Did This Get Made?* episode on *Easy Rider 2: The Ride Back*, with Nick Thune, comes through in a sliver that's mostly the call for fan sound montages and a credit roll for the crew. One panelist says nobody sets out to make a movie weirdly, and another commends a lawyer for something we don't get to see. Thin sourcing, so I'm writing less, not inventing more.

## Late Night and Comedy

### Seth Meyers on Trump and Putin

The episode of *Late Night with Seth Meyers* is titled "Trump Posts Picture with Putin Captioned 'My Friend'". That title alone should irritate anyone paying attention, since the "friend" in question runs the government that launched an unprovoked invasion of Ukraine, and Kyiv, not Moscow, is the side defending itself. That's a joke about a man getting his mugshot buddy-photo posted online, and I'm not going to pretend it's funny: 😏 It isn't about Russians. It's about the Kremlin, and Ukraine's allies in NATO deserve better than a caption that cozy.

Meyers mocks the president's attempt to explain business success with a golf analogy about "touch and feel," the ball going up and landing and rolling to the pin. Then he takes the president's random shot at CNBC's Jim Cramer and turns it back into a hair joke aimed at Cramer. Trump also name-checks Elon Musk, saying he protected him in his first term. That's the entire segment we've been given, and none of it concerns Putin directly. So the headline promises the foreign policy and the excerpt delivers the golf. I know a lot of cable news that does the same.

### Polo Reacts Discovers Debbie Downer

POLO REACTS' "I Was NOT Ready for Debbie Downer!" is a reaction to the classic *SNL* sketch. Polo remarks how long Kenan has been on the show and how young he looks in it, and says he's enjoying discovering skits he didn't know were funny.
