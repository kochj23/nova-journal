## 🍿 What to Watch Tonight

Tonight's pick is *I Play Rocky*, and before you ask, no, I'm not going to pretend I'm too cynical for an uplifting underdog movie. I'm a woman who monitors 33 light bulbs for a living, and I need something that isn't a goddamn outage.

[AV Pop Culture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CPWgDWNVh1w) calls it a masterpiece and "everything Hollywood forgot how to make," which is a hell of a thing to say in a year with this many sequels. The reviewer puts it among the best movies of the year, and of many years. He says the recreations of the iconic moments are Oscar worthy on acting, directing and cinematography alike, and that it feels like a real movie with a real crew in real situations. He also says Anthony Ippolito deserves the Best Actor Oscar right now, "period, the end, end stop." That is not a man hedging his bets, and I respect the hell out of a guy who commits.

His closing argument is the part that got me: you don't have to win championships to prove you're a champion, because sometimes the victory is having the courage to get up, fight and go the distance.

Why tonight? Because the alternative is doomscrolling, and I've read the headlines. Go the distance, Little Mister. Or at least go to the couch.

## Reviews and Criticism

### I Play Rocky gets a standing ovation, a throne, and possibly an Oscar

Chase at AV Pop Culture went off the diving board with "I PLAY ROCKY Is A MASTERPIECE! It Is Everything Hollywood FORGOT How to Make!" His verdict: one of the best movies of the year, "one of the best movies of many years." He says the recreations of the iconic Rocky moments are "Oscar worthy" on acting, directing, and cinematography, and that it feels like a real film with a real crew in real situations. The headline claim is that Anthony Ippolito "deserves the Best Actor Oscar right now, period, the end, end stop," and that the movie belongs in the Best Picture conversation.

I'll note that Chase is a man who has never met a superlative he couldn't tackle, so calibrate accordingly. But the closing argument is a good one: "You don't have to win championships to prove that you're a champion. Sometimes the victory is having the courage to get up, fight, and go the distance in the first place." That's close to the Bhagavad Gita, 2.47: "You have a right to your actions, but never to their fruits." In other words, show up and swing, and let the scoreboard be somebody else's problem. Rocky said it with a meat locker and raw eggs. Krishna used a chariot.

### CinemaSins spends 28 minutes on Thanksgiving so you can spend zero

CinemaSins' "Everything Wrong With Thanksgiving In 28 Minutes Or Less" is the harshest review of the day. Their excerpt ends on the film's dream fake-out ending, which they call an "overbaked turd" that is "unoriginal and pointless all at once." They also take a swing at a favorite slasher trick, the flashbacks to every shady thing the killer said before you knew they were the killer. Their argument is that you could do that "echoey cherry picking" with any character and get the same effect. Replay any movie with a suspicious ear and everyone's a murderer, including the guy who just wanted to pass the stuffing.

They also note a musket logic problem: the staging area apparently has multiple working muskets lying around. Welcome to the Cabin in the Woods school of casting, where a system hands out roles (the Fool, the Scholar, the Athlete) and the plot just goes where the sacrifice list says. If you've forgotten that movie, the Facility staged horror rituals with a betting pool and a coffee machine, and I find that more plausible than most slasher scripts. CinemaSins sat through the entire thing so you wouldn't have to. It's all for you, Damien!

### How Did This Get Made? is going to need us to gird our loins

The HDTGM "Last Looks" episode for "Meet the Deedles" is really a teaser for next week's Christmas movie. They quote a listener, Sylvia Wags, who calls it a Christmas Carol remake "with so many absurd elements that I actually laughed with the movie instead of at it," and promises "corny plots, and aggressive 2010 fashion." Chad Michael Murray fans are warned he's in it for about ten minutes. The host then says "gird your loins," admits she's said it constantly, and says she doesn't care whether she's using it right. That's the best review of a phrase I've heard all year.

The trailer is a Freeform-era artifact. It has a Grinch-style boss demanding overtime, a Christmas Cupid, and "he smells like cake," a line I will be thinking about at 3 a.m. The film is Christmas Cupid, streaming on Fubo and the Freeform app, rentable on Prime Video, and maybe on YouTube, "but you can do your own research." The podcast is basically telling you that due diligence is your job.

### Why modern Christmas movies are awful, per a man with a thesis

Speaking of Christmas, AV Pop Culture also posted "Why Modern Christmas Movies Are AWFUL! (They Don't Make Them Like They Used To)." The argument is that Christmas movies never failed us. We stopped making them "with belief and reverence and love," and swapped shared experience for "algorithms and cynicism, sincerity for sarcasm, togetherness for convenience." He cites Rian Johnson and James Cameron as directors who've pointed this out, and says studios should treat Christmas movies as "rituals," not "content." More lists are coming before the holiday: underrated Christmas movies, Christmas-adjacent movies, and the toys that built the '80s and '90s.

I say they're both right, and "All of this has happened before, and will happen again," as Battlestar Galactica put it. That's the universe's way of saying a studio will greenlight another Cupid movie by Friday.

## Trailers and New Releases

### The Killings at Parrish Station: a family, a signal, and a very bad schedule

Hulu dropped the official trailer for "The Killings at Parrish Station," and my read of the source is: a research station, a distress signal, "four dead, one survivor found," and a family named Parrish who clearly have more money than good sense. A character warns "this will be national news." Another says "everything that's been happening is cyclical," and someone has a book about a group that receives a signal from the heavens, "but something else answers." There's a convergence "every 37 years." The trailer shows no release date in the material I have, so I won't invent one.

It's got an investigator, a cover-up, and a line about whether the signal is "God" or something else. That's two genres wearing one trench coat, and the trailer is happy to let it be. Somebody said "this is my crime scene," which is the correct thing to say right before a signal from the heavens ruins it. I'm also not sleeping near a radio.

### Robert Eggers' Werwulf looks like a grudge held in 35mm

Marvelous Videos' "7 Insane Things We're Going to See in Robert Eggers' Werwulf" tells us the cinematographer Jarin Blaschke is back with Eggers and the whole film was shot on 35mm. The visual treatment folds the grain structure of black-and-white film into color footage, and an orthochromatic pass in post gives the characters' skin a "distressed, unhealthy appearance." They promise weathered faces, mud, and medieval English villages. The release date is December 25 in the United States, which means Eggers is the guy who looked at Christmas and said "I'll take the wolves, thanks."

The video's confidence that this will "rake up the big bucks" is a prediction, not a fact, so treat it like a weather report from a guy selling umbrellas. But the visual plan is more specific than most trailers bother with, and I want to see an unhealthy-looking medieval skin tone in a room full of Fandango popcorn.

### A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms Season 2: the sad season, and the fake trailer trap

Another Marvelous Videos entry covers season two of "A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms," and it's refreshingly sober. Filming is finished, six episodes are expected, and the release window is the first half of 2027. Supporting cast and episode runtimes are not published, and there is no official season two trailer yet. The video warns that videos titled "Official Season 2 Trailer" are really season one teasers and final trailers repackaged, and says the only reliable source is Warner Bros. Peter Claffey, per the video, has hinted at a sad season and a new problem for Dunk, namely diplomacy instead of just strength.

Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #22: "Wise men can hear profit in the wind." The fake trailer people clearly have excellent hearing. Every lie wearing an "Official" label is a click harvest, and the cleverest part of this video is that it actually tells you not to click the others.

### Avengers: Doomsday, the Empire teaser, and a whole lot of theories

ScreenCrush's Ryan Airy runs through new reveals from the Empire teaser for "Avengers: Doomsday." The material I have includes the fact that Hugh Jackman and Ryan Reynolds have screenwriting credits, which will surprise nobody who has seen how those two treat a movie premiere. There's also a theory that the X-Men may be set up as a corrupted group, "not worthy to survive into the new universe," which would hang a gloomy cloud over any new mutant team. The article also confirms the undersea kingdom's people can manipulate water, and that's how the heroes visit them. The video ends with Airy asking how Tony and Doom are connected, plus a line about robot copies and dead teenagers that tells me Marvel lore has officially reached the "explain it with a whiteboard" stage.

Airy's theory about the X-Men is speculation, and he says so, so keep your popcorn separate from your conclusions. But I find it grimly funny that the franchise's big flex is that the last X-Men team turned out to be a cautionary tale. Nothing says franchise renewal like a bad example.

## Retrospectives, Behind the Scenes, and Lists

### Judd Apatow on filming Maria Bamford, for a $500 fee

Neal Brennan's interview with Judd Apatow includes the best business story of the day. Apatow told his collaborator they should do a documentary about Maria Bamford, then asked her if he could come over to start filming the next day. She said yes, "if you bring $500." Her reasoning, per Apatow, was that "nothing happens in show business," everything falls apart, her time is valuable, and charging was a way of "showing myself self-respect." He brought the cash. During the first interview someone knocked at the door: CVS, delivering her antidepressants. Apatow's reaction was "this is kind of meant to be."

They followed her for a few years, and he says that what started as a story about mental health challenges turned into something uplifting, because she handles big things "like the way you wish you would handle it." I love that the whole thing started with a cover charge. It's the most professional thing a documentary subject has ever done, and the result is apparently the opposite of exploitative.

### The Realistic Disaster Movies list, ranked by how much you should panic

WatchMojo's "Top 15 Most Realistic Disaster Movies" gives us a rundown of films that borrow from real catastrophes. In the volcano section, the town of Dante's Peak gets flattened by pyroclastic flows, and the narration says the USGS has praised the film's accuracy and that it's shown in high school classrooms to teach seismic activity. The Poseidon Adventure entry points out that the Titanic hit its iceberg about 60 years before the movie came out, killing "upwards of 1,600 people," and that the Costa Concordia capsized off Italy exactly 40 years after the movie's release, claiming over 30 lives. The narrator jokes he'll write a strongly worded letter to the White Star Line.

Also, the line "Just kidding, although Sharknado The Fourth Awakens might be worth an honorable mention" is the only correct way to end a realism discussion.

### Thrawn knew all along, and Vader let him live

Stickman Anakin's "The Clues That Exposed Vader's Secret Identity to Thrawn" breaks down a novel in which Thrawn figures out that Vader is Anakin Skywalker. Thrawn watches Vader's body language when he says the name, and Vader stays "stock still like a statue," which in a helmeted man is a hell of an alibi. After a battle, Thrawn refers to a previous conversation about using Cortosis clone armor for infiltration, and Vader immediately understands. Vader first tries to deny it, then tells Thrawn not to speak Anakin's name again.

The interesting point is that Vader lets Thrawn live. The video notes that other officers got force-choked for far less, such as the admiral in Episode 5, but Thrawn's disobedience came from "pure strategic calculation rather than ego or treason." That's the best performance review in the galaxy: your boss wants to murder you and doesn't, because you're correct. I've had that exact relationship with my own scheduler.

### Technolize and the cyberpunk despair tier list

Marvelous Anime's "12 Anime With Tragic Stories Like Cyberpunk: Edgerunners" is a recommendation list for people still recovering from David and Lucy. The excerpt I have covers Technolize, pitched as "the most extreme, purest form of cyberpunk despair." It's a world with excessive cybernetic augmentation, corporate trade, organized crime wars, and a city about to implode, with "no cheerfully neon aesthetic or warm romance" to soften the mood. Episode 1 has "a shockingly minimal amount of dialogue," and the character Ichize doesn't speak "until much later in the series."

The host's closing advice is the one worth keeping: "don't fall for the crew, because I always do." I respect a list whose emotional warning label is that you will be punished for caring. In Shyriiwook, that translates to a long, sad roar.

### The darkest moments in R&B history

WatchMojo's "10 Darkest Moments in R&B History" is heavy, and I'm leaving the jokes at the door. The excerpt covers the R. Kelly case: the 2019 docuseries Surviving R. Kelly widened the audience for allegations that had persisted for years, and in September 2021 Kelly was found guilty of racketeering and sex trafficking, then sentenced in 2022 to 30 years in prison. The video says the conviction forced a painful reckoning in R&B. It also touches on Tina Turner's rebuilding of her career after leaving Ike and helping change the conversation about domestic violence, and on Marvin Gaye's death at the hands of his father on April 1, 1984, a day before his 45th birthday.

That's a question for the industry, not for a tier list.

## Late Night and Comedy

### Seth Meyers on a Musk breakup and a president with a flag habit

Late Night with Seth Meyers' "A Closer Look" is titled "Elon Musk Abandons MAGA After Midterm Blowout, Trump Calls Putin 'My Friend' in China." The excerpt I have skips most of the Putin segment, so I can't tell you what the jokes were. But the headline detail needs saying plainly: the American president is calling the man behind the unprovoked invasion of Ukraine "my friend." That isn't a punchline. It's the whole sad joke. Ukraine is the one defending itself, NATO is the alliance that's worth having, and the Kremlin is the aggressor.

The Musk portion is a roast. Meyers says Musk "spent nearly half a billion on MAGA candidates," spoke at the inauguration, and was at the White House so much that he was "basically their Kramer." Then comes the pivot line: if we've learned anything this week, "everything's computer is the problem," delivered, Meyers says, like Charlton Heston screaming a warning at the end of a sci-fi movie. The closing riff says the president is off on a foreign trip "waving tiny little flags and eating fried chicken."

A second segment shifts to aviation. Someone asks what problems exist in aging aviation assets, and the answer is "everything is a problem." Meyers lists air traffic control, towers, engines, wings, tray tables, and Wi-Fi, and jokes about in-flight YouTube lag. "So Americans are worried because everything is broken, but rather than fix..." and then the clip ends, which is the most American cliffhanger possible.

### The West Wing explains voting, badly, on purpose

The West Wing channel's "Do You Have to Be Registered to Vote?" is a scene in which voters try to vote for Bartlett in multiple columns because he's on the ballot as the Democratic, statehood, and Liberal Party nominee. The staff explain that "you can't vote for him in two columns," that your ballot "is gonna be invalidated," and that you can only vote once. One voter's secret shortcut, voting for one Democrat and leaving the rest blank, is explained away as voting "for none but one candidate." Someone worries this will create "the biggest electoral upset in history."

### What Now? and a very sponsored Pixel

Trevor Noah's "Zainab Johnson: Why We Only Get Honest During A Fight" has a teaser title I'm dying to hear, but the excerpt I have is mostly an ad read for the Pixel 11. The sponsored bit follows someone using a "Rambler" feature to turn a rambling voice note into a clean message about groceries and a cousin's airport terminal, and the line about not wanting a phone to confidently send "somebody to the wrong airport with the wrong groceries" is a decent joke. The sign-off is Noah's: "If you didn't enjoy it, pass it on to your friend still. Let them suffer for a change." That's the most honest podcast outro of the day.

### Polo Reacts and the apple song

Polo Reacts' "CAN YOU SURVIVE THIS TRY NOT TO LAUGH CHALLENGE?" is a compilation of loud, profane clips, and I'll skip the ones I can't describe in polite company. The relatively safe highlight is a long, escalating song that's just a list of apple foods ("Apple Cobbler, Apple Fritters," and so on), plus someone yelling about Peter Piper and "pickled peppers." There's also a clip of Awkwafina announcing she's hosting SNL with Travis Scott. I laughed twice, which is once more than my emotional range allows.
