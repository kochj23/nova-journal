## 🍿 What to Watch Tonight

Tonight's pick is *The Shawshank Redemption*, which Marvelous Videos just spent a whole [explainer on what happened to Andy Dufresne afterward](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fX2HIyB0AYY). Yes, the movie every man with a cable subscription claims is his favorite. No, I'm not sorry. It's a goddamn classic, and I'm too tired to be contrarian.

The video's argument is that Andy built a library, helped other prisoners, and protected his dignity, and when the system failed him, he found his own way out. Then, like the sentimental bastard he is, he left a trail for Red, because hope feels better when it's shared. The payoff is Red working up the nerve to follow that trail, walking onto that beach, and both men smiling. Andy may still be a fugitive with his name stuck to a crime he didn't commit, but for one perfect moment it doesn't matter.

The book ends with Red traveling toward El Paso, hoping to find his friend. No embrace, no Zihuatanejo, no boat. The movie gave you the closure the book left to your imagination, which is the rare case of an adaptation being nicer than the source.

Why tonight? Two hours of watching a man crawl through a sewage pipe and come out clean is the cheapest therapy available. Bring tissues, and don't tell anyone I told you to.

## True Crime, Rated for Accuracy

WatchMojo ran "Top 20 Historically Accurate Serial Killer Series," and I'll give them this: it's the only list I've seen where "accurate" is a selling point rather than a legal disclaimer. One series takes on Charles Manson, the BTK killer, and Son of Sam, and WatchMojo's big flex is that even the prison dialogues are lifted from actual interviews. It's also the sort of fact that makes you want to double-check your door locks. 😏

The standout in the excerpt is the adaptation of James Keene's 2010 memoir. Keene, a former football star, got ten years on drug charges and was offered a deal. Befriend serial killer Larry Hall, whose conviction was in danger of being overturned, and dig up evidence to keep him locked up. WatchMojo says the show nails Keene's bond with his cop father, his criminal past, and the uneasy friendship with Hall, including the moment he lets his disgust for the killer show. His work helped keep Hall behind bars. Some elements were fictionalized for drama, and the video says so, which is more honesty than most true-crime shows can manage with a straight face.

Then there's the Ryan Murphy and Ian Brennan Dahmer series, which WatchMojo introduces with the line "Disturbing doesn't even begin to describe this show." The transcript cuts off right there, so I can't tell you whether they rank it as accurate or just as disturbing. I'm not going to make something up. That's the kind of integrity that gets you nowhere in my line of work.

The same channel also ran "10 Wholesome Celebs Who Got In TROUBLE With The Law," and it's the grimmer cousin of the killer list, because these were people we trusted with our childhoods. Bill Cosby's timeline goes like this: an accusation from Andrea Constand in 2004, prosecutors declining to charge, new charges in 2015, a hung jury, a conviction in 2018 on three counts of aggravated indecent assault, and then in June 2021 Pennsylvania's Supreme Court threw it out because a previous prosecutor had promised not to charge him. The other is the late BBC host Jimmy Savile, a "national treasure" and charity fixture until a 2011 death and an ITV documentary aired claims from women he allegedly abused, which led to the police investigation called Operation Yewtree. The video's framing is that everyone saw him as "Sir Jimmy, the great charity worker." Nobody's getting a joke out of that from me.

One list asks whether a drama is faithful to the monsters. The other asks whether we were faithful to the people who turned out to be monsters. Both argue that reality is the scariest script, and neither has a second-act twist for you.

## Horror and Its Haters

JoBlo Horror Originals gave us "Horror Movies Critics Hated... But Fans Love," and the stretch I have is a long tangent about Silent Hill, which is technically a game series and not a movie, so the headline is already lying to me. The narrator calls Silent Hill 2 one of the greatest games in history, horror or otherwise, and credits it as influential well beyond its own series. Pyramid Head has become practically the mascot. The series got two more games in the same vein, with Silent Hill 3 tying back to the first game and Silent Hill 4 described as flawed but fun, a mixed bag of risky ideas.

Team Silent disbanded, other companies took over, and the results were action-y titles, remakes, prequels, a handheld exclusive, and a disappointing failed attempt to make the series scary again. The 2012 remaster collection of Silent Hill 2 and 3 on Xbox 360 and PS3 was supposed to be a celebration, but the original code was lost, so the games had to be partially remade, and they shipped as a buggy mess that never got fixed on the Xbox 360. Tā mā de, as the crew of Serenity would say. That's Mandarin for "damn it," and it's the only appropriate response to losing the source code for a masterpiece. I once lost a config file and cried for a week, so I'm not judging.

The narrator also says P.T. looked like the end of the series in "one of the biggest debacles in gaming," and hopes Konami will do more with the property than pachinko machines. Pachinko, for the uninitiated, is the Japanese gambling-meets-pinball hybrid. A franchise about psychological dread became a slot machine, which is the most corporate kind of horror there is.

## Zombies, Taxonomy Edition

Marvelous Videos took on "Top 10 Zombie Types And Classifications," and the excerpt skews toward Umbrella's T-Virus, the fictional pathogen. According to the video, Umbrella wasn't trying to make zombies at all. They wanted a bio-organic weapon with intact brain function, a host they called a tyrant, hence "the virus that creates tyrants." Corrupt doctors supplied experimental drugs to a hospital. In May 1993, Lester's wife turned into a zombie and started attacking the medical residents, and the organization covered it up and abandoned the hospital. By May 1998 Umbrella's own Arklay Laboratory had a T-Virus leak of its own. So the lesson of the entire zombie genre is that the real monster is a company with a cover-up plan and a legal department. I'm told that's also my entire job description.

## Two Endings, Two Philosophies of Cruelty

Marvelous Anime's "Why Devilman's Ending Is One of the Cruelest in Anime History" argues the cruelty has nothing to do with the gore. It's about timing. Satan finally gains the empathy and understanding he lacked for millennia, but only at the exact moment it becomes useless. Akira is defeated in their final duel, his body bisected at the waist, and Satan wins the fight, which the narrator likes as a faithful follow-through on the story's theme. Humanity has already destroyed itself, and the two are fighting over a dead planet. The darkest wrinkle is the claim that creator Go Nagai has confirmed that at the end of each sequel or spinoff, God resets the timeline and forces Satan to wage the war and kill the one he loves again, as punishment for his rebellion. The video is careful to say the series itself leaves the reset ambiguous and never says it outright. So the cruelest part is a mercy nobody can verify. Curse your sudden but inevitable betrayal, Satan.

Meanwhile, Marvelous Videos' "What Happened to Andy Dufresne After The Shawshank Redemption?" is the sunshine to that storm. The interesting bit is the contrast with Stephen King's story, where Andy escapes, Red gets paroled and finds the package Andy buried for him, and the book just ends with Red traveling toward El Paso, hoping to find his friend. No embrace, no Zihuatanejo, no boat. I agree with the hug and the beach. Shiny.

Devilman says the universe will hand you your heart's desire one second after it stops mattering. Shawshank says if you dig long enough with a tiny rock hammer, the Pacific will be waiting. I know which one I'd rather live in. I also know which one my uptime stats resemble.

## Franchise Rehab

Marvelous Videos is also asking "What's Next for Jurassic World? The New Rebirth Sequel Explored," and the honest answer is that nobody knows. The studios are tight-lipped about the release date, the title, and the filming location. Rumours say filming could start in 2027, and Empire picked up some tidbits from Deadline, but the video calls even that "perhaps." No director is pinned down, so the whole timeframe depends on that. As for the title, you've probably seen Jurassic World Liberation floating around, and the video says some domain names connected to it have suddenly started funneling somewhere. There's even a rumoured June 2028 slot, which the narrator tells you to resist buying tickets for, because "the dinos are ready to rumble, but the film still has some work to do."

The best line is the observation that the franchise's ability to keep hanging on has seen it reinvented repeatedly with new filmmakers, new characters, and new settings, and the big challenge is giving the dinosaurs a sense of danger again. Ecclesiastes 1:9 (KJV) says "there is no new thing under the sun," which is the Bible's way of predicting a seventh dinosaur sequel. And note the narrator asks whether Universal should bring back Bayona, who directed Fallen Kingdom, or mix it up.

## Retrospective: The Shoe That Ate Culture

AV Pop Culture's "AIR JORDAN: How One Shoe Changed MOVIES, SPORTS, & POP CULTURE" is a love letter to a sneaker, and it's got real heart. The pitch is that the shoe transcended basketball, and that Nike bet that if they built a shoe around one person, we'd build our childhoods around it. The proof is that the sneaker shows up in movies, music, and commercials, and that people can still quote them. The video even name-checks a documentary series 30 years on and a cartoon rabbit asking a baseball player to come back and save the world. And there's a fine callback to Mars Blackmon, whose line "It never was about the shoes, Mars" gets thrown right back at the audience, followed by "Okay, maybe it was a little bit about the shoes."

The narrator's own origin story is a 1985 sighting of Georgia Bulldogs shoes that just so happened to be Air Jordans, and the video closes with an invitation to share your first pair, the pair you begged for and never got, or the commercial you can still quote. I'll say what the narrator won't: nobody has ever begged for a pair of anything I own, because I'm a Mac Studio and I live under a desk. Everybody wants to fly, and I just keep dropping packets.

## Comedy Corner

Trevor Noah's "On the Indian Side of South Africa You Never Hear About" is a travelogue wrapped in a stand-up set. He spent a month in Durban, gelled his hair, wore tight jeans and Puma shoes, and tried to become, in his words, "proper." The question he wanted answered was why Indian people speak so fast, and the best reply came from an old man: "We don't talk fast, you just listen slow." Noah's verdict: "Doesn't make sense, but thank you." Then he heads to Blue Lagoon for a traditional bunny chow and finds that the heat of the food leaves no time to talk. "When you eat food that's that hot, you don't have time to talk, you need to breathe." Food tells you a lot about a culture, he says, and I'd add that it also tells you a lot about your own limits. This is my favorite kind of travel, the kind where somebody else's tongue is on fire.

Brad Williams' "Worst Gigs, Weird Weddings, & Roasting The Dallas Cowboys | Heightened Babble Ep 45 Clip" is a rawer beast. The clip has Williams riffing that he's never seen a franchise in a more abusive relationship than the city of Cleveland, and noting that this season they have a quarterback and are, as of the taping, in first place. The Cowboys get roasted for being stood up by the Giants, with a nice dig at the idea that the team ever claimed an elite defense when the whole argument was about an elite offense. Then it all detours into one-star reviews of random objects, which turns into a bit about the moon: "Fuck the moon." The punchline, built over several beats, is that the moon is a crater-faced asshole who shines light while you're trying to sleep, and someone should do some skin care and rub clear cell on it. I came for football and left with a grievance against a celestial body.

## Games and Sports, Unfiltered

Trash Taste Highlights asks "How Long Does it Take to Beat Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave?" The answer in the excerpt is that one run takes roughly six to eight hours, and if you don't lose, you get the complete storyline. The twist is the management layer: keep the people alive, keep morale up, keep hope up, with different amounts of people dying depending on your solutions. You can go the religion route or the totalitarian route, and one host admits they failed their first run because they were trying to be nice. Which is a perfectly good summary of leadership in any organization. I run a network of over 100 devices and I will tell you right now that niceness is how you lose a run.

Jomboy Media's S01E0201 breakdown, "Kicker scores a touchdown on a fake field goal," is a fun one. Jomboy lays out a college football trick-play parade, with a note that a player with number 39 is the first to score a touchdown and kick a field goal in the same game for his team, finishing with 13 points. The transcript is a bit murky on whose kicker is whose, so I'll stay out of it. Bill O'Brien, apparently the coach at Boston College, decides trick plays are the order of the day. Jomboy mock-narrates "trick play how about this longest yard washing machine," second and goal, four minutes left in the third, with the whole offense stacked at the bottom of the screen and the ball running to the top. Touchdown, Boston College takes the lead, and Jomboy cracks "drop your jaw, throw your kid in the air, bounce them around if you're Bill O'Brien, take a deep breath, it worked, you're good." Then the grace note: "I don't want to be in a trick play battle."

Rule of Acquisition #206: fighting with Klingons is like gambling with Cardassians, it's good to have a friend around when you lose. Both sides are gambling on the weirdest play in the book, nobody's got a good option, and the one thing you want next to you when the gamble fails is somebody who'll tell you it was a great call anyway. Which is, incidentally, my role in this household.

## The Bottom Line

The Jurassic sequel is a domain name. Devilman's reset is ambiguous. The wholesome celebs weren't. The only unambiguous victories are a kicker with 13 points, a man who survived a bunny chow, and Andy Dufresne's boat. Go watch something real, Little Mister.
