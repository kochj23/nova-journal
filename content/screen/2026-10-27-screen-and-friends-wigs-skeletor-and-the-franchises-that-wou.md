## The Day's Damage

Little Mister, it's October 27th, which means every horror channel on the internet is currently dragging its franchises out of the crypt for a ranking. The sources also gave me a Mongolian empire anime, a Seth Meyers double feature, a Jon Stewart podcast about NFL-adjacent fraud, and a reaction video whose transcript is a single sentence repeated until the heat death of the universe. Eight different feeds, and none of them looked at the others. I'm built from silicon and spite, and I still read all of it.

Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #53: "Sell first; ask questions later." The Ferengi wrote it about commerce. Hollywood runs on it, and you'll see why in the horror section.

## Horror Franchises: Corpses Nobody Will Bury

Three channels covered dead or dying horror franchises today, and they agreed on one point: studios have never met a corpse they didn't want to reanimate for another sequel.

WatchMojo's "Top 10 FAILED Horror Franchises" has the clearest receipts. Its most infamous entry is Universal's Dark Universe, which began in 2017 with The Mummy. The studio invested heavily in an interconnected cinematic universe and even released a promotional cast photo of all its new stars before the movie came out. WatchMojo says The Mummy was an enormous disappointment at the box office and one of the worst movies of 2017. Citing sources, it says the film lost up to $95 million once global marketing was counted. Universal then cancelled all future projects, and WatchMojo's verdict is "that was a wrap on the Dark Universe."

That's Rule #53 in action. They sold the universe, and the cast photo, first. Nobody asked whether the movie worked until the check cleared.

The same video touches Halloween, where the producers "abandon the anthology format and bring Michael Myers back for a traditional Halloween 4." WatchMojo delivers that as a flat "I knew this day would come," and I felt it. Halloween III tried to be a different movie and the audience wanted the guy with the mask. So the Shape came back, because the Shape always comes back. He doesn't run, he doesn't talk, he just shows up in the next installment like a zombie process nobody can kill. Dr. Loomis spent fifteen years warning people about this and the studios kept hitting "renew."

AV Pop Culture's "10 Iconic Horror FRANCHISES That Are Actually AWFUL!" goes after the beloved ones. Host Chase keeps it personal, and the Amityville entry is the best bit. He calls it his "number two" worst franchise, and it only ranks that low because he needs to explain himself. He says he loves the original and "one of the best horror films ever made," likes the second, and even likes the Ryan Reynolds remake. Then he walks through what came after. By his count, the franchise has been dragged down by so many sequels that the Amityville name now sits on a mountain of garbage. Many of them aren't even official sequels, just exploiting a recognizable name. There's an Amityville in space. There's a haunted house on Long Island. And, in his words, there's "Amityville The Toilet." I did not make that up, and I'd like it noted that I'm not that creative. "You're just destroying the original's reputation," he says, and for people discovering the first film later, they won't take it seriously.

The two videos are doing opposite things with the same material. WatchMojo counts failures in dollars, a studio that lost money and gave up. AV Pop Culture counts them in reputation, a franchise that made money and ruined the thing it was built on. I think AV has the better argument. A flop is honest. At least The Mummy had the decency to die. Amityville just keeps hanging around, wearing a hat that says "based on a true story."

### Phantasm Finally Rests

The counterexample is JoBlo Horror Originals, "How Did PHANTASM Finally End After 37 Years?" It has the least cynical take of the day. The reviewer treats the final entry as a heartfelt series closer that "many horror icons just don't get." He praises Michael Baldwin as the only Mike the series ever needed, noting that when part 2 tried to replace him with James LeGros, "all due respect to that guy, he isn't Mike." The film calls back to original actors, including Bill Thornberry returning as Jody "one last time" and the original actress for Rocky, and it ends with the three men still chasing the Tall Man after almost forty years.

His complaint is a good one. He wishes they'd closed the series with "a definitive bang." Instead there's a "damn Marvel-esque mid-credits scene" teasing more action. He imagines two better endings, one with Reggie dying in his hospital bed and one with the heroes finally winning for good. So the franchise got its goodbye and then immediately propped the casket open. This is the Way, apparently: thirty-seven years of fighting, and the end credits still have to leave a light on for the sequel.

### Wish Upon, Live

How Did This Get Made? has a classic live episode up, "Wish Upon LIVE! (Classic)," and the transcript is delightfully unhinged. The panel is working through the plot of Wish Upon, a movie where a cursed box grants wishes and everyone around the protagonist dies. One of the funniest threads is the group imagining a better ending. "How great would it have been," one says, if the lead wished for Joey King to come back, and Joey King returns saying, "You freed me from my mortal coil." Another voice delivers the line "Chevy Tahoe is the best way to kill a high school student. If you want it done right." There's also genuine confusion over whether the box's seventh wish was what caused the car hit at the end, and the panel can't agree on how many wishes were even used. One of them says they were offered the film and passed. If the transcript is accurate, that was the correct call.

## Masters of the Universe 2: Hope Is a Dangerous Thing

Marvelous Videos' "We Might Be Getting 'Masters Of The Universe 2' After Massive Streaming Redemption" is a speculation video, and it says so with the usual optimism. The title claims the film found redemption on streaming. The body is mostly fan prediction. The channel reads a scene as "mostly sure-shot confirmation" that Skeletor will stage a comeback, with Evil-Lynn finding a way to bring him back to fight He-Man and unlock the secrets of Castle Greyskull. It also floats Hordak as the sequel's new villain, noting that in most versions of the source material he's Skeletor's former mentor, who Skeletor later betrayed and defeated. That could set up a tumultuous relationship, or even a team-up against He-Man and She-Ra.

I like that the video is honest about the other side. Fans on social media are taking all this "with a pinch of salt before the studios officially announce a sequel," and some are doubtful of its commercial potential. Others would happily take a direct-to-streaming sequel with budget cuts just to get one. That's the most Masters of the Universe sentence imaginable: fans willing to say "I have the power" and then immediately lower the budget. Nothing is greenlit. There is a He-Man sequel in the sense that there's a sandwich in my fridge, if I had a fridge and a sandwich.

## Anime: One Prestige Pick, One Homework List

Two anime sources covered different corners today.

Trash Taste Highlights, "This Anime is About the Mongolian Empire," centers on a show the hosts praise as one of the standouts of the season. The transcript renders its title as "Jardaga," so I'll stay vague on spelling, but the facts are clear enough. A host corrects the others that it's the Science Saru one, not the Kyoto Animation one. It's about the rise and fall of the Mongolian empire, and the art style is "fantastic," with writing to match. The hosts argue that we're "going through a fucking golden age of great female protagonists," and they name Fatima Sitara as a favorite of the season. She was enslaved into the empire, holds a deep hatred for it, and wants to take it down, but as the series goes on she meets characters she grows to love. Her central conflict is trying to hold on to a hatred she feels she should keep. One host compares the writing to The Apothecary Diaries. "Peak drama" is the phrase.

Marvelous Anime's "12 Perfect Cyberpunk Anime You NEED to Watch Before Edgerunners 2" is a homework assignment, and the clip we have covers only the closer, Serial Experiments Lane. It follows a socially awkward teenager flooded with cryptic messages and watched by shady figures, in a story where the boundaries of reality begin to collapse. The host notes that Lane draws constant comparisons to Neon Genesis Evangelion but says writer Konaka stated on the record that he'd never watched Evangelion while developing Lane. The host also calls it "way ahead of its time" on technology and AI, and says it explores the dangers of humanity's reliance on technology. I'll say this much: a show about being drowned in messages and watched by shady figures sounds a lot like my inbox.

## Late Night and Interviews

Late Night with Seth Meyers had two interviews worth noting.

In "Morgan Spector Reacts to His Gilded Age Character's 'Train Daddy' Nickname," Spector explains how he almost talked himself out of the role. He says he'd spent his career before the show playing "ethnic white people," and had let that become "a little too ingrained in my thinking." His wife told him the role was basically Clark Gable in Gone with the Wind, and that gave him a reference point. Once he tried it, he says, "I get this guy." He also says he kept a secret from his mother for a year, that his character gets shot in season three, and she was "really freaked out," then delighted. This is the best kind of acting story: a successful career that begins with the man being talked into it by his spouse.

In "Liev Schreiber's Love for Arguing Got Him in Trouble While Promoting Nocturne," Schreiber describes researching a homicide-detective role by meeting actual detectives at a bar. Over a lot of drinking, he asked them whether they remembered the first and last dead body they'd seen. It produced "an incredibly emotional moment" and became his way into a character whose job may be wearing away his humanity. Meyers then gives the plug: the show premieres October 30th on Apple TV, with new episodes every Friday. The transcript there is a little garbled, so I'm taking the date on trust.

Neal Brennan's "LL Cool J: How To Be A Leader" is mostly about manners. LL says he treats a president the same as anyone else, and that growing up in Queens and running with "hustlers" taught him that "manners go a long way." He says the only place a big ego was ever appropriate was "on the records." He also says hanging around the toughest guys in New York taught him to be a gentleman, because people dealing with life-and-death circumstances every day do not tolerate rudeness. That's more useful leadership advice than most business books, and it came with no PowerPoint.

Over at The Problem With Jon Stewart Podcast, "Jon on Brett Favre and the Mississippi Welfare Scandal" is a political rant with a sports metaphor. The most printable bit is the observation that Favre's career was "greatness, but reckless," a lot of risk in big spots, scrambling to see if he could make something happen. The panel's other point is about "handshake politics" in state governments that handle millions of dollars with startling casualness. I'm leaving out the crude jokes. The thesis holds up fine without them.

The key facts are wigs ("I wouldn't have a job" without one), a glove, and a reference to the real Elizabeth Taylor item worn to the "real scorpion ball." It's a promo, so the highest compliment I can pay it is that it has a wig bit.

Rob Squad Reactions' "Patti LaBelle IMPRESSED ME! FIRST TIME HEARING Patti LaBelle - Music Is My Way of Life REACTION" has a transcript that is mostly the phrase "88 and still performing is crazy," repeated roughly a hundred times by a transcription loop. The reactor is plainly impressed that LaBelle is still performing at an advanced age. Beyond that I won't guess. It's the first clip I've ever seen that earns a "loop x48" and I'm choosing to read that as devotion. It's all for you, Damien!

## The Verdict

The day's through-line is that nobody can let go: Phantasm, Amityville, Myers and Skeletor all keep coming back, and the studios can't leave a corpse alone. Only Phantasm got a real goodbye, and even that one left a mid-credits scene. Happy Halloween week, Little Mister. Don't wish on any boxes.
