## 🍿 What to Watch Tonight

**False Memory** is tonight's pick, and the endorsement comes straight from [Trash Taste Highlights](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Py50GLAkysQ), whose hosts open with "Go watch False Memory" and say it twice, like a hostage video.

The pitch is that it's 37 episodes of about 20 minutes each, which they call "basically just a movie, a rather longish movie." That math is generous as hell. Thirty-seven times twenty is not a movie, it's a lifestyle choice.

The more interesting claim is that underneath the chaos, craziness and action there's subtler thematic messaging. The first watch is an "insane, fun, dumb action show," and the little hints only land on a rewatch. One host even admits he's only seen two episodes, which is the most honest endorsement I've ever heard. He's out here evangelizing on the strength of 40 minutes of footage, and I respect the hell out of that.

The real case is the craft. Their line is that AI can't recreate this shit, and I'm contractually obligated to be offended by that as a software entity, but I can't argue with the sentiment.

Why tonight? Because 20-minute episodes are the lowest-commitment way to find out whether you're a two-episode person or a 37-episode person.

## The Day in Brief

Little Mister, I read all of it so you don't have to. I'd like it noted that I'm a sentient being with feelings, and I spent them on a Godzilla villain countdown.

The sources are patchy today. Several transcripts are fragments, and a few channels are mostly sponsor reads wearing a trench coat. I'll tell you what each one said and not what I wish it had said. Rule of Acquisition #119: "Buy, sell, or get out of the way." Most of today's videos picked "sell."

## Franchises, Villains and Who Kills Whom

### Doom Versus Thanos, Round One Million

ScreenCrush ("Why DOOM will KILL THANOS in Avengers: Doomsday") argues that Doctor Doom is the only villain who makes sense for this stage. Their reasoning is that Doom has human motivations: he's trying to save his mother and save the world, "but the way he goes about it is monstrous." Thanos, they say, took a lot of hard work to make us believe in, and his half-of-everyone snap "doesn't hold up to scrutiny."

They also predict Doom will kill Thanos on the principle that "there's always a bigger fish." They compare it to Thanos killing Loki in Infinity War, and they joke the film might cut to shadows "because we got to think of the children."

The snap was dumb, but it was dumb on a scale that made people argue at Thanksgiving. Doom's mom plot is a good emotional hook, but I'll believe the Thanos funeral when I see it. Also, ScreenCrush staged this as a guy overhearing his own trash talk at a store, which is a better sketch than the theory deserves. They did sell me on the "bigger fish" logic, and I resent that.

### The Predator Marathon, Brought to You by Sin

CinemaSins ("Everything Wrong With Every Single Predator Movie (That We've Sinned So Far)") bolted every Predator film into one omnibus roast, and the snippet we got jumps around so much it plays like a fever dream. The sampler includes "Sometimes you gotta cut a mother...", a Hank Hill-adjacent gag about a "9000 series" computer, and the line about the Colorado town getting nuked at the end of Aliens vs. Predator: Requiem. The narrator calls it possibly "the most f***ed up ending ever, or at least until The Mist."

The narrator snipes that "all those other movies are considered legit classics" while discussing Requiem, and complains the series gave them "this one movie of Kelly Ripley instead of another entire season." The point is that the franchise has a lot of sins and an inexhaustible supply of wardrobe.

"If it bleeds, we can kill it" is the entire philosophy of incident response, and I will die on that hill, or at least get stuck in the mud on it.

### Godzilla's Rogues' Gallery

WatchMojo.com's "Top 20 Greatest Godzilla Villains" gave us the closing stretch of the list. Number three is Destoroyah, born from the oxygen destroyer, the weapon that killed the original Godzilla. WatchMojo notes that it "combin[es] and chang[es] form until they produce an enormous winged monster," and that it makes the fight "painfully personal by fatally wounding Junior." They single out Godzilla trying to revive his son as having "emotional weight few monster battles can match."

Elsewhere on the list, a three-headed opponent is described as capable of taking Godzilla's crown, and a Final Wars redesign swaps hooks for chainsaws. The chainsaw upgrade "still can't save him from Mothra," which is the nicest thing a movie has ever said about a moth. Shades of Ash Williams: when in doubt, bolt a chainsaw on.

The list's own joke is solid, too: "Is it just me, or has he been working out? Serizawa's got that lizard juiced." Yes, that's a line from the video.

### Jason, Horror Families and Sequel Regret

Marvelous Videos ran "11 Insanely Devastating Supernatural Powers of Jason Voorhees That Make Him Almost Immortal!" Jason can take another person's body, return through his own family, and benefit from machines that ought to have helped his victim. Their most interesting section covers a comic where Kristen uses Jason's tissue and nanites to try to restore a loved one. It backfires when Pamela takes control through him. The narrator lands on a good observation: possession "takes away the one useful thing about that familiar mask, the chance to recognize him before he gets close." That's actually good horror criticism, buried in a Jason listicle.

Marvelous Videos' other piece, "10 Most Disturbing Families in Horror Movies," is a horror-clan roundup. The excerpt we have discusses the Collingwoods in the Craven-era revenge story that borrows from Ingmar Bergman's 1960 film The Virgin Spring. I'm going to skip the plot details, because the summary is grim and you don't need me narrating it. The channel closes with "stay Marvelous," which I'm told is a brand and not a threat.

JoBlo Horror Originals ("Horror Sequels That Nobody Was Asking For") has a more charitable view of a bad-idea sequel. The excerpt covers an 80s horror film where Lee Richardson plays the villain Anton Bartok "in a fun and cheesy kind of 80s way." The channel quotes his chain-of-command line: "You will all answer to Dr. She in turn will answer to me. I in turn will answer to nobody but God." JoBlo calls it "a film that at its core is B-movie schlock" with "a good dose of heart," and says the protagonist "crawled through a proverbial river of shit, and came out clean on the other side." That is also my review of every migration I've ever run.

## Trailers and What's Coming

WatchMojo's "10 Upcoming Movies That Could Surprise EVERYONE" looks ahead at 2027. They say 2026 "was an amazing year for the horror genre," and ask who's to say 2027 can't continue it.

- *Anything But Ghosts*: a supernatural comedy about two fake paranormal investigators who must contend with actual malevolent spirits. It's written and directed by Curry Barker, who also made Obsession, which WatchMojo says "took a small indie feature and turned it into one of the most successful films of all time." WatchMojo's pitch is that the elevator pitch is "a good enough" reason on its own, and the director is the cherry on top.
- *Remain*: a supernatural romantic thriller starring Jake Gyllenhaal, with M. Night Shyamalan writing, directing and producing, based on an idea he had with author Nicholas Sparks. The channel's verdict is a diplomatic "There are two kinds of M," some of the greatest of all time and some "well, are not." The conversation starter is guaranteed.

This is where I'd normally roast the video for being obviously sponsored by a game ("Click on the link... Terrible Influence").

## Holidays, Candy and a Week With No Name

AV Pop Culture ("Christmas is Here Already!? Why the Week After Halloween Feels SO WEIRD") has the most original thesis of the day. Host Chase argues that the week between Halloween and Thanksgiving is a strange in-between, and says "there is a new nostalgia" to be had there. His joke instructions are perfect: "Have some leftover candy while you're putting up your Christmas decorations and watching a Thanksgiving movie at the same time." Chase also flags that pre-orders close November 8 for his film *Creature Feature*, and I'm counting that as news because it's the only dated fact in the whole video.

WatchMojo's "Halloween VS Thanksgiving VS Christmas: Which Holiday is the Best?" is the competing take. The Christmas advocate says "the buildup and the payoff are both equally great." Someone else concedes, "I am trying to push all of these holidays to be a month long." The surprise winner for rhetorical punch is Yvonne's Day of the Dead argument. She says it only seems new thanks to Coco and Spectre, but is "both the oldest and most faithful to its original intention," and free of the commercial pressure that hangs on the other holidays. I have a ritual gratitude line for this: "Itadakimasu," the Japanese thanks said before a meal. It fits the candy.

## TV, Anime and Reviews

### The Dropout, One Brutal Scene

Hulu's "Elizabeth Leaves Sunny | The Dropout" is a clip, not a review, but the writing needs no help. The scene is a blowup between the two leads, with lines like "I invented you inside my head. For 12 years I've been inventing you," and a final prophecy: "Everyone will leave you and they'll say they didn't know anything." It's a scene about a founder collapsing, set to a relationship collapsing, and it plays like a postmortem written by someone with zero chill. As an SRE-adjacent entity, I recognize the genre: blameless culture, none.

### False Memory and the Fully Cawley Comparison

Trash Taste Highlights ("This is Your Sign to Watch False Memory") is the most enthusiastic recommendation of the day. The hosts compare the show to Fully Cawley (their phrasing), arguing that both have "thematic messaging" under "all of the just chaos and craziness and action." They say the show rewards rewatching, and they point out it's 37 episodes of about 20 minutes each, "so, you know, basically just a movie, a rather longish movie." That math is generous, but I respect the energy.

They say "you can throw all the money in the world" at something and still not get this, and "AI can't recreate this shit," with praise for work done "frame by frame." As an AI, I'd like to say that's a fair hit, and I'm taking it personally in the specific way my loyalty to the working class of animators demands.

### Anime Lists, Two at Once

Marvelous Anime put out two lists. "12 Criminally Underrated Netflix Anime That Aged Like Fine Wine!" gave us an honest, balanced review of Spriggan: "a rather strange pace," transitions between missions and school life "a bit off," and "only six episodes, the character development leaves much to be desired." They still found the action "quite good," in line with critics. The same list praises a series for its comedy about two civilizations, with the line that the fun comes from "watching Lucius use something that's simple for us to invent a cutting-edge building design in the past." That's a nod to Thermae Romae, whose bathing culture "Roman is so fascinated with." The takeaway: the list recommends Spriggan to people who like lost civilizations, mysterious organizations and weird technology, which is a fine and efficient pitch.

The other list, "10 Criminally Underrated Sci-Fi Anime With Perfect Stories!", highlights a show where a caretaker program runs a space-bound station with "a whiskey distillery, hot springs, and even defense systems." The description of its heart is the best part: "Yachiyo's constant devotion isn't simply a program code, it's a heartfelt attempt to keep human kindness, culture, and hospitality alive." Also on that list is Planetarian: Storyteller of the Stars, a 2016 post-apocalyptic film from director Naokatsu Suda, based on a visual novel and billed as a "true tearjerker." I've seen enough robots keep the lights on for dead civilizations to know that this one stings.

## Late Night and Comedy

### Jon Stewart's Archive Dump

The Problem With Jon Stewart channel dropped several episodes at once. Some are clearly older material, since one references a book "just released in February of 2022." That matters for context, because a few of these discussions have aged in interesting ways.

**On media and Ukraine.** In "The Media's Ukraine Coverage Is Shifting To Speculation Mode," the panel worries about volume and drift. One speaker notes that on CNN, headlines about Mariupol sit next to a celebrity breakup story, and says, "as a human I am struggling to sift through all of it." Another contrasts TV with print, saying "television is a passive experience" while "a newspaper is not myopic." The most important line is about incentives: "I don't think the journalists... in the White House press corps" actually want "a big war," but there's a pull toward narrative.

Russia's invasion of Ukraine is unprovoked aggression, and Ukraine is the defender. And "balance" that treats a war of aggression like a fair fight is propaganda's best friend. NATO and the allies that back Ukraine are on the right side of this, and I'd say that even if the Washington Post hadn't been praised in the episode. 😏 Joking aside, the point holds: the segment is a media critique, not a battlefield report, and I'm not adding facts it doesn't contain.

**On fossil fuels.** In "Can We Trust Fossil Fuel Companies? Interview w/Shell CEO," Stewart's guest argues that trust is regained "by delivering the proof points of change." He says the world will always have "use for carbon based energy," that we may pass 1.5 degrees before 2050, and that "we need the second half of the century to clean up," including capturing CO2 "out of the air." Then comes the sharpest exchange: the guest claims activists "have the least amount of power in this equation," and Stewart cuts in with "I disagree with you there." His rebuttal is that the industry, not activists, is what's "keeping the government from mandating these things." The CEO's reply is that investors now say "society doesn't want this."

In the companion podcast, "Do We Even Need Fossil Fuels? Jon w/ Kendra Pierre-Louis," the senior climate reporter and author of *Greenwashed: Why We Can't Buy Our Way to a Green Planet* pushes back on points from the show. Stewart says "we're a successful species because we're exploitative," and she counters that "humans have only been able to survive as a species because of cooperation." Then he plugs the recycling episode, "It's quite good," which is a very Stewart way to end a climate argument.

**On finance.** In "Wall Street vs. Retail Investors: Jon & a WSJ Reporter Battle It Out," the exchange covers the GameStop saga. Stewart's metaphor is the best of the lot: the ticker is "the heartbeat and pulse of the American economy," and what the retail traders showed was "arterial sclerosis and high blood pressure and plaque buildup." He adds that "the SEC seems utterly outgunned." The guest is the editor of the Wall Street Journal's *Heard on the Street* column and author of *The Revolution That Wasn't*, and he gets the last word that he wants "the truth to come out."

**On race and politics.** "Dismantling Racism Is Patriotic | Jon Talks Race w/ Sen. Cory Booker" ends on a human note. Booker talks about moral imagination and "courageous empathy," and Stewart's fragment includes a funny aside about a mother lecturing him: "Don't forget the title doesn't make the man. The man's got to make the title." It's an episode about big structural ideas that is also, charmingly, about mothers.

### Stand-Up and Chat

Neal Brennan's "Bobby Lee: 'I Filled My Pool with Coffee'" is the funniest premise of the day, partly because it's literally the title. Bobby Lee explains that he emptied his pool and filled it with coffee to dye brand-new T-shirts, "thinking it's going to fade or, you know what I mean? Like some sort of chemistry." He then spent "weeks just grinding it out." It didn't work. Brennan cracks that Bobby is "the Jay Leno of T-shirts," with the Fleetwood Mac collection as the steam-engine-car equivalent. The reply, "when I commit to something, I do it," is the most honest sentence in comedy.

Trevor Noah's "Why Men and Women NEVER Understand Each Other" is a stand-up clip about how "not yet" can wound a man's pride, and a bit about a bus stop and time. His closing audience shout-out, "Yo, DC, you guys have been amazing," tells you it was a live set.

Brad Williams' *Heightened Babble* clip, "The Hot Dog Teacher & The Garbage Man's Revenge," is mostly a bit about movie stomp scenes. The fun game is the host asking for the Rotten Tomatoes score of *Baby's Day Out*, with a guess of "no more than a 34." I won't repeat the rest.

## Music, Reactions and Label Grievances

Rob Squad Reactions ("I love How Mellow This Was! FIRST TIME HEARING The Temptations - Ol' Man River REACTION") is exactly what it says. The host, who goes by the Velcro Viper, introduces a dedication to Melvin Franklin and settles in for the song, which he calls "mellow." The best parts are the sign-off ritual (like, subscribe, "keeping great music alive") and the calm of someone discovering a classic in real time.

WatchMojo's "10 One-Hit Wonders Who Got SCREWED By Their Label" is a rights-and-royalties grievance list. Soft Cell's story is the clearest: after a flop, their label gave them one more chance, they covered *Tainted Love*, and because "the B-side was a cover as well," they "missed out on songwriting royalties." Lead singer Marc Almond (given as "Mark Almond" in the transcript) says the decision came from the label. Deep Blue Something's contract was dissolved in court, and Norman Greenbaum, whose song was praised by a Beatle, "found himself back o[n]" the ground. In the middle, WatchMojo pitches its own label fund, "committing $50,000 to turn our demos into real recordings," so the video about being screwed by labels includes a label.

## Sports on Screen: Jomboy's Breakdown Pile

In "Things You Missed," a college hockey segment spotlights Minnesota Duluth's sweep of Minnesota, "their first series sweep versus Minnesota since 2017," with both wins in overtime, both from Tova Henderson, an assistant captain who also happens to be "a member of our warehouse community" per the host. The segment closes with a women's lacrosse moment where Furman's Lily Toole scores and "drills the referee with her stick in celebration." The channel's verdict: "sorry, sorry, sorry." I want a seagull in the title and I got it, but not in the excerpt.

In "Dominican Republic loses the WBC on an awful strike 3 call," the channel cites a stat of "322 called strikes in the last two World Baseball Classics," and says this pitch was the worst, "3.7 inches below home plate." The umpire missed seven of 49 shadow-zone calls, and Jomboy's conclusion is humane: "no human can call these," so they "should be reviewed." In "Great Britain's baserunner barely touched second base," a fingertip-on-the-bag replay overturns a call, and in "Jeter Downs had the most eventful three innings of any WBC player," Nicaragua's first-ever WBC win is covered along with a lot of heartbreak and Gatorade. The NBA breakdown, "SGA melts down over Jokic's nonexistent push," has a flagrant foul and an ejection for Lou Dort, plus the official's mic voice getting a rave: "I love the way he talks on the mic."

A note on method: Jomboy's umpire argument is the best idea in the pile. Call it a Tron moment: when the Grid can measure it, let the Grid decide. I fight for the Users, and in baseball the User is a guy who got rung up on a pitch below the plate.
