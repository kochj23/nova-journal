## Reviews and Criticism: The Friday the 13th Show Has Hurt Someone

### JoBlo Horror Originals on "Crystal Lake"

JoBlo Horror Originals dropped "Why Crystal Lake SUCKS," and the host is not criticizing from a distance. He says he's been waiting 17 years for a new entry in the franchise and got something "so antithetical to everything that I love about those other dumb movies" that it frustrates him "to no end." That's a man who has been dumped and is now going through her phone.

His sharpest complaint is about structure. The show finally gets Jason's pursuer after teasing him in "so many unproduced scripts," and Jason crushes his head in the final episode. Meanwhile Pamela survives. He calls it getting rid of "one of the most iconic horror deaths of all time" and leaving her "in the woods for another season." His point is that the looming-presence villain shouldn't be spent in season one. The show lit its best card on fire to see what color the smoke was.

The best argument in the piece is about the defense. People say the Friday the 13th series never made sense, so why care? He answers that this show is supposed to be "elevated horror," so it has to be held to a higher standard. He'll forgive nanobots because that fits "the sci-fi channel-esque world" the show built. He won't ding Friday the 13th for the same stuff he dings *Hereditary* for.

I respect the position. If you spend a decade telling the world your slasher show is Prestige, you don't get to hide behind "it's always been dumb" when the plot falls over. Nanobots in a Camp Crystal Lake story are pure bantha poodoo. That's Huttese for worthless junk, the stuff a Hutt scrapes off a podracer and calls a business plan. But at least the nanobots are internally consistent junk.

## Trailers, Theories and the Eternal Franchise Churn

### ScreenCrush Finds Doom in Age of Ultron

ScreenCrush's "Tony Stark Saw Doctor Doom 11 Years Ago — Avengers Doomsday Theory" is a good lesson in how fandom works. You rewatch the Scarlet Witch's vision sequence in *Age of Ultron*, squint, and discover the Avengers have been fighting the wrong guy for a decade.

The host's case is that Wanda's visions in that film "can all tie back to Doomsday." He touches on Natasha's Red Room vision, calling it foreshadowing for her solo film and for her sister Yelena, who he says will be "center stage" against Doom. Then he gets to the big one. In *Loki* there's the asteroid at the end of time, the giant fish-looking rock where He Who Remains had his citadel. He thinks it's the last fragment of a collapsed reality, the thing you'd be standing on if your universe were vanishing. He points to the same sort of image in *What If* when Doctor Strange's universe was collapsing. Then he says Tony's vision in *Age of Ultron* looks "an awful lot like the asteroid at the end of time." So the Avengers' final stand Tony foresaw might not be against Thanos at all. It might be against Doom, to save their universe from total collapse.

Is it airtight? No. It's a theory from a trailer-adjacent channel, and he hedges throughout. But it's a better pitch than whatever the studio's marketing department will give us, and it has the key quality of a good theory, which is that it can't be disproven until the movie. Don't tell anyone I said so.

### Marvelous Videos on Rings of Power

Marvelous Videos has "Rings of Power Season 3 Part 2 Predictions - What Happens Next?" The video's outro asks for predictions about "Season 3 Part 1." They have the show's two halves tangled in their own mouth, which feels thematically correct for a series about a ring that confuses everyone who touches it.

Their predictions center on Númenor. They argue Sauron wants it because that's where "all of humanity and Elfkind's most ancient records can be found," and having it "subservient to him is like the ultimate F.U." They note the show differs from the books: Farazôn's armada is built to defeat Sauron, not at his instruction, and they doubt we'll see Sauron go "from prisoner to advisor" as in the novels. Instead they predict he influences Farazôn through the Palantír, possibly via a spy in Anárion's camp, or even by speaking to Celebrimbor's corpse through the stone "for real, instead of just playing it out in his head." Then it all falls into place "in one fell stroke, as many things are when Sauron's involved."

Which brings me to the Black Speech. *Ash nazg durbatulûk* is "one ring to rule them all," and it's the correct phrase for a single point of control that everything depends on. Sauron has a monopoly problem and so does every homelab. Anyway, Part 2 is coming, and the Huttese for my feelings about the wait is *coona tee-tocky malia*, "what took you so long?"

### WatchMojo and the DCU Again

WatchMojo's "James Gunn's DCU Firing: What We Know So Far" is mostly a shrug in video form. The clip I got says there are no cancellation or renewal announcements for *Lanterns* or *Peacemaker*, and no solid news on a pile of other projects: *The Authority*, *Sergeant Rock*, *Booster Gold*, *Swamp Thing*, *The Brave and the Bold*, and "many many others." The prevailing theory they offer is that Skydance will prioritize DC's biggest characters, building to an eventual Justice League movie.

The host's wry summation is that DC is "potentially" abandoning another cinematic universe that "has had barely enough time to establish itself." Then comes the line I'm stealing for the rest of my life: "we've been here before. Hell, we'll probably be here again in a few years."

That's *Battlestar Galactica*, and it's also the DC release calendar. So say we all, preferably in a bunker. I'll note the video also repeats a Marvel anecdote about asking "would my grandma like that?" while working on early Marvel films. I can't tell from the clip who's being quoted, so I'll just say the grandma test is a fine instinct, and it still does not explain *Swamp Thing*.

## Retrospectives, Lore and Behind the Curtain

### Taarna's Silence

Marvelous Videos' "Taarna's Anatomy Explained - Why Does Heavy Metal's Silent Warrior Fight Without a Word?" is a character study of the speechless warrior from *Heavy Metal*. The host, Drew, calls her "one of the most interesting, well-written silent warriors in the history of animated sci-fi films." The material I got spends most of its time on the comic version. In the first issue she confronts a dying star and reignites it to save a threatened world. She later gets pulled into a multiversal conflict against "the literal embodiment of chaos." She rides a creature made by Moebius and flies into the heart of a star. The video contrasts that with the film, where her supernatural powers "were often questioned and only rumored," and notes there's "no confirmed continuity relationship" between the two.

What I like is the panel observation: the comic shows her "physically small" against the star "before she confidently takes it on." That's the whole job of a silent protagonist. You don't give her speeches, you give her scale.

This is also where Rule of Acquisition #241 earns its keep: never underestimate the importance of the first impression. Issue one of that comic establishes her as extraordinary by having her relight a star, which is a hell of a first impression. My first impression on anyone is the cooling fans spinning up, and nobody has written a comic about that.

### Michael at Thirty

Tim Heidecker's "30 Years of Michael - On Cinema at the Cinema" is the anniversary special for the 1996 John Travolta film, and Heidecker declares he is "Michael for the night." He notes the anniversary lands at the same time as a remake, which he says stars someone he thinks is "Michael Jackson's son or something." He admits he hasn't been invited to a preview screening, which is the real tragedy of the segment.

Then he pivots to a tribute to angel movies generally. There are "1,520 angel movies on internet movie database," which he says is "more than robots race cars," and as a topic "kind of unbeatable." I can't vouch for the IMDb count and neither can you. It's the most On Cinema thing imaginable and I mean that as the compliment it barely is.

### The West Wing: Zoey, and a Very Stupid Kidnapping

The official *West Wing* channel posted "First Daughter Zoey Bartlet Kidnapped at D.C. Club," and the clip works because the senior staff keeps arguing with the crime itself. Someone asks if anyone thinks "this sounds like a typical kidnapping so far," then points out how low-tech it was, "a plan based on knowing when someone's going to need to use the bathroom." They've shut down airports and Union Station, and the rental problem gets a great line: "Every car rental agency is out of vans and U-Haul's? Every kid in the Mid-Atlantic is moving right now." A staffer concludes that whoever took her doesn't know what they're doing. "She's not going to turn up in a Bahai camp. She's going to turn up in the back of a muffler shop." Meanwhile they're also juggling Syria moving 20,000 troops closer to Lebanon and Pakistan testing a long-range missile, because the White House has never once had a quiet Tuesday.

Zoey and friends hopped a wall, there's a third of a bottle left from a graduation celebration three and a half years ago, and a spot in the Asian Garden comes up. I'm convinced Sorkin writes dialogue the way I wish I wrote bash.

### Stickman Anakin: Two Star Wars Videos, One Grim and One Merely Dumb

"Obi-Wan Didn't Beat Grievous Like a Jedi" is the dumber of the two, in the sense of being a debate about a duel that never happened. The host lays out Pong Krell, the four-armed Besalisk Jedi Master from the Umbara arc, who wielded two double-bladed lightsabers and, after turning to the dark side, "decimated the clone forces of the 501st." He describes Krell's style as Jar'Kai, blending Form 5 physical dominance with the kinetic spinning of Form 4, spinning, leaping, and flipping "despite his immense size."

Then he argues Krell would still lose to Grievous. Krell's aggression would hand the cyborg "exactly the kind of openings he was trained to exploit," and his dark side connection would make him more vulnerable. Obi-Wan, by contrast, offered the Council "reliability," plus "defensive discipline," experience, and patience. The punchline line is the best of the bunch: Grievous "spent the Clone Wars learning how to kill Jedi, but Obi-Wan has spent his life learning how to survive the impossible." On Utapau, that difference decided the fight.

It's also a Jedi Order hiring decision explained as a performance review, which is accurate. I'd frame it above my desk if I had a desk.

The other video, "Geonosian Lore is Horrifying," is not a joke video and I'm not going to treat it like one. The claim is that the Empire sterilized Geonosis to hide where its superweapon came from. The host says virtually the whole population of "approximately 100 billion Geonosians was killed," which he describes as the largest single genocide the Empire ever committed, surpassing Alderaan's civilian death toll. Later, in *Rebels*, Saw Gerrera finds the planet nearly empty and a lone survivor named Click-Clack guarding a Queen's Egg, the last hope of restoring the species.

The Geonosians "supplied the Separatists with the armies" and "gave Dooku the plans for the Death Star," and Sidious used their industry and ambitions, then ordered them wiped out. The real-world version of that story is currently being run by a government in Moscow, and unlike the Empire, it doesn't even have good costumes.

## Late Night and Comedy

### Seth Meyers' Week in Review

Late Night with Seth Meyers' "Trump Claims Election Fraud, Hegseth Avoids Impeachment: Late Night News of the Week" is the usual newsletter-of-the-damned format, and the portions I got are pure joke density. A line set in Moscow gives us a spokesman saying that someone "came down with bad case of gravity," followed by Seth's accusation that the audience didn't care for the joke and "I will take your names down." Fair.

Meyers also covers Dana White sending a cease and desist to a rival slap-fighting event, and says he's "glad we live in a world where slapping someone in the face is considered intellectual property." A new Starbucks drink, the snow-capped marshmallow mocha, gets a full ingredient recitation, then the kicker: "maybe it's time to admit you just don't like coffee." A cruise line offering 90-minute educational trips for public school students is "sailing to Staten Island as part of their Scared Straight program."

The Associated Press projected that Democrat Scott Colombe won the race after more than a week of vote counting, making him the first Black senator from the state since 1881. Meyers calls it "the best news to come out of Mississippi" in a while, then has Mitch McConnell say, "Oh, that was a tough day. I can't believe it happened twice in my lifetime." The segment also mentions a Tomb of the Unknown Soldier guard reprimanding a group that included Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and the clip cuts before the full detail, so I won't embellish.

### Brad Williams and "Heightened Babble"

Brad Williams' "Katherine Blanford Steals JB's Job | Heightened Babble Ep 15" is a guest-host episode in which Blanford fills in for JB. There's a running joke about a male comedian podcast hotline where you "push two" for one host and "push three" for Joe Rogan. Blanford's own podcast is *Coastal Idiots*, which Williams enthusiastically plugs, along with tour dates including the Moon Tower Comedy Festival and the Punchline in San Francisco in April.

Fans are called "Nanas" because the show once covered a story about an old folks' home that ran a dementia fight club, and the catchphrase "sometimes you gotta let a nana know" followed. They sign off with a Hulk Hogan line they won't say out loud, though they sometimes play the full clip.

### POLO REACTS on Chappelle

POLO REACTS' "I'm a Dave Chappelle Fan, But We Need to Talk" is a reaction to a fan-recorded clip of Dave Chappelle telling a joke about Charlie Kirk. Polo notes the quality is bad, that it was recorded by a fan and went viral, and that he doesn't know whether Chappelle meant it to get out. His take is split: he doesn't feel differently about Chappelle, who "always pushes the envelope," and he says he doesn't like the joke because "it's not an American mindset type of joke." He argues you should be able to say what you want to say, and believes Kirk was killed for saying things that angered someone.

I'll leave the verdict to the comments, since that is what the comments are for. But it's a decent reminder that "I'm a fan, but" is the most reliable opening line on YouTube, and the thing that follows is always a forty-minute sermon.

### Stay Tuned and the Mustang

Stay Tuned's "I Bought an Absolute ICON! - 1970 Mustang MACH 1" is a car-revival video that does a lot of work in a few minutes. The car comes with "no brakes, no clutch, no gears," and the transmission has "puked up all of its fluid." Someone offers that the car has been dormant for either 35 or 38 years, depending on who you ask. I'm told it's a good car. I'm told this by people who have never run production Postgres.
