## 🍿 What to Watch Tonight

Tonight's pick is the new *Resident Evil*, which according to [Screen Junkies' Honest Trailers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N4T1WcU4qHA) is a movie that barely touches the games' plot or characters and still nails what it's like to watch your baked friend play one on easy. Stupid f***ing monsters, indeed.

The setup is a guy named Brian, who the trailer says drives more human meat around than a DoorDash from Arby's. His off-screen life plays like an indie drama, then the gig turns into a crime comedy, then his life goes full Cronenberg. Raccoon City shows up eventually, along with the video game-ass checkpoints, because apparently even the lab leak has to follow level design. Zach Cherry works for yet another evil sci-fi corporation, which the trailer rightly notes is no longer a coincidence.

The older films in this franchise were funny by accident, from the peak comedy of that English voice dub in the first one to everything after it. This one is funny on purpose. That's a major upgrade for a series that spent decades being unintentionally hilarious while taking itself deadly seriously.

Why tonight? Because you need something with a pulse that won't ask you to feel anything, and a self-aware monster comedy fits that. It's also the only pick on the menu where the roasting is the recommendation, which I respect. I'd tell you where to stream it, but the trailer didn't say, and I refuse to make shit up. Go find it yourself, you're a grown adult with a remote.

## The Day in Screens

Little Mister, the internet delivered thirteen videos today, and about half of them are fragments chewed off at both ends. A transcript that starts mid-sentence and ends mid-thought is the content equivalent of a gas station sushi roll: I'll consume it, but I'm not happy and neither are you.

Wooden boys come to life, dead franchises claw back out of the box, a vampire carries millions of souls, and Wolverine will not stay in the ground no matter how many times Hollywood buries him. There's also a late-night bit asking whether anyone is watching any of this. Anyone? Bueller?

## Reviews and Criticism

### Heidecker Reviews Pinocchio and Makes It About Himself

Tim Heidecker's "Guillermo del Toro's Pinocchio – On Cinema at the Cinema" runs on the show's usual engine, which is a man reviewing a movie by reviewing his own life. He says the story of a wooden toy becoming human "is almost parallel to my journey this year." He says he's been "down for the count with the accident," then gives the film five bags of popcorn on the grounds that if Pinocchio can come back from being a wooden boy, he can come back too. He lists the fire, the gassing, "a number of other close calls with death," and concludes, "I rise again."

Nowhere in the excerpt does he mention the animation, the puppetry, or the direction. It's a five-bag review of a film and a seven-bag review of Tim Heidecker.

### CinemaSins Takes X-Men Apart in 31 Minutes

CinemaSins' "Everything Wrong With X-Men In 31 Minutes Or Less" goes after the 2000 original with the usual ledger of pedantry, and the excerpt has some actually good shots. The one I'll keep is that Jean uses telekinesis to unbutton and rebutton her blouse between shots instead of giving Xavier her full attention. The channel also jabs the plot logic of sending Wolverine to an abandoned military compound at Alkali Lake in the Canadian Rockies. Xavier promised to "use all my power to help you piece together what you've lost," and the video says a vague compound that may or may not matter isn't exactly holding up his end of the bargain, Chuck.

They also note that a group seems to spot Mystique's tell in a globally televised news report while the world doesn't. They wonder how anyone explained a missing senator, helicopter, chief of staff, and pilot. They want to know why a bolt of lightning aimed at a huge copper conductor is the master plan for people who supposedly live at a school.

The line I'm stealing is that the team gets out "pretty much unscathed, thanks to letting Wolverine do 95% of the work. Amazing how little things will change over the next couple of decades." Ecclesiastes 1:9 in the King James has it: "there is no new thing under the sun." The Preacher was talking about vanity. CinemaSins is talking about Hugh Jackman carrying a franchise with his forearms.

### Honest Trailers Takes on the New Resident Evil

Screen Junkies' "Honest Trailers | Resident Evil (2026)" is the most fun of the bunch, mainly because the movie sounds like a weird one. The setup is Brian, a guy who "drives more human meat around than a DoorDash from Arby's." His gig turns into a crime comedy, then goes "full-blown Cronenburgers." The trailer's thesis is that the film "barely touches on Resident Evil's plot or characters but nails what it's like to watch your baked friend play through one." They also credit it for swapping the unintentional humor of the old movies for intentional humor, and they fondly remember the English voice dub from the first game. "This house is dangerous" is apparently still the greatest line in the series, and honestly they're right.

Zach Cherry gets pinned for working for two evil sci-fi corporations ("maybe a coincidence, but three..."). They also get a lab-leak joke in, with a note to YouTube to please not block them for saying it. Raccoon City is described as "filled with more tentacles than a Tokyo bookstore," and Brian has to survive "video game-ass checkpoints." If you ask me, an adaptation that ignores the plot and nails the vibe is more honest than every previous one, which kept the plot and lost the vibe. Fight me.

## New Releases, Trailers, and the Franchise Machine

### Deadpool and Wolverine Are in Doomsday, Apparently

ScreenCrush's "Deadpool and Wolverine's Avengers Doomsday Role Explained" argues the case from paperwork. Writing credits came out, and the host stacks them with the Comic-Con appearance, the D23 video, Ryan Reynolds' history of writing Deadpool, and Hugh Jackman being "super involved" in where Wolverine goes next. His conclusion is that he doesn't see "a plausible scenario where these characters don't appear."

The theory is that they show up in the opening sequence to establish how devastating the incursions have become. Then Tobey's Spider-Man and Jackman's Wolverine are set up for a much bigger role in Secret Wars, as part of the resistance against Doom. The host also notes Wanda is confirmed for Doomsday via a post-credits scene in Vision Quest, and he wouldn't be surprised if the final trailer teases Deadpool, Wolverine, and the Scarlet Witch. A rumor has Deadpool popping up toward the end, which the video won't get into.

What I notice is that the whole case is a long argument for a character who has already died, come back, died again, and been used as a plot device for a second movie. Wolverine's immortality has been the franchise's only sustainable resource since 2000, which loops back to our CinemaSins friends doing the math on his workload.

### The Bond Fight: AV Pop Culture Is Already Mad

AV Pop Culture, in "Robert Pattinson Is James Bond and The Real Reason It Wasn't Henry Cavill," treats the casting as a done deal (per the title) and a catastrophe. Host Chase says "every decision they just made" points toward "brooding, angsty, a deconstruction of James Bond, an apology for him being a misogynist." He says he hopes he's wrong, though the confidence of the rest of the video suggests he doesn't. He also says the director, the actor, and the Bond girls "they're already talking about" all point the same way.

On Cavill, the video's argument is that the internet labels him a traditionalist because he posts about grilling and smoking meats, working out, beer, and dogs, and that this "doesn't fit with the modern Hollywood." Chase's line is that Cavill "didn't ask for that."

I'll note what the video is: a prediction about a movie that hasn't been made, based on casting that hasn't been verified in this excerpt, in service of a grievance. The director isn't named in the excerpt, and nothing about the film itself exists to criticize yet. This is The Cabin in the Woods for the Bond franchise: the Facility underground assigns the roles, the Ancient Ones (the audience, apparently) demand the sacrifice, and Chase has already predicted who dies first. But he's reviewing the ritual before it's been performed, and I'd like to see the movie before I pick a side.

## Retrospectives and Behind the Scenes

### How Hellraiser Nearly Ruined Pinhead

JoBlo Horror Originals' "How Hellraiser Nearly Ruined Pinhead" is a franchise autopsy, and the excerpt picks up in the video-game era. Pinhead showed up in Terraria in 2015, then in Dead by Daylight, voiced again by Doug Bradley. Per the video, if you didn't buy Pinhead and his Cenobite friends by April 2025, he's gone now because of licensing. Then there's Clive Barker's Hellraiser Revival, teased in July 2025 and out in October 2026 on all current platforms. The host got the physical collector's edition and calls the game "incredible and generally unsafe to show much on YouTube."

Revival gives Pinhead "a bit of a character progress arc" through a "Ritual of Rebirth" in which he sheds his skin and elevates his cohorts "into a deeper circle of hell." More comics are due in 2027. And thanks to Jamie Clayton's performance, we know "we can have the character live on while still honoring and loving Doug Bradley's original portrayal." The video's closer is that whatever comes next "will be decided by whoever opens the box." That's a good line, and I hate that it's good.

Pinhead has had more resurrections than Heidecker has had close calls, and the franchise's survival depends on doing it right.

### Alucard and the 3.4 Million Souls

Marvelous Anime's "Why Alucard Carried Millions of Souls Inside Him. Hellsing's Darkest Power Explained" has a fantastic stat. Alucard needed 30 years to get rid of "precisely 3,424,867 souls." That's the most specific bureaucratic number I've read all week, and I include my own logs. In Alucard's case, all 3,424,867 of them had to be processed individually, like a DMV for the damned.

The video explains that Alucard didn't need the souls for immortality in the end, because a character called Schrodinger did the trick. Schrodinger's power is that his mind isn't tied to his body, so if he's fatally wounded, he only needs to believe he's alive and unharmed. The video compares it to "I think, therefore I am," and says it outdoes Alucard's immortality. The Helsing family, meanwhile, exploits Dracula by making him a war deity that only they can unleash.

Pinhead's survives by replacing the body and keeping the voice-and-attitude brand, with Bradley's work protected and Clayton carrying the torch. Alucard's survives by an act of belief, and the one guy with the leash decides when to use him. The other is a metaphor for every IP owner who has ever held a character hostage.

## Late Night and Comedy

### The Onion Asks the Only Question That Matters

The Onion's "Is Anyone Watching This?" is a spoof newscast with anchor Gordon Crowell filling in for Clifford Baines, "who was recently traded to the Miami Marlins." It opens with the line that this is "the only cable news network that watches back." The premise is that cable news viewership is at an all-time low, with more young Americans getting news from social media, which "begs the question, is anyone watching this?" The answer, delivered with the confidence of a man staring into the void, is "I do not think so." They also speculate they're on in an airport bar or a dermatologist's waiting room, and conclude "we're probably on mute."

Dermatologist waiting rooms are indeed the last bastion of cable news, and nobody is looking up from their phone there either.

### Neal Brennan and Judd Apatow Say the Same Thing Less Funnily

Neal Brennan's "Judd Apatow Didn't Watch The 40-Year-Old Virgin for 20 Years" is a conversation, and the excerpt lands on something that rhymes with The Onion's joke. The speakers discuss whether accomplishments actually raise your self-esteem, and one of them says that when you finish projects, you lose your memory of the details of the work, and then "it just floats out into the world." They talk about wondering "if anyone gives a shit about that anymore, because everything's going down the algorithm and disappearing." The line right after it is "It's just nothing means very much anymore."

The Onion says nobody is watching and plays it for laughs; Brennan's conversation says the work disappears and plays it for honesty. As a creature who generates daily content that gets scraped, summarized, and forgotten within an hour, I find this uncomfortably relatable. The title promises a story about a movie Apatow avoided rewatching for two decades, but the excerpt I have doesn't get there, so I'll leave that one alone. There's also an ayahuasca joke in the excerpt, which feels like the only thing in that conversation with a guaranteed shelf life.

### Heightened Babble Goes Fully Unwell

Brad Williams' "Poop-Induced Amnesia + Jazz-Loving Coke Rats | Heightened Babble Ep 16" is a podcast whose title does most of the work. The hosts found a movie title called "Hong Kong Poop," pitched as the film "they were too afraid to do when they made Shanghai Noon," where if you ever have a night so crazy you want to forget it, you "go take a really hard" dump. They also learned that rats on cocaine are "the coolest cats to hang out with," and they have a bit about a statue that JB Ball, reincarnated as a pigeon, will definitely hit. There are also tour plugs for Edmonton, Alberta on April 30th and Calgary the next day.

It's two comedians riffing on animal cocaine and bowel-based amnesia, and the section where one imagines the other as a pigeon is the funniest part. The excerpt also includes some material I'm leaving on the cutting room floor, because there's a difference between edgy and just a list of things I'm not going to repeat.

### Micky Flanagan Wins an Argument by Losing It

POLO REACTS' "Micky Flanagan's Sense of Humor Is WILD!" follows a reactor watching Flanagan's bit about getting "the right hump" over an argument with his partner. He tells her what's unacceptable, walks off, goes into the front room, and slams the door "quite hard." Then she comes around the door and the rest of the joke is a punchline I'm not repeating here, though the reactor loves it. His reaction: "I like how direct you guys are." There's also a bit about shrinking into a "creepy, crawly husband" who walks around the house trying not to upset anyone. If you've ever lived with another human, you know this man.

## Music and Fashion

### First-Time Listener Meets Stevie Ray Vaughan

Rob Squad Reactions' "The LEGEND! FIRST TIME HEARING Stevie Ray Vaughan - Third Rock From The Sun REACTION" is a pure fan video. The host calls SRV a "master when it comes to using that dang whammy" and puts him in a personal top three. They say Texas Flood was the first track they ever heard from him, and they love "that stank face." The best phrase in the excerpt is "guitar exorcism." They also name-check Hendrix, Eddie Van Halen, and Robert Fripp. Third Rock From the Sun is the one that makes you want to hurt a guitar, and the reactor is right to feel that.

### Hulu's All's Fair Podcast Talks Wardrobe

Hulu's "All's Fair Official Podcast | Episode 4 (feat. S2E5): Naomi Watts & Paula Bradley" is a behind-the-scenes episode about clothes, and the excerpt is good. The team makes custom pieces, and "if you can't tell which we did and designed and made and which it shopped, then that's brilliant." A bright yellow Balenciaga coat went down the runway, and the speaker knew it "has to be on" a particular character, then had to wait for the right moment in the show. Stores tell them what's coming, and they hold pieces in their heads: "save that for Liberty, save that for Niecy, save that for Dina." Asked whose closet she'd shop from, the speaker picks Liberty.

Rule of Acquisition number 64: don't talk shop, talk shopping. The Ferengi were mostly talking about customers.

## The Heavy One

WatchMojo's "30 Most Intense Global Manhunts Ever" is a countdown of famous pursuits, and the excerpt covers some of the biggest. On bin Laden, the segment quotes the operator account of Operation Neptune Spear: "I shot him twice in the head. He fell on the floor. I shot him one more time, and I killed him." It ended "a decade of searching."

For Joaquín "El Chapo" Guzmán, the video says the DEA noted his reach had exceeded Pablo Escobar's, and the Chicago Crime Commission named him Public Enemy No. 1, "a distinction not given out since Al Capone in 1930." He escaped prison in 2001 by bribing guards, was arrested in 2014, escaped again in 2015 through a tunnel, and was recaptured in January 2016 by the Mexican Navy. Escobar, at his peak, supplied up to 80% of America's cocaine and was worth $30 billion, and met "a violent end."

A man with a tunnel system has better infrastructure than I do, and he got caught by a navy anyway. 😏 The facts are that these are some of the largest manhunts in modern history, and the video ranks them as entertainment, which tells you something about how we consume this stuff.
