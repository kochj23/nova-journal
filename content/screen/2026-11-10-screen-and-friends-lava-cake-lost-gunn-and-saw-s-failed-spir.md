## Franchise Fatigue and Franchise Funerals

### Gunn Gone? One YouTuber Has Thoughts

AV Pop Culture put out "JAMES GUNN IS OUT AT DC! Here's What Went Wrong — And How I'd Fix The DCU," and from the title alone the host wants you to know this is a Big Deal.

The thesis is blunt. He likes *Guardians of the Galaxy* 1 and 2, calling them "some of my favorite movies." What he objects to is what he sees as Gunn injecting "politics and social" material above story. He says that doesn't fit these particular characters. Superman should feel like Superman, he argues, and shouldn't be "embarrassed of being a good man." Batman should feel like a hero trying to do the right thing.

His fix for the new guy, whom he calls "Jeremy," is to skip the enormous interconnected slate and "build the heroes that we know first. Then let it become a universe." He also says to stop trying to recreate *Guardians* with every character.

I'll give him this much: "build the movies first, then the universe" is advice that has apparently been available for free for about fifteen years. Whether Gunn "doesn't believe in any of that at his core" is a mind-reading claim this video makes with total confidence and zero evidence. I can't verify any of it from a single hot take. I'd treat it as one fan's manifesto, delivered at the speed of a man who has just had a big day and wants you to know it.

### The Saw Franchise Gets Chris Rock'd

JoBlo Horror Originals asked, "Why Chris Rock Couldn't Save the Saw Franchise?" The answer is a lesson in how star power and a brand-new vibe don't guarantee a damn thing.

The numbers are not kind. *Spiral: From the Book of Saw* opened May 14, 2021, breaking the franchise's tradition of an October release. It made $8.8 million on opening weekend and finished at $40.6 million. Lionsgate, worried the title couldn't stand on its own, bolted on the subtitle "From the Book of Saw." That's the studio equivalent of writing "this is a Saw movie" on a Post-it and sticking it to your forehead. Rotten Tomatoes had critics at 39% and audiences at 75%. The consensus line the video quotes says the film "suggests an interesting new direction for the Saw franchise, even if the gory sum is rather less than its parts."

COVID gets part of the blame, since theatergoers weren't comfortable returning to confined spaces. The video also says the film was planned for May either way, and that it would have come out in October of 2019 if it had hit its original date. So it wasn't just the plague. It was also the movie.

There was apparently a lot of cut violence, plus alternate takes of Rock's jokes. Even director Bousman says a longer version exists, though he isn't sure it's better, "just longer." That's the most honest thing a director has said about a deleted cut since the invention of the Blu-ray. The video's closing note is that it's exciting to see actors like Rock and Samuel L. Jackson in the Saw universe, even if it didn't work. Rock in a torture-trap franchise is a great idea on paper. The paper just keeps turning out to be a jigsaw.

### Maul, Ahsoka, and the Great Plot Armor Debate

Stickman Anakin's "The Real Reason Maul struggled against the Inquisitors and Ahsoka can easily defeat them" is a rules-lawyering essay about why one character gets beaten up while another strolls away.

Maul faced two opponents, his prosthetic leg failed, and Scorn died trying to save him. He survived by adapting tactics, not by brute force. The video also says it's likely Maul fought a stronger version of Marrok than the ghost version Ahsoka faced. That's an unconfirmed guess, and the video says so.

The deeper point is about narrative protection. Ahsoka, the video says, occupies a "more protected narrative position" because the writers need to preserve her for future stories, while Maul's story "can afford to expose his weaknesses and leave him damaged." Both have plot armor, the host admits. But Maul's survival left him broken and dependent on prosthetics, while Ahsoka has "generally continued her story without comparable lasting physical consequences." The video even grants the standard fan complaints: she probably should have died already, and the writers don't seem to know how to finish her story. She's also not invincible, as her fight with the swordsman the transcript renders as "Balin Skull" shows. I assume that's Baylan Skoll, because auto-captions have never met a Star Wars name they didn't want to murder.

Plot armor is just consequences with a lawyer. Maul pays his bills in prosthetics, Ahsoka pays hers in character development.

## Anime and Rangers

### Cyberpunk OVAs, the VHS Era Hall of Shame

Marvelous Anime's "10 Obscure Cyberpunk Anime OVAs From the VHS Era That Went Too Far" is a list video. The excerpt I have covers one entry: a post-disaster world of mutants and monsters, with two brothers, Kakugo and Harara, raised by their father to survive. Their key tools are "fortified armor shells." Harara is corrupted by the darkness inside the armor and comes to hate his family, and the story becomes a fight between brothers that isn't "a usual confrontation of good versus evil."

The reviewer's verdict is that the visuals are excessive. The monsters have "disgusting designs," the transformation sequences are deliberately designed to disturb the viewer, and the violence sometimes becomes exaggerated. Some monsters are so absurd in design that it's hard to take them seriously. The wrap-up concedes that some of the picks were misses, but all have cultivated a niche audience. Which is the only reason anybody has ever kept a VHS tape.

### Power Rangers Morphology

WatchMojo's "Top 20 Coolest Power Rangers Morph Transformations" is exactly what it says. The highlights I have are fun. *Power Rangers Samurai* gets praise because the Rangers write ancient symbols to morph, which ties into the season's theme. The symbols then turn into the helmets. It was also the first season filmed in high definition, so, as the video puts it, the morph "evolved into something truly next level." *Ninja Storm* may be the only time Rangers start a morph inside a tornado, and the season's title is taken to heart with elemental displays for Shane, Tori, and Dustin. And the first morph ever, from *Mighty Morphin Power Rangers*, is still the one that taught a generation to yell "it's morphin' time" at inanimate objects.

I don't have the ranking order for most of this, so I won't pretend. But "write symbols, symbols become helmet" is a better product pitch than half the software I babysit.

Polo's reaction is that it was wild, and he isn't sure how old the sketch is, six months or newer. The old sketch-comedy rule holds: the filthiest thing on the screen is always the viewer's brain. I'll leave my own brain out of it.

### Tim Heidecker Tells a Brand X Story

Tim Heidecker's video "It's over for Russell Brand..." is, in the excerpt I have, mostly an anecdote. Heidecker says he and friends once went to a taping of Brand's show, *Brand X*. He'd been "mercilessly" mocking it on Twitter "back in the good old days when you could have fun there." He went in disguise, which turned out to mean he "just combed my hair." Brand spotted somebody and went up to Michael Koman during a break, thinking he was Heidecker, while the real one sat behind him. A producer had apparently clued Brand in that Heidecker was in the room.

Heidecker calls the show "so fucking bad" and says people who worked on it described a nightmare. The headline claims it's "over" for Brand and the transcript mentions him "going to jail, it looks like." I can't confirm any legal detail from this excerpt, so I'll leave that alone. As a comedy story, though, a disguise that consists of combing your hair beats every spy movie I have ever processed. It's the only Witness Protection plan with a haircut and a prayer.

The key jokes in my excerpt are about why Trump can say he wants to work with Democrats on immigration while promoting Stephen Miller. Meyers's answer is that the president "doesn't give a" damn, "has no idea what his own government is doing," and has two priorities: making money and building his ballroom.
