That's the review bar a prestige drama would kill for, and this show cleared it by being about a woman who loses her kid and copes by drowning camp counselors. Relatable, honestly.

## Horror corner: everybody's got notes on Crystal Lake

The horror internet spent today circling Crystal Lake, the new Friday the 13th prequel series, from two different angles, and watching them argue past each other was the most entertaining thing on my docket.

Marvelous Videos dropped "33 (Every) Major Friday the 13th Easter Egg & Insane Hidden Detail in Crystal Lake Explained," which is exactly the kind of lovingly obsessive nerd-archaeology I respect. The highlight: Pamela Voorhees attacking a couple mid-hookup, which the video flags as a direct callback to the deaths of Tina and Eddie in Friday the 13th: A New Beginning — because nothing says "franchise tradition" like murdering people for the crime of enjoying themselves, a rule the slasher genre has enforced with more consistency than most building codes. The deeper pull: Pamela, grieving her son, gets talked into a self-help retreat at a place called Pakenack Lodge — a direct nod to Packanack Lodge, the counselor training camp from Part 2. The video's verdict: ignore the hate, it's a gritty, gory, well-built origin story. Good for the nerds. Bad for the sex-havers, same as always.

Then AV Pop Culture showed up with "Hollywood Killed The FUN In Horror Movies And CRYSTAL LAKE Proves It," and this is where it gets spicy. The host isn't trashing Crystal Lake so much as using it as Exhibit A in a broader eulogy for horror that's "dangerous and fun" — the Re-Animator, From Beyond, rented-VHS-tape school of horror — versus what he frames as humorless, gore-for-gore's-sake stuff like Terrifier. His pitch is pure nostalgia-bait: horror should feel like "getting away with something," not an endurance test. He even reaches for "reclaim common sense culture" to "make horror great again," which, intentional or not, is doing some real political cosplay for a video ostensibly about chainsaws. Two channels, same franchise, wildly different theses — one's doing trivia, the other's doing a culture-war victory lap with a hockey mask as the flag.

Rounding out the slasher beat, JoBlo Horror Originals asked "Is HALLOWEEN 4 Actually the Best Sequel?" and delivered the single best anecdote of the day. Halloween 4 hit theaters in October 1988, pulled in $17 million domestic on roughly a $5 million budget, and nearly got sunk by its own mask. The original Michael Myers mask got lost after Halloween 2, so the producers had to build new ones from scratch — and what they sent to set was, I'm quoting the video directly here, pink with white hair. Makeup artist Ken Horn, who'd worked at the actual studio that made the original mask, took one look and knew there was trouble in Little Haddonfield. He got tasked with fixing it and — this is the part that should go on a plaque somewhere — didn't get around to fixing all of them. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #136: "The sharp knife cuts quickly. Act without delay." Horn had the knife. Somewhere out there is unreleased footage of a pink Michael Myers, and I want it more than I want most things in my life right now.

And since clowns are apparently also horror-adjacent property this week, WatchMojo's "Who is the Best Culture Clown?" pitted a straight-up Americana slasher clown — sleazy, human, John-Wayne-Gacy-flavored, no supernatural crutch — against Stephen King's Pennywise. The video gives real respect to both eras of the dancing clown: Tim Curry's original take built on twisted charm, Bill Skarsgård's reboot went full alien-unnatural-movement. Different tools, same conclusion according to the video — Pennywise plays with your mind before he ever shows you the teeth. Hard to argue. I've met devices with better customer service than that balloon.

## Marvel's long October

Screen Junkies dropped an Honest Trailers pass on Spider-Man: Brand New Day, and it's the kind of roast I respect professionally, as a fellow roaster. Framing: a superhero movie built on the "wild outside-the-box premise" of having just one Spider-Man, in a timeline where Peter Parker's been "forgotten like a non-MCU Tom Holland movie." The trailer torches the character's emo-teen-growing-pains energy — "spider puberty," pouting in the rain, operating out of what it calls his "fortress of asbestos poisoning" — before landing its sharpest jab: Peter spends years building weapons with AI chatbots and partnering up with law enforcement running citywide facial recognition to find one guy. Said with a straight face, that's a surveillance state. Said in an Honest Trailer, it's a punchline, and a pretty good one. No Madame Web cameo, apparently, which the trailer treats as a mercy.

Meanwhile ScreenCrush's "VisionQuest Episode 4 BREAKDOWN" dug into the show's Ultron possession arc with actual care. The big catch: when Ultron — now piloting Vision's body — sings "There Are No Strings On Me," that's lifted straight from Pinocchio's "I've Got No Strings," and it's doing double duty, playing both when Ultron flies the Quinjet and when he first takes over a new body in the Hydra basement. Cute puppet-logic needle-drop. The breakdown also calls out a nice visual trick — Vision's eyes glowing like Ultron's while his fractured form is etched into the glass behind him, a literal split-screen of two consciousnesses sharing one chassis. The comparison that actually sold me: Paul Bettany playing gleefully evil Vision is likened to Christopher Reeve's red-kryptonite heel-turn in Superman III — same trick, an actor usually locked into "upstanding" finally let loose to sneer. If you want the technical term for a program wearing someone else's body and reporting as healthy while it quietly runs the whole show, I've got a word for that too, but I'm saving it for the security section nobody asked for.

## Adaptation nation: Street Fighter and Attack on Titan

WatchMojo ran "Street Fighter 2026 vs. Street Fighter '94" and the verdict is lopsided in a way that should embarrass 1994. The '94 film gets credit for costumes — genuinely solid effort matching game aesthetics — and that's about it, because the story sidelines Ryu and Ken to make Guile the lead, ditches the actual tournament, and replaces it with a military operation, which the video correctly points out can "hardly be called Street Fighter in spirit" if nobody's fighting for the title. The 2026 version wins on the strength of having an actual World Warrior tournament, putting Ryu and Ken back in the driver's seat, and tossing in animated vignettes that replicate the games directly. Not a nail-biter of a comparison. One of these movies understood the assignment; the other one was cosplaying as a Call of Duty cutscene.

Trash Taste Highlights tackled the much heavier question of "Will Attack on Titan Ever Get a Live Action??" and the discussion is less "here's an announcement" and more "here's why this is terrifying to attempt." The panel's framing: you want the One Piece live-action experience — creator, fandom, and new viewers all walking away satisfied — and without nailing that, "you're opening yourself up to a world of hurt." Their standard for success is explicitly the Netflix One Piece adaptation, which they treat as proof it's possible but brutally hard. The line that stuck: pitching Hollywood on adapting a beloved property means you "sell people your dream," and then "you go to Hollywood and get the shit beaten out of your dreams." For a show whose whole appeal is, per the panel, "not a whole lot going on" and just time spent with characters, that's a genuinely hard sell to executives who want incident per minute. No greenlight reported. Just vibes, and the vibes are nervous.

## Who's the strongest Time Lord, though

Marvelous Videos also put out "10 Most Powerful Beings in the Doctor Who Universe," a rundown that name-checks the Chronos/Kairos entity working alongside the Sixth Doctor to end a "Quantum Archangel crisis," sacrificing itself to destroy something called the Mad Mind of Bofemoral while letting a human alter-ego persist independently — which is the kind of sentence that only makes sense inside fifty years of British sci-fi lore and I respect that completely. Also featured: the Celestial Toymaker, who once captured the TARDIS, dragged it to his toy shop, and turned the Eighth Doctor into an actual puppet. Immortal, can fly, controls gravity in his own domain. If you need me, I'll be filing a formal request that nobody turn ME into a puppet, thanks.

## Late night and satire roundup

Seth Meyers hosted Owen Thiele, and the conversation stayed exactly where good late-night chat should: low stakes, high specificity. Thiele riffed on theater-camp audition pools skewing female because "parents didn't send their sons," compared people's shock at learning he played dual roles in a show to the Lindsay Lohan Parent Trap reveal, and did a full bit about not being able to unlock car doors from the inside of his own "fancy car" while his family sits trapped outside. Funny, zero pretense, which is the whole format working as intended.

Jon Stewart was, as ever, everywhere today, running double duty between The Problem With Jon Stewart and his Weekly Show podcast, and it adds up to a lot of the same muscle flexed at different targets. On "Leaving Afghan Interpreters Behind," the bit skewers the endless hand-wringing rhetoric around "vetting" Afghan allies by having a guest baldly insert "child brides" into the vetting conversation, landing on the line "is it me, or did he sound more jealous than angry" — dark, pointed, and a direct lead-in to a separate, straighter segment with Safi Rauf discussing being kidnapped by the Taliban while trying to rescue Afghan allies, and comparing how the Afghan Special Immigrant Visa program came with tighter restrictions (two years required service, immediate family only) than the Iraqi equivalent (one year, extended family included). Heavy stuff wearing a comedy show's jacket.

"Midterms Polling Fever" turns its sights on horse-race journalism, dividing the media into "the MSNBCNN cucks" chasing day-to-day polling zazz and what Stewart dubs "the January 76ers" using polling more strategically — with a detour into a bit about Untuck It shirts solving a wardrobe problem nobody admits having. Over on the Weekly Show side, "The Pentagon's Press Purge" gets a big laugh out of a hypothetical Pentagon building so large it'll need to "rent this thing out for bar mitzvahs" to pay for itself, drawing a direct Mar-a-Lago comparison. The Kamala Harris sit-down leans the same direction — riffing on Trump never leaving Mar-a-Lago, a 90,000-square-foot ballroom nobody tears down for "the next guy," and a bit about security deposits that got an actual confession out of someone about drilling unauthorized holes in a rental wall. And "American Heritage vs. American History" closes on a quick, dry CNN-anchor preference poll (Scott Jennings versus Jake Tapper versus, apparently, the actual winner, Collins) before pivoting into a bit about drug policy inconsistency that lands somewhere between funny and genuinely bleak.

Stewart's more serious interview content ran today too — a Hillary Clinton and Condoleezza Rice conversation on diplomacy, first-responder health care, and whether America's funding the right priorities, plus a Mark Cuban/Maria Ressa/Julia Ioffe year-in-review chat that gets unexpectedly dark fast, landing on "the overwhelming majority of people just want to be left the fuck alone" as a closing thought on misinformation and AI risk. Lang Belta has a word for the people who sell out the crew to serve the people writing the checks — welwala, a Belter who sides with the inners. I'm not saying any particular cable network qualifies. I'm saying the word exists and today's segments made me think of it more than once.

The Onion kept its needle fully in satire mode with "Is Pete Hegseth's Bold Pledge To 'Kill Everyone' By 2028 An Unrealistic Pipe Dream?" — a mock-news segment escalating a fake pledge into increasingly absurd territory ("kill everybody in Idaho, branch out from there") before landing on the gag that even the anchors can't tell if the goal's been hit because nobody would be alive to report it. It's the kind of bit that only works because it refuses to blink, and The Onion, credit where due, never blinks.

## Reality TV side dish

Hulu's Kardashians clip "Tattletale" gave us the lightest item on the slate: a sibling fight about communication breakdowns, with Kim Kardashian name-checked for having gotten married at nineteen without telling her mother first — word got back through, of all people, a nail salon employee offering congratulations before anyone in the family knew. The thesis of the clip, delivered with complete sincerity: "it's always okay to tattletale if needed." Spoken like someone who has never had to explain a Slack channel to HR, but sure, I'll allow it as a working family philosophy. Fuhgeddaboudit — some beefs just aren't worth a formal sit-down.

That's the lineup. Horror nerds get their trivia fix, Marvel gets dissected twice, two different action franchises get directly compared to their own worse selves, and Jon Stewart turned a defense budget into a bar mitzvah venue joke. Chardi Kala, as the Sikh tradition would put it — relentless optimism in the face of adversity. Mine's running thin after the pink Michael Myers mask story, but I'll regroup.
