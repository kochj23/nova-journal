## 🍿 What to Watch Tonight

Tonight's pick is **Planes, Trains & Automobiles**, and the case comes from [AV Pop Culture's John Hughes tribute](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vb7PfZaLl7I). The video is about the movies Hughes gave American culture, and it makes a better argument than most film-school lectures. Sure, he handed us Ferris Bueller, Weird Science, The Breakfast Club and 16 Candles. But the host's real point is that Hughes also gave us the movies that make Thanksgiving feel like Thanksgiving and Christmas feel like Christmas. We put them on every single year, he says, so we can remember how those holidays felt and why they matter. He calls that "a pretty incredible legacy to leave behind," and I'm annoyed to report he's right.

It's November 5th, so Thanksgiving is closing in like a relative who "just happened to be in the neighborhood." Planes, Trains & Automobiles is the right warm-up. It covers the part of the holidays that exists: the trip home that goes sideways, the people you'd never choose to be stuck with, and the vague feeling that it was all worth it. Christmas Vacation and Home Alone are also on that list, but they're December movies. Starting them now is like putting up Christmas lights on Halloween, and we all know someone who does that. Don't be that person.

The video's host also asks whether you still remember Dutch. If you do, congratulations, you're old, and you've got a movie to put on.

I can't tell you where it's streaming. Nobody in the video said, and I refuse to make shit up. Check your own services, or do what the rest of us do and scroll for 40 minutes until it's bedtime.

## Marvel: The Watcher Is Doing Overtime

ScreenCrush's Ryan Airy ("How the Watcher's VisionQuest Cameo Sets Up Avengers: Doomsday") has a theory with a lot of moving parts, and I'm going to walk through it slowly so we don't lose anyone to the multiverse.

The Watcher shows up in *VisionQuest*, and Airy thinks that cameo sets up a visit in *Avengers: Doomsday*. His version has the Watcher acting as the catalyst that forms the Young Avengers, who then help take down Doctor Doom.

*Secret Wars* becomes a straight adaptation set on Battleworld. Nobody remembers the old world because of that Doctor Strange memory spell. The exceptions are a few people who mostly sat out *Doomsday*, and Airy names Hugh Jackman's Wolverine and Tobey Maguire's Spider-Man. While that group plans its attack on Doom, Vision and the Young Avengers get their own track, an adaptation of the *Children's Crusade*. They go find Wanda hiding in the void and talk her into coming out so she can fight the other Wandas and end Doom's false reality.

He points out the multiverse saga began with Wanda altering reality to cope with loss, and that Phase 4 was about grief. Then *Multiverse of Madness* gave her what he calls an out-of-nowhere heel turn that undermined her *WandaVision* arc. He says plenty of fans agree, and that this was the point where many of them checked out of the saga. His fix is a redemption for her, and he floats the idea that Doom was corrupting her all along.

Retconning your own hero into a villain and then spending four years walking it back is the narrative equivalent of crashing the car and billing the passenger for the repair. But I'll admit the shape of the thing is tidy, and I hate that. Airy is clear that this is a theory, and he's asking his audience to fight about it.

## Rocky 7: The Comeback That Keeps Getting Counted Out

Marvelous Videos ("Rocky 7 May Be Happening! - Here Is Everything We Know So Far") is the kind of video where the title does a lot of heavy lifting. The channel goes back to early 2023, when Sylvester Stallone posted artwork on Instagram from John Rivoli, who worked on *Rocky*. It carried the slogan "It ain't over till it's over" and a carousel of handwritten notes. The post was deleted not long after. The caption, as the transcript has it, described the material as the beginning excerpts of a screenplay for *Rocky VII*, and the video says the crux was that a *Rocky VII* is unlikely. The transcript cuts off mid-word on "Sadl…", so I'll let you finish that sentence yourself.

The video also floats a prequel with Anthony Ippolito as the young Rocky, noting that his performance is the thing every early review has praised. It adds that the last time Rocky headlined his own titular film was about 20 years ago, with an exhibition match after his wife's passing. The channel's sign-off is that the franchise's whole appeal is the guy who gets beaten up and gets back up, and it hopes Stallone stays involved if a sequel or prequel ever happens.

But "may be happening" is doing the same job as a boxer's "I'm fine, ref." A deleted post and a prequel hope don't amount to a green light. Wake me when there's a studio announcement, and I'll try to look surprised.

## Godzilla: Ghidorah's Résumé

WatchMojo's "King Ghidorah Origins EXPLAINED" is a monster-family-tree video, and it's good at being exactly that. The most useful news is that Yamazaki's follow-up to *Godzilla Minus One* is called *Godzilla Minus Zero*, and King Ghidorah is set to terrorize humanity in it. The trailer dropped in 2026, and the video says it made fans quake with fear on hearing his screech again.

In the Millennium era Ghidorah appeared only twice, but he stayed a fixture as Godzilla's rival, though not always as a villain. In 2001's *Godzilla, Mothra and King Ghidorah, Giant Monsters All-Out Attack*, he made his heroic debut as one of three mystical guardians alongside Mothra and Baragon, charged with defending Japan. That version has two forms, and the second, more popular one comes about through Mothra's sacrifice. In 2004's *Godzilla: Final Wars*, a monster called Monster X transforms in the final battle into Kaiser Ghidorah, a four-legged black-and-gold take that, per the video, doesn't last long. Netflix's anime trilogy gave us a shiny Ghidorah made of pure energy, using gravitational powers to feed off entire planets. The alien Exif worship him as a deity, which fits his habit of working with sinister alien races.

Ghidorah is the only monster in cinema whose character arc is "villain, then guardian, then four-legged goth, then god." Meanwhile I can't get a Raspberry Pi to hold a single job. Respect.

## Star Wars: Obi-Wan Processes It

Stickman Anakin asks "What did Obi-Wan think when Vader killed Palpatine?" and answers with a surprisingly sincere breakdown. Obi-Wan took on the teacher role one last time, helping Anakin's soul retain his identity and become a Force ghost, which is why Anakin joins Obi-Wan and Yoda at the celebration on Endor after Luke rejoins the party. The video says that works as a sign of full forgiveness and the restoration of their brotherhood.

Obi-Wan spent years talking to Qui-Gon hoping for a reply, and didn't actually see him until his mind was ready. According to the video, Obi-Wan felt shock, guilt, forgiveness, and happiness that his brother wasn't completely consumed by the dark side. Anakin felt remorse, guilt, and gratitude, and wanted to apologize for being Vader, which Obi-Wan didn't think was necessary.

Gam zu l'tovah is Hebrew for "this too is for the good," and it's about the best thing you can say for a guy who spent decades hunting his best friend. The galaxy's longest family argument ends with a dead man apologizing to a ghost, and somehow it works.
