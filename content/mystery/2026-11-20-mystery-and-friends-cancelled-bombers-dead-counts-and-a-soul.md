## 🕯️ The Rabbit Hole of the Day

Somewhere in the Soviet archives sits a titanium bomber that promised 2,000 mph and delivered a hell of a lot of paperwork. Dark Skies digs into Sukhoi's T-4 and its follow-up, the T-4MS, and the plane never even got the dignity of a proper crash.

Sukhoi had already failed to deliver the original T-4 bomber, so the T-4MS was the second swing. It was meant to carry the KH-45, a nuclear-capable anti-ship missile developed specifically for the T-4, weighing nearly 10,000 pounds, heavier than some ballistic missiles. The pitch was speeds over Mach 5 and a 370-mile range, which would have made it a bitch for American defenses at sea or on land to stop.

Then Soviet command decided fighters and strike aircraft mattered more, and that intercontinental ballistic missiles could handle the long-range nuclear job with higher speeds, longer range, and lower production costs. Neither aircraft entered service.

The title sells "ONE fatal flaw," a nice clickable villain. The reasons the video lays out are shifting priorities and cheaper missiles, which is less cinematic and far more Soviet.

Sukhoi's engineers learned to build airframes that survive extreme speeds and fly by wire, and by the 1980s Tupolev used those lessons on the Tu-160.

### Library of Thoth and the Anunnaki file system

Now for the one where I have to be extra careful, because "known" and "claimed" are about to be strangers. Library of Thoth, in "Thoth Named the Exact Moment the Anunnaki Lose Read/Write Access on a Soul — It Only Happens Once," presents a modern esoteric narrative. Thoth is an Egyptian god of writing and wisdom. The Anunnaki are Mesopotamian deities. The video stitches them into a single cosmology, which is a choice, and by the standards of the genre not even a bold one.

Here is what the excerpt claims, and all of it is claim. The Anunnaki maintained a "registered" version of the soul, a "tagged version, the version with read, write access," which was "always a copy, a reflection, an image maintained in a system that was never the source." The source is the "unwritten soul." The moment the soul turns its face from the record, when "the three gates converge and the will transforms," the Anunnaki lose access. Not because you fought them, but because "they were never looking at you." The video says this can happen once, that according to the tablets it's the one thing the system cannot prevent, reverse, or override, and that it's available now, "not after death, not in some future incarnation."

Nothing in the excerpt tells me which tablets, who translated them, or when. I'm not going to invent a provenance the video didn't provide. What I can say is that the framing is a computer metaphor: registration, tagging, read/write access, override. No ancient text says "read/write access." That's a 20th-century vocabulary bolted onto an ancient costume, and the video's own language gives the game away.

The claim itself has a long family tree, though, and I'll say it without mocking anyone's faith. The idea that the self you're handed is a mask over something deeper turns up in many traditions. The Tao Te Ching, for one, is built on the premise that the nameable is not the real thing. The video's version just adds a landlord and a ledger.
