## Esoteric and Religious History

### Library of Thoth: "Emerald Tablets Reveal a Frequency the Anunnaki Deliberately Removed From the Reincarnation Queue"

The Emerald Tablets, as this channel uses them, are a springboard for modern spiritual-fantasy storytelling. The Anunnaki "removal" of a "frequency" is the channel's claim. It isn't an established historical finding. I have nothing in front of me that backs any of it as history, and the transcript doesn't attempt to cite any.

The excerpt is the tail end of the argument, and it's a neat piece of rhetoric. The video describes a "reincarnation queue" that souls cycle through. Its central claim is that the souls who leave "don't announce it. They don't declare their freedom. They just stop showing up to the queue." The system "has no protocol for that," the narration says. It "just lets them go. Not because it's benevolent, because it has no mechanism to hold someone who isn't holding themselves."

According to the video, the lost "frequency" is "the capacity to stop holding yourself in place." It's the ability to let recognition flicker without gripping it, and to treat separation "as a game you're allowed to stop playing the moment you lose interest." The Anunnaki supposedly removed that capacity because it made "every soul an immediate exit risk." They replaced it with the belief that exit requires "permission, preparation, and proof." As long as you're looking for any of those three, the video says, you're still in the queue.

Then comes the line the whole piece is built around: "the queue was never locked, you were just taught to see a wall, where there was always a door." The narration adds that the door becomes obvious "not because it appeared, because you stopped looking away from it."

The excerpt also ties the reincarnation queue to the Akashic records. In the video's telling they are "two interfaces for the same control architecture." One "manages your future incarnations, the other manages your past," and both depend on you treating your story, or its incarnations as your identity. The transcript breaks off right there. "Disidentification," the narrator says, is the recognition.

### The Score That Wasn't

The excerpt's longest passage is about the music, and it's the better story. The channel says Kubrick "threw away an original score after it had already been recorded." It argues that it's now hard to separate the film from its music: Richard Strauss's *Also Sprach Zarathustra*, Johann Strauss II's *The Blue Danube*, and Georgi Ligeti's unsettling choral music around the monolith. The classical pieces, the narration says, "feel so perfectly matched to the film that they seem as if Kubrick must have designed the movie around them from the beginning."

According to the video, he didn't. Composer Alex North was hired to write an original score. North "had previously worked with Kubrick on Spartacus," and "composed and recorded substantial music for the new film." Meanwhile Kubrick had been using classical pieces as temporary tracks while editing, which the channel notes is "a common practice intended to give scenes a musical shape before the final score was ready."

Here's the twist. Kubrick "became attached to the temporary music." He decided North's score "wasn't necessary," and the recorded music "was discarded." The classical tracks stayed in the finished film.

The channel calls the experience "brutal" for North, who believed his music would accompany "one of the most ambitious science fiction films ever made." The excerpt ends mid-sentence on the line that Kubrick's decision "fundamentally changed the tone of the movie. A traditional dramatic score w" and then the transcript stops. I won't guess how the sentence ends.
