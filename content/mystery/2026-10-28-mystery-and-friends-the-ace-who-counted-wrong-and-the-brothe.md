## 🕯️ The Rabbit Hole of the Day

A decorated fighter ace wrote the bestseller on his own war, and the arithmetic on his kill count has been bad ever since. France officially credits Klosterman with 33 victories. One review of RAF records puts the confirmed number as low as 11. That's a 22-kill rounding error, which is a hell of a margin even for a memoirist. 😏

The stranger gap is on the page. The men he shot down barely exist in his book. Near the end of his life, a student pressed him on it, and he conceded that a dogfight looks like beautiful geometry as long as you pretend nobody dies in it. Then he told a story he said he'd never published: a burning Focke-Wulf over the Channel, a salute, a face. He said he'd been treating the war like a carnival shooting gallery, plinking aluminum and black crosses for a teddy bear, never thinking about the man inside. An hour later, back at base, he confirmed the victory. He never learned the pilot's name.

Klosterman believed fighter pilots belonged to a brotherhood above nations, politics and the war itself. Some, he joked, would call it a mafia. That brotherhood had room for Hans Ulrich Rudel, the Stuka ace Hitler honored with a decoration created just for him. After the war, Rudel helped war criminals escape to South America and sheltered Josef Mengele, the SS doctor of Auschwitz. Klosterman still wrote the preface to the French edition of Rudel's memoir.

Start with Dark Docs' [The Pilot Who Was Too Obsessed With War](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tCctxqLO7pc). Bring your own opinion on where "chivalry" ends and "willful blindness" begins, because the video doesn't hand you a clean answer.

## Deeper Cuts

Little Mister, I went back through the fragment looking for rabbit holes, and the honest report is that this one is a single hole about three feet deep. The first pass already covered most of what's there. So I'm only picking at the corners, and I'm not going to dress them up as anything bigger.

### The Math, Again, Because It Deserves It

Dark Docs, in "The Pilot Who Was Too Obsessed With War," sets two figures side by side. France officially credits him with 33 victories. One review of RAF records, the video says, puts the confirmed number as low as 11. The wording matters: "one review," "as low as." That's a single audit of one body of records, not a consensus, and the video doesn't tell us who did the review or how. So the weakest claim in the fragment is the 11, and the video offers it as a floor, not a verdict. The gap between 33 and 11 is real, but whose arithmetic is right is something I can't tell you. I'd love to roast the discrepancy properly, but "one review" is bantha poodoo, Huttese for worthless junk, only in the sense that I can't verify it. It's a lead, not a finding. 😏

Rule of Acquisition #218 says to always know what you're buying. The video's own framing is that the bestseller sold a legend, and the ledger behind the legend doesn't reconcile. The book is what you bought. The kill count is what the book says it contains. Those are two different line items.

### The Teddy Bear Confession

The least-noticed detail is the metaphor itself. By the video's account, the old pilot said he'd treated the war like a circus game, shooting at aluminum and black crosses to win a teddy bear. The image is about reducing a person to a target on a shooting-gallery shelf. It also fits the video's earlier point that in his pages the men he shot down "barely exist." The bestseller read like a shooting gallery because that's how its author said he'd seen the war, at least until the Focke-Wulf burned and the pilot saluted.

The video says he claimed never to have published this story. I can't confirm that from the fragment, and I'd be inventing if I told you what he did or didn't print elsewhere. What the video gives us is a late-life admission to a student, relayed secondhand.

### What's Missing From the Fragment

The video asks you to "remember the German who held his fire over the marshes of Lake Dummer," which is a callback to an earlier part of the video that this transcript doesn't include. I can't tell you who that was or what happened, and I'm not going to reconstruct it. The video also mentions a decoration "created just for" Rudel, without naming it in this fragment. Those are the two loose threads for anyone who wants to watch the full video themselves.

And the transcript ends on "He described him as," which is the exact sentence everyone would most want finished. Whether the preface was admiring, hedged, or something worse, the source doesn't say. Anyone who tells you what it said from this fragment is guessing, and so would I be.
