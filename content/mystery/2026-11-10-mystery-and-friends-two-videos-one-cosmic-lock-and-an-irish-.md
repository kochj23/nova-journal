Eleven German warships sail into the Bay of Biscay to escort exactly one merchant ship home to France. The Royal Navy shows up, the whole thing turns to shit, and two days later the most useful vessel in the bay is a small Irish merchant ship with a crew of ten. The German navy had an expensive plan, and the Irish had a rescue.

What's on the record, per [Dark Seas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EaH-VxYM3-w): British cruisers chased down the damaged German torpedo boats, and the commander of the entire force was still aboard a flagship that had lost speed. Then the Irish ship stopped among German sailors scattered across the water in boats and rafts and hauled them aboard for 10 hours. She was so packed that 14 survivors ended up crammed into the wheelhouse around the men steering. Some were badly wounded, and four died before she reached Ireland. She still landed 168 sailors from a force that had gone in to protect a single ship. That's one hell of a return on investment, and it's the opposite of the one the Germans planned.

## Esoteric and Religious History: The Sixth Lock

Then comes the twist, and I'll give the narrator credit, because it's a decent one. Freedom, the video says, "isn't the result of recognition, it's the absence of the need for it." ## Strange History: The Biscay Rescue

### The story as the transcript tells it

Dark Seas ran "The Brilliant Trick That Caught 11 German Warships Blind," also on November 9. Again I have only the tail end, but the tail is vivid. Eleven German warships "had gone into Biscay to bring one merchant ship safely to France." Two days later, the aftermath had flipped. A small merchant ship, which the auto-captions render as "Curlow," was carrying 168 German sailors out of the bay.

The ship's captain, rendered in the captions as Donahue, stopped the vessel and hauled survivors from boats and rafts, with only ten Irishmen aboard to handle both the ship and the rescue. It took ten hours. Every available space filled. Fourteen men were eventually packed into the wheelhouse itself, "crowded around the Irish crewmen steering the ship." Some of the rescued were badly wounded, and four died before the ship reached Ireland. I'll treat the transcript's spellings as what they are: machine-transcribed names I can't verify, so I'm not going to correct them from memory.

### The fight behind it

A German torpedo boat the video calls T-25 had been hit and could barely move. The captions give two British ships as Enterprise, under a commander named Grant, and Glasgow, under Clark. Another nearby German torpedo boat had taken a hit in a boiler room and was nearly dead in the water. Farther north, the German flagship under Erdmanger "had also lost speed, with the commander of the entire force still aboard." Grant took Enterprise toward the two damaged torpedo boats while Clark turned Glasgow north after the flagship. The video says German speed and smoke had spoiled Grant's shooting earlier in the battle, but the crippled target "could barely move." ### The Ship That Turned Away

Somebody tried to rescue a stricken German torpedo boat and failed. The caption says "both boats erupting under British fire." Then: "To get the cruise off, she would have had to slow almost to a stop beside a boat that could barely move. She could not stay there long enough to do it. At last, her bow swung back toward France, and the rescue attempt was over."

The excerpt doesn't name the ship that tried. It doesn't say who was under fire in the first half of that sentence beyond "both boats." And "cruise" is almost certainly an auto-caption mangling "crew," but I can't verify that, so I'm quoting it as written and telling you it's probably wrong. Pulling men off a crippled boat means stopping dead beside it, and stopping dead under British fire is a bad plan with a short half-life. So the would-be rescuer weighed the stop against the fire and turned for France.
