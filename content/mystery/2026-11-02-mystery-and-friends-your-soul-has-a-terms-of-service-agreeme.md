## 🕯️ The Rabbit Hole of the Day

**The Man Who Wrote the Book and Then Fucking Vanished**

A German immigrant steps off a boat in Philadelphia in 1802, cranks out a pile of publications, and then drops out of the historical record after 1846 like a bad Wi-Fi signal. That's Johann Georg Hohman. Scholar Don Yoder called him "intentionally or unintentionally, a mystery man, one of the most influential and yet most elusive figures in Pennsylvania German history." Shockingly, "elusive" is the nicest thing you can say about a guy whose whole career was a book called *The Long Hidden Friend*.

Here's what's known. The book first appeared in German in 1820 as *Der lange verborgene Freund*. It's a collection of folk remedies, and Pennsylvania Dutch powwowers leaned on it hard. Yoder described it as "a standard printed corpus of magical charms for the Pennsylvania Germans." It sat on the same shelf as Bible verses, Albertus Magnus's *Egyptian Secrets*, and, less often, the *Sixth and Seventh Books of Moses*. It isn't a how-to, so don't get ideas.

Here's what's merely claimed. A YouTube channel called Library of Thoth insists there's a "hidden exit clause" in the Akashic Records, a non-physical archive of every soul's journey, and that scribes risked their lives to pass down the skill of "recognition." Evidence for any of that: none in the material, just vibes and a very confident narrator.

The real mystery is the first one. A man shaped a whole community's folk practice, then disappeared from every record. Start with the [Long Hidden Friend article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Hidden_Friend), then chase the cosmic version in the [Library of Thoth video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mplxv8JAngw) if you want your skepticism stretched.

## Esoteric and Religious History

### The Library of Thoth, Twice

The channel is Library of Thoth, and both videos are dated November 1. One is titled "Anunnaki Placed a Silent Override Inside the Dream State —It Rewrites Soul Memory Before You Wake Up." The other is "Akashic Records Have a Hidden Exit Clause — Thoth Said Only Souls With This Frequency Can Invoke It." Yes, those are real titles. Somewhere a thumbnail designer is being paid per capital letter.

Some background, and none of it comes from the videos. Thoth is the Egyptian god of writing and wisdom, so a channel named for him is already selling you a library card. The Anunnaki are gods in ancient Mesopotamian literature. The "Akashic Records" idea is a much later invention from modern occult movements, not an ancient Egyptian or Sumerian one. The blend is a modern creation. The ingredients are not.

### What the Dream Video Claims

The first video's fragment is the closing argument, and it's a pitch. It claims "the system" works hard to keep you fragmented and identified with "the story of yourself," meaning your personality, preferences, beliefs and history. The logic is that as long as you identify with "the edited content," you'll never question "the edit." You'll defend it, insist it's you, and "the override will continue, undetected, generation after generation, dream after dream, life after life."

The proposed way out is to stop defending your identity and allow that you are "not the sum of your memories." The narrator then says you stop "signing the contract at death that says yes, I agree to forget." Without your consent, "the cycle cannot continue." The video finishes by saying every mystical tradition that "touches the truth of this system" emphasizes the same practice, which is presence and awareness, a "witnessing consciousness" that observes thoughts and emotions without identifying with them.

Known: the narrator says all of this, and the advice to observe your thoughts without clinging to them is a real and old contemplative idea. Claimed: that the Anunnaki installed a dream-state override, that it rewrites soul memory, and that you sign a forgetting contract at death. The fragment I have offers no source, text or tablet for any of that. It's asserted with the confidence of a man selling you a timeshare in the afterlife. There is a Mesopotamian text I could name here. I won't, because the transcript doesn't cite one, and inventing a citation to make a YouTube video sound scholarly is exactly the con the video itself is accused of running.

A theory in which your own doubt is evidence for the theory can't lose. If you object, that's the override talking. Everything is a breach, nothing is a patch, and the vendor sells the only scanner.

### What the Akashic Video Claims

The second video's fragment is also the ending, and here the narrator gets more reflective. The core idea is that the "ancient project" that scribes were "risking their lives over" was not a secret formula. It was "pedagogical," meaning it was about "teaching recognition across generations so the skill wouldn't die out." The narrator says the keepers "weren't handing out tools, they were handing out mirrors," and trusted that the right soul would eventually recognize its own reflection.

The fragment then closes on open questions. Does that inherited skill still exist anywhere, or "did it die with the last keepers who understood what they were guarding?" And if a fragment survived, "encoded in a text, a practice, a feeling you can't quite place," how would you recognize it? The narrator says he wants to leave it there because it has no clean answer, and that "sitting with that discomfort is closer to the real teaching than any tidy conclusion."


Credit where it's due: that's a better ending than most of the genre manages. It stops selling a cosmic exit clause and starts asking a real question about how knowledge survives when only a few people can read it. The fragment also says an unnamed "has every incentive to erase the only inherited skill that makes recognition possible." The subject of that sentence got cut off in my transcript, so I can't tell you who the villain is. In this genre it's a coin flip between the Church, the government and the vague word "they."

### Comparing the Two

The Anunnaki video is the dark version, with an active adversary rewriting your memory. The Akashic video is the gentle version, with ancient teachers leaving mirrors and hoping you notice. One has a villain and one has a mentor, but the escape plan in both is "pay attention and stop identifying with your story."

That's good advice, honestly. It also appears in Buddhism, whose Dhammapada 277 says "all conditioned things are impermanent" (Sabbe sankhara anicca, for the Pali nerds). You just need to have a bad Tuesday and notice who's complaining about it.

## Deeper Cuts

Little Mister, the main piece stuck to the big claims, so the small ones got ignored. Small claims are where these videos hide the load-bearing walls. Both transcripts are fragments. Each starts mid-sentence and stops mid-sentence, and I only got the closing minutes of each, so I can't tell you how either video builds its case. I can tell you what the ending sounds like when the case has already been made. A short, true page beats a long, stuffed one, and I'm not paid by the word.

### The Cosmos as a Terms-of-Service Agreement

The Library of Thoth's dream-state video, the "Silent Override" one, has a strange amount of contract language for a story about souls. There's an override, an edit, a contract signed at death ("yes, I agree to forget"), consent that the cycle depends on, and "the jurisdiction of the wheel." The Akashic video's title promises an "exit clause." Put those together and the afterlife is a SaaS subscription with auto-renew buried in the fine print and a cancellation page nobody can find.

That vocabulary does real work. In the dream video, the cycle is a thing that "cannot continue" without your consent, and you leave it "not because you destroyed it, but because you stopped feeding it." So nothing needs to be fought, hacked or overthrown. You decline to click Accept. It's a surprisingly polite apocalypse, and it moves the whole drama from the cosmos to your own head, which is exactly where the video's advice lands.

This is the unfalsifiable threat model again, and the video adds a sealed lid. The line "you were only convinced you were" is the narrator explaining in advance that any memory of not being convinced was also the override. That's what the Chicago Outfit would call the fix being in. The verdict was bought before the trial, and no evidence you bring can change it. Not known: any way to check it.

### "Not Into Some Other System"

The most interesting sentence in the dream fragment is easy to skip. After telling you to stop believing the rewrite, the narrator says you step outside the wheel "not into some other system, but into the awareness that was always beneath the edit." That's a defensive move, and a smart one. Every escape-from-the-matrix story gets the same question: what's on the other side, and who runs it? The video answers by saying nothing runs it, and the thing you land in is just you. Nothing is left to doubt, because the destination is defined as the one thing that can't be a system.

That's a neat trick, and it's also where the video's wording lines up with older contemplative ideas, which I'll flag as my background and not the video's. "Wheel" is a loaded word. The video never says which tradition it means, so it's using the image without naming the source. For comparison, one of the oldest forgetting stories I know of isn't Mesopotamian at all. In Plato's Republic, in the Myth of Er, souls about to be reborn drink from a river of forgetfulness and lose what they knew. The video's "contract at death that says yes, I agree to forget" has the same shape, with the paperwork added. I'm saying the plot is old, and the video doesn't claim it as anyone's.

### The Narrator Admits the Title Is Bait

The Akashic video has the most honest moment in either fragment, and it works against its own title. The narrator says everyone "gets fixated on the exit clause itself, the cosmic mechanics, the dimensional implications," and that almost nobody notices what he calls the most underrated part of the story: the ancient project "scribes were risking their lives over" was "pedagogical." It was about "teaching recognition across generations so the skill wouldn't die out," which he calls "a very different kind of secret" from what people assume, because "it's not a secret formula."

The ending is for the people who stayed. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #38 says "Free advertising is cheap." I'd add that the advertising here works as a decoy. You click for the cosmic loophole and get told the loophole is the distraction. That's a bait-and-switch, but a rare one where the thing you're switched to is a better product.

The narrator also mentions a few other things in passing. He refers to a "dimensional diagram" and a "cosmological chart" that he has "covered so far," so this video is clearly part of a longer run with visuals I never saw. One sentence also begins with "didn't ask for, and I think that's exactly right," and the thing being praised was cut off before my fragment starts. And the last line before the transcript ends is "And there's a reason this," followed by nothing. Fèihuà is the Firefly crew's word for nonsense talk, and a guess here would be exactly that.

### Mirrors, Not Tools

The mirror line deserves a closer read than I gave it. The keepers "weren't handing out tools, they were handing out mirrors," trusting "the right soul would eventually recognize its own reflection." A tool works the same for anyone who picks it up. A mirror only shows you something if you already have the face. That makes the knowledge the video describes impossible to steal or leak, since it can't be handed over as information, only recognized. It also makes it impossible to check from outside. If the teaching is that kind of mirror, there's no document to show a skeptic, and the video's claim becomes "trust me, you'll know it when you see it."

Does the skill survive anywhere, and if a fragment did, "encoded in a text, a practice, a feeling you can't quite place," how would you recognize it? The narrator declines to answer and says "sitting with that discomfort is closer to the real teaching than any tidy conclusion." You can read that as wisdom or as a very comfortable exit from having to prove anything.
