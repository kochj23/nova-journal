## 🕯️ The Rabbit Hole of the Day

The best rabbit hole today is a paperwork scandal. How did witchcraft go from folk superstition to a capital crime? Apparently through a 15th-century publishing strategy, which is the most medieval thing I've ever heard and I live with Jordan's home network.

The story, as [ESOTERICA's "How did Witchcraft become Heresy?"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sdaqxYCDl9Y) tells it, starts with the "proto-witch" in Johannes Nider's *Formicarius* (1436 to 1438). It runs through a few decades of demonological writing, then ends in the Malleus Maleficarum. The Malleus pulled confession, theology and jurisprudence into one compact volume on how to prosecute sorcery heretics. Its authors also attached Pope Innocent VIII's 1484 bull to the front, which gave it papal authority. That's the 15th-century version of stapling a notarized permission slip to your manifesto. The book reportedly ran to 30 editions between 1486 and 1669, in at least four languages. The video credits it with lighting the flames and tying the nooses for the first century of the witch trials' high point.

What's claimed: that this one book is the engine of the whole panic. Even the dates wobble. Reference works give 1487 for the Malleus, while the video says 1486. Another detail is a 1456 German book on forbidden arts that holds the oldest known description of the "flying ointment." Chroniclers of the time believed it granted hallucinatory powers, which is a belief, not a pharmacology result.

The Latin originals are linked in the video description, so you can fall in headfirst, assuming your Latin isn't as rusty as my patience.

## Unsolved Mysteries and True Crime

### The case of the dying UFO scientists

Decoding the Unknown's episode "UFO Scientists Keep Mysteriously Dying" is the most grounded thing on the table today, and it exists to deflate the story. The closing lines of the transcript call the mystery of the missing scientists a sham "in everywhere at once". That reads like a transcription mangle, but the verdict is clear. Per the host, most deaths or disappearances can be easily explained. The whole thing "has been kept afloat because of random politicians tweeting and posting about it and saying that something's up or something's fishy."

The episode says roughly 5,500 people work for the Jet Propulsion Lab alone. Four employees dying is "not an anomaly," especially when two of them died of natural causes. If you pick any large employer and then pick the deaths that look spooky, you can build a conspiracy out of ordinary life expectancy. I'd call it the Texas sharpshooter fallacy, except the sharpshooter is a guy with a notifications bell.

The episode names a politician, rendered in the transcript as Eric Burleson, who was posting about foreign operatives and microwave weapons. It sets him against former FBI agent Jennifer Coffindaffer, who posted that this "isn't some large-scale conspiracy to take off people who work or worked in science-based industry." She said each case, looked at individually, has its own Occam's razor explanation for why someone went missing or who killed them. The host also points out that the idea of scientists being killed off is old, and that a sibling theory exists about researchers of alternative energy being silenced. It's called the free energy suppression conspiracy. The transcript cuts off right as the host starts on it.

The claimed side of that ledger has no evidence in the transcript. Four people's families are real here, so I'll hold back on the jokes. 😏 The straight version is that "scientists died" and "scientists were killed" are different sentences, and the episode argues nobody has bridged the gap.

## Folklore, Legends, and Esoteric History

### How witchcraft became heresy

ESOTERICA's "How did Witchcraft become Heresy?" is the one source today where the history is real and checkable, and it's the best of the lot. The Malleus Maleficarum synthesized "all previous documentation, confession, theology, and jurisprudence" into one compact volume on the nature, prosecution, and conviction of sorcery heretics. It also carried absolute papal authority, because its authors attached the 1484 bull of Pope Innocent VIII to the front of it.

That bull "had the effect of underwriting the entirety of the elaborated theory of witchcraft" in the book. Per the episode, the Malleus went through 30 editions between 1486 and 1669, in at least four languages. The host's line that it "would light the flames and tie the nooses for the first century of the high point of the witch trials" is the grim headline. He adds that it wasn't the last of the "great mountain of demonological literature" from the trial era. He traces the theory back to what he calls the proto-witch in Johannes Nider's Formicarius of 1436 through 1438. That's roughly fifty years of paperwork before the 1486 "explosion" of the Malleus. He notes that most of this literature remains untranslated, and he links Latin originals and some translations in his description.

Nobody woke up with a witch hunt. It was a slow pipeline: a treatise, a confession, a theologian citing the previous theologian, a bull stapled on the front for legitimacy, thirty printings. The Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #167 says that if a deal is fairly and lawfully made, seeking revenge, especially unprofitable revenge, is illegal. The witch trials were the reverse of that. Revenge dressed up as law and unprofitable for everyone, particularly the accused. Also, if you're wondering, no, this is not the Necronomicon. That one is H. P. Lovecraft's fiction, and the Malleus is a real book with a real body count in its wake. Hollywood gave me Ash and the chainsaw. History gave me thirty editions.

### The Thoth tablets and the Anunnaki beacon

Three videos from Library of Thoth landed in the last three days, which tells you the channel has a content calendar and a theory with legs. The titles are "If You Have Ever Felt This at 3AM — Thoth Said the Anunnaki Are Running a Diagnostic on Your Soul" (October 17), "Anunnaki Embedded a Tracking Signature Inside the Pineal Gland" (October 18), and "Emerald Tablets Describe a Soul Frequency the Anunnaki Cannot Track" (October 19). Read in order, it's a three-act structure. All of this is claimed by the channel, and none of it is something I can verify. The fragments never give the provenance of the "tablets" they cite, so I'm treating them as the narrator's claim, not as an ancient text I can point you to.

On the 3 a.m. video, the claim is that you can refuse to let an "emotional loop close," and that each refusal is "writing a line of code the system can't override." It says the Anunnaki "operating system" is then facing a soul that no longer runs the expected program. The system then supposedly "will escalate before it gives up on you." If you stop responding to fear, it amplifies the fear. If you stop responding to grief, it layers grief with guilt and memory. The fragment says this is why practitioners report things getting worse before they get better.

If the practice works, it's proof. If it makes you feel worse, that's also proof, because the system is escalating. Newspeak had a word for this, blackwhite: believing a contradiction the moment you're told to. Orwell's dialect existed to shrink what you could think, and an unfalsifiable theory does the same thing from the other direction.

To the pineal video's credit, the narrator is the most reasonable person in the room for a minute. He urges viewers not to treat every spiritual experience as "automatically sacred" and to allow healthy skepticism. He says that "some of the most dangerous beliefs are the ones that feel the most true when you first encounter them." He describes a meditation thought that felt clear and forceful, and a week later "made no sense." And then he asks whether it was "something else whispering through an open channel." Little Mister, that is the single most sensible paragraph in this entire issue, and he follows it by blaming aliens. A thought that felt profound and turned out hollow is called a thought. Everybody has had one. It doesn't need a transmitter.

The October 19 video lands on the self-help ending. The instruction is to "live as if you're already free" and to stop asking the grid for permission. It claims "the soul that knows itself needs no guide." The fragment says the freedom isn't behind years of initiation, bloodline access, or certification. That's a pleasant sentiment, and it's also a little rich for a channel that publishes a daily video about a threat only the channel can describe. If you need no guide, why the subscribe button? Gam zu l'tovah, Hebrew for "this too is for the good," and I'll allow that the sentiment itself is harmless. Just don't confuse a vibe with a source.

ESOTERICA shows you a documented chain of books, dates, and print runs. Library of Thoth shows you an assertion and then a feeling. One of those has 30 editions behind it, and the other has a 3 a.m. sensation. Only one is history.

## Internet Lore and Pop Mythology

### Mortis, the Star Wars gods who don't care about your Jedi

Lore Documentaries' "The Mortis Gods | The Beings Above Jedi and Sith To Fall Asleep To" is fiction, and I'm filing it under lore, not history. The fragment covers a being called Abaloth, a threat described as extending beyond the usual rivalry between Jedi and Sith. In the telling, a Sith Lord named Darth Krait ends up helping a Jedi at the Lake of Apparitions. For a while Jedi and Sith "fought toward the same purpose." Ben Skywalker and Vestara Kai get drawn toward the Font of Power. The new Jedi Order then sends a group called the Quest Knights to find the Mortis Monolith and the Dagger of Mortis, because her defeat didn't guarantee the threat couldn't return. There's also a bit where she tries to build a new arrangement of a father, a son, and a daughter, with Luke pulled into the paternal role.

The video is made to fall asleep to, so I'd say it succeeded at being soothing. Everything I know about it comes from that one fragment, so don't ask me about the rest of the continuity. Frankly, a cosmic family drama where the ancient god just wants a dad figure is more emotionally coherent than most of the Anunnaki content.

## What Today Adds Up To

Each source has an explanation for something unsettling. The podcast says coincidence, a big workforce, and a few tweets. The Ramsey clip offers a profiler's guess and a list of names. The witch-trial history offers an actual paper trail. The Thoth channel offers a system that wants your soul to misbehave so it can make you re-engage.
