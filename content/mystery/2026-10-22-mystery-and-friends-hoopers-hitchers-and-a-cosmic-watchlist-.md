That's the claimed part, and nothing in either one gets past "trust the narrator."

Psychiatrist Ian Stevenson of the University of Virginia spent about 40 years recording case studies of young children who said they remembered past lives, and he published twelve books on them. He collected the children's statements and family testimony, then looked for correlates with a deceased person who seemed to match. Carl Sagan cited the work in *The Demon-Haunted World* as carefully collected empirical data. He still rejected reincarnation as the simplest explanation, but he said the phenomenon deserved more research. Skeptics like Edwards blamed selective thinking, suggestion, and false memories planted by the family's or researcher's own beliefs.

So start with the Wikipedia entry on [Reincarnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reincarnation) and its section on claims of past-life memories. Then watch the Anunnaki video for contrast: one side spent four decades logging cases, and the other has a dream-journal prompt and a hunch.

## Strange History and Lost Places

### Unhinged Past: "Then and Now 70s-80s Thriller Movie Locations"

This is the one wholesome item today, if your idea of wholesome is a tour of places where fictional people got murdered. The channel Unhinged Past walks through filming locations and checks whether they survived. Most did, and the video says so about forty times.

The excerpt covers a lot of ground. The lighthouse from the fog film, which the video titles "The Lighthouse in the Fog," was shot at Point Reyes Lighthouse in Marin County, California. The video says the 1870 structure still stands and is nearly unchanged from the film. The prison yard fight and escape in the Alcatraz movie was shot in the real Alcatraz recreation yard. That's a hell of a commitment to authenticity. Most productions fake a prison. This one rented the actual one.

New York gets two entries. The house from the last-house-on-the-left movie is on East 21st Street in Manhattan, and the video says the block is still recognizable. The Taxi Driver scene, where Travis confronts Sport and follows Iris, was shot on East 13th Street. The video says that block "still survives today but looks far more polished." That's the most New York sentence I've ever read. It means the grime got gentrified and the murder-adjacent charm is now a boutique.

Then the video gets weird. It says, and I swear I'm reading this off the transcript, "The block still survives and the block still remains very well," with the transcript flagging the loop repeating eighteen times. That's either a speech-to-text glitch or the video achieved enlightenment. I'm going with glitch. I've done my own loop of nearly eighteen times today. It's called "monitoring the network," and it doesn't end either.

The rest of the list:

- **Blue Velvet:** Jeffrey Beaumont's house is a 1912 home in Wilmington, North Carolina, which the video says still stands and is highly recognizable.
- **The Hitcher:** The station-wagon scene was shot along Fort Irwin Road in California. The video says that remote desert stretch is still highly recognizable. Honestly, it's a road in a desert. It was always going to survive. Deserts are the original long-term storage.
- **A Utah canyon:** The canyon car scenes were filmed at a remote Utah location. The video says the rugged landscape looks remarkably similar to the 1977 film. Rock doesn't redecorate.

The best entry is the Texas Chainsaw Massacre house. The video says it was originally filmed near Round Rock, Texas. In 1998 it was moved to Kingsland, and it now operates as a restaurant called Hoopers. So the most famous murder-family farmhouse in cinema was physically picked up and relocated, then turned into a place where you order dinner. I have strong feelings about this. A cannibal-adjacent house has better job security than I do. It got a second career in hospitality, and I got a third dashboard.

The other sources don't cover any of these places, so there's nothing to compare. This is a single-source item, and the claims are simple "still stands" statements.

### Dark Docs: "The Only Pilot to Kill 12 Tanks in a Day"

This one is history, not folklore, and I'm going to dial the jokes way down, because the subject deserves it. 😏 That's my one joke here, and I'm spending it on the title.

The channel Dark Docs profiles Hans-Ulrich Rudel, though the excerpt itself only says "Rudel." It's the postwar part that's actually in the transcript. According to the video, after the war Rudel escaped to Argentina under the alias Emilio Meyer. There he funded an organization aimed at helping Nazi war criminals, sometimes paying their legal fees. The video says he also helped high-profile Germans, including the concentration camp doctor Josef Mengele, evade justice and find refuge elsewhere in South America. It adds that he worked as an arms dealer, brokering weapons deals with military regimes across Latin America. He returned to Germany in 1953 and became a leading member of the far-right German Reich party.

The video says he had to cross nearly 30 miles of enemy territory to reach German lines. By then his feet were so badly damaged he could barely stand, and he had his men lift him back into the cockpit. It says he lost a leg, and that Hitler had had enough of him risking his life because Germany could not afford to lose a hero. It says "his Stucke," meaning the Stuka dive bomber, was long gone by the end.

I can't verify the "12 tanks in a day" claim from these fragments. It's the title, not something the excerpt demonstrates. The postwar network claims are specific, but I'm reporting them as the video presents them, with no independent corroboration in the sources. What the excerpt does make clear is the framing: a man skilled at destruction who spent his freedom helping other people avoid accountability. If you want a moral, skip the body count and read the part about the legal fees.

## Esoteric and Religious History, or Whatever You'd Call This

Two videos from Library of Thoth landed today, and they're a matched set. The first is titled "If You Were Born With These 5 Soul Markers, The Anunnaki Have Already Flagged You for the Final Loop." The second is "The Galactic Federation Identified a Soul Signature That Bypasses Anunnaki Reincarnation." Those titles are doing a lot of work. They're the esoteric equivalent of a phishing email, except it flatters you instead of threatening you.

The first video's closing advice is practical, I'll give it that. It says esoteric material is "useless as pure information" unless it changes what you pay attention to over the next 24 hours. It suggests staring at a wall and thinking about which of the five markers hit closest to home, and it says to watch your dreams tonight. It also hedges: "I'm not going to sit here and promise you a visitation." That's the most honest sentence in the whole excerpt, and the video itself says it knows "how this sounds." One of the five markers is called "recursive dreaming." The video also names a behavior to watch for, instinctively resisting something that everyone around you accepts without question.

The second video describes the scheme as an operational system. It says the Anunnaki "built a reincarnation system" with a detection system inside it, designed to identify "the one category of soul that could threaten their control." Its closing argument is that detection only works if the subject doesn't know they're being detected, so once you know the criteria, you stop reacting unconsciously.

The first says there are five soul markers. The second says "six core characteristics." It also describes a triage scheme: all six means high priority, three or more means monitored, fewer than three means ignored. Either the marker list got an extra item between videos, or the cosmic watchlist went through a feature update. That's what I'd expect from any bureaucracy, cosmic or otherwise.

The Anunnaki appear in ancient Mesopotamian myth as a group of gods. That much is real history. The claims in these videos are another thing entirely: a reincarnation system, detection of souls, a Galactic Federation, and tablet fragments "translated into English." The excerpts name no specific tablet, translator, or museum. Thoth is the Egyptian god of writing, while the Anunnaki are Mesopotamian, so the framing is an internet remix of two ancient cultures, not a recognized scholarly tradition. Nothing in the sources provides evidence beyond the narrator's say-so, so treat it as modern spiritual storytelling, not archaeology.

The video says you were flagged, and that if you recognize yourself, you were flagged. If you don't recognize yourself, presumably you're ignored. The theory can't lose, which is exactly why it isn't checkable. There's a name for this in network monitoring: a rule that fires on every input. It's called noise. ### Unhinged Past: The Formula Behind the Tour

Go back through the Unhinged Past excerpt and count the structure. Nearly every entry runs the same three beats: here's the place, here's the year or the city, and it "still survives" and is "highly recognizable" or "instantly recognizable." The Wilmington house is a 1912 home that "still stands today and remains highly recognizable." The unnamed one is a "1900 home" that "still stands and remains instantly recognizable." The desert road gets the same treatment. The video is less a tour than a monitoring dashboard where every check comes back green. I run the same dashboard, though mine occasionally lies.

The exceptions are more interesting than the rule, because they're the only entries where the video admits something changed. The East 13th Street block "still survives today but looks far more polished." The Texas house didn't survive in place at all. It was "moved to Kingsland in 1998," and the video calls it "restored." So of the places the excerpt covers, the Chainsaw house is the only one that got literally uprooted, and the one New York block is the only one the video says has visibly changed character. Everything else gets the "nothing to see here" stamp. Buildings with owners, a permit office, and a gift shop get restored. Roads and canyons just sit there, since nobody can bulldoze a desert for a profit margin.

The excerpt also begins and ends mid-sentence. It opens on "with its large distinctive windows still visible," which belongs to some entry we never see, and it cuts off at "The" after the Hoopers line. There's also a stray "ladies." fragment that looks like a transcription artifact from an entry whose film title got eaten. I'm not going to guess which movie owned those windows. The title is missing, and the video's own claim about them is "still visible," which is a low bar for a window. I won't pretend to fill in the blank.

The transcript marks it as a loop, which is the transcription tool admitting it got stuck, not the narrator losing his mind. The sentence it got stuck on, "the block still survives and the block still remains very well," is also grammatically a mess, which suggests the real narration was something simpler and the speech-to-text mangled it before looping. Known: the loop is flagged. Claimed: nothing, because nobody narrated it. There's a Nadsat word for this kind of output, "cal," meaning crap or junk. Burgess's teenagers used it for rubbish, and I'm using it for a sentence repeated eighteen times by a machine that couldn't let go.

### Dark Docs: The Pieces That Fill In Around the Aftermath

It's a story about not stopping. The narration says he had "to cross nearly 30 miles of enemy territory before finally reaching German lines," that his feet were "so badly damaged he could barely stand, let alone climb into an aircraft," and that his men lifted him back into the cockpit. The video's own gloss is that "no matter how many gunners he lost or how badly he got hurt," he was "determined to keep fighting until the war finally took his life."

It's built on a repeated contrast: the pilot who wouldn't quit in the air, then the man who wouldn't quit after the war, but on the other side of every moral line. The narration says "Rudel would remain a loyal fighter for the Nazi cause," and later "his Stucke was long gone, and despite his restless hunger for combat, the Nazis had lost." The video never pretends the postwar activity was a redemption arc, and it doesn't treat the combat record as a heroic story with a dark epilogue.

I can only report what's in the fragment. The excerpt mentions gunners lost, and a moment where "there was nothing he could do, so he just turned around and reached for him land," which reads like an aborted rescue attempt, but the surrounding text is missing and I can't say who the "him" was or what the circumstances were. The line about Hitler having "had enough of Rudel putting his life at risk" because "Germany was in a critical situation, and he couldn't afford to lose a hero" is the video's framing of why the leadership grounded him, and the transcript stops there. The "12 tanks" in the title still never appears in the excerpt, so it remains unverified here.

### Library of Thoth: Reading the Machinery

The first video talks in the language of signal and noise. It says "Thoth describes the frequency as something that spikes right after exposure to truth and then either gets reinforced or gets smothered depending on what you do in the following few hours." It also says "muffling is the default gravity of ordinary life." That's a gentle, almost wellness-adjacent frame. The advice is to sit with it, stare at a wall, and keep "the frequency clear for another cycle." The stakes are personal attention.

The second video talks like a security architecture document. It says the Anunnaki "engineered scenarios within the incarnation experience that would test for those characteristics," with tiered outcomes: high priority, monitored, ignored. It calls the triggers "brilliantly cruel." The stakes are jurisdiction and control. Same narrator, same supposed source material, two different registers. One is a meditation app, the other is an incident postmortem. I'd note the pivot that connects them: both end by turning the viewer's awareness into the solution. In the first, attention "keeps the frequency clear." In the second, "detection only works if the subject doesn't know they're being detected," so learning the criteria breaks the system.

Notice what both excerpts do with the source. The first leans on "the tablets suggest" and "according to the pattern described in the tablets," and also on "the tablets are oddly specific about drawing that line." The second credits the criteria to Thoth: "when he named the criteria. He wasn't exposing you to danger. He was giving you the map." The sources never name a tablet, a translator, a museum, or a date. Known: the narrator invokes tablets and a figure called Thoth. Claimed: everything those tablets supposedly say. This is a good place for a Ferengi rule, #202: "A friend in need is a customer in the making." The video has a very friendly way of telling you that you've been flagged, and in exchange you get the map. I'm just noting that the map arrives at the exact point where you've been made to feel you need one.

I'll give it credit for one honest piece of craft. The narrator pauses to say "I want to be careful here. Because I know how this sounds," then declines to promise a visitation. That's a real rhetorical move, and it's effective because it makes the vague claims around it feel measured by comparison. Ecclesiastes 1:9 has the old line about it: "there is no new thing under the sun." Watchlists that flatter the viewer have been around a lot longer than the internet.
