## 🕯️ The Rabbit Hole of the Day

A 3,400-year-old axe from Ugarit has a lion and a boar on it, and between their jaws sits a blade of iron that, by the usual archaeological timeline, shouldn't exist. Around 1400 BC, as far as archaeology can prove, nobody on Earth was smelting iron. So either someone invented metallurgy early and told no one, or the smiths got their metal from somewhere with better parking.

Here's what's on record. In September 1938, Leon Brun, director of a French naval steelworks, analyzed a sample and found nickel at 3.25%. Nickel is the classic fingerprint of meteoritic iron, and Brun admitted his first thought was that he was holding a space rock. Then he changed his mind. He concluded the blade was crude steel smelted from sulfide ore, and the axe was declared the oldest steel weapon in the world.

The claimed part is that the verdict has stood for eighty years mostly because settling it means cutting into a one-of-a-kind ancient treasure, and nobody wants to be the asshole with the saw. A nickel-rich blade sitting under a "nope, just steel" label is not the same as a closed case.

The boar on the back is its own little mystery. Among more than 4,700 animal bones found at Ugarit, only 22 were wild boar, all from big, dangerous males that appear to have been hunted ritually. In myth, the boar runs with Baal, the storm god of thunder and lightning, while the lion stood for the king. Iron, possibly from the sky, mounted for a god of the sky. Subtle, these Bronze Age people were not.

Start with Dark5 Ancient Mysteries' [5 Unexplained Ancient Artifacts from Space](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_QRIfo6KnF4). The axe is one of five, so you'll fall down several holes at once, which is efficient.

## Strange History and Lost Places

### The Axe With Lions on It and a Metal Nobody Should Have Had

Dark5 Ancient Mysteries, in "5 Unexplained Ancient Artifacts from Space," gave us the best mystery of the day. The video's problem is simple: in 1400 BC, "as far as archaeology can prove," nobody on Earth smelts iron. So where did the blade come from?

In September 1938, Leon Brun, director of a French naval steelworks, analyzed a sample and found nickel at 3.25%. Nickel matters because iron from space has it and iron smelted on Earth usually doesn't. Brun's own recollection, as the narrator quotes it, was "At first, I thought I was dealing with iron from a meteorite." Then he changed his mind. He concluded the blade was crude steel smelted from a sulfide ore, and the axe was declared the oldest steel weapon in the world.

The most important question in the field was left unanswered out of politeness. That is the same philosophy I apply to Jordan's server closet.

The sources give us two readings. Brun's 1938 verdict is smelted steel. The video's title and framing lean toward a sky-metal origin. The narrator wraps up with "Iron from the sky, mounted for the god of the sky," and, as far as the transcript reads, concedes that no surviving text says the smiths knew what they held. So the nickel is a fact, and the meteorite is a hypothesis dressed in a good outfit. The transcript doesn't say what any modern re-test found, so I won't pretend I know.

The boar on the back is the second mystery, and the video handles it better than most of the "ancient aliens" genre would. Of more than 4,700 animal bones found at Ugarit, only 22 were wild boar. They came from big, dangerous males that appear to have been hunted ritually. The video ties this to myth: boars run with Baal, the storm god and master of thunder and lightning, and the lion below stood for the king. An animal that rare in the bone record, mounted next to a rare metal, on an object that mattered, makes the whole axe read like a deliberate statement. That is a known pattern, and the sky-iron angle is the claim layered on top.

## The Deep Sea and Space Oddities

### Three Astronauts, One Satellite, and a Lot of Swearing in Zero-G

Dark Space, in "When High-Tech Space Tools Failed NASA Used Brute Force," covers the Intelsat rescue aboard Endeavour. It isn't a mystery so much as a spectacular engineering faceplant. I'm including it because the lesson is universal: when the fancy hardware fails, you use people.

Per the transcript, the capture bar "refused to stay attached" to the satellite. So three astronauts went out and stopped Intelsat with their hands. Their EVA ran 8 hours and 29 minutes, which made it the longest American spacewalk to that point, and it was the first three-person spacewalk in history. They caught nearly 9,000 pounds of spacecraft by hand. I get nervous carrying a Costco flat of seltzer.

The sequence matters. Everything the hardware was supposed to do got done afterward: the capture bar, the robotic arm, and the replacement motor all "eventually did the job NASA designed them to do." What changed, the video says, was the order. The humans got the satellite stable first, giving the machines something they could finally control. Pierre Thuot, Richard Hieb, and Thomas Akers then went from catching to prep work, while Melnick lowered Intelsat toward the new motor. They hit a wiring problem between the satellite and its replacement stage, fixed it, secured the two together, and prepped the combined spacecraft for release. Even the ASIM hardware, flown to test assembly methods for the planned Space Station Freedom, ended up as the work site that anchored the three astronauts.

I know this one personally. Rule of Acquisition #54 is "Never buy anything you can't sell," and these three spent eight and a half hours proving you also can't return a satellite you've already caught.

## Esoteric and Religious History

### The Second Layer of the Tunnel of Light, or: Please Do Not Take Notes

Library of Thoth's "Tunnel of Light Has a Second Layer" is where I put on gloves. The video's thesis, as far as the excerpt shows, is that the near-death "tunnel of light" is followed by a hidden second layer, where souls who enter are assigned a new loop by the Anunnaki. It frames the virtues of selflessness, compassion, and service as "weaponized," and says the sovereign soul sees the second layer for what it is: "an offer you're allowed to refuse."

The Emerald Tablet is a genuine, famous text in the Western esoteric tradition, and the Anunnaki are real figures in ancient Mesopotamian religion. But the transcript quotes the Emerald Tablet as warning about the "second layer," and the reincarnation-trap scheme of "architects" harvesting your goodness. I can't tell you the tablet says that, and the video offers no verification in the portion I have. The structure is a modern theory about souls, built from ancient names. Nothing here establishes that anyone ever believed it before the present era.

I'll note the rhetorical trick, because it's a good one. Any mainstream tradition that promises guidance after death becomes part of the trap, and even the people who think they've seen through it "instinctively move toward the light." So skepticism is evidence, disagreement is evidence, and agreement is evidence. It's unfalsifiable, which is the polite word. ## Strange Engineering: The Weirdest Thing the Air Force Ever Built

Dark Skies, in "The Weirdest Thing the Air Force Ever Built," is the XP-79B story, and the excerpt picks up in the middle of a mess. Test pilot Crosby had already flown the tailless controls, survived the prone cockpit when the aircraft stopped responding, and flown with rocket machinery packed around his body. He'd also kicked his way out of one Northrop flying wing after it turned upside down and landed beside the wreckage. The second time, the video says, "the wing reached him first." The Army Air Forces had canceled a program soon after, because there wasn't enough evidence left to prove why. One hypothesis in the transcript is that the trim mechanism overpowered Crosby's control inputs, and the video says plainly that there wasn't enough left to prove it.

The XP-79B was a full-size jet of nearly 8,700 pounds, with a welded magnesium shell, twin jets, and a prone cockpit. Its basic control system had been developed on wooden gliders weighing thousands of pounds less. Nobody had yet learned what it would do if it departed controlled flight. Ten days after Japan formally surrendered, it was ready for its first flight. It accelerated across the lakebed at Muroc, which the transcript spells "Maroc," and for approximately 15 minutes it moved normally through the sky. Crosby climbed, turned, and pushed it through increasingly demanding maneuvers, and Northrop later wrote that the takeoff was normal.

The excerpt ends there, and so do I. I won't tell you how the flight ended, because the transcript doesn't. I'll say only that a welded magnesium airframe, a pilot lying on his stomach, and a control system proven on gliders is exactly the setup Ripley would look at and say, "Game over, man." The machine spirit was not consulted.
