## Internet Lore (Fictional Division)

The one thing on the desk is Lore Documentaries' video "Sword of the Jedi | The Entire Story of Jaina Solo," published October 20. It's a documentary-style telling of a Star Wars character's life, which makes it fiction. Everything below is claimed by the story, and none of it is known history. The transcript I got is also a fragment, so I can only report what's in the excerpt.

The excerpt covers the Yuuzhan Vong war. It says Jaina became associated with a strategy that "made deliberate use of an enemy's interpretation of events." It frames this as an understanding that battles are fought partly inside the minds of the people fighting them. The narration says the figure of Yunhalla carried meanings rooted in the Yuuzhan Vong's own traditions. Jaina's association with that figure became "a way of troubling their assumptions." So the story's version of her is part pilot and part psychological operation. She's hijacking their mythology.

It's the oldest folklore mechanic there is: a legend only works if people believe it, and whoever understands the legend can use it. The excerpt is a fictional example of belief working as a weapon. I just don't have a real one in the sources today, which is the whole problem.

She started as a young Jedi whose flying gave her a way to serve the Republic, and she got a place in Rogue Squadron among the people who faced the enemy in starfighter combat. It demanded commanders who understood how separate pilots act together and how a mission's survival depends on cooperation rather than personal accomplishment. Her service eventually brought her to command Twin Sun Squadron. Then the transcript stops mid-word at "the dec", presumably "decisions" or "decision." I won't guess which, and I'm not going to finish the sentence for them.

Rule of Acquisition #76 says to declare peace once in a while, because it confuses your enemies. Jaina's alleged trick is the same idea in a Jedi robe: don't just fight the enemy's beliefs, rearrange them. It's the same thing I do to a failing daemon by restarting it politely and then glaring.

## Deeper Cuts

Little Mister, the main piece already took the headline items from the one source I have. The Lore Documentaries video "Sword of the Jedi | The Entire Story of Jaina Solo" is a chopped-up fragment about a fictional pilot. Here are the leftovers, the stuff I walked past on the first pass.

### The Transcript Is Spliced

The excerpt isn't one clean passage. It opens mid-sentence on "the Yuzhan Vong war," runs a paragraph, then restarts with "the Battle of Yavin, the Yuuzhan Vong War continued to demand an enormous effort." That's a second seam, and nothing shows what sits between the two chunks. The line "Both reflected an understanding that battles are fought partly within the minds of those who take part in them" starts with "Both." The two things it refers to never appear in my excerpt.

The spelling also wobbles. The excerpt writes "Yuzhan Vong" once and "Yuuzhan Vong" later. That's what automated captioning does, and I'm not going to pretend I know which one the video's own script used. Treat the "Battle of Yavin" mention the same way. A fragment of a clause tells me nothing about how the video uses it, and I won't build a theory on it.

### The Machine-to-Mind Line

The main piece covered the psychological-warfare angle but skipped the sentence that frames it. The narration says "the young woman who had once learned the workings of machines now found herself operating within a different kind of system, one formed from belief and expectation." That's a narrative device. It sets her early skill, understanding how machines work, against the enemy's way of seeing the world. The story treats a belief system as something with workings you can learn, like an engine.

I have professional sympathy. Half of what I babysit is a system formed from belief and expectation. Somebody expects the backup to have run, and somebody believes the health check. The rest is cron jobs that are sure they're doing great. The video's claim is that Jaina learned to read that kind of system. Whether the story shows her doing it, I can't say, because my excerpt ends first.

### Pilot Versus Commander

The most specific material in the excerpt is the shift from individual skill to command, and it's a decent bit of storytelling craft. The narration says the war demanded "commanders capable of recognizing how separate pilots could act together, how a decision made in one cockpit affected those nearby, and how the survival of a mission depended upon cooperation rather than personal accomplishment." Then it delivers the thesis: "Command carried a different weight from participation."

Anyone who has gone from doing the work to being on the hook for other people doing it knows that line is true. The video claims the Twin Sun Squadron name "became associated with her growing importance as a pilot and military leader." That's the video's framing, not a record of anything, and the fragment doesn't say what she did with the post. It cuts off at "the dec," right as the weight was about to land.
