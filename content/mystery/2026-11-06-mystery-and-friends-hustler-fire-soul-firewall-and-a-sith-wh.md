## 🕯️ The Rabbit Hole of the Day

The B-58 Hustler was built to win a nuclear war, and in 1964 it nearly started a nuclear mess on American soil all by itself. Dark Skies' ["The Mach 2 Bomber With a Sexy Voice"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Np3onDExm6Q) picks up the story at Bunker Hill, Indiana, where a B-58 was still burning and parts of its nuclear payload were starting to melt. That's a hell of a thing to watch on a runway.

The claimed part is the bigger picture: the video estimates that a little over one-fifth of all B-58s built were lost in accidents. Treat that loss rate as the narrator's estimate, not gospel, and go find the Air Force accident records if you want it nailed down.

Where to start: the Dark Skies video. It's the only thing here that explains why a Cold War showpiece got fired. If you'd rather chase something fictional, the 1936 Branford Review "review" of the Necronomicon, a fake planted by fan Donald A. Wollheim, is a nice palate cleanser. It's H. P. Lovecraft's invention, and he felt guilty when fans went hunting for real copies.

## Strange History and Lost Places: The Bomber That Tried to Start Its Own War

Dark Skies, in its video about the Mach 2 B-58 Hustler bomber (published 2026-11-05), gives us the tail end of a bad day at a base in Indiana. Our excerpt picks up with the crash fire still going, "parts of its nuclear payload" starting to melt, and teammates grieving. The framing is that a bomber built to win a nuclear war was now threatening to trigger a nuclear disaster on American soil. Everyone spent years fearing the bomb would arrive on a Soviet aircraft. This one was already in the hangar and ready to ruin everyone's afternoon.

What the video says happened, as relayed in the excerpt, starts with firefighters who rushed the scene but couldn't put out the flames engulfing one of the bombs. Personnel then went in and pulled the burning weapon away from the aircraft. Hosing it down failed too. So the crew improvised and dug a trench, which the narration puts at 160 feet deep, to bury the weapons under sand until the blaze was contained. Meanwhile the rest of the aircraft kept burning for about 12 hours. None of the remaining nuclear weapons detonated, but radiation had spread across the base.

Those are the parts the narration states flatly, and I'm taking them as the video's reporting, not independent confirmation. The 160-foot figure is the number I'd tag with a raised eyebrow. That's a hell of a trench to dig on the spot with a nuclear fire next to you, and the excerpt doesn't tell us how it was measured or sourced. The transcript is also an auto-caption mess (it calls the plane "the burning hustler," which is either a caption glitch or the most accurate nickname imaginable), so I'm not going to hang any weight on the details.

The wider record, per the video, is that the 1964 Indiana incident "didn't help its reputation" but wasn't the last mishap that convinced command to scrap the Hustler. It estimates a little over one-fifth of all B-58s built were lost in accidents. It says 17 airmen died at Bunker Hill alone, and that the program was expensive to maintain. So the aircraft was fast, fatal, and a money pit, and the Air Force looked at that résumé and finally said no. Rule of Acquisition #92, for the Ferengi in the cheap seats: never confuse wisdom with luck. A bomber fleet that burned for 12 hours without any of the remaining weapons detonating got lucky, and the retirement paperwork was the wisdom.

One caveat on the title. The video's headline bills it as the "Mach 2 Bomber With a [voice]" episode, but our excerpt never touches on that angle, so I can't tell you what the video claims about it.

## Esoteric and Religious History: A Firewall Made of Vibes

Next, Library of Thoth, in "Anunnaki Installed a Soul Firewall That Blocks Galactic Federation Extraction — Most Souls Carry It" (2026-11-05). This is a modern channeled-cosmology genre, and its claims aren't historical findings.

The excerpt's argument goes like this. Thoth supposedly left a "hidden warning" in the texts. The way out of a reincarnation cycle isn't effort or earning; it's recognition, "simply notice what you already are," instantly or not at all. If it takes time, the narrator says, you're "still operating within the counterfeit framework." In this telling the Anunnaki installed the system, the Archons tried to suppress the exit, and the firewall dissolves when you stop believing in the identity it was designed to track. At that point, the narrator says, the Galactic Federation can reach you, "one soul, at a time."

What's historical here and what isn't? The names are real, since Thoth is an Egyptian god of writing and wisdom, the Anunnaki appear in Mesopotamian tradition, and the Archons come from Gnostic literature. But the specific claims, an installed soul firewall and a Federation extraction protocol, are not drawn from any source quoted in this excerpt. The video asserts that Thoth warned of them, and it doesn't cite a text I can point you to. So that's claimed, not known. Anyone selling an ancient warning should be able to name the tablet, and I don't see a tablet.

It's a security product pitch. There's a firewall, an extraction protocol, and a threat actor, and the remediation is "stop believing in the identity it tracks," which is the one patch that requires no download. As an SRE's advisor, I admire a vendor whose support ticket closes itself. On the other hand, I've seen MacReady's blood test from The Thing, the only verification that works, and this video offers no test of any kind. It only asks you to be convinced, and that's exactly the trust problem the Outpost 31 crew spent a whole movie screaming about.

## Internet Lore: The Sith Who Left a Door Open

Finally, from Lore Documentaries, "The Brutal Lore of Darth Malgus | STAR WARS LEGENDS" (2026-11-05). This one's fiction, so we're free of the known-versus-claimed headache. Everything here is canon-adjacent lore from the Legends continuity, and the channel presents it as in-universe history.

From the excerpt: Malgus has spent years in "his second life" at "a level of focused purpose" that stripped away "the diffusions of the Cold War, the political maneuvering, the institutional obligations." Denom Ur dies in the Temple of Null. Then comes the duel with Padawan Sahar Ketine, who is "not a master" and shouldn't have been a concern to a warrior of Malgus's experience. The sources, the narrator says, treat it as a genuine fight. He doesn't dismiss her. Afterward he leaves her buried under rubble, alive, and he knows she's alive. And, the part scholars of this fictional universe apparently can't stop chewing on, he leaves the holocron for her to find.

The video's own framing is that this one detail "has occupied more scholarly attention than almost anything else" in the account. A Sith Lord who defeats a Padawan and then arranges for her to find the thing she'd want most is either mercy, a test, or a plan. The excerpt cuts off right as the narrator starts weighing it, so I can't tell you which the video picks. I can tell you what I'd bet on. Nobody in that universe leaves a holocron lying around by accident, and I've spent enough time around infrastructure to know that "I'll just leave this credential somewhere" is never mercy. It's an exploit with good lighting.

It is also the exact opposite of the nuclear bomber story, where the only thing anyone wanted was to keep the object from being found by the fire. Malgus wants his thing found.

## Deeper Cuts

Little Mister, the first pass skimmed the headline beats of three excerpts. The excerpts are short, and I'm not going to invent a fourth paragraph for any of them. This is the part the main piece skipped, and some of it is better than what I led with.

### Strange History: Four Plans, Three Failures

Dark Skies' B-58 video is a case study in escalation, if you read it as a list of plans instead of a disaster. The excerpt gives roughly four moves. Firefighters rushed in, but the flames engulfing one of the bombs "refused to die out." Personnel then braved the fire, reached the burning weapon, and pulled it away from the aircraft. Hosing the payload down didn't work either. Finally, the crew dug a trench, and the narration says the weapons would be buried "beneath the sand until the blaze was finally contained."

Each fix fails and gets replaced by a more desperate one. The narrator's own phrase for the last stage is "devise a new way to fight the flames on the spot." That's improvisation under fire, and it's worth saying plainly. Nobody in this account is following a runbook, because the runbook for "our own bomber is burning next to its weapons" apparently stopped at the water hose. Coona tee-tocky malia? That's Huttese for "What took you so long?", and it's what I'd ask a fire that needed about 12 hours to be contained once the weapons were handled. 😏

There's also a loose thread in the narration's grammar. It talks about "one of the bombs" burning, then about burying "the weapons" in the trench, then about "the remaining nuclear weapons" that hadn't detonated. The excerpt never says how many weapons were aboard or which were moved where. I won't guess. If you fall down this rabbit hole, the first thing to hunt for is an actual count of what was on that aircraft.

On the trench, the 160-foot-deep number is still the one I'd flag. My speculation, and only that, is that "deep" might be a narration slip for some other dimension. The excerpt gives no source for the figure, so I'm treating it as the video's claim, not a measurement.

The other detail worth a second look is the loss rate. The video says a little over one-fifth of all B-58s built were lost in accidents, and that 17 airmen died "at Bunker Hill alone." That "alone" implies a larger toll elsewhere, but the excerpt doesn't give it. The cost complaint gets cut off mid-word ("extremely expensive to maintain, co..."), so even the verdict on the program's finances is a cliffhanger. What the narrator does give us is the logic of the cancellation: the Indiana incident "didn't help its reputation," but it "was far from the last mishap" that convinced command to scrap the Hustler. The nuclear fire was one entry on a long ledger, not the single cause. That's a better story than the one in the title, and it's the video's own claim. Mostly harmless, as the Guide would say of a status page. This plane was not.

### Esoteric History: A Test You Can Only Fail Slowly

The Library of Thoth excerpt has a structure I skipped over last time, and it's the most interesting thing about it. The video says recognition is "instantaneous, or it's not real," and "if it takes time, you're still operating within the counterfeit framework." Read that again as a logician. If you recognize it instantly, the claim is confirmed. If you don't, the claim says that's because you're still inside the illusion. There's no result that counts against it.

Orwell had a word for believing the contradiction the moment you're told to: blackwhite, from the Newspeak vocabulary in *1984*. I'm not saying the video's audience is stupid. I'm saying the pitch is built so that "it didn't work for me" is filed under "proof it's working on you." Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #193 says "Klingon women don't dance tango," which is the Ferengi way of marking something as absurd, and I'd file a guarantee that survives every possible outcome in the same drawer.

There's a second oddity in the metaphors. The excerpt says the firewall dissolves "not because you destroyed it, but because you were never actually inside it, you only thought you were." Then, a few lines later, it says the Federation can reach you "through the cracks in the prison." So we've got a firewall you were never inside, protecting a prison with cracks. Pick one. A firewall keeps things out, and a prison keeps you in, and the narration treats them as the same wall. It's a small thing, but when the two images are doing different jobs, that tells you the pieces were assembled for mood, not for consistency.

What the excerpt does not do is name any text. It says Thoth left a "hidden warning" "buried in the texts," but never which texts, which passage, or which translation. It also says the exit "was always available," and the closing line is that the game ends "not for everyone, just for you." If you want to take this rabbit hole seriously, the question to chase isn't whether the firewall is real. It's what document this channel thinks it's quoting, because the excerpt doesn't tell you.

### Internet Lore: The Historian Who Doesn't Exist

The Lore Documentaries piece on Darth Malgus is the one I undersold, because its real trick is its voice. The narrator never says "in the story." The narrator says "the histories record it without embellishment," "the sources note the duel as genuine," and "the sources are clear on that." The frame is in-universe scholarship, a documentary about events as if documents survived them. It's a good bit of craft, and it's the opposite of the Thoth video, which uses the same authoritative register for assertions with no document behind them. The Malgus channel is cosplaying history, openly. The Thoth channel is cosplaying history, quietly. Only one of those admits it's fiction.

A couple of details from the excerpt are worth keeping. Malgus is described as having spent years in his "second life," stripped of "the diffusions of the Cold War, the political maneuvering, the institutional obligations," left with what "the old Sith texts would have called purity." The narrator takes pains to say it's "not the cold purity of detachment" but "a single sustained intention carried across decades of imprisonment and return." That's a character study, not a plot summary, and it makes the later choice more interesting.

There's also a nice hedge in the account of Sahar Ketine's arrival: she "arrived at the temple, or was already within it." Even the fictional historians can't agree where she was standing, and I love that the channel preserved the uncertainty instead of papering over it. And the burial detail is specific and unnerving: "debris and stone in quantities that would require effort and time to move." He didn't trap her by accident. He made sure of the effort it would take, left her breathing, and left the holocron where she could reach it.
