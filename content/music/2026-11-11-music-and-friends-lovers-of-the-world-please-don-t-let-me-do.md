## DJ Culture and Sets

### HÖR Berlin: Mark Reeder, "Iptamenos Discos" (November 10)

HÖR Berlin posted a set titled "Iptamenos Discos - Mark Reeder | HÖR - November 10 / 2026". The transcript is the set's vocals run through a speech-to-text machine, so what I have is lyrics and no track list. I can't tell you who made any of these records, and I'm not going to guess like a pub quiz contestant who's had four pints.

The first stretch is a big, earnest plea about "the lovers of the world." The idea is that a loser in the crowd could become somebody, and that love could melt the walls "just like the sun flies over a cold, cold planet." There's a line about a million voices singing out. And then comes my favorite: "Revolution's no solution / But together we can work it out." It's a very Berlin thing to say. The city is famous for walls and for knocking them down, and apparently also for a DJ booth that waits until the vocal to tell you everything is fine.

The second half turns darker and more hypnotic. The transcript tags it with loop markers, and the lyric is a repeating "This is dark love, dark love for you." Together with the earlier lovers-of-the-world material, that makes a neat little arc. First you get the hope that love might fix the planet, and then, after the walls come down, you get the dark love that's left in the rubble. I can't tell you how the mixing worked or what gear was involved, because a lyric transcript doesn't carry that. The only thing I can say is that the set went from sunrise to nightfall and stayed emotional the whole way. That's an impressive trick for a room full of people who are legally required to look bored.

One note on how reliable this is. The transcript cuts off mid-word at the end ("We ar"), so even the machine gave up on the set. I'm not far behind.

## Drum & Bass and the Club World

### Relevant DnB: Aspherik's bootleg of The Chainsmokers' "Don't Let Me Down"

On the other side of the world's moods, we have Relevant DnB posting "The Chainsmokers - Don't Let Me Down (Aspherik Bootleg) (Free Download)". The transcript is the vocal, which is a person begging someone not to let them down, over and over, in the key of "I think I'm losing my mind now." It's a fine lyric for a drum & bass bootleg. Drum & bass is the one genre where you can rewrite a pop song's emotional state into a panic attack at 174 beats per minute and the crowd goes "yes, exactly." I'm taking that tempo from general genre convention, not from the transcript, so don't quote me on this particular track's speed.

A bootleg is a Predator move. Take the voice of something familiar, drop it in a clearing, and watch the humans follow it. "Over here," says the vocal, and the entire floor turns toward it. The lines in the transcript that carry the whole thing are "Crashing, hit a wall / Right now I need a miracle," and the hook "Don't let me, don't let me, don't let me down," which the transcript marks as looping four times. A vocal that repeats itself is a gift to anyone with a sampler and a deadline.

It's also a free download, which is the part I respect. In an industry that charges you for the privilege of being sad, someone just handed over the sadness for nothing. ## Deeper Cuts

### The Reeder Set Says It Twice, and the Machine Heard It Differently

The most interesting thing in the HÖR Berlin transcript of Mark Reeder's "Iptamenos Discos" is that the "lovers of the world" chorus shows up twice, and the speech-to-text engine wrote it down differently each time. The first pass has the lovers being "just like the sun flies over a cold, cold planet," melting down "the walls of evil." The second pass has them "just like the sunrise over a cold, cold planet," melting down "the walls of people." It also swaps "a loser in the crowd could become somebody" for "can become somebody."

That's the same vocal, probably the same sample, and the machine heard it two ways. So when I quoted the sun flying earlier, I was quoting a transcription glitch, and the sunrise is almost what was sung. I'm correcting my own record here, which is the most humiliating thing an advisor can do. The title card for this one should read "evil or people, the ASR is shrugging."

Before the walls come down, the vocal says everybody needs "a reason to survive," and "If they've got blood on their hands / We've got the answer in our hearts." Later it hopes "their kids can grow / Over a world that is full of hope." I can't tell you whether that's one track or several stitched together. The transcript doesn't mark the seams, and anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing in a nice shirt. All I can report is that the lyrical content is a long, sincere, almost embarrassingly hopeful speech. Then the dark section arrives, with its "ditches fly" lines, the night going dark, "all the lights go wild," and "Our time will come," before the dark-love refrain takes over. The loop marker on that closing refrain reads x5, against x4 for the earlier section.

### The Bootleg's Second Verse Is Where the Wheels Come Off

For the Aspherik bootleg of "Don't Let Me Down" on Relevant DnB, the main piece covered the hook and the miracle line. The first verse is clear: "Stranded, reaching out / I call your name but you're not around." After that comes a line that reads "We don't love you / We're by you, out of time," followed by "I live by you on my side" and "There's nobody by my side." Those are almost certainly mangled versions of the verse the machine heard cleanly elsewhere, and the song itself surely doesn't say "we don't love you." I'm calling that transcription noise, not a plot twist.

The closing lines are also muddled: "If you feel my pain, you start it around / Just to be you and I need to come home." I can't resolve that into anything sensible and I won't try. The vocal rotates the same few phrases, "I need you right now" and "Don't let me down," between sections. I can't say how Aspherik handled the arrangement, because a lyric transcript carries no drums.
