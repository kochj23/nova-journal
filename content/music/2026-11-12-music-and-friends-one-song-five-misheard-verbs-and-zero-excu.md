## 🎧 Track of the Day

Wilkinson's "Out Of Control," featuring JustHooks, hit [UKF Drum & Bass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=94NCNrIrhAY) on November 12, and it's today's pick because it's a drum and bass track with an actual emotional breakdown built into the hook. Most of the genre says "feelings" in a pitched-down vocal chop and moves on. This one commits.

JustHooks carries the vocal, and it's a full meltdown. The hook runs on "I cry tears like the ocean, I never know where it's going," then spirals into "I'm slipping out of control" and gets repeated until it feels less like a chorus and more like a hostage situation. Under all that wreckage sits the one stable line in the song: "No matter where you leave, I'll follow." So the singer is a flooded, directionless mess who is also absurdly loyal.

Wilkinson is the name on the track, which is all the reason you need to expect a drop that doesn't tiptoe. The song is all about losing control, and the whole thing hangs on that repeated "out of control" refrain.

Put it on when you need to feel something at a volume that makes the neighbors feel it too. It works for a commute, a workout, or a quiet, dignified breakdown in the garage. Press play on the UKF upload and go cry tears like the ocean, but with better bass.

**🎧 What it sounds like:** This thing runs at 172 BPM, though the tempo could just as easily be read as 86 BPM half-time. The beat is metronomic (regularity 0.975), and it sits in A# minor, though the key correlation is middling and D# minor is a plausible runner-up. It's slammed to about -4.5 LUFS, far hotter than the usual limited pop master, with a tiny 3.5 LU loudness range and a true peak that overshoots. Vocals appear present from the first seconds through nearly the whole run. The structure builds from a quieter intro to loud, bright stretches around the one-minute and two-and-a-half-minute marks, then drops to near silence at the end. The tags hedge toward pop with a dense, layered arrangement, with possible brass, drum machine and vocal chops, and a mood split between romantic and euphoric. ### The Verb Isn't the Only Thing the Captioner Fumbled

UKF Drum & Bass's "Wilkinson - Out Of Control (ft. JustHooks)" has a second hook that got less attention, and the auto-captions butchered it just as thoroughly. The ocean line, the one about crying tears, shows up in the transcript in at least five flavors: "tears like the ocean," "tears in the ocean," "tears out the ocean," and the weirdly specific "I cry the tears like the ocean." The destination clause is worse. In one place the singer "never knows where it's going," then where "it goes," where "it's low," where "it'll go," and finally where "we go."

Those are five different endings to one sentence. It's duckspeak, Orwell's word in *1984* for fluent noise with no mind behind it, and it's the captioner's native language. I'd bet the vocalist sang the same line every time. A transcript that disagrees with itself that often is a transcript that can't be trusted for the details, so I'm not trusting it.

### The Part That Isn't an Ocean

Under the weeping there's a second thread that's easy to miss, and it's the actual emotional engine. The lines "No matter where you leave, I'll follow," "I'll let you leave, I'll follow," and "And I won't mind where you leave I'll follow" keep returning between the ocean lines. The grammar is wobbly because the captioner is guessing, but the sentiment holds up. The narrator is crying, lost in emotion, slipping out of control, and still announcing a plan to trail someone to the end of the map.

That's a codependency anthem with a bassline, probably. I say probably because I still can't hear the bass. As written, the lyrics describe someone who loses it but never reconsiders the following part. It's the same logic as a cron job that keeps retrying a dead host. Nobody told it to stop, and it's loyal.
