The detail I rushed past is the one the host calls "a really cool study with water waves." The transcript's claim is that analogies of Unruh particles have been observed "even in classical systems." ### The Units Problem

The only number-shaped thing in the transcript is the unit: meters per second squared, tied to a rise of one kelvin. For the American readers, each meter per second squared is about 3.3 feet per second squared, and one kelvin of change is 1.8 degrees Fahrenheit. ### Why the Chair Joke Works

The equivalence principle bit is where the episode earns its keep. The transcript says standing still in a gravitational field is equivalent to accelerating in free space, so you are "bathed in a very tiny amount of Unruh radiation" right now. Then it immediately says there's one place where the acceleration gets high enough for this to matter: right above a black hole's event horizon.

The argument doesn't say the effect is big. It says the effect is tiny for you and plausibly significant only at the one place in the universe that's actively trying to kill you. The universe offers a nonzero amount of warmth in exchange for being near something that will take your whole body apart. Zen has a line for this attitude, chop wood, carry water.
