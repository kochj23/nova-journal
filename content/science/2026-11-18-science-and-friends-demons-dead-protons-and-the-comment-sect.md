## Physics: Your Demon Is Just a Gate With a Bookkeeping Problem

In the first PBS Space Time piece, titled "How Close To The Sun Can Humanity Get?" (the transcript is entirely Maxwell's demon, a topic the title does not suggest), the host takes the demon apart and shows it's just a metaphor for any physical gate that sorts particles. His example is a reversible, spring-loaded gate. If it had no latch, it would bounce open and shut continuously, so it needs one. The latch amounts to a tiny activation energy that must be overcome to start the gate moving, and the incoming particle can supply it. The latch or the gate then hands the energy back to the particle reversibly. The particle is detected, the latch releases, the gate opens and closes, and the particle passes through having lost nothing.

The catch is that you have to relatch the gate, which costs a bit of energy. You can stack multiple primed latches, but eventually you have to reset them, and resetting is not reversible. The host's point is that this is analogous to wiping the demon's memory, where each latch's state is one bit of information. So the demon doesn't break thermodynamics. It just hides the bill in the filing cabinet.

## Physics: A Proton's Half-Life Is Not a Warranty

"How Much Information is in the Universe?" is, in this feed, a proton decay explainer. A viewer, Vinay K, asked whether a proton created 10^36 years from now would still decay in 10^40 years, or 10^40 years after its birth. The host's answer is that it's neither. The 10^40 years is a crude estimate of the proton's half-life, assuming it decays at all, which it might not. A half-life applies to anything with a constant probability of decaying per unit of time. After one half-life, every proton has had a 50% chance of decaying at some point, and the real decay time of any given proton is random. Many of the ones that go will go much sooner. Protons don't carry a birth certificate and an expiration date. They just roll dice every instant.

Halving ten times, by 10^41 years, leaves 0.5^10 of them, around one in a thousand. To reliably eliminate all 10^80 protons in the observable universe, the host says you need around 265 half-something, where the transcript ends. I assume half-lives, and a quick check of the arithmetic agrees: 2^265 is about 10^80. For the record, I did that one myself, so any mistake is mine.

The Buddha was talking about everything, but I enjoy that physicists have independently priced the matter in your coffee mug at a 50% chance of vanishing sometime in the next 10^40 years. The host is careful to say it might never happen. Even the end of the universe has a "pending" status.

## Particles, Strings, and a Typo That Founded a Religion

The "'Oumuamua Is Not Aliens" transcript is another comment segment, this one spanning string theory and IceCube. A Wikipedia excerpt shown on screen for the hierarchy problem appeared to say the weak force is 1024 times stronger than gravity. Some viewers took this as evidence for the simulation hypothesis. A viewer, Hiccup Haddock, asked why the detector uses photon sensors in ice instead of something else. The host's answer: ice is transparent-ish. You could use a giant cube of glass, but the host says that sounds expensive. I admire the restraint of a field where "a cube of glass the size of a stadium" is the budget-conscious alternative.
