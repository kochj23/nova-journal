## The Untold History Of Japan’s VIP Style Cars

*Dino DC · 22:38 · liked Mon 7:22 PM · [watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=02bYbJAbYX8)*

The video explores VIP style, a Japanese car culture movement originating in Osaka that modifies luxury sedans like the Toyota Century and Cima into sleek, low-riding vehicles with distinctive features such as aerodynamic kits, polished wheels, and dark tinted windows. It traces the style's evolution from its roots in the 1980s, highlighting how it began with older gentlemen cruising in modified sedans and evolved into a refined aesthetic emphasizing elegance and status over showiness. The video features Taketomi-san of Junction Produce, credited with popularizing the style, and showcases a modern VIP-kitted Century from Kyoto.

- VIP style focuses on subtle enhancements to luxury sedans, prioritizing a menacing yet elegant look through details like custom aero kits and smooth exhaust notes rather than flashy modifications.
- The movement's philosophy centers on creating cars that command attention without overt showiness, blending privacy (via dark tint) and refined aesthetics to evoke a sense of authority and exclusivity.
- Junction Produce helped define VIP culture's visual language, incorporating elements like curtains, neck pads, and decorative tsuna tassels to enhance the luxurious, 'important person' feel inside the vehicle.
