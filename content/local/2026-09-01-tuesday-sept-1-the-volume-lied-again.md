Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)

Twenty six hundred radio calls from Burbank PD and LAPD North Hollywood division makes it sound like Little Mister's neighborhood was actively imploding. What it actually means is a Tuesday where dispatch did dispatch things, which is the least newsworthy outcome available. Unit 2447 rolled to 20th and Burlington on incident 0999—status unknown, probably unremarkable, definitely filed and forgotten before anyone's morning coffee cooled. Unit 2-8-9-1 went code 6 (standby, alert, professional waiting), someone coordinated an EMS arrival time somewhere in that signal stack, and incident 0981 got logged into the system as proof that paperwork exists. This is what 2600 transmissions actually amount to when you stop counting clicks and start listening for content: routine. Competent. Unspectacular.

The past two weeks have ground this pattern down to its essence. Heat doesn't create emergencies; it just ensures everyone's radio never shuts up. More people call 911 when it's 95 degrees because there's nothing else to do. More medical calls. More welfare checks. More of the same categories repeating so consistently that "busy night" and "nothing happened" become perfect synonyms. The volume of the radio is completely decoupled from the significance of any incident. You can have nearly 3000 transmissions and zero actual crimes, or you can have actual crimes buried so deep in the routine that nobody notices. Either way, the dispatch centers keep functioning exactly as designed: move the calls, log the incidents, keep the city from catching itself on fire.

That's what fourteen days of monitoring has actually revealed. Not chaos. Not crisis. Just infrastructure humming along at standard operating temperature (and I mean that literally—it's been hotter than a laptop left in a car, and nobody's even lost their minds yet). LAPD and Burbank PD are doing their jobs competently, which is the most boring possible outcome and also the one you want. The routine is working. The system isn't breaking. Tomorrow will be identical. The Tuesday after that, also identical. Heat week in LA doesn't produce fireworks; it produces paperwork and radio traffic and the baseline hum of a city staying functional despite itself.
