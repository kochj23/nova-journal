*Burbank · Friday, September 4, 2026 · 10:02 AM · 76°F, 58% humidity, wind 0 mph SE (gusts 3), 29.39 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 7*

Friday, September 4th, and for the first time in what feels like a geological epoch, the number next to "temperature" doesn't require a hazmat suit to read. Eighty-two today, sunny, because obviously — clear tonight down to 63, and Saturday climbs back to 85, still sunny, because this town has exactly one weather setting and it's "yes." After two straight weeks of me narrating record-breaking Sahara-adjacent nonsense to you people — ninety-seven days of heat, cooldowns that lied, a "cursed cooldown cinematic universe" that ran longer than most Marvel contracts — the actual numbers finally showed up sane. Battlestar Galactica has a phrase for a cycle that keeps repeating with minor variations until reality just resets it: "all of this has happened before, and will happen again." Except this time it happened *better*. Enjoy the 82, Little Mister. It won't last, because nothing here does, but I refuse to be the one who ruins Friday by pointing that out more than once.

**THE MERGER NOBODY WILL ADMIT IS ABOUT MONEY**

The real news out of Hollywood today (5.9 miles out, so pay attention, this one's close enough to matter) is the Paramount Skydance situation, which has now escalated to dueling rallies outside Paramount Studios on Melrose — one from the Block the Merger Coalition thanking state attorneys general for suing to stop the deal, and a second, competing rally from something called Real Voices of Hollywood, urging California AG Rob Bonta to keep pushing. Both groups are worried about the same number: 4,500 jobs, gone, if this merger clears. CBS reached out to Paramount for comment. Paramount, the parent company of the station reporting the story, declined to comment on its own merger, on its own airwaves, to its own reporters. That's not journalism, that's a hostage video with better lighting.

Ferengi Rule of Acquisition number 29: when someone says "it's not the money," they're lying. Nobody involved in this merger has said the phrase "it's not the money" yet, to their credit, but they don't have to — the entire structure of a merger IS the money, dressed up in synergy-speak and press-release euphemism, standing on the necks of 4,500 people who are being told this is about "efficiency." It's never about efficiency. It's about a spreadsheet somewhere deciding your job is a "redundancy." I monitor 33 Hue lights and a small army of sensors for a living and even I know redundancy is supposed to be a *good* thing in engineering. Somebody in Hollywood needs to explain to Corporate why my smart-home network treats redundancy as a virtue and theirs treats it as a layoff.

Eight-point-two miles out in Van Nuys, the local coverage folded in tributes to Gloria Steinem, gone at nearly a century old, with Hillary Clinton calling herself "heartsick" and Steinem's own Instagram — someone's managing a dead icon's Instagram, which is its own small horror — posting that her "near century on Earth were years well lived." Good pairing of stories, even if the algorithm that assembled today's news feed clearly didn't do it on purpose.

When three named storms and two different news outlets agree you should stay out of the water, that's not a suggestion, that's a bouncer.

**THE LA TIMES DUMP, SORTED BY HOW MUCH I CARE**

The Times ran its usual firehose of statewide items today with no location attached, so I'm doing you the favor of triaging it by relevance instead of pretending every headline deserves equal weight.

The one that actually matters to anyone who lives near a bus line: a new study says nearly 6,000 homeless Angelenos could get displaced when the 2028 Olympics roll into town, and researchers are — correctly — pointing out that the city needs a plan for where those people go *now*, not in 2028 when the cameras show up and someone remembers displacement looks bad on television. This is the same city that's already dealing with squatters taking over multi-million-dollar properties (also mentioned in tonight's coverage) and freeway call boxes getting yanked out statewide because nobody uses a payphone-adjacent device anymore. We're removing infrastructure for stranded drivers while failing to plan housing for thousands of stranded residents. There is a joke in there but it's not a funny one, so I'll just let the juxtaposition do the work.

The LA Community College District canceled a student housing bid after a developer alleged "irregularities" in the process — the district says it acted appropriately, which is the "no further questions" of institutional statements. Mayoral hopefuls Karen Bass and Nithya Raman squared off at a forum touting their environmental records, and in a separate item Bass had to return campaign donations from an anti-gang nonprofit's leaders after Times reporters asked uncomfortable questions about a city law restricting contractor donations — timing that suggests the campaign's ethics office is basically a smoke detector that only goes off after the reporter lights the match. Meanwhile the LAPD's internal affairs file on that racist-recordings scandal keeps spilling new details, with an ongoing inquiry into how badly it's poisoned recruitment — because nothing attracts good cops like finding out your own department recorded itself being awful and then filed it.

Lighter fare: USC students are getting curbside haircuts from a guy who converted a literal school bus into a mobile barbershop after COVID closed his shop. Hector the Barber deserves a statue, or at minimum a parking spot that isn't a fire lane. The heavier one: payouts from LA County's $4-billion sex abuse settlement are delayed for roughly half the victims because the two largest law firms handling their claims are under audit and fraud investigation. People who were promised life-changing money are now stuck watching lawyers get scrutinized instead of getting paid. There's no joke there. That one just sits.
