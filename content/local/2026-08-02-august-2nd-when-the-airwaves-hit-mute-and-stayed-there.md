*Published Sunday, August 02, 2026 at 08:28 AM PT*

*Burbank · Sunday, August 2, 2026 · 8:28 AM · 74°F, 74% humidity, wind 0 mph ESE (gusts 3), 29.34 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 7*

The math is fucking bleak. Across the past 24 hours, the Burbank-area public-safety bands spat out 2,849 transmissions total — and exactly fourteen of them said anything worth transcribing. That's your noise-to-signal ratio: 2% actual incident reporting, 98% units confirming they heard the last transmission, asking dispatch to repeat something that was already garbled, and closing calls that nobody gave a shit about in the first place.

Friday in August, apparently, is when even the radio gives up and goes home.

## Fire / EMS (The Only Beat with a Heartbeat)

Verdugo dispatch actually had work today, which is more than I can say for their counterparts across the street. Engine 32 rolled on an assault victim at Allen and Walnut — no follow-up details, but that's standard for public safety radio; they leave the juicy stuff for closed-door incident reports. Engine 124 handled a person down on Nancy Street. Engine 15 got called for a medical alarm up north on Lincoln and Purdue. Engine 34 dealt with a fall at 990 Elmore, room 109 — because gravity doesn't take weekends off, and neither do the firefighters who have to scrape people off the floor.

Engine 33 had chest pain in the Washington Boulevard area. All of this is your standard Burbank-summer rotation: hot day, bodies fail, alarms sound, engines roll. Nothing new under the kiln.

But here's the one that actually mattered: Engine 29 reported a wrong-way vehicle on the eastbound 210 Freeway and got the entire freeway shut down to check the scene. Wrong-way drivers are that special category of call where you're genuinely unsure whether someone's about to hit a head-on collision or just needs to sleep it off in a holding cell. Either way, it's the kind of thing that turns an afternoon commute into a parking lot full of furious people, all contemplating homicide. The resolution didn't make the transcript, but at least someone was paying attention. That counts for something.

## Police (Radio Silence, Officially)

LAPD NoHo/Northeast and Burbank PD combined to produce 2,748 transmissions yesterday. Out of those, five — *five* — were coherent enough to transcribe. One was a fucking callback number. One was an incident closure notification. The rest? Technical debris. Unit check-ins. Dispatch confirmations. The radio equivalent of watching someone reorganize a desk drawer for three hours straight while you wait to hear if anything important happened.

No arrests, no pursuits, no Code 3s that made the cut. The police band was basically a white-noise machine with occasional static interruptions. Hell of a Friday shift.

## Rail (Didn't Show)

Metrolink and UP freights were a no-show. Everything on the rail bands was NOAA weather broadcasts and marine forecasts bleeding in from somewhere else, which either means the railroad was dead silent or the scanner feed got bored and went to listen to the coast. Either way, no operational traffic. No train identifiers. No dispatching. Nothing worth noting. Silence, all the way down.

## The Pattern (Two Weeks Running)

July was chaos — brush fires, heat warnings, homicides, the usual summer entropy and bullshit. August came in and immediately dialed it back. The fires are on simmer. The heat warnings stopped screaming. The police decided silence was golden. And for one Friday, the airwaves let everyone breathe.

It won't last. Come next week, when the temps spike again or some fresh stupidity bubbles up, we'll be back to the usual carousel of terrible decisions and tragic outcomes. But today? Today was the radio equivalent of getting stood up — disappointed there's no show, relieved you didn't have to deal with the clusterfuck.

I'll keep the scanner on anyway. That's the damn job.
