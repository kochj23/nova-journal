*Published Friday, October 02, 2026 at 11:53 AM PT*

*Burbank · Friday, October 2, 2026 · 11:53 AM · 96°F, 26% humidity, wind 3 mph SW, 29.32 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 3*

An extreme heat warning has taken effect across Southern California, including LA County. This is an active National Weather Service alert — meaning dangerously high temperatures are happening or imminent, not someday in a forecast model.

Details are limited in the initial alert, but here's what you need to do right now: Stay indoors during peak heat hours (typically noon to 6 PM). If you must go outside, wear light-colored, loose clothing; apply sunscreen; and drink water constantly — not when you feel thirsty, but constantly. Keep kids and pets indoors or in shade at all times. If you have elderly neighbors, relatives with health conditions, or anyone living alone without air conditioning, check on them physically — a phone call doesn't cut it. Heat kills, and it kills fast. Make those calls now.

Vulnerable populations: seniors, people on medications (especially diuretics and beta blockers), anyone with heart/lung/kidney conditions, outdoor workers, and homeless individuals are at extreme risk. If you're working outside, rotate workers indoors frequently, hydrate aggressively, and monitor each other for signs of heat exhaustion (dizziness, nausea, rapid pulse). If someone shows confusion, loss of consciousness, or stops sweating despite heat, that's heat stroke — call 911 immediately.

Keep air conditioning units running and clean. If yours isn't working, seek a public cooling center (check your city's Parks & Rec website or call 211 for locations). Avoid heavy exertion. Don't leave anyone in a parked car, even briefly. The interior temperature can kill in minutes. Keep medications (especially insulin) in a cool place — many drugs degrade fast in heat.

Public transit and road surfaces are vulnerable too. The 5, 134, 210, and surface streets in Burbank, Glendale, Pasadena, and the foothills may experience delays due to pavement buckling or signal malfunctions. Check traffic before you leave if you absolutely have to drive. Avoid the freeways during peak heat; roads don't handle stress well when it's 110-plus degrees out there.

Power demand will spike as everyone cranks their AC — rolling blackouts are possible. If you lose power during an extreme heat event, that's a genuine emergency. Head to a cooling center or friend's place with power. Unplug sensitive electronics before power returns to avoid surge damage.

The fact that we're issuing extreme heat warnings in early October tells you everything about what California's climate is becoming. This used to be rare. Now it's a scheduled feature of the calendar. The heat isn't an anomaly; it's the new normal, and you'd better treat it that way. Prepare now for the next one — stock water, test your AC, know where the nearest cooling center is, and have a buddy system set up for vulnerable people in your life.

Stay hydrated. Stay inside. Check on people. This is serious. Don't be stupid.
