## LAPD / Burbank PD

Two thousand five hundred and twenty-three radio transmissions to say essentially nothing—which, honestly, speaks volumes about Burbank's Friday night ambitions. Out of the static, we got a family dispute on Dexter Street (because someone's air conditioning died and they decided *each other* were the problem), a couple of guys apparently settling a disagreement the old-fashioned way at South Alvarado (fists first, questions never), and something at the airport terminals involving a blind elderly passenger being tracked by a Skycap. That last one would make a decent Lifetime movie if anyone had the energy to film it. The rest? Routine radio checks and end-of-shift paperwork talking to itself.

## Verdugo / Fire EMS

This is where it got real. Between the 794 transmissions Verdugo fielded, the pattern is relentless and depressing: medicine before mayhem. Medical call after medical call—diabetic emergencies, blood pressure crises, someone's poor circulation acting up on Orange Grove, an overdose on East Colorado Boulevard at Chester (because Thursday is apparently *everyone's* rock bottom). Engine 31 and 33 rolled on a possible structure fire between Lake and Hill, Engine 36 pushed Code 3 for a person not breathing at Adina Street (apartment 9—always bet on a fourth-floor walk-up or above when the sirens scream), and there was a traffic collision eastbound on the 210 at Lake with CHP already roasting the scene by the time Verdugo got the tone. One fire alarm at 125 West Olive got its moment—Engine 101 did a reset, Truck 101 got cancelled (which is fire-dispatch speak for "never mind, it's just someone's burnt toast dream"). By day's end, they were still handling miscellaneous medical calls on East Colorado Street like it was a terminal shift at a 24-hour clinic with sirens.

The trend from the last two weeks holds: summer heat doesn't bring fires and crime—it brings medical collapse. People don't rob each other in August; they call 911 because they can't remember to hydrate and their blood pressure went to hell. Every day is just medical calls screaming over a soundtrack of someone's diabetic crisis or someone else's poor choices catching up at midnight.
