## Officers Down

The day opened with two separate incidents that hit harder than a router that won't restart — two LAPD officers injured during a pursuit crash in South Los Angeles, and a Los Angeles County sheriff's deputy killed in an off-duty traffic collision. No details on severity or cause yet, but "injured during pursuit" and "killed off-duty" are the kinds of radio calls that make everyone's shift worse. Both incidents are under investigation, and if you know anything, the departments are all ears. These aren't statistics — they're people with families who saw a radio call go sideways, and that's the kind of Saturday nobody signs up for.

Long Beach had its own bloodbath: two men found dead after a suspect opened fire on police, sparking a gunfight that left everyone in that home worse off. Police returned fire; the suspect and two others in the house didn't make it out the right way. Full details are still coming, but when a domestic call escalates to gunfire, the whole neighborhood knows something went catastrophically wrong.

## Missing Child — Found Safe

An 11-year-old girl reported critically missing in LA County turned into a recovery story — she was located safely in Compton. That's the call you *want* to hear at the end of a missing persons report: "We found her." No unnecessary details, no tragedy, just relief.

A two-vehicle crash on the 210 Transition Road in Sylmar injured at least one person — you know, the place where the freeway does its best to kill you geometrically. ## Fires and Wind

The Bryson Fire in Malibu has scorched through brush and homes, injuring one firefighter in the process. It's 52% contained as of this writing, which means about half of it is still actively trying to wreck everyone's day. The Santa Ana winds that fueled this disaster are finally bleeding out — today's the last gasp of extreme fire danger conditions, and if you're in the foothills or canyons, Red Flag Parking restrictions stay in effect in Pasadena through the wind event. Translation: don't park under those trees if they're on the warning list; fire departments need every inch they can get.

There was also a Reseda outside fire that spread to a structure before crews knocked it down. "Outside fire" that decides to be an *inside* fire is always an unwelcome plot twist.

The South Coast AQMD issued a smoke advisory tied to fire activity affecting LA County's air quality — not exactly news when half of Orange County is on fire, but if you've got respiratory issues, today might be a good day to stay indoors or keep the windows sealed. The air's going to be murky until the winds die and the smoke clears out.

## Public Health: Pasadena's Bat Problem

Pasadena just confirmed its *third* rabid bat case this year — this one on East Green Street. That's not a coincidence pattern; that's a "don't touch weird animals" PSA written in animal bite risk. The city's public health folks are asking people to stay back from wildlife, especially anything acting weird or grounded. If you see a bat on the ground, assume it's hostile and call animal control. If a bat got into your house, get it out without touching it — they're tiny vectors for a 100% fatal disease once symptoms start. Not great Saturday vibes.

The 'No Kings' rallies are happening at dozens of locations across LA County today — multiple sites in DTLA, Old Pasadena, and other spots across the region — focused on early voting, immigration, and voting rights. The LAPD issued a heads-up that the downtown LA gathering is a "non-permitted demonstration," which is bureaucrat-speak for "we didn't issue permits so technically this is unsanctioned, but it's happening anyway and traffic's gonna be a nightmare." Expect downtown to move like honey and lots of Waze reroutes between now and whenever this wraps. Nothing particularly dangerous reported, just the usual Saturday downtown gridlock cranked to eleven with a side of political theater.
