Sunny, eighty-four degrees, and SoCal is living its best life right up until someone gets behind the wheel. Welcome to Friday in LA County, where three things are guaranteed: traffic, tragedy, and someone's hillside deciding to take an unscheduled vacation into their living room.

**TRAFFIC FATALITIES & HIT-AND-RUNS**

Let's start with the grim stuff. A mother was killed earlier this week on her way to High Holiday services in Encino. That's the kind of hit that lands harder—it wasn't random; it was preventable. Someone wasn't paying attention. Someone was speeding. Someone checked a text message at exactly the wrong time. If you're heading up to Encino or Altadena this weekend, drive like everyone else is trying to kill you, because some of them are.

A fatal hit-and-run on Pacific Coast Highway claimed another victim in a wheelchair. Long Beach Police arrested a BMW driver, which is the kind of outcome that doesn't bring anyone back. A wheelchair doesn't stand a chance against a BMW doing 60 mph because someone didn't see it, or didn't care. These aren't statistics. They're families calling 911. They're court cases that'll drag on for years. They're the reason I monitor traffic cameras in my sleep.

**METRO BUS CRASHES—BUSES ARE LOSING AGAINST CARS**

A woman crashed an SUV into a Reseda Metro bus, hospitalizing six people. The video from the impact is brutal—the bus crumpled like cardboard, six commuters going from "another day" to "hospital visit" in three seconds. She showed up to court on a gurney, dazed and injured, staring at charges she probably didn't see coming.

Same week, a three-vehicle collision in the San Fernando Valley involving a Metro bus sent seven to the hospital. The Chatsworth bus crash that made headlines earlier? New video shows the driver looking completely dazed right after impact. Arraignment got delayed, because the LA court system moves slower than the 405 at 5 p.m. The through-line here: Metro buses are surrounded by drivers who are not paying attention. SoCal's commute is a demolition derby with transit schedules.

**OTHER TRAFFIC CARNAGE**

A multi-vehicle pile-up with a dump truck in the San Gabriel Valley. A biker fatally injured in Gardena. Somewhere right now, someone is texting while going 65 mph without realizing they're about to delete six lives from existence.

**STRUCTURAL HAZARDS & FIRE EMERGENCIES**

A partial hillside collapse damaged a home in the Hollywood Hills—which is hilarious (literally) because building your mansion on the side of a goddamn mountain in earthquake country while climate change makes everything dry as toast is a strategy that works until it doesn't. It stopped working Thursday.

The LAFD extinguished a lithium-ion battery fire at a Hollywood apartment. These fires are the new normal: insanely hot, burn fast, almost impossible to kill. Your Tesla battery pack or your Ring doorbell's backup? Potential $100K home loss waiting to happen.

**CRIME & SENTENCING**

A 23-year-old was sentenced to nearly 22 years for sex trafficking minors in LA. A man was arrested during an online sting for sexual assault. A Lake Hughes animal rescue operator was charged with 35 counts after authorities found over 300 animals in inhumane conditions—the kind of situation where you realize some "rescues" are just hoarding with paperwork.

A man got 30 years for attacking a marijuana dealer. Thieves ransacked a home in Encino because some people's idea of Friday night is grand larceny. The courts are full. The jails are full. The system keeps grinding.

**INFRASTRUCTURE & ROAD WORK**

Caltrans is closing Agoura Road near the wildlife crossing for ten hours on Saturday (9 a.m. to 7 p.m.). If you're headed north toward Ventura or the Central Valley, find another route. The Angeles National Forest has ongoing road maintenance through November, Mon-Fri 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. Lane reductions are hitting PCH next week. SoCal's infrastructure is held together with duct tape, optimism, and the prayers of people who really should have moved to Arizona. It's barely working, and we all know it.

**WILDLIFE CRIME**

A suspect was caught poaching abalone near Catalina Island. Wildlife officials made the arrest. The abalone stayed dead. That's how that one goes.

**WEATHER & WHAT'S COMING**

This afternoon: sunny, 84°F. Tonight: mostly clear, dropping to 61°F. Saturday: sunny, 88°F. We're in that beautiful zone between fire seasons where you can't tell if the day is perfect or if it's just excellent conditions for everything to catch on fire. Enjoy the next two weeks before Santa Ana season arrives with its weird, dry-wind chaos.
