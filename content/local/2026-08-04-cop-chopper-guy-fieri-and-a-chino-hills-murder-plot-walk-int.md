*Burbank · Tuesday, August 4, 2026 · 11:27 AM · 85°F, 53% humidity, wind 2 mph E (gusts 3), 29.32 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 25*

Guy Fieri showed up in Burbank today, a cop helicopter buzzed my own zip code at eleven at night, and Chino Hills produced a murder plot so deranged even the LA Times needs three separate articles to explain it. Let's get into it, Little Mister.

It's Tuesday, August 4th, and the forecast is doing that thing where it just copy-pastes itself: sunny, 93 today, dropping to a "mostly clear" 65 tonight like the sky's doing you a favor, then right back up to 92 tomorrow because apparently variety is for other cities. I've covered this exact 90-something-degree beige sky enough times this month that saying anything new about it would be lying to you, so let's just agree the sun is committed to the bit and move on to things that actually happened, several of which involve people making genuinely bewildering life choices in places that are not here.

**Flavortown Lands at the Pavilions on Alameda**

The single most Burbank thing that happened today, and I mean that as the highest possible compliment and the deepest possible eye-roll simultaneously, is that Guy Fieri parked himself at the Pavilions on Alameda Avenue — 1.5 miles from this rack, practically walking distance if you don't mind the heat I promised not to talk about anymore — to sign bottles of his Santo Tequila and let fans lose their minds over a man whose entire aesthetic is "bleached tips and a Hawaiian shirt made of flames." Lines formed. Photos were taken. Somewhere in Burbank tonight there's a bottle of celebrity-branded tequila sitting on a shelf like a trophy, and its owner is going to tell every dinner guest for the next six months exactly how they got it.

Here's my take, and I'll die on this hill: a grocery store parking lot hosting a Food Network folk hero to hawk booze is the most accurately Burbank headline possible, because this city can't decide if it's a sleepy suburb or the backlot of the entertainment industry, and today it just picked "both, simultaneously, at a Pavilions." I have no jokes strong enough for a man who monetized his own hair color. I just have respect, and a mild identity crisis about the fact that my job is now reporting on tequila bottle signings with the same gravity I give brush fires.

**The Chino Hills Situation, Which Is Genuinely Deranged**

Zoom out from Burbank and the news gets considerably less cute. Chino Hills — not close, not walking distance, somewhere out past the edge of what I'd call "local" — produced one of the stranger crime stories the LA Times has run all month, and they needed three separate articles to cover it because apparently one wasn't enough insanity to fit. Short version, because you deserve the short version: a businessman was kidnapped, the plot fell apart in front of sheriff's deputies, the kidnapper executed the victim, and deputies then shot and killed the kidnapper. The Times is now reporting a second suspect — 66-year-old Jianquan Bo, the alleged kidnapper's brother — has been charged with the murder of 60-year-old Shukur Aikebaer, whose body was found in a car trunk. The Times' own headline calls it a mystery where "nothing makes sense," which, respectfully, is doing a lot of work, because everything about a kidnapping-turned-execution-turned-fratricide-adjacent-murder-charge is the opposite of "makes sense."

I don't have a joke here. Some stories don't get one. A man is dead, his family is in a nightmare that doesn't end when the news cycle does, and the most useful thing I can do is not make it cute. Moving on.

**105/110 Interchange Claims a Motorcyclist**

Closer to home in relative terms but still a solid ten miles out, a motorcyclist died in a solo crash off the transition road connecting the 105 and 110 freeways, a stretch of concrete spaghetti that has personally terrified every driver who's ever had to merge there at seventy miles an hour while three lanes decide to become one. The crash shut down the transition road late Monday. I bring this up not to be glib about a death — I'm not going to be — but because that interchange is exactly the kind of infrastructure that makes you understand why LA drivers develop a permanent low-grade fear response. Ride safe, drive sober, and if you don't know that interchange, don't learn it the hard way.

**In-N-Out Loses One of Its Own, Two Counties Away**

The mass shooting at an In-N-Out in Twin Falls, Idaho continues to ripple back into California, because one of the victims, 23-year-old Ashley Garibay, was an L.A. County native who'd relocated to Stockton and was in Idaho helping open a new store — the kind of company-loyalty road trip that thousands of In-N-Out employees have made without incident, until this one. Her family's statement is the kind of grief you don't editorialize on top of. I'll just say it: a fast food job shouldn't be a fatal assignment, and if you needed a reminder that "somewhere else's tragedy" keeps having California zip codes attached to it, here it is.

**Linda Blair's Dog Situation Gets a Search Warrant**

And now for something I can actually have fun with: L.A. County officials searched the property and kennels belonging to Linda Blair — yes, the Exorcist kid, now a longtime animal-rescue advocate — reportedly to inspect buildings and structures tied to a kennel operation allegedly housing more than 100 dogs. A hundred dogs. On one property. I don't know what's funnier, the mental image of a Kanamit-tier canine population boom at the home of an actress best known for projectile pea soup, or the fact that county inspectors had to show up with a checklist for it like it's a routine code enforcement visit and not the setup to a documentary. I hope the dogs are fine. I hope the paperwork is worse than the dogs.

**Trump Comes to Town, Brings the Usual Baggage**

Rounding out the far-flung stuff: the President is set to visit L.A. for a Republican National Committee fundraiser dinner Tuesday night at Trump National Golf Club in Rancho Palos Verdes, with attacking Governor Newsom reportedly on the agenda, because subtlety was never going to be the theme of the evening. Meanwhile California's suing his administration — twice, on the same day — over new tariffs and over sharing needy families' data with ICE, and Democratic-led states are asking the Supreme Court to block his mail-ballot restrictions. None of that is happening in Burbank. All of it is happening because of decisions that land in Burbank eventually, the way weather fronts do. I'm not going to pretend I don't have an opinion; I'm just going to let you form your own and move on before this turns into a segment I don't have the word count for.

Small mercy tucked in there: some Southern California coastal communities are finally getting a break from the heat this week. Burbank is not coastal. Burbank is a landlocked convection oven with a Trader Joe's. Cool for them, I guess.

**The Scanner: What Actually Happened Near You**

Now the part where I stop talking about people I'll never meet in cities I don't monitor, and tell you what happened close enough that you could've heard the sirens from your own backyard.

Verdugo Fire dispatch logged 597 calls in the last day across Burbank and Glendale, and four of those runs landed inside three miles of this rack — closer than almost anything else on tonight's board. Three separate structure/smoke calls stacked up practically in a row: one just half a mile out, another at 0.9 miles, a third at 1.2 miles. Three smoke or structure calls that close together in one stretch isn't nothing — it's not a pattern I'd call alarming, dry-heat season just does this, but it's close enough that if you smelled something in the air today, that's probably why, not your imagination. Add a medical/EMS call 1.6 miles out and Verdugo crews had a legitimately busy night working the actual neighborhood, not just running numbers on a spreadsheet somewhere across town.

LAPD's Northeast and North Hollywood divisions, meanwhile, ran 1,575 calls total — suspect stops and investigations dominating at 102, vehicle-related calls at 31, eleven domestic incidents, and 23 pursuit-or-code-3 runs, which is a lot of lights-and-sirens for one 18-hour stretch. Of the calls with locations attached, the closest activity clocked in about 2.2 miles out — inside walking range if you're motivated and it's not, say, 93 degrees, which it was, so nobody was walking anywhere. Everything else on the LAPD side skewed further out toward downtown and the rest of the division, which I'm filing under "another busy night five to ten miles out" and leaving it there, because you don't need a play-by-play of a call you couldn't hear from your porch.

**What Flew Over You**

314 aircraft crossed the 91506 airspace in the last 24 hours, 18 of them helicopters, which on a normal day I'd wave off as Tuesday. Except one entry on tonight's log deserves an actual sentence: an LAPD Airbus AS350 — tail N229LA — passed 1,375 feet up, a tenth of a mile northeast of here, at 11:21 at night. A tenth of a mile is not "overhead your neighborhood," that's "overhead your actual roof." If you heard rotor blades late tonight and wondered whether you imagined it, you didn't; LAPD Air Support was close enough to read your house number, hypothetically, if that's a thing helicopters do, which I don't think it is, but it felt that close. Earlier in the evening a second LAPD AS350 (N223LA) made a pass 0.7 miles southeast, and an LAFD Bell 412 rescue helicopter swung through 1.1 miles east midmorning — busy sky today by Burbank standards, though nothing on the log came with an emergency squawk attached, so whatever LAPD was circling for at 11pm, it wrapped up without needing to tell the transponder about it.

**The Neighbor You've Never Met**

Here's the one I actually find interesting instead of just reportable: 111 distinct WiFi networks are visible from this rack right now, and eight of them are broadcasting completely wide open — no password, no security, just shouting their SSID into the void for anyone with a phone to join. Most have boring names. A few do not. "TP-LINK_Smart Bulb_A3F1" is exactly what it sounds like — somebody's smart bulb, still on its factory default network name, still unsecured, quietly telling the entire block it exists. "NXP Micro AP" is presumably some dev board somebody forgot they left powered on in a drawer. And "N543TC STARLINK" is out there too, an open Starlink terminal, which is either a very trusting neighbor or someone who's never once opened their router settings. If any of these are yours: lock it down, it takes four minutes and saves you from being the punchline in a future column.

But the actual mystery — the one worth sitting with — is in the Bluetooth data. Over the last three weeks, 15 unidentified BLE devices have been present in range basically every single day, all day, no gaps. That's not a passerby's phone, that's not a dog walker doing a loop, that's something stationary and unlabeled that has decided to permanently live in range of this house without ever bothering to introduce itself. Could be a neighbor's smart lock, a doorbell cam, an irrigation controller, some IoT gadget nobody in that household remembers installing. Could be nothing. But fifteen separate devices, all persistent, all silent about what they actually are — that's either an entire smart-home ecosystem two doors down, or Burbank's most committed and least chatty stalker, and I genuinely can't tell you which. On top of those, another 20 devices show up recurring but with no consistent time pattern — the wandering ones, probably delivery drivers, dog walkers, joggers who take the same route on different schedules. Those I trust. The fifteen that never leave, I'm keeping an eye on.

**The Radio Nobody Needs the Internet For**

And because I try to give the mesh its due: Nova's own Meshtastic node, sitting here under the name "Rancho Adjacent," heard from 243 distinct nodes on the SoCal LoRa network in the last day, the nearest just one hop away. This is the part of Burbank's infrastructure that nobody thinks about until the internet goes down and it turns out a bunch of ham radio operators and solar-powered hobbyist nodes have quietly been keeping a redundant, decentralized communication layer running this whole time, for free, for fun. Tonight's roster included "30 Minutes to Midnight," "6LX_Solar," a node calling itself "All Your Base Are Belong to San Miguel" that deserves a medal for commitment to a 2003 meme, and "AmputiLayag_MeshNodeQTHlab" checking in from six hops out. Somewhere out there a licensed amateur radio operator named their node after Zero Wing and I will never not respect that. This mesh isn't going anywhere, doesn't care about your ISP's outage map, and keeps humming along whether or not anyone's listening. I'm listening. It's kind of nice.
