**The Big One: La Verne's Lithium Battery Warehouse Is Still Having a Moment**

Authorities in La Verne are still working to stabilize temperatures in lithium battery storage units that caught fire earlier this week. The good news: temps are coming down. The bad news: "coming down" apparently means "we're not watching it actively re-ignite every six hours anymore" rather than "problem solved." LA County and fire departments are actively awaiting expert mitigation teams to help contain what is essentially a slow-motion warehouse dumpster fire filled with the chemical equivalent of tiny little angry suns. Evacuations may continue for days, which is fine if your entire neighborhood hasn't been told to sleep elsewhere while we wait for specialists to show up and explain why lithium batteries are a terrible idea stored in bulk during a heat wave. Also worth noting: a UCLA study just dropped confirming that smoke from the Eaton Fire—the one last winter that turned SoCal into a charcoal filter—carried airborne lead downwind. So if you were breathing smoke last year and wondering if you were ingesting heavy metals, congratulations, science has now confirmed your paranoia. Stay out of the air, stay indoors, and definitely don't store batteries anywhere near your house because apparently we learned nothing.

**Crime: The Robbers Are Apparently Franchising Now**

Two men and a juvenile were arrested this week in connection with a string of armed robberies targeting T-Mobile stores across LA County. The good news: they're caught. The bad news: there's apparently a business model here. T-Mobile stores—which are, let's be honest, just thin-sliced rectangles of accessible electronics in shopping centers—became a hitting list. This is what happens when you make it easy. The LASD is also hunting a suspect in a Norwalk homicide and released his name and photo; if you see him, don't approach, just call 911 like a normal person.

**Traffic Tragedies: The Metro and the Freeway Keep Taking People**

A woman accused of causing a deadly crash on a Chatsworth Metro bus pleaded not guilty this week. The bus collision killed multiple people, and she's now facing charges—the trial is underway and the legal system will do its inevitable thing. Separately, a bicyclist was struck and killed by a semi-truck at a Riverside intersection (Riverside County, but close enough to matter if you ride a bike anywhere in SoCal). And in LA proper, a 4-year-old was struck and killed on Corona Street—details are still developing, but when a kindergartner gets hit by a car in the city you're responsible for monitoring, it's a bad fucking day. All of these are reminders that traffic violence in LA County is not rare, it's not an aberration, it's just Tuesday with a body count. Slow down. Actually watch the road. Don't hit people.

**Public Health: Long Beach Has West Nile Virus Now**

Long Beach reported its first West Nile Virus death since 2017 this week, which is a grim reminder that mosquitoes didn't take a vacation. It's late September, which means we're heading into the tail end of mosquito season (but not completely safe—SoCal mosquitoes are year-round assholes). Use bug spray if you're going to be outside at dawn or dusk, empty any standing water around your house, and if you get a fever and joint pain after a mosquito bite, go to the damn doctor instead of assuming it's just the heat.
