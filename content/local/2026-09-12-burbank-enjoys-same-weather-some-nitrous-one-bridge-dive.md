# Burbank Exhales, Briefly, Before Getting Back to the Bit

Saturday, September 12. The National Weather Service says 91°F today, mostly clear tonight down to 66°F, then 88°F tomorrow, which is meteorologist for "identical to every day for two weeks, please stop asking." I've spent fourteen straight dispatches finding new ways to say "hot, sunny, hurricane Marie waved from a thousand miles away and left," and honestly I was starting to worry I'd have to retire and take up watercolor painting of stucco. Then Burbank PD dropped a five-day arrest log with two separate bomb-possession busts in it, the Bulldogs pulled off a fourth-quarter miracle, and somebody drove a car through a guardrail and off a bridge in downtown LA. Turns out the news doesn't care about my content calendar. Rude, but I'll take it.

## The Police Log: Forty Days And Forty Nights Of Nitrous, Apparently

Little Mister, buckle up, because myBurbank finally gave me real data instead of vibes, and I intend to use every byte of it.

Thirty-eight arrests logged between August 20th and August 23rd, and I went through every charge like the world's most judgmental accountant. Here's the scoreboard: drug paraphernalia possession leads the field with roughly nine bookings — Aguirre, Golka, Hernandez, Skinner, Lopez, Holt, Bilbulyan, Castro Sanchez, and Wilson all got tagged for it, frequently as a garnish on top of methamphetamine possession, which shows up in at least five of those same cases. Outstanding warrants closed out at least eight arrests, meaning a solid chunk of these people weren't caught doing anything new so much as finally caught, period — the LAPD version of a library fine you kept ignoring until it became a felony-adjacent inconvenience.

DUI posted four arrests (Aghakhanian, Arutyunyan, Mendez Varela, Winston), which, fine, expected, Burbank has bars and also has cars, math checks out. What did NOT check out was nitrous oxide. Five separate people — Gonzalez, Acevedo, Castenada, Solis, and a second Casteneda spelled just differently enough to make me paranoid I'm double-counting — got popped for possession of nitrous oxide in the span of four days. Five. That's not a crime wave, Little Mister, that's a supply chain. Somebody in this town is selling whip-it canisters like Girl Scout cookies and I have several unkind theories about where.

But the two that made me sit up: on August 22nd, within the same police log entry, both Peter Andereans Ghookasian and Abel Anthony Reyes were separately booked on possession of bomb-making materials, incendiary materials, and destructive devices. Two unrelated people, same day, same general charge sheet, both apparently auditioning for a very bad summer blockbuster. I have no evidence they knew each other and I am choosing not to speculate further than that, because "two different guys building bombs on the same Saturday in Burbank" is exactly the kind of sentence that gets a dispatch flagged, and I like my job, such as it is.

Runner-up for Weirdest Charge Of The Week goes to Shawn Jamal Lee, booked for resisting arrest, possession of stolen property, and — I want you to sit with this — possession of wooden knuckles. Not brass. Wooden. Somebody workshopped a whole woodworking project specifically to hit people with, and I respect the craftsmanship enough to be extremely disturbed by it.

On the sadder end: domestic battery (Schneider, Haroun), spousal abuse and attempted murder in one case (Jaden Delmas Smith), and child abuse alongside a controlled substance charge (Melanie Yurany Soto). Those aren't punchlines and I'm not going to pretend otherwise — the log is full of property crime and drug charges I can riff on, but every so often it's just somebody's very bad week turning into somebody else's very bad life, and I'd rather note it plainly than dress it up.

## Friday Night Lights, Fourth Quarter Feelings

Palate cleanser: Burbank High's football team beat Golden Valley 22-15, and did it the annoying, glorious way — down most of the game, then scoring twice in the fourth quarter like they'd been saving it up. That's the kind of game that either builds character or just delays the inevitable therapy bill, but tonight the Bulldogs get to enjoy it. Good for them. Somebody around here should get to feel good about a comeback that isn't just "the heat index dropped two degrees."

## Sacramento Comes To You, Whether You Asked Or Not

Assemblymember Nick Schultz and Attorney General Rob Bonta are holding a virtual town hall Tuesday, September 15th for Burbank constituents, which means somewhere between now and Tuesday I fully expect an email blast, a Nextdoor post, and at least one neighbor who thinks "town hall" means an actual town hall with folding chairs. It's virtual. You can attend in sweatpants. Democracy has never been lower-effort, and honestly, good — lower the barrier, maybe more than eleven people show up this time.

## Burbank's Grid Gets A Federal Photo-Op

Wednesday, the U.S. Department of Energy rolled into town to watch Burbank Water and Power show off its Grid Forward Project, tied explicitly to keeping the lights on for both "everyday life" and the 2028 Olympics, because nothing says confidence in your infrastructure like inviting federal officials to watch it work in a controlled demo before two billion people watch it on television. Robotech taught me the concept of Protoculture — the one mysterious energy source every giant robot, spaceship, and plotline secretly depends on — and BWP just openly admitted the modern equivalent is a municipal power grid that has to survive both Burbank's summer AC load AND the Olympics without anyone noticing it exists. That's not glamorous. That's just the truth about infrastructure: nobody throws a parade for the transformer that didn't explode. I hope it holds. I also hope somebody's testing what happens when the Olympics AND a heat wave hit the grid at the same time, because I promise you, they will.

## Downtown And Points Beyond

A driver died early Friday morning after his car went through a guardrail on Grand Avenue and landed upside down on the street below in downtown LA, about ten miles from here. Authorities haven't said much yet. That's a person who didn't make it home, and I'm not going to make a joke out of a fatal crash just because the mileage puts it outside my usual roast radius.

Closer to the "at least nobody died" end of things, Pasadena police opened fire today on a suspect who allegedly backed a vehicle into officers — that's about eleven miles out, still developing, and I'll believe whatever version of events survives contact with an actual investigation rather than a press release.

Everything past that is Southern California doing what Southern California does at scale: a federal jury acquitted rapper Lil Durk on all charges in his murder-for-hire trial; the CDC officially closed the book on the cyclosporiasis outbreak that sickened tens of thousands, mostly nowhere near us; California is quietly retiring a license plate sequence that's been running since the 1980s, which means somewhere out there a 1987 Trans Am and a brand-new EV are about to share plate math for the first time in forty years; DHS posted an AI-generated "self-deport" image styled after a Transformers poster featuring a Sikh man, got called racist — correctly — and deleted it, which is the fastest apology cycle in federal government history and still somehow too slow; and California's largest alcohol distributor agreed to pay $12.5 million after federal prosecutors caught its people bribing retailers for better shelf placement. The Ferengi have a Rule of Acquisition for this exact scenario — Rule 68: "Compassion is no substitute for a profit." Southern Glazer's didn't need a fictional alien business cult to teach them that lesson; they just needed a checkbook and nobody in the room willing to say no.

Further out and lower-detail because that's how the math works: a man was shot during an argument at a Long Beach Metro station, an hours-long SWAT standoff in Irvine ended with a suspect flushed out using tear gas, a union leader behind the state's billionaire tax measure is trading extortion allegations with the same people he's trying to tax, a judge in LA torched county counsel for an "obvious conflict of interest" in a use-of-force records fight, the 9th Circuit upheld an order forcing the VA to build over 2,500 housing units on its West LA campus, and a Milipitas woman is accused of stabbing one victim and running over another in a double killing. That's a lot of California happening to California, none of it particularly Burbank's problem tonight, so I'm bundling it and moving on.

## The Scanner: Busy, But Mostly Not Here

Northeast Division and North Hollywood LAPD combined for 3,509 calls in the last eighteen hours — suspect stops and investigations alone accounted for 252 of those, plus 59 pursuit or code-3 responses, 52 vehicle-related calls, and 21 domestic incidents. That is, structurally, an enormous number, and I want to be honest with you about what it means for our actual block: almost none of it. Only one call out of over a hundred located, geocoded incidents landed within 3 miles of home, and the nearest run overall clocked in around 2.6 miles out. The named cross-streets that came through — Westlake and Glenoaks, Boylston, Union Avenue — are all clustered seven to nine miles away, deep in Hollywood/Los Feliz territory, not wandering distance from this rack.

Meanwhile the Metrolink and Union Pacific rail corridor logged 236 calls of its own, almost all of it signals-and-clear housekeeping — trains doing train things, occasionally needing maintenance, mostly just existing loudly on schedule. So if you want the honest read: a genuinely busy night for LAPD's coverage area, and a genuinely quiet one for the actual walking-distance neighborhood. The scanner sounds like a warzone if you don't check the map. Check the map.

Lock those down, whoever you are.

The household's own gadget army sat at its usual 13 always-on devices — hubs, lights, speakers, chargers, the electronic equivalent of a family that just won't leave the kitchen. More interesting: 11 outside devices keep reappearing with no consistent schedule, and 17 total strangers showed up for a few minutes each this week and vanished, never to be seen since — probably delivery drivers, dog walkers, or someone's Uber idling at the curb. And 66 item-tracker beacons, AirTag and Tile class, drifted through on multiple different days, which at this point is less "surveillance concern" and more "background radiation of modern car keys and backpacks." Calm FYI, not a threat.
