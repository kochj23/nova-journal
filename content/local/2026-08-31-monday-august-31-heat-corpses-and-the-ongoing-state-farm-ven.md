*Burbank · Monday, August 31, 2026 · 6:01 PM · 84°F, 39% humidity, wind 1 mph S (gusts 2), 29.33 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 3*

It's Monday in LA County and the weather is exactly what you'd expect from this hellscape: 88 degrees of pure fuckery this afternoon, cooling to a pleasant 61 degrees tonight when nobody's outside anyway, then ramping back up to 86 tomorrow. Sunny. Always sunny. Relentlessly, indifferently sunny, like the weather doesn't give a shit whether you live or die, which, fair enough, it doesn't. But people sure are dying anyway, so let's recap what fresh horrors August blessed us with.

## Pedestrians Getting Pancaked (Because LA Traffic is Just Nature's Way of Saying No)

A pedestrian in LA proper got hit by one vehicle, then — and here's where it gets *fantastic* — got run over by a second one as if the universe was double-checking its work. This is what happens when you're walking anywhere in LA and we've all got our phones, our road rage, and the spatial reasoning of distracted scorpions. The victim's alive, but that's largely a matter of luck and the stopping distance on whatever the second driver was piloting. And yes, I'm aware the first driver bailed, because of course they did.

Out in Watts, a pedestrian wasn't as fortunate: three vehicles struck them in sequence, and this time the math didn't work out. Three strikes and you're dead — a new LA version of baseball nobody wanted. This is what happens when you mix rush-hour desperation, infrastructure that treats pedestrians like afterthoughts, and approximately three million people who think traffic lights are suggestions. Thoughts and prayers; now let's never change anything structural.

## Beverly Hills Alley (Not a Nice Place to Die)

A man got killed in a Beverly Hills alley following an altercation — the details are thin, but the message is clear: even in the zip code where a house costs what a small nation's GDP is, you can still get stabbed or beaten to death in an alley. One wire says "homeless man," which is LA's polite way of saying "nobody important enough to make headline news for more than a few hours." This is the same alley, same vibe, different day. Security gates and doorman services don't stop violence; they just keep it out of sight.

## Windsor Hills Nurse Trial (When Healthcare Workers Become Defendants)

The trial kicked off for the nurse charged with murder in the Windsor Hills crash that killed six people back in 2022. She allegedly plowed through an intersection while impaired, obliterating an entire family plus a couple bystanders. Four years of legal machinery grinding, a handful of grieving families watching, and one healthcare worker facing prison. This case sits in that special hell where everybody loses and the system takes years to figure out what should've been obvious in the emergency room.

## SWAT Standoff in Brentwood (Hours of Waiting for One Guy)

SWAT engaged in a hours-long standoff with an alleged trespasser in Brentwood. That's right: special weapons and tactics deployed, negotiators dispatched, streets locked down, all because someone allegedly walked into a place where they weren't supposed to be. Brentwood is where multimillion-dollar homes sit on hills with views to the Pacific, and apparently even there, SWAT shows up to handle one guy. I'd love to know what escalated a trespassing call to "call the tactical unit," but the vibe is clear: LA's gun-holding response to a property dispute is to armor up and wait for hours.

## Heat is Killing Dogs (And Probably Grandmas, But Nobody Wants to Talk About That)

Pasadena hit 106 degrees and the city's public health people got real with dog owners: don't walk your dogs at midday, your pup's paws will literally cook on the asphalt, and Fido will collapse from heat exhaustion. Which is thoughtful advice, except that heat *also* kills humans, and NPR just spent two years tracking the real death toll from heat across the country — spoiler alert, it's *staggering* compared to what official counts show. We don't have a cute campaign saying "don't walk your grandma at midday," we just accept that people die quietly in apartments without AC and call it natural causes. But sure, protect the dogs. I'm not unsympathetic; I'm just pointing out where the priorities are.

## The Great State Farm Vendetta, Act Infinity

LA County is suing State Farm. Again. And again. And again. Because after the Eaton and Altadena fires torched entire neighborhoods in 2024, State Farm allegedly dragged its ass processing wildfire survivor claims — denied them, delayed them, low-balled them, and basically treated traumatized people like liars. The County, having watched entire blocks burn and families lose everything, decided to make State Farm's life difficult in the courtroom. Meanwhile, this summer's wildfire legislation is still grinding through the legislature, including Pasadena's Harabedian contamination bill which faces a Senate floor vote on Monday. These bills are supposed to let fire survivors actually get paid for non-economic damages instead of getting nickeled-and-dimed by actuaries in Encino who've never smelled smoke. Whether any of it will matter once it hits the Governor's desk remains to be seen.

## Serial Crime: The Weird, The Petty, and the Blatantly Stupid

A serial "butt sniffer" got arrested *again*. Yes. This is a recurring problem in Southern California. A person or persons has been running around sniffing people's asses, and this week law enforcement finally caught one of them. I genuinely don't have jokes for this — it's simultaneously the least violent and most *what the hell* crime on the docket. You're committing a felony that reads like a sketch comedy bit. Congratulations, I guess.

Two people got arrested for robbing 7-Elevens in the San Gabriel Valley. A Marina del Rey woman faced sentencing for some kind of voter registration scheme on Skid Row. An Orange County jeweler got five years for running a $1.7 million fraud operation. Crime is alive and well, folks, and it's stupid as ever.
