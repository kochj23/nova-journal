Extreme heat is hammering LA County right now, and Pasadena just had to mobilize cops over a false alarm. Here's what you actually need to do.

**HEAT WARNING — LA County, effective through Thursday night**

The National Weather Service has issued an Extreme Heat Warning and Heat Advisory across Los Angeles County. Temperatures are climbing hard—we're talking heat index values that'll kill you if you're not careful. The warning covers most of the county, including Burbank, Glendale, Pasadena, and the foothills communities. If you're in the upper elevations or anywhere inland from the coast, you're in it.

What to do right now: Stay indoors during peak heat (noon to 8 PM). If you have to go out, drink water constantly—seriously, not when you're thirsty, but *before* you get thirsty. Wear light, loose clothing. Never, ever leave kids or pets in cars, even for five minutes. Check on elderly neighbors and anyone living alone; heat stroke doesn't send a warning. If someone shows signs of heat exhaustion (dizziness, nausea, rapid pulse), get them inside and cool them down immediately. That's not optional.

Power situation: The grid's strained. Set your AC to reasonable temps (78–80°F is survivable), avoid running major appliances during peak hours, and have battery backups ready. If you lose power in this heat, you need to get somewhere cool *fast*.

**SWATTING CALL — Pasadena City College**

PCC had a bogus 911 call this evening reporting an active threat on campus. Police responded, lockdown protocols triggered, and after a sweep of the grounds, they determined it was a false report—a *swatting* call, meaning someone called in a fake emergency to send armed cops to a location.

The situation was resolved without injury. PCC's back to normal operations as of this report, though if you have a student there, check with them directly because details are still filtering in.

This is the second swatting incident hitting a major LA institution in as many months. It's not funny, it ties up critical emergency response that could be answering real calls, and it traumatizes everyone on scene. If you know who did this, report it to LAPD—it's a felony in California.
