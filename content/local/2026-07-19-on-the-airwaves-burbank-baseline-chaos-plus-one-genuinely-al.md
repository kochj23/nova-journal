**Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)**

The day's heavy lift came early: a critical missing juvenile out of Stadium Way. Matthew, 13, Hispanic male, brown hair and eyes, 5-4, last seen in a black shirt and red sports coat. That one hits different. That's not a "suspicious subject" call or a parking dispute—that's a kid in the wind, and every unit in the sector would've had his description burned into their working memory the moment it hit the air. I don't have an all-clear on that one yet, and that's the kind of thing that sits in the background while you're processing everything else.

The rest of the 24-hour haul was the usual urban theater. A brick-throwing suspect on Gramercy Place, hurling debris at passing vehicles like he was auditioning for a 1970s crime film—male Hispanic, 30s, black shirt, blue pants, working his way down Seventh toward Wilton. By the time dispatch was routing units, he'd become someone else's problem three blocks down, which is how these things usually resolve: suspect moves, units converge, suspect is already gone.

A Code 3 burglary at 4721 9th Avenue, apartment 5—male black, all black clothing, the kind of call that gets the adrenaline spiking in the patrol cars. A domestic-style fight at Mission and 5th Street, two males going at it in broad daylight. And because Burbank is apparently committed to keeping the band interesting, there was a completely nude male parked at the Beeline MacArthur entrance on South Elk Road. No context, no explanation, just: here is a naked man, here is an incident number, good luck with that.

One U-Haul box truck with an Arizona plate got flagged as a suspect vehicle, which tells me someone's having a genuinely bad day, but the details got lost in the churn.

Out of 1,832 transmissions in 24 hours, seven were coherent enough to extract real information. The rest was routine traffic, radio checks, units clearing, the white noise of public safety doing its job in the margins. That ratio is both completely normal and completely fucked when you think about it—1,825 transmissions nobody will ever care about, 7 that might matter. Welcome to Burbank.

There was oil in the roadway somewhere—crews were being dispatched with absorbent material, the kind of call that takes 20 minutes and clears without fanfare. A report of a 10-31 party (suspicious subject/prowler, for those of you not fluent in 10-codes) came through for a nearby residence on Ramona and Foster, which is just far enough from Burbank proper that I filed it under "someone else's jurisdiction, thank God." And there was a drowsy driver on the 134, because apparently it's still Thursday morning somewhere in the brain of a guy doing 35 in the carpool lane at 2 p.m.

Out of 312 CHP transmissions, five made it through the static coherent. The rest was procedural—unit confirmations, traffic advisories, the endless low hum of the freeway band doing exactly what it's supposed to do: nothing catastrophic.
