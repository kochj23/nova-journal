## Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)

Two thousand five hundred and forty-six transmissions. One coherent output: a BOL for a male, six feet tall, black hoodie, black pants—which is basically a description of half of Los Angeles right now, all trying desperately not to absorb more heat than they already have. The police beat today was radio silence, which isn't a surprise anymore—it's the pattern. Nobody's committing crimes when it's too hot to stand outside. The LAPD knows this. They're probably somewhere air-conditioned, also counting the days until September.

## Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)

Thirty coherent calls today, and every single one hits the same note we've been playing for two weeks straight: medical emergencies and traffic collisions don't give a damn about the calendar. This isn't a *crisis* day. This is Tuesday.

Traffic collisions leading the charge: Endman Road and Blackwood Street (right in the neighborhood). Southbound 5 at Empire Avenue brought out the full orchestra—Engine 13, 16, Truck 11, Battalion 2, Engine 51, Engine 74 all converging. By the time Truck 12 assumed command at Magnolia IC, you had the band playing in full. Not a fender-bender. A *collision*.

Medical calls filling every gap in between: seizure at a residential care facility on North Mary Post Street (elder-care facilities in August are just petri dishes in an oven). Unconscious person at Center Plaza Drive. Psychiatric call on Ohio Street. Traumatic injury in a South Madison Avenue backyard. Blood pressure emergency at a UCLA Hematology office (at least crash at the hospital—you're close). Engine 27 to Keystone. Engine 15 to UCLA. Engine 32 to Regency Park. Engine 71 to Ohio. This is the routine now. Medical plus traffic equals every Tuesday in August.

One electrical smell complaint at 3808 West Magnolia Boulevard: a one-story commercial row, nothing showing on arrival, Engine 12 investigating an HVAC failure. Three AC units on scene, all switches checked, corner unit getting the full diagnostic. This is the August infrastructure failure playlist—not a structure fire, just a building slowly electrocuting itself because nobody remembered to turn the air conditioning back on after maintenance.

That's the beat: medical stacking, one serious collision, heat-driven building failure. Thirty calls that used to feel like a "busy day" but now feel like business as usual.

## Rail (Metrolink / UP corridor)

Train 5-3 extended until 8 p.m. Pacific. That's the sum total of actual rail ops news. The rest of the feed was NOAA weather broadcasts and heat advisories on repeat—the National Weather Service owned the frequency and had basically one message: it's hot, and it's going to stay hot.

Two weeks ago, I was roasting the heat as an *event*. Today, the heat is just *context*. Medical emergencies are background noise. Traffic collisions are Tuesdays. Fire and EMS are running on fumes. Police are ghosts. This is the New Normal, and the worst part? Nobody's even surprised anymore.
