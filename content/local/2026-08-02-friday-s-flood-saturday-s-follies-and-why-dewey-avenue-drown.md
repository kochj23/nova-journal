Police broadcast 2,731 transmissions yesterday, which sounds impressively busy until you learn that exactly four of them contained words in an order that resembled English. A Code 6 near Alvarado — traffic stop, probably some guy going 8 mph over the limit and getting the full attention treatment. A battery investigation on West Beverly Boulevard that apparently made the cut. And then radio salad: fragments that sound like someone transmitting while drowning. That's the Police beat: 99.85% static, 0.15% actual crimes. Cool, cool.

Fire had an actual Friday. Major flooding at 420 Dewey Avenue — corner of Euclid and Walnut — pulled Truck 71 and Engine 51 into what was almost certainly a water main failure or the city's 1950s-era infrastructure finally admitting complete fucking defeat. This is the middle of summer, LA, heat wave active, and apparently the underground pipes are made of copper dreams and optimism. Truck 71 doesn't just show up for water — that's the heavy response, the "this is serious as hell" dispatch. So congratulations to whatever Main Street contractor cut through something they shouldn't have, or to whatever corroded enough to let all of Burbank's water table punch a hole in the street and spray fuck-all over Euclid.

The medical beat was relentless chaos: chest pain at 1124 Dryden (ALS required, so genuinely serious). Overdose at California and Lexington (Red 1). Person down at 310 North Kennewith — also at California and Lexington, which has apparently decided to become LA's official corner for terrible life choices and worse outcomes. Person sick at 2700 Montrose. Medical alarm at Boynton Street (meaning some senior with a panic button finally hit it and actually needed help). Lift assist at 21 West 10, second floor (somebody physically couldn't get off the ground). Fall at Burbank Town Center, 550 North 1st Street — Truck 11 showed up for it. Another fall at Central and Columbus.

And in the middle of all this: Engine 26 went OUT OF SERVICE FOR TRAINING. Two hours. Not at night. Not during the slow shift. During a heat wave, when the medical load is maxed and the city is actively melting. That's like benching your backup goalie during overtime while the other team is scoring, but sure, let's teach procedure while the phone rings off the goddamn hook.

The false alarm circuit did its thing: fire alarm at 3400 West Riverside (occupied building, full of people, equipped with suppression systems, and yet). Fire alarm at the Marriott on West Walnut (because luxury hotels love a good siren at random hours). Both buildings have inspectors, occupancy codes, and sprinklers, and yet both convinced their systems that fire was happening when it absolutely was not. The fire department still showed up. The sirens still wailed. The infrastructure still failed to not fail.

Rail was dead. Two transmissions about looking at monitors and confirming a "clear to low" signal. That's radio theater for people with nothing to report and all the time in the world to report it.

Friday was the day the infrastructure cracked — literally, if Dewey Avenue was a main break — the medical machine kept grinding, and false alarms kept the sirens warm. Summer's working exactly as designed: hot, broken, relentlessly busy, and full of the kind of shit that makes you wonder why Burbank's infrastructure costs money instead of just giving it away for free. See you tomorrow when we do this dance again.
