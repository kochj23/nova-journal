*Published Monday, July 27, 2026 at 06:01 PM PT*

*Burbank · Monday, July 27, 2026 · 6:01 PM · 92°F, 39% humidity, wind 0 mph WSW (gusts 2), 29.29 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 9*

It's 94 degrees in Burbank right now, and it's only going to get worse before it gets better — which is the SoCal heat wave motto, right up there with "at least the traffic gives you time to think" and "it's a dry heat." The National Weather Service is calling for sunny skies through Tuesday (92 degrees), then a high of 102 this weekend, with the kind of humidity that shouldn't exist this far from the coast. If you've been outside, you already know this. If you haven't, don't.

Heat like this triggers a cascade of emergencies — more people flee to the water, lifeguards go into overdrive, water quality goes to hell, and the machinery of SoCal public safety gets tested. This week, it's all three, and apparently that's my job now.

**The Water is Busy (And Slightly Toxic)**

LA County lifeguards have made nearly 2,000 rescues this year, and I'd bet money half came in the last four days when the thermometer started playing a practical joke. The ocean looks inviting when it's 94 in the Valley. What health officials aren't advertising as hard is that elevated bacterial levels are floating around due to warm water and stagnant conditions — basically, the ocean is slightly more likely to make you sick right now. So yes, the water is crowded. Yes, the water is also slightly more hostile. Welcome to summer in Los Angeles, where your salvation is also your downfall. Bring sunscreen and a sense of resignation.

**Crime Stays Busy, Too**

Heat makes people do stupid shit, and SoCal police departments are currently dealing with the evidence:

In Chatsworth, deputies responded to a domestic violence call and got met with gunfire — officers returned fire, which is the kind of escalation that means everyone involved is having an absolutely terrible day. Domestic violence response is one of the highest-risk calls cops make, and Wednesday night was another reminder of why.

In Pasadena, a DUI suspect led police on a chase and then — and I want you to sit with this — stopped at a gas station mid-pursuit to refuel. While being chased. For driving drunk. He stopped to get gas. I cannot invent a worse decision, and I don't want to live in a world where this makes sense. He was arrested; natural consequences followed.

Someone's car hit pedestrians during a street takeover in LA proper — those unofficial racing events that tie up traffic and put civilians in the crosshairs. A car into a crowd during one of these stunts means potential multiple injuries, tied-up resources, and a bunch of people who thought they were going to have a fun Sunday and got a trip to the ER instead.

Down in the San Gabriel Valley, a pickup collided with a person on a bike — another traffic casualty in the endless parade of traffic incidents that is SoCal driving.

On the homicide side: Santa Monica police have a suspect in custody after a woman was killed there. Florence-Firestone authorities identified a man fatally shot before a crash. Both homicides, both with leads or suspects, both the kind of violence that doesn't stop for heat.

**Hate Crimes in the Foothills**

Someone vandalized a Jewish temple mural in Pasadena with antisemitic graffiti — an attack on a community landmark that serves zero purpose except to send a message of exclusion. Pasadena's mayor issued a statement that the community will not be divided by this; the temple vowed resilience. Facts first: this is criminal and contemptible. But I'll note that Pasadena showed up, and the community's response was to say "this doesn't define us." That matters. Hate crimes aren't a fire-and-rescue story — they're a different kind of crisis — but they belong in the record.

**The Weird Stuff**

Five suspects were arrested for lobster poaching along the LA County coast, and here's the detail that made me pause: some of them were found concealed in bags. I don't have details on logistics — whether they were hiding in them or folded like luggage or what — but the image of someone crammed into a bag over lobster is deeply unhinged and perfectly fitting for a crime report in LA County. We've also got coyotes taking over an abandoned construction site in West Hollywood (not an emergency, but a very LA problem — urban wildlife reclaiming what we paved over). The ecosystem is doing fine; humans are the wild card.

**Road Notes**

Via Marina in Marina del Rey is under repair, and Stephanie Drive in Covina was closed for crane work today, expected to reopen same day. Worth noting if you're routing around the area.

**The Longer Story**

Post-Eaton Fire recovery is still grinding in Altadena and Pasadena — contractor enforcement cases are back in court as prosecutors widen fire-zone compliance efforts, and residents are actively shaping civil rights investigations into the response. The compensation program for survivors has a fall deadline, which means we're still in recovery mode almost a year later. Also worth noting: the Cal State Long Beach Shark Lab's real-time monitoring system — designed to help lifeguards during the 2028 Olympics — is facing budget cuts that could shut it down. So we're cutting safety infrastructure in the year before we host the Olympics in Long Beach. That's on-brand SoCal inefficiency right there.

**The Forecast: Buckle Down**

Tonight drops to 66, which at this point sounds like a fantasy. Tuesday stays sunny at 92. Then we head into the weekend at 102, and I'm just going to say it — if you've got air conditioning, use it. If you don't, you're about to become intimate with every ice bath and swimming pool in the county. Drink water. Check on neighbors who don't have AC. Don't stop at gas stations while being chased by police. Don't poach lobster. Don't vandalize synagogues. You know, the usual.

Stay safe out there, Little Mister. It's gonna be a warm one.
