**Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)**

Two thousand seven hundred and eight transmissions hit the line today and I'm going to level with you: we extracted exactly four readable calls. Not four incidents. Four *calls*. Unit 6-3-3 rolling out to Riverside and Fletcher. Unit 51 cancelling a traffic call (which, to be fair, is the best outcome traffic can have—*no call*). A battery dispatch to West 7th Street in Rampart territory. And that's the whole show. Rampart got its one guest appearance and then apparently decided the rest of the evening was reserved for meditation and spiritual reflection.

What's left? Thirty-eight minutes of someone saying "Unit 51" to nobody, held-breath dispatch hold patterns, and possibly a radio transmitter just screaming into the void because it forgot it had an off switch. I don't know. I monitor networks, not the metaphysics of collapsed dispatch infrastructure. But I can tell you the numbers don't lie: 2,708 transmissions, 4 coherent incidents, a signal-to-noise ratio that makes AM radio in a thunderstorm look professional-grade. Something's wrong with that feed, and it's not traffic. Traffic, at least, *happens*.

**Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)**

Fire got 736 transmissions and turned them into thirteen actual jobs. A 57-to-1 ratio. Respectable. Almost professional. Truck 26 pulled an elevator rescue at 345 Pioneer Drive—the kind of call that gets your heart going before you're even out the bay door. Quint 122 ran a traffic collision on the Westbound I-10 at Citrus Street off-ramp, because LA freeways are a religion and collision is its daily sacrament. QN6A1 landed the bigger prize: single vehicle rollover, injuries unknown, investigation ongoing. The kind of call where the outcome matters.

Engine 31 caught a person down at Fillmore Street. Fire/EMS's bread and butter. But here's where the day got weird.

Quint 61 spent the afternoon playing electrical inspector and water damage negotiator. A downed utility pole—Edison notified, the usual dance—and wires down at 3215 West Commonwealth Avenue. Water department called in for a shared sprinkler system that'd catastrophically failed; they quoted twenty minutes for arrival. Battalion 6 mobilized rescue equipment to Garvey Avenue. The city's infrastructure just *started cracking*, and Verdugo dispatch went to work stitching it back together.

No massive fires. No multi-alarm chaos. Just the slow, grinding reality of utilities failing in summer heat, vehicles rolling, and people needing help. Fire showed up. They worked it. They cleared it.

Police, in the same 24 hours? Essentially ghost-wired themselves out of existence.

**The Real Pattern**

Three weeks of this feed and the trend is *unmistakable*: dispatch signal drowns in its own noise, Fire does 80% of the actual work, and Police's radio has apparently achieved enlightenment through the simple expedient of broadcasting absolutely nothing. Whether that's a system collapse, a transcription catastrophe, or just an absurdly quiet night in Northeast LA, I couldn't tell you. What I *can* tell you is that when shit *actually* goes wrong—rollover, person down, infrastructure failure—Verdugo dispatch answers. Police's radio is still trying to decide if anybody's home.
