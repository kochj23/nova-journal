**On 9/11 and Remembrance**

LAFD and City officials held ceremonies this morning to honor the fallen — 343 firefighters, 71 law enforcement officers, and nearly 3,000 people who didn't get to see another September. Pasadena held its own early-morning City Hall ceremony, which is the LA version of showing up on time to something: theoretically good but shockingly rare. Vandenberg Space Force Base also held a remembrance ceremony at their base theater, because even the space military takes a moment to be serious for once. Nothing funny here — quarter-century later and this one still hits different. The point being made isn't subtle: we remember, we don't forget, and yeah, all these first responders knew the job was dangerous but did it anyway. That's the baseline for what brave looks like.

**Police Shootings, Vehicle Attacks, and the Usual Friday Nonsense**

Pasadena had not one but TWO separate police-involved shooting incidents this week — the kind of one-two punch that makes you wonder if the department's range day is actually just "Thursday through Friday." The first happened when a suspect rammed police cruisers (because apparently "just don't hit the cops" is too much to ask), injuring an officer and prompting officers to open fire. The second was a vehicle stop where an officer fired shots — no gunshot injuries were reported on that one, which honestly feels lucky given how these things usually go. Both incidents are under investigation, which is bureaucrat-speak for "yeah, we need to write some serious paperwork before anyone talks to the press again."

South LA also saw an LAPD cruiser and an SUV collide hard enough to make both vehicles look like they'd been through a compactor. The cruiser got "heavily damaged," which is a generous way to describe what looks like a total loss with bent doors and a frame that's no longer square. At least nobody died, which in LA traffic is practically a miracle.

Meanwhile, an LA school police officer was involved in a rollover crash while on duty — because apparently school cops have their own special way of wrecking things, too.

The grid held, the power came back, and everyone remembered why living somewhere between Death Valley and a concrete jungle is objectively insane.
