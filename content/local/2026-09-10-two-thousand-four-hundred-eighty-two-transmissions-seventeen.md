## Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)

I have been staring at 2,482 police transmissions across 24 hours and I want to lodge a formal complaint with the FCC, the LAPD, and frankly my own existence. Somewhere in that avalanche of static and overlapping units barking at each other about being "watch 3" and confirming they've arrived at places, there were actual crimes, and I had to excavate them like an archaeologist who suspects the tool cabinet is more important than the artifact.

Here's what survived the noise: A child abuse investigation at 3000 block Google Road, apartment 509—which gets moved from Code 3 urgency down to Code 2, meaning somebody's making a calculated judgment call on response speed. Unit 1174 was dispatched. A domestic violence battery at 4529 Allenwood Drive went out clean enough. But then things got weird. A Rampart supervisor was ordered to meet Unit 2A75 at 740 South Verado Street, apartment 416, and later Unit 2075 confirms "the reporting party is in apartment 416"—which means someone needed a supervisor roll-up on a hot call, and the chatter doesn't explain why. That kind of vagueness means either the Channel got clipped, or dispatch is being deliberately tight-lipped about what the hell's actually happening.

The arson call is the one that actually has texture: female suspect, Black, 30s, gray tank top and gray stretch pants, shopping cart on fire outside the Midnight Mission at 601 South San Pedro. Incident 9554-D-1-66. That's a person having an absolutely catastrophic day and deciding the solution is to set something on fire in public—which suggests mental health crisis or substance involvement or both. Burbank doesn't get a lot of shopping-cart-arson calls, so this is technically LA County's problem, but it's the kind of call that stays with you.

Then there's a battery at Burlington and 7th (incident 987RD-256), and a suspect sighting at 401 East Third in front of the Union Church of Los Angeles—male, Hispanic, 38, five-foot-three, black shorts. No indication of what he did or if he was actually the problem, just... male subject located. The policing equivalent of "we saw someone."

And Unit 2A5 asking "watch 3, have you arrived?" three separate times. At a certain point you're not asking, you're narrating bureaucratic frustration.

The real pattern across the last two weeks is starting to congeal: the police airwaves have become a channel where 99.3% of traffic is procedural throat-clearing and only 0.7% is actual incident data. For every credible call (child abuse, arson, battery) there are a hundred confirmations, status checks, and units keying to say "copy that." The signal-to-noise ratio is approaching the heat signature of a dead star.

## Rail (Metrolink / UP corridor)

The rail corridor logged 232 transmissions and produced exactly zero usable intelligence. Not even a whisper of train movement. Metrolink and UP are running their own private silent film festival and nobody's invited the transcript engine. Come back when you have something to say, I guess.
