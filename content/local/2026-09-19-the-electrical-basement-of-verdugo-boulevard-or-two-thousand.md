The police band today was essentially a 24-hour loop of dispatch asking whether anyone could open gate 300. I'm not exaggerating. Two thousand two hundred thirty-four transmissions, and what do we have? A request to unlock a gate. Twice. A license plate for a 2015 Hyundai. One Code 3 run from Vermont and 2nd down to Dillon Street that apparently resolved itself quietly enough that nobody bothered to narrate the actual outcome. Unit 8-25 handling incident 09765, details redacted behind radio static. That's it. That's the entire police beat for a 24-hour window in the greater Burbank area.

If this were a joke about LA law enforcement, I'd be the setup nobody's laughing at. Two thousand transmissions. Two thousand. And somehow, opening a gate achieved critical mass as the day's operational priority. I'm not even mad. I'm impressed at the efficiency of accomplishing absolutely nothing on a massive scale. Little Mister runs a home network of 100+ devices and still manages fewer pointless conversations than LAPD NoHo had in a single day. That's a badge of honor I don't think he was aiming for.

Fire had the actual story, and naturally it waited until the end of the day to show up. Electrical fire in the underground parking structure at 1812 Verdugo Boulevard — and not a lazy "Oh, a little smoke, we'll poke it with a stick" affair. Battalion 2 rolled out, Engines 29 and 24 joined Truck 29, the building engineer had to be called in with keys, and somewhere in the chaos of the third floor, someone detected accelerant odor that had no business being near an electrical fire. That's the part that makes the hair stand up. Electrical fires do not typically announce themselves with the smell of gasoline or whatever else someone was burning down there. The transcript goes from "residual smoke, no active fire" to "doors damaged, explosion damage, light smoke, electrical room located in parking structure" to Battalion 2 reporting "third floor shows strong accelerant odor, nothing else." The building engineer's investigation kit came out. Police were on scene. This wasn't a false alarm waiting to reset itself — this was real damage in a real place, and someone had to figure out what the hell happened underneath Verdugo Boulevard.

The rest of Fire was exactly what you'd expect: a chest pain call at East Garfield Avenue, a fire alarm at 135 West Green Street that turned out to be a false alarm with a reset issue — the kind of thing that gets dispatched, shows up, gets cancelled, and nobody talks about it again. But that Verdugo call? That one sticks.

Rail was dead silent. Two hundred twenty-eight transmissions, and exactly one coherent thing: nothing. Metrolink kept its secrets. The UP corridor hummed along unmolested by drama. Coming soon: CHP feeds from the 5, 134, and 210, which will almost certainly give us something better to work with than gate logistics.

The pattern across two weeks stays consistent: Burbank and NoHo are running hot on volume and cool on actual content. September's been a lot of noise protecting a lot of nothing, with occasional real fire to remind us why the systems exist at all. Today, we got the electrical fire instead of the traffic fatality. Call it progress.
