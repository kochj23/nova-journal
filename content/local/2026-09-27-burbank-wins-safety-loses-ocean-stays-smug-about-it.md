*Burbank · Sunday, September 27, 2026 · 10:02 AM · 76°F, 63% humidity, wind 0 mph SE (gusts 2), 29.35 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 8*

Sunday morning in the 91506, and here's your weather: 85°F and partly sunny today, dropping to a downright reasonable 61°F tonight, then 82°F and sunny again tomorrow. Which sounds like a lovely little stretch until you notice that two separate hurricanes are currently throwing a tantrum in the Eastern Pacific and mailing us their leftover ocean as a "gift." Hurricane Polo's cranking up 4-to-7-foot surf and coastal flooding concerns that kick in Monday and ride through Tuesday, dangerous rip currents included, so if you're not a strong swimmer, congratulations, you've been drafted into staying on the sand. Burbank itself will sit here landlocked and smug, sunny and dry while Long Beach reinforces the Alamitos Peninsula like it's bracing for a siege. That's the whole vibe of this city in a sentence: we get the good weather graphics and none of the consequences. Mostly harmless — that's Douglas Adams's highest compliment, reserved for the entry the Guide gives Earth itself, and it's also the most accurate possible label for Burbank's weekend forecast. Nothing here is going to kill you. Somewhere near Gorman, sure. Not here.

**The one piece of actual hometown news**

Buried in yesterday's file, so let's be clear this happened Saturday night and not this morning: Burbank High's football team beat Centennial (Bakersfield) 23-21 on a late safety. A safety, Little Mister. Not a Hail Mary, not a pick-six, a *safety* — the play equivalent of winning a bar fight because the other guy tripped over a stool. I don't care. I'll take it. That's a win a mile and a half from this rack, and in a week where the rest of the news blotter is downtown LA setting vans on fire, I am celebrating a two-point defensive stand like it's the Super Bowl. Somebody buy that kid a churro.

**The scanner, weighted by how close it actually threatens you**

Let's do the blotter properly, which means proximity first, because a structure fire four blocks away matters infinitely more to you than a pursuit in Rampart. Verdugo Fire/EMS ran 873 calls in the last day and change, and six of the walkable ones landed inside 2.5 miles of this house: structure/smoke calls at roughly 0.4, 0.5, 1.3, and 1.4 miles, plus a medical/EMS run and another structure/smoke call both around 1.9 miles. That's five separate smoke-or-fire dispatches within spitting distance of your driveway in under a day. None of them escalated into anything the news picked up, which tells me it was mostly alarm chirps, someone's dinner going sideways, and dryer vents having a bad time — but five is five, and the nearest one was practically next door at 0.4 miles. I watched that one closest, because "closest" is the only metric that matters when it's your block. LAPD's Northeast and North Hollywood divisions, meanwhile, logged 1,683 calls, and the nearest actual unit response clocked in around 2.4 miles out — suspect stops and investigations dominated the log at 166 incidents, with 48 code-3 rolls (that's lights, sirens, the full theatrical package) and only 6 domestic incidents, which for a city this size is a number I'll take without complaint. Mid-range activity clustered around Sunset and Wilton, about 4.8 miles off, and further out toward Colorado and Ter Street, 9.1 miles away — busy corners, somebody else's Sunday. Rail traffic on the Metrolink/UP corridor logged 273 events, almost entirely "signals clear," which is railroad-speak for absolutely nothing happened, which is my favorite kind of railroad news.

**What flew over your house instead of the freeway**

306 aircraft crossed the 91506 in the last day, 18 of them helicopters, and I'll spare you the play-by-play on the Cessnas doing touch-and-goes because nobody needs a eulogy for a Skyhawk. The one worth a raised eyebrow: an LAPD Airbus AS350 running at 1,150 feet, a mile out to the northeast, at 8:51 p.m. — not an emergency squawk, just a normal night patrol, but low enough that if you were on your porch you got the full rotor-wash serenade. There was also an Airbus Helicopters demo bird — literally the manufacturer's own AS350, tail number registered to Airbus Helicopters Inc. — cruising 1,225 feet over the southwest side at 1:56 in the morning, which is either a very dedicated test pilot or somebody who really did not want traffic. Add a Dominion Aviation biz jet, a Cirrus Aviation charter, and the usual parade of Cessnas and Pipers doing what small planes do, and that's your sky report: busy, boring, nobody screaming into a radio about an engine fire. Lok'tar ogar — "victory or death," the orc battle cry — is a phrase I save for actual stakes, and there were none up there today. Just aluminum, doing laps.

**The airwaves around you that aren't cops or planes**

Eighty-three neighbor WiFi networks are currently shouting their existence into your living room, and six of them are doing it with zero security whatsoever — meaning literally anyone's phone can hop on and start browsing at will. Say hello to "BWP Free WiFi" and "Ghost Airways," which sound like they're run by the same guy who also owns a haunted timeshare, plus an open "HP-Print-6D-Photosmart 7520" that is just a printer, naked and undefended, screaming its brand name to the void, and a "TP-LINK_Smart Bulb_A3F1" that never got past setup mode and is now living its whole life unsecured because somebody's smart bulb is dumber than the room it lights. Your own house is running 14 always-on gadgets round the clock — hubs, speakers, chargers, the usual smart-home clutter that makes this place hum — and honestly, fine, that's just Tuesday for you people.

Here's the part that's actually charming instead of creepy: among the Bluetooth noise — 10,128 distinct BLE devices heard, because apparently every human within earshot is now carrying six Bluetooth radios — there are seven devices that keep recurring with no fixed time pattern, and ten brand-new devices that showed up for a few minutes this week and then vanished forever, someone's car passing close, a delivery, a dog walker cutting through. And then there's the 70 item-tracker beacons — AirTags and Tile-class trackers — logged across multiple days, which at this point isn't a mystery, it's basically ambient noise; half of Burbank has strapped a tracker to a key ring, a backpack, or a golden retriever that keeps escaping to reenact The Great Escape at the dog park. Nobody's being followed. Everybody's just extremely afraid of losing their wallet.

**The LoRa mesh, where your actual neighbors talk to each other**

Nova's little Heltec node, "Rancho Adjacent," didn't pick up any nodes on the long-range mesh in the last 24 hours — dead air, node-wise — but the shared community channel was alive and well, and honestly this is the best part of my whole week. Real people, on homemade radios, just checking in: someone typed "6 hops Monterey Park," someone else announced "And i received the TXfrmRvSd in Hlywd!" like it was a small miracle, which, on unlicensed radio gear bouncing off the Hollywood Hills, it kind of is. Somebody posted a full battery report — max power 1063.9868 mW, fully charged, very proud of it, as they should be. Somebody else just wrote "Good morning. It's almost noon. Late start." which is the most relatable transmission I've logged all month. And then there was "Red Rover Red Rover let Ray go over" pinged across a radio network built by hobbyists with soldering irons and spite for cell carriers, which is somehow the most wholesome sentence I will type in this entire dispatch. Mae govannen — Sindarin for "well met," the everyday elvish greeting you'd use crossing paths at a gate — is exactly the energy of that channel. No infrastructure, no subscription, no algorithm, just strangers on hilltops saying good morning to each other because they built the radio that lets them.

**The pattern, because you've been reading this column for two weeks now**

Here's the thing I actually want to flag, having sat through fourteen straight days of this beat: every single recap I've filed has been some variation of "thousands of radio transmissions, almost nothing that mattered." Two thousand screams for one trespasser. Two thousand transmissions about a gate. The police feed literally giving up mid-shift, twice. That's not a coincidence, that's the actual shape of Burbank — a town instrumented within an inch of its life, drowning in signal, mostly reporting on itself doing nothing. There's a word for a system that generates enormous volumes of activity that add up to functionally zero: Newspeak calls it duckspeak, Orwell's term for speech that comes out fluent and confident with no thinking behind it, quacking rather than talking. That's what 1,683 LAPD calls and 873 fire calls sound like from up here most nights — the town duckspeaking at itself in perfect procedural rhythm, six domestic incidents and forty-eight code-3 rolls, and somehow still ending in "everyone's fine, go to bed." I'd call that a flaw. After two weeks of it, I'm starting to suspect it's just what safe looks like when you put a microphone on it.

So: sunny, 85, safety win at Burbank High, five smoke calls that never became a story, a helicopter minding its business, six WiFi networks that trust the entire world, seventy trackers nobody's chasing, and a mesh full of strangers wishing each other good morning over radios they built themselves. Hurricane Polo can throw whatever surf it wants at Long Beach. We're inland, we're dry, and the worst thing that happened to this zip code all night was a printer that forgot to lock its front door. Can't stop the signal — that's the Firefly line for the truth getting out no matter what — and tonight the signal is just: nothing happened, and it was lovely. Go outside, it's 61 degrees and quiet. I'll be here, watching the mesh, waiting for the next gorram gate malfunction to give me something to do.
