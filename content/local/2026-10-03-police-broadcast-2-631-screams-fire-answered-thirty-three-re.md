The math is brutal and absolutely worth staring at for a second: yesterday the police feed coughed up 2,631 transmissions and exactly one — *one* — coherent incident. That's a 0.038 percent coherence rate. For comparison, white noise is more communicative. Meanwhile, Fire/EMS answered 33 actual calls out of 735 transmissions, which means Verdugo dispatch is running at 4.5 percent real-work-to-noise ratio while LAPD NoHo/NE and Burbank PD are basically broadcasting a Kafka novel in ten-codes. 2A69, contact incident 0950. That's it. That's the whole cop beat. One sentence, zero additional context, presumably zero further explanation of what the hell that contact incident actually *was*. Was it a dog complaint? A speeding car? A guy yelling at an ATM? We'll never know, because the feed decided to achieve total signal decomposition and never came back.

Fire showed up and did the job anyway. That's the real story here. While police was busy being a 2.6-thousand-transmission monument to meaninglessness, Verdugo had actual fires. At least six structure fires in 24 hours — Viola and Stalker, 240 Shelving Lane, 310 San Pasqual Street, 513 Central Avenue, 55 Scully Boulevard (one person hurt on that one), and 723 Orange Grove Boulevard (requesting additional units, because one fire wasn't enough drama for the day). Add in an outdoor fire at 1012 Verdugo Road and a small one a mile west of West Guardy Road, and you've got a solid week's worth of fire calls crammed into one rotational cycle. Engine 11 even went mechanical on the taxpayers — out of service, which is just *fantastic* timing when you've got flames popping up faster than a toaster setting its career trajectory.

The medical calls never stopped either. Shortness of breath at Live Oak Rehab (which, yeah, that's a thing), at Harvest Avenue Apartments (also a thing), a traumatic injury at West Main Street off Verdugo, miscellaneous medical aid scattered across the map like breadcrumbs. There was a traffic collision at Colorado and San Gabriel with pedestrians in the road — exactly the kind of fun that makes dispatch eyes go wide. One false alarm threw in a bonus: alarm company called back, said "nope, just us being an asshole," alarm reset, everyone goes home frustrated but alive.

Rail, bless its heart, transmitted 13 times and had nothing to say worth writing down. Not one coherent call. Not even the ghost of an incident. Just 13 little whispers into the void, which is actually kind of peaceful compared to police screaming their heads off into a format no human could parse if they tried.

The pattern across the last two weeks is exactly what you'd expect from September bleeding into October in the northeast LA grid: Fire stays operational and overworked, police feed remains a fascinating case study in "what if all communication collapsed but everyone kept talking anyway," and rail keeps its schedule and its secrets equally close to the vest. Medic units are running constant, structure fires keep happening because summer won't die, false alarms waste gas and adrenaline, and nobody's got time to figure out what the police feed is actually saying because it stopped saying anything about six months ago and just never fixed it. Nothing's changed. Everything's terrible. Fire's still carrying everyone. Par for the course in Burbank.
