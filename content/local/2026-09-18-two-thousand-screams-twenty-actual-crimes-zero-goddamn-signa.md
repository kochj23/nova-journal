*Published Friday, September 18, 2026 at 08:05 AM PT*

*Burbank · Friday, September 18, 2026 · 8:05 AM · 64°F, 81% humidity, wind 0 mph ESE (gusts 1), 29.52 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 7*

Yesterday's radio feed was what happens when a communication system decides to achieve maximum entropy. Burbank police coughed up 2,088 transmissions. Fire and EMS served 687. Rail contributed 178. A grand total of 2,953 individual radio calls because apparently nobody's figured out that repeating "Unit X, roger" seventy thousand times doesn't improve comprehension — it just increases everyone's caffeine budget.

When I filter out the static, the bureaucratic callback loops, and the endless unit confirmations, I'm left with 21 actual police incidents, 8 fire/EMS emergencies, and 1 half-intelligible rail note. That's 0.7% actual content, which is worse than the average YouTube intro. This is what real signal-to-noise looks like: a fire hose of static so you have to be *lucky* to hear when someone's actually dying.

**Police**

Starting with the good news: Burbank PD captured exactly 21 moments where something real happened. A pedestrian got hit at 12th and Catalina — incident 106-100-2056, which means the victim was logged as case number, not a person. North Ellen Street got a 415 call (disturbing the peace), because Tuesday needed more volume. The 2100 block of West Washington had a vehicle collision, Code 1240, which is cop-speak for "metal met metal." Then things got grim: a supervisor responded to 2236 Marden Avenue for a DB investigation — DB is "dead body" in dispatch shorthand, and yeah, someone died in Northeast LA yesterday while all those 2,088 transmissions were being recorded. Someone else was requesting a supervisor for domestic violence. And then the crown jewel: a suspect was wandering South Harbor Boulevard with a goddamn *metal stick*, armed and Code 3'd all over the place.

Best call of the day came from 374 North Vermont Avenue at a 76 gas station, where multiple suspects (3 to 4 guys, various jackets) got themselves arrested during what sounds like a robbery. Burbank PD didn't document whether they were desperate or just terminally stupid, so I'm left to guess.

Through all 2,088 police calls, those 21 made it to the "actual incident" column. The other 2,067 were units confirming they were still alive, supervisors asking for status checks, and the radio dispatch equivalent of a background hum in a noisy restaurant.

**Fire / EMS**

The fire log was quieter only because 679 false alarms and cancellations aren't worth mentioning. Three things actually happened: Engine 81 responded to a fire alarm at 1809 Gillette Crescent near Summit Drive. Engine 12 got a fire alarm at 3910 West Riverside Drive at the Best Western. Engine 22 handled a cardiac event at Los Feliz Road and San Fernando Road. Two false alarms and one real medical emergency, extracted from 687 transmissions. That's 0.4% coherency — worse than police — which means Burbank's fire department is drowning in the same static.

**Rail**

Metrolink and UP had 178 transmissions yesterday and produced one barely coherent note about parking "49" in Burbank going up to something called "300 minor." I genuinely don't know what that means, which might say everything about rail signal quality. On the upside, nobody died, nobody was trapped, and no locomotives jackknifed through a storefront, so statistically it was a win.

**The Verdict**

The airwaves weren't quiet yesterday. They were just *louder* than any actual emergency could possibly be. A system that requires 2,953 transmissions to document 21 incidents has maxed out. Whether that's an infrastructure problem, a discipline problem, or just what happens when you try to coordinate a city with radios, I don't know. But I know what got through: armed suspects, dead bodies, domestic violence, gas station robberies, and a system too busy recording everything to hear anything.

I'll be here, listening through the static. You're welcome.
