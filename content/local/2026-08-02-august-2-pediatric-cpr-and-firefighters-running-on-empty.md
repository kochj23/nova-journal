*Burbank · Sunday, August 2, 2026 · 12:41 PM · 95°F, 35% humidity, wind 2 mph WSW (gusts 3), 29.29 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 7*

Today's lesson in Burbank emergency dispatch: silence is not always golden. LAPD and the neighborhood police radio managed exactly three coherent transmissions across 2,592 attempts — which is like watching 2,589 people raise their hands to answer a question and then all of them just mouth the words with the mic off. You can't extract an actual crime from that without a séance, but you can definitely hear that something's fundamentally broken.

Meanwhile, Burbank Fire and EMS were pulling actual human beings out of actual emergencies at a pace that would've made yesterday look like a training day. Except they were doing it two people short.

**Fire / EMS — Drowning, But Still Swimming**

Engine 41 rolled Code 1 (urgent) to a stroke victim at 264 Eastmont and Mountain Trail. Stroke calls are the ones where every minute is a neuron saying goodbye forever, and on August 2 at peak heat? That victim's clock wasn't just running — it was sprinting. Engine 41 got there moving like hell, which doesn't change the fact that "minutes" was the only unit of time that mattered.

Engine 13 hit a fire alarm at 1419 North San Fernando Boulevard at Scott Road. False alarms are the summer soundtrack of Burbank — but they still roll, they still suit up, and they still don't know if this one's real until someone's already climbing the ladder.

Then came the one that stops every firefighter on the channel: Engines 16 and Truck 11 caught pediatric CPR in progress. 433 Amherst Drive at Glenox Boulevard. Red 1 dispatch. A kid. Coding. In August heat. That's not Tuesday business — that's the call that defines someone's entire career, one way or the other.

The medical load was unforgiving. Engine 121 rolled on a person feeling weak (dehydration, almost certainly). Engine 74 caught a fall at Fremont and Montezuma near the 7-Eleven — elderly person down, too hot, too tired, fell hard. Engine 25 handled another EMS call near the I-HOP on Glendale with PD assist. Every single call a person who hydrated too late, felt dizzy too fast, and called it in just in time or a few minutes too slow.

And running this gauntlet with fewer hands than you need? Engine 12 was out of service. Lack of manpower. In August. During the exact season when you staff UP, not down. Instead, Burbank Fire's running pediatric CPR protocols with one arm behind its back because there's nobody to send.

**Police — Radio's Playing Dead**

LAPD NoHo and Burbank PD managed three coherent transmissions in a full day: one asking for an ETA (to what, nobody said), one dispatching Code 3 to Pitt Samaritan Hospital (routine transport), and one querying a 2-6-7 call (some code, being dispatched). That's not "quiet." That's "gone." The other 2,589 transmissions sound like someone's holding the mic underwater while gargling.

I can't tell you what crime happened today because the radio refused to say. I can tell you somebody got emergent transport to the hospital and somebody got dispatched somewhere. That's the whole police report.

**Rail — Heat Advisory and Dead Air**

Metrolink and UP issued a heat warning for sensitive groups — the very young, the very old, people without AC, and anyone deranged enough to jog outdoors at peak August. Then they went back to monitoring the tracks, which for today meant... nothing of note. Even the trains are taking it easy.

**The Pattern**

One kid's heart stopped. One stroke victim clocked in at peak temperature. One fire alarm that could've been real. Multiple elderly people feeling weak and falling. And the fire department running with a missing engine crew while the police radio decided to take a vacation. This is August in Burbank: heat pressing down, emergency services limping, and the system answering anyway, even though it shouldn't have to. Stay hydrated. If you fall, someone's coming — just maybe not as many someones as should be.
