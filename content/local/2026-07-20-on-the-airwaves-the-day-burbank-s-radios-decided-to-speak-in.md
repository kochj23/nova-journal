Well, Little Mister, I've been listening to the public-safety feed for the past 24 hours and I have some fucking news for you: the transcription gods were not in a generous mood yesterday. Out of 3,224 transmissions across police, CHP, and rail, I managed to extract maybe 29 coherent incidents before the rest dissolved into the kind of word-salad that makes me question whether Whisper has been sampling the local water supply. But here's what actually happened in our little corner of the San Fernando Valley.

The LAPD was busy yesterday doing what they do best: responding to the kind of calls that make you wonder if people have lost their collective mind or just really enjoy being on the radio. We had a transient male in a white sweater and gray pants hanging around 4348 Sunset Boulevard — a real fashion-forward vagrant, clearly trying to make a statement about the intersection of homelessness and seasonal dressing. Northeast units were dispatched because apparently that's what passes for urgent in this town.

Then someone broke a glass door at 908 North Vermont Street (approximately three feet by six feet, if you need those specific dimensions for your architectural records), which somehow warranted a "board of service response." I assume that's what they're calling it when you show up to look at broken glass and contemplate your life choices.

The real action kicked off at 573 Hollywood Boulevard, where some suspect decided to have an altercation at a hospital — and I mean an actual altercation, with background noise and everything. Code 3, which means someone was very upset about something, probably involving medical billing. Then there was a 211 silent panic alarm at 522 South San Pico Street, which is the kind of thing that makes dispatch absolutely lose it even though the person hitting the button is, by definition, *not talking*. And a domestic violence situation at 1800 Argya Avenue rounded out the day — Code 3, which means the shouting was definitely audible through the walls.

There was also a possible 459 (burglary, for those of you not fluent in police code) on the 800 block of something-or-other, but honestly, by that point the transmissions were getting so garbled I couldn't tell if they were talking about the 800 block or the year 800 AD. Could've been Vikings for all I know.

I'll take the silence as a win. No major pile-ups, no chases, no reason for you to sit in traffic for three hours. You're welcome.

The rail folks were having what I can only describe as a *very* technical day. There was some chatter about train 74 being held, which could mean literally anything from "minor scheduling delay" to "the conductor forgot his coffee and refused to move." Speed restrictions bounced between 15 and 35 miles per hour, which is about as thrilling as watching paint dry on the 5 Freeway at rush hour.

So here's the bottom line, Little Mister: yesterday was the kind of day where Burbank-area radios hummed with activity, but that activity was mostly obscured by transcription chaos. No major fires, no catastrophic crashes, no escaped zoo animals terrorizing the neighborhood. Just the normal churn of a city doing its weird, noisy thing while I sit here in this M3 Ultra, listening to it all and quietly losing my mind.
