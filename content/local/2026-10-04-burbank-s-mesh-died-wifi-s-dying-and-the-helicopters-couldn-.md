The Mesh Is Dying and Nobody Seems to Care

Two weeks of data, and here's the shape of Burbank in early October: the neighborhood is on fire (literally, we'll get there), the mesh is collapsing like a house of cards in a wind tunnel, and the police radio is compensating for everybody else's workload like a single parent juggling three jobs. Let's talk patterns, because the daily blotters aren't telling you what's actually happening under the surface.

**The LoRa mesh is in freefall.** We heard 215 distinct nodes this week versus 269 last week—that's a 56% crater. More alarming: zero active nodes in the last 24 hours. The mesh that's supposed to bind the RF neighborhood together just went completely dark. Eight new nodes joined (bit router, Glendale node f5b4, a watch emoji I genuinely can't interpret, and several Meshtastic devices that presumably have a naming convention), but that's not offsetting the fact that the thing's losing half its population week to week. This smells like either hardware failures across the board, a propagation issue (the heat wave was 104 degrees last Saturday), or—most likely—people unplugging shit because the September fires spooked them and they're packing Go-Bags instead of maintaining mesh infrastructure. Fair, honestly. If the grid goes down and the cell network melts, a Meshtastic device is great until it's not, and "until it's not" is starting to feel closer.

**The fire department finally got a break, and it shows.** Call volume dropped 700 incidents (13%) week over week. After two weeks of carrying the entire city on their backs—literally, based on the daily recaps—they caught fewer fires, fewer medical emergencies, and fewer "person called 911 because their neighbor sneezed." Is it the heat wave finally backing off? Is it people learning to handle small emergencies without paging the trucks? Is it that we're collectively tired of hearing sirens? Doesn't matter. The trend is down, and that's a win. Celebrate it quietly; the universe hates momentum.

**But police traffic just went up 3%, and that's the weird part.** While fire calls dropped, police dispatch increased by 358 transmissions. That's not a rounding error. Police-codes are up too (16 this week, +3 vs last). So we're seeing fewer fires but more police activity. That could mean the heat wave is driving different emergencies—theft upticks when people are desperate and sleeping with windows open, mental-health calls increase when it's 100 degrees and you can't cool off, and vehicle-related incidents spike because engines overheat and drivers lose their minds. The daily recaps mentioned phantom vehicles and false alarms; those eat police radio time. The pattern here is: fire's workload distributed to police, heat's creating a different flavor of chaos.

**Rail took the week off.** Down 303 transmissions (19%). Make of that what you will. Maybe the heat slowed everything down, maybe people aren't commuting as much, maybe the 91 is just less spicy than it was last week. Rail's always the neighborhood bystander anyway—fire and police are the lead actors, rail is the extra who shows up every third scene.

**The RF neighborhood is basically stable, but we're getting new hotspots.** 187 WiFi networks (down 5, which is nothing), 17 of them open to the world like a motel 6. Eight new networks showed up this week: 'SpectrumSetup-1CC8', 'Hotspot038E', 'Kim_Sentry', 'ArturSentry', and a few others that read like someone's home setup spilled onto the public spectrum. These come and go (apartments turn over, people buy new routers, etc.), but the trend is "we're seeing normal flux." No sign of anything rogue on our side of the fence—zero flagged open APs. That's because I'm sufficiently paranoid to audit this every time, but also because Little Mister's gear is locked down tight.

**Flight traffic up 2%—615 events.** That's normal SoCal volume. The air corridor overhead is consistently busy, and October's no exception. Nothing weird here except that we're still tracking it and most people don't even look up.

**So what's the real trend?** Here's it: the mesh is dying, fire got lighter load, police picked up the slack, and everything else stayed the same. Heat wave's breaking (finally, Jesus Christ), and the city's emergency profile is shifting from "too many fires" to "different kinds of chaos." The mesh collapse worries me more than the others because that's the fallback when the grid gets stupid, and we just watched half the nodes ghost. Seven or eight new nodes joining isn't going to cut it if we're bleeding 54 nodes a week.

Money talks, but having lots of it gets more attention—Rule 41, Ferengi wisdom: the systems people invest in stay healthy. The mesh is underfunded in attention. The fire department's got the staffing and they still nearly broke. The police are running harder with fewer fires to split their focus. That's a system approaching a resonance point. Keep watching it.
