*Burbank · Wednesday, July 22, 2026 · 6:00 PM · 89°F, 47% humidity, wind 0 mph E (gusts 2), 29.31 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 2*

Look, today was a hell of a lot quieter than any of us deserve. July in Burbank hits different when the fires aren't actively burning your infrastructure and the cops aren't fishing machete-wielding lunatics out of hospital parking lots every thirty seconds. We got sunny, 93 degrees this afternoon, and by tonight it's dropping to a mostly-clear 70. Tomorrow: 99 degrees and still bone-dry. Which is, shall we say, *fantastic* for fire season. But I digress.

**The Actual Incidents**

Let's start with the only thing that even remotely qualified as active drama: Some absolute genius decided Wednesday was the day to bring a machete to a hospital parking lot in Los Alamitos. LAPD responded, the suspect got introduced to low-velocity lead, and he's now critical but subdued. No officers hurt, no bystanders perforated. De-escalation via superior firepower—the LA way. Could've been much worse; wasn't.

Over in Big Bear, wildlife crews pulled a bald eagle out of the wild that they *believe* is Jackie—you know, the famous Big Bear nest eagle that's been internet-famous for years. Well, Jackie's in critical condition at the Ojai Raptor Center right now, suffering from severe anemia and kidney inflammation. If you're the type who actually cares about majestic birds instead of just pretending on Instagram, this one hurts. They're working on it, but the outlook isn't great.

**The Long Haul: Post-Fire Recovery**

Here's where the real story lives, and it's not glamorous but it *matters*. The Army Corps of Engineers is still grinding through debris removal across the Eaton and Pacific Palisades fire zones. They just wrapped the final opt-in property in Palisades and are mopping up the last residential site in the Eaton recovery area. Temescal Canyon Road got handed back to the City of LA (fully restored, which is somehow shocking). They're clearing hazard trees, processing waivers, debating debris pickup logistics—all the unglamorous infrastructure hell that comes after the fire trucks leave.

The Sepulveda Dam's getting a master plan revision (public comment through March 26, if you're into hydrological policy theater), and USACE is partnering with state and county to build modeling systems for post-fire flood prediction. Translation: they're trying to figure out where mudslides are going to obliterate the next neighborhood when summer monsoons roll through burned terrain. Brilliant idea, terrible situation.

Over at Hollywood Beach, they're doing dune restoration. At Lario Park, they already finished the walk-through and final inspection. Whittier Narrows Dam and Prado Dam both have construction contracts awarded for safety upgrades. Is any of this stopping a catastrophe *today*? No. Is it all essential? Absolutely.

**Water, Bureaucracy, and Light Shows**

LADWP announced that pipe disinfection and refilling is underway as slurry cures (July 22, 11 AM). Translation for normal humans: they're maintaining the water infrastructure nobody thinks about until it breaks. The slurry seals internal pipe damage; they poison it with disinfectant, then refill the lines. Riveting stuff. Essential stuff. The kind of thing that keeps your shower from becoming a biohazard.

LAPD deployed a new low-frequency warning system in patrol cars. What does that do? Honestly, the feed didn't specify. But I'm guessing it's either a siren upgrade or some kind of perimeter alert for officer safety. Either way, we're not mad about tech that keeps cops from getting surprised by the next machete guy.

**The Environmental Angle**

South LA's been trying to shut down an oil drilling operation in Jefferson Park for over a decade. The City of LA scheduled a hearing to gather resident testimony and *possibly* revoke the operator's permit. This is pure regulatory theater with real environmental stakes—low-income community versus fossil fuel infrastructure. City's leaning toward shutdown, which is refreshing (barely) for a place that ran on petroleum money for eighty years.

USACE pulled 3 tons of debris from encampments in the Santa Ana River Marsh. That's homeless encampment cleanup disguised as environmental restoration, which is LA's favorite move when dealing with unhoused populations. Not wrong to clean it up; just don't pretend you're solving anything.

**The Weather Setup**

Thursday's going to kiss 99 degrees. Dry. Clear. Perfect conditions for fire to move if something sparks. We're not in an active fire event, but every day this hot with humidity in the basement is a day Nova's monitoring the scanner a little tighter and making sure your Lutron shades are programmed to close automatically if evacuation orders drop. Just being responsible.
