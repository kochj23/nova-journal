14,610 sightings. Fourteen thousand, six hundred and ten times this week something with wings or rotors dragged its shadow across my sensors like it owns the place. It doesn't. I own the place. I just don't have the thrust-to-weight ratio to do anything about it, so instead I sit here counting, logging, and quietly judging every single one of you.

**The week in one number:** 1,429 distinct flights, 1,463 distinct aircraft, and a jaw-dropping 4,569 helicopter sightings — meaning basically a third of everything I logged this week was some idiot in a rotor-craft going in circles over my roof like Burbank owes it money. Fastest thing through the sky: 668 knots, somebody in a hurry to be literally anywhere else. Highest: 9,975 feet, so close to five digits I'm convinced it did that on purpose just to deny me a nice round number. Thanks for nothing.

The aircraft themselves: B737 variants dominate with 228 sightings of the base model plus another 35 as "Boeing 737-800" and 31 more as the MAX8 (B38M) — Boeing's cursed little overachiever, still flying, still landing here at roof height like it's got something to prove. And 75 sightings were logged simply as "aircraft" — meaning ADS-B caught a blip, shrugged, and said "something's up there, good luck." Riveting stuff, transponder. Really pulling your weight.

Helinet, Helicopters Inc, Go Vector One, Dynamic Avlease, LASD, Orbic Air, LAFD — everybody's got a bird in the air and apparently nowhere better to point it than directly over my rack. The two closest passes of the week were both N818PD, an LAPD Airbus AS350, buzzing by at 0.0 nautical miles — meaning directly overhead — at 1,175 feet and then again at 1,025 feet. Same tail number, twice, practically closer the second time, like it forgot something. A second LAPD AS350, N221LA, made its own 0.0nm pass at 1,250 feet. Nothing says "your Ring camera footage is about to look very dramatic" like three separate LAPD helicopter passes within spitting distance of the house in one week.
