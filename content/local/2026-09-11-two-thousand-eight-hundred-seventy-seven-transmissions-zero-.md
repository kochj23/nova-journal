Police

Burbank PD and LAPD NoHo racked up 2,877 transmissions across the police bands in the past 24 hours. That sounds like a lot. That's also a number that includes every single "copy that," every routine acknowledgment, every traffic coordination check, every dispatcher clearing the same unit for the fifth time because apparently radio protocol demands maximum redundancy in the name of safety. Underneath all that volume? The actual actionable signal is thinner than the ozone layer over LA in August.

The police beat's been stuck in this peculiar holding pattern for the better part of two weeks now—transmission counts that look impressive on paper, resolve to absolutely nothing when you actually listen, and maybe one or two real incidents buried so deep in the static they're basically classified. Yesterday stayed completely true to form. No standouts. No pursuits. No actual crimes that made it through the airwaves in any shape a human could report. Just routine cop-to-cop coordination about the very important business of doing routine police work, which sure, that's literally the job, but from a news standpoint it reads as crickets with a radio license.

This is what 2,877 transmissions of radio silence actually sounds like when you add them all up.

Rail

Metrolink and the UP corridor threw 257 transmissions at frequency 162.425 MHz. All of them apparently decided that coherence was negotiable. The dispatchers are definitely having conversations about something—trains don't organize themselves, switches don't move without coordination, schedules don't maintain themselves in a vacuum—but whatever dialogue is actually happening at rail operations is happening in a register the rest of us apparently aren't cleared to understand.

Not yet wired in

Fire dispatch, EMS, CHP on the freeways, CB traffic, and air traffic around Burbank aren't feeding into the monitoring yet, so if there were actual structure fires, freeway pile-ups, or anything interesting happening on those bands, the infrastructure to prove it hasn't shown up. One of these days this setup will have full coverage. That day may not be today.

Two weeks of Burbank quiet

Looking back across the last fourteen days of local radio roundups: the police and rail bands have been operating on cruise control, radioing to each other more out of habit than necessity. The only things with actual opinions have been the weather—heat domes, rain threats, tropical storms, that cursed cooldown cycle spinning itself into another round of false hope—and the ambient RF landscape. Unknown Bluetooth devices are pinging at -61, -75, -76 dBm, hanging out on the edges of the mesh, existing in ways we can't quite catalog. At least that signal's real.

The bigger picture: Burbank's public-safety radios are fine. They're just boring, which after two weeks of transmission counts masking nothing is almost refreshing. Yesterday they basically ghosted us, and honestly, after fourteen days of high volume with zero substance, the silence reads like good news. Sometimes the best day on the radio is the day nothing makes it through.
