## POLICE (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)

Columbia Avenue got a code 6. Union got one too. Somewhere on Sutton and Valencia, a unit needed relieving. That is literally everything coherent the police department transmitted into the ether in the last twenty-four hours. One thousand eight hundred and sixty-nine dispatches reduced to three sentences about relief crews and location checks. Either the LAPD NoHo desk finally discovered the mute button, or Whisper had a stroke and decided "code 6" was the only phrase worth remembering. Either way, if there was an actual crime in Burbank yesterday, I didn't hear it. Which means either nothing happened (statistically unlikely) or the feed was too garbled to translate. I'll cheerfully pretend it's the former, because the alternative is admitting the transcription quality makes a flip phone sound like a podcast.

## FIRE / EMS (Verdugo Dispatch)

Three structure fires, one vehicle fire, one traffic collision, and a cast of medical calls that suggests half of Burbank decided Tuesday was a good day to have simultaneous medical events. Fireworks without the fun.

Start with the architecture disasters. Engine 11 and Truck 15 rolled out to 31st Street and 6th Avenue for a structure fire. Then Engine 11 and Truck 15 (you'd think they'd tag up after the first one) hit 130 West Huntington Drive for another. Then the boys came back for thirds—Engine 11, Truck 15, and Medic Unit 63 stacked the apparatus for a structure fire on the 200 block of Foothill Boulevard. Three fires in a Burbank Tuesday feels like a callback to the first week of October, when fire was running a permanent open house tour of the city. The pattern's back. October knows what it's doing.

Engine 11 also caught a vehicle fire at 1603 Virginia Avenue, which is the kind of call that starts as a bad day and ends as a insurance claim form. Traffic collision came through—Engine 11 and Truck 15 again—which is code for someone drove poorly and then got hauled out by people who are much better at their jobs.

Then the medical gauntlet. Person dizzy on East London Avenue. Someone not breathing (Engine 11 handled that one). Shortness of breath call (Medic M-15 on it). Blood pressure problem at 121 North Kenwood Street (Squad 21 got that). Medical call on Lafayette Street (Engine 11, Medic M-29, no other context, which means it was probably somewhere between "might be serious" and "definitely getting transported"). Traumatic entry at 630 West Broadway—"traumatic entry" is department-speak for "something bad happened to this person and we're going to have to cut their way out"—Engine 11 and BLS E-21 on scene. Medical aid at 1601 Avenue of the Stars (yes, that's a real street and yes, fire goes there just like everywhere else). Stroke symptoms on South 10th Street. Cardiac issue at 521 West Colorado Street (Squad 21 again). And a medical call at 1299 East Green Street.

Somewhere in there, Engine E-31 took an hour out of service for training, which is very responsible and also means there was one less unit available to handle all of this. The universe's timing is impeccable.

One test warning got broadcast too, which is the bureaucratic equivalent of "we're checking that this thing still works," conducted at full volume across the radio during a day when every available engine was already committed. Because efficiency is a suggestion.

The count: three fires, one vehicle incident, one collision, and enough medical calls to fill a clinic. Engine 11's log looked like a line at the DMV by noon. Verdugo dispatch threw everything at this Tuesday, and Tuesday threw it all back.
