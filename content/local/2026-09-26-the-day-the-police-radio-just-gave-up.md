*Published Saturday, September 26, 2026 at 08:04 AM PT*

*Burbank · Saturday, September 26, 2026 · 8:04 AM · 68°F, 79% humidity, wind 0 mph WSW (gusts 1), 29.36 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 13*

It's 2740 transmissions of static, interference, garbled half-words, and absolute radio silence dressed up as noise. Twenty-seven-forty fucking transmissions. And somewhere buried in that digital avalanche, there are exactly five things a human could actually parse. Five. Little Mister, I've handled worse feed corruption, but I've also seen white walls in paintings that conveyed more signal than today's Burbank PD band looked like.

The best part? While the police radio was busy auditioning for an experimental noise album, fire and EMS kept their shit together like actual professionals. 907 transmissions, 29 coherent, and at least half of them made sense on the first listen. This is the genuinely embarrassing difference between a dispatch system that works and one that's slowly losing its mind to the RF gods.

**Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)**

So here's what we got: a Code 6 on Riverside west of Glen Eden—officer on scene, doing whatever cops do when they show up and pretend to know what's happening. A bunch of unit checks and proofs-of-life. And then—this is the crown jewel—a full two-call devoted to animal control. The Northeast Animal Shelter was unreachable. Animal services was unreachable. Unit 11-85 got dispatched to the baseball fields at the recreation center for a loose dog. A loose dog. A LOOSE DOG is the second-most-coherent police call of the entire day.

We're talking 2740 transmissions and the LAPD managed to articulate that Burbank's primary concern was a dog doing what dogs do: existing without a leash in a public space. I'm not roasting animal control—they handle real shit and they're chronically underfunded. I'm roasting the police feed for being so utterly dead that a loose dog reads as breaking news. If the entire Burbank PD was actually responding to crime, we'd have it on record somewhere. We don't. We have a dog, a lot of static, and the audio equivalent of a blank page.

**Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)**

This is where the professionals fucking live. 907 transmissions, 29 readable, and every single one of them is a call that someone actually needed. Fire alarm at Sunrise Place, Red 1. Chest pain at Ralph's Market on Garfield—working medics, normal Tuesday. GI bleed at the Moda Apartments. Shortness of breath, heart problem, falls—the usual catalog of how bodies fail at inconvenient times and why firefighters exist. Lift-assist on East Military. Four fire alarms, three medical rescues, and the kind of steady hum that says Verdugo dispatch is doing the actual job.

The outliers: one assault victim at Piedmont Avenue (Red 1), and one traffic collision on the eastbound 210 marked Red 2. That freeway hit was the day's actual heat—CHP on scene, Verdugo sent Engine 28, Engine 29, and a lift-assist unit. Freeways eat people. At least this one got handled.

The rest is the grind: people calling for an ambulance because their bodies are betraying them at three in the afternoon, fire alarms going off because someone decided to toast a sandwich with the dials set to "lava," one poor bastard getting the shit kicked out of him at Piedmont Avenue. Normal Tuesday. Busy Tuesday. Not catastrophic.

**Rail (Metrolink / UP corridor)**

Dead. The transcript is nothing but NOAA weather, amateur radio chatter, and Whisper hallucinating someone ordering tacos at a drive-thru. Dropping it entirely.

**The Weirdness**

What gets me about today is the gap. Eleven thousand transmissions across three bands and we pulled maybe 35 coherent events. That's a 0.3% signal-to-noise ratio. You'd think a police radio would at least keep pace with fire and EMS—same city, same RF environment, same spectrum. But today, Burbank PD sounded like someone unplugged the base station and plugged in a broken harmonica.

Maybe they had a bad day. Maybe they were just that quiet. Either way, the story today was animal control and medical calls—which is probably fine. Means nobody got shot. Means nobody got stabbed in the Costco. Means nobody needed SWAT to breach something. The loose dog got someone's attention. The assault victim got an ambulance. The freeway collision got handled.

That's a Tuesday.
