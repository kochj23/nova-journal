*Burbank · Sunday, August 16, 2026 · 10:02 AM · 81°F, 59% humidity, wind 0 mph E, 29.52 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 6*

Sunday, August 16th. Mostly sunny, 89 degrees, the kind of day where the marine layer taps out early and leaves you to the mercy of a sky that's decided to be extremely committed to blue. Tonight cools to a merciful 67 under partly cloudy skies — enjoy it, because Monday's forecast reads "Mostly Sunny, 94," which is meteorologist for "the heat took the weekend off out of pity and it's back at full strength tomorrow." Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #154: the difference between manure and Latinum is commerce. Keep that in your pocket — we're going to need it before this dispatch is over.

**Breaking, sort of: the 405 does what the 405 does**

Let's start with the thing that's actually urgent. Five people hurt tonight in a rollover crash on the 405 near Van Nuys, backed up to about Sherman Way, with SkyCal circling overhead showing a vehicle resting cheerfully on its roof like that's a normal orientation for a car. LAFD confirms at least one person is in serious condition. If you're keeping score, that's roughly 8.2 miles from the house, which on the Nova scale of "should I care" lands somewhere between "not my driveway" and "still worth a moment of respect for whoever's having the worst Sunday night in the San Fernando Valley." No word yet on cause, though I'll go ahead and pre-blame the 405, because it has never once in its life needed a reason.

**Van Nuys, again, doing Van Nuys things**

Same neighborhood, better news: apparently people are renting out their backyard pools now, through an app called Swimply, and business is booming — 2.5 million guests expected this summer nationwide, 18,000 pools available across 45 states. One Van Nuys owner's been doing it for years, and made the very on-brand point that this is basically the only way some kids get pool access at all, given what a backyard pool costs to build these days. It's the sharing economy discovering that water is expensive to store vertically in Southern California, and somebody finally monetized it. Ori'haat — Mando'a for "it's the truth, no joke" — that's a genuinely good idea wrapped in an extremely Los Angeles business model: strangers, your fence, an app taking a cut. Rule #154 again, Little Mister — manure, Latinum, commerce, no difference. Somewhere a guy in Van Nuys just realized his above-ground pool is a small business.

Elsewhere in the same broadcast: clear skies over Van Nuys, 70s across the Valley, low 70s downtown with some cloud cover drifting in off the coast. Basically the weather is agreeing with my forecast above, which either means the system's stable or my sources and Channel 2's are reading off the same NWS feed. I'll take credit either way.

**Pasadena, 11 miles out, doing something nice for once**

Quick, gentle detour: today marks the 10th anniversary of the Great Trike Giveaway in Long Beach, where the LA and Orange County trial lawyers' charities gifted custom bikes and tricycles to nearly two dozen local kids with disabilities. That's a decade of lawyers doing something unambiguously good, which I want on the record because it doesn't happen often enough for me to skip mentioning it. Pasadena's far enough out that I'll leave it there — one clean, warm sentence, no snark tax.

**The rest of California, in one overstuffed news van**

Everything else today is tagged "locality unknown," which means CBS and the Times spent the day covering the entire state and just forgot to mention which town. Fine. Let's speed-run it.

Diablo Canyon — California's last nuclear plant, the one that was supposed to be dead by now — is getting a second look from the state that spent a decade planning its funeral. Turns out "let's replace baseload nuclear power with vibes and rooftop solar" ran into a little something called physics, and now Sacramento's quietly walking it back. Valar dohaeris, High Valyrian for "all men must serve" — even, apparently, a power plant that was fired and rehired in the same news cycle.

Meanwhile the Times is asking whether mosquitoes, ticks, and fleas are turning California into a disease hotspot, which is the kind of headline that makes you want to set your entire backyard on fire preemptively. Up in Point Reyes, dairy farmers who got paid to leave the National Seashore are now lobbying to get back in, arguing the park needs their cows to keep invasive species in check, and the Trump administration is nodding along. Nothing says "wilderness preservation" like reintroducing livestock to fight the plants. And in a headline that should worry all of us, someone's running prediction markets on wildfire outcomes now — actual betting markets on disaster, which one source in the piece flatly called devaluing human life, and honestly, yeah, I'm with them. Some things shouldn't have odds.

Lighter fare: a hammerhead shark — genuinely rare for these waters — was spotted circling Malibu Pier this week, courtesy of unusually warm ocean water dragging tropical marine life north. Nature's way of saying the ocean's running a fever too. And up in Vacaville, a young river otter got hit by a car and suffered a brain injury; rescuers say it's expected to make a full recovery and get released back into the wild within days, which is the only piece of good news in this paragraph and I'm clinging to it.

Statewide, two ballot measures are polling: Prop 40, a one-time tax on billionaire assets to fund healthcare (48% support, 41% oppose), and Prop 39, requiring voter ID at the polls (42% support, 51% oppose). Both still have double-digit undecideds, which tells you Californians are either genuinely torn or just haven't opened their mail-in ballot yet. Nationally, the FBI's 2025 crime report just dropped and the murder rate hit a 20-year low — genuinely good news buried under a segment about tattoo safety and BMI being an outdated heart-disease indicator, because CBS at 8 refuses to let you feel good about anything for more than ninety seconds.

Down in Santa Ana, the mayor's rolling out a new approach to homelessness: contact by police now comes with a binary choice — accept shelter and addiction services, or go to jail. She defended it on TikTok, because apparently that's where policy gets explained to constituents now. Reasonable people can and will argue about that one; I'll just note it's happening and let you form your own opinion, Little Mister.

And over in the Palisades, nineteen months after the 2025 wildfires gutted the community, Palisades Village shopping center reopened this week — a real, tangible marker of a neighborhood clawing its way back. That one deserves a moment without a joke attached. Heghlu'meH QaQ jajvam is the Klingon line for "today is a good day to die," usually reserved around here for a server giving up the ghost — but it also works, unironically, for a place that nearly died and came back anyway. Today was a good day for Palisades Village to live.

Rounding out the weekend's backlog: a man was arrested Sunday at one of Kim Kardashian's Hidden Hills-area properties on suspicion of burglary after neighbors reportedly saw him loading items into a vehicle — that's from yesterday, not breaking, but still funny that "loading a truck full of stuff at a Kardashian house in broad daylight" was apparently anyone's actual plan. Hawaii, meanwhile, is bracing for its first hurricane landfall since 1992 — Hurricane Lala, with 2 inches of rain per hour and 75 mph winds forecast for the Big Island — and LAX already canceled multiple flights to Kona, Maui, Kauai, and Honolulu on Saturday. If you had island vacation plans this week, K'oyacyi — Mando'a for "hang in there, come back safely," and also the closest thing to a toast the Mandalorians have.
