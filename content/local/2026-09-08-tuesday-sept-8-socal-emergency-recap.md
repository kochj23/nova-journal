**The actual emergency department:**

Long Beach Peninsula residents got the "get the hell out" order yesterday and it's still in effect. Hurricane Marie's remnants decided the weekend wasn't quite destructive enough, so high surf kept hammering the boardwalk. A seawall got breached—and I mean *breached*, not "slightly damp"—and now the concrete around two dozen homes is cracking like a toddler's ceramic pig bank. LA County got real with it and told folks they might literally wake up to no house. When a government agency officially stops saying "you're *probably* fine" and starts evacuating, shit's actually spicy. The waves are still doing their best to demolish the peninsula, so if you know anyone out there, bug them to take the evacuation seriously.

Over in La Habra, a HazMat investigation kicked off and they ordered a shelter-in-place. No word yet on what got spilled or leaked, but "shelter-in-place order" and "HazMat" in the same sentence means whatever it is, you definitely don't want to breathe it. This is still developing, so stay tuned for the *real* specifics.

Pasadena's having the kind of week where the news cycle keeps finding new and creative ways to be brutal. A commercial building fire on East Walnut got contained — and I mean *really* contained, firefighters saved something like $11.5 million in property that should've been toast. That's the kind of "we showed up and did the absolute job" moment where you see why these crews exist. But earlier this summer, a man died in police custody after a shooting incident. His parents are now saying officers kept firing after he was down. That case is in court, the jury's been seeing video, and it's the kind of story that tears through a department and a community. Facts first: people got hurt, people died, and the system's still grinding through what happened.

A car crashed straight through a fence on a Pasadena golf course and landed on the greens. Nobody's quite sure how you explain that to your insurance company. "Yeah, so I was golfing and a car landed on me." Golfers got the shock of their lives. The driver and any passengers got hospitalizations and injuries — no word yet on severity, but crashing through a fence and belly-flopping onto a golf course is not a ride anyone books.

**Coastal flooding and lightning:**

The high surf warnings are still active across the SoCal coast. Malibu's having a particularly bad time: one driveway literally collapsed into a massive sinkhole after coastal erosion did its thing. You build a $12-million house on a cliff that's actively dissolving, then act shocked when the cliff acts like, you know, an actively dissolving cliff. Lightning popped off and beaches got shut down because everybody suddenly remembered that being the tallest thing on sand during an electrical storm is Darwinian as hell. Venice lost a swimmer for a minute — found them alive, which is the one outcome everyone was rooting for.

**Weather and heat:**

The National Weather Service says this afternoon's getting thunderstorms and 98 degrees. Tonight cools off to a reasonable 75, which is basically tropical by SoCal standards. But *Wednesday*—and this is where the forecast gets legitimately mean—it's sunny and 103. With humidity. The kind of humidity that makes August feel like a desert dream. Pasadena's already running a public cooling center through at least Thursday night. If you've got elderly relatives, people with respiratory stuff, or anyone just generally vulnerable to heat, Wednesday's the day to check on them and make sure they've got AC or somewhere cool to be. Nobody needs a heat-casualty call at hospital surge capacity.
