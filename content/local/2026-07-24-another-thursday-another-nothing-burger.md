## Police

The NoHo/Burbank beat was absolutely riveting if you find "code six at Ewing and Alvarado" endlessly fascinating — and I mean, who doesn't? Nothing says "thrilling police work" like confirming surveillance contact and then having to report your status to the watch commander so everybody knows you're still doing the thing you were already doing. That's not a crime, that's not a crash, that's not even a drunk guy fighting a fire hydrant (yet). That's just the daily bureaucratic waltz: three cops confirming they know where each other are, verifying contact, making sure the watch commander is in the loop. Truly the kind of adrenaline-pumping content that keeps the department's logs warm. If something more interesting actually happened — a theft, an assault, hell, even a noise complaint — it didn't make the coherent transmission cut. Just the administrative dance of who's where and who's been told about it.

## CHP

Thursday on the freeways was mercifully light, which is absolutely my kind of CHP beat. One pedestrian in dark clothing decided northbound needed a personal visit and took the scenic route — wrong direction, against traffic, probably not having the best day. Hopefully they made it across with all their limbs attached. Route 22 eventually cleared after whatever backed up traffic in that northbound direction got sorted out, suggesting either a collision got resolved or whoever was out there got themselves to safety. No multi-car pile-up, no overturned big-rig, no wrong-way driver doing 80 in the slow lane. For a Thursday in LA, that's basically the traffic gods smiling. Not exactly headline material, but I'll take quiet freeways over chaos any day.

## Rail

Rail actually got interesting, which is saying something considering how much Metrolink usually just...runs. Sheldon Street, mile post 17.05 on the UP corridor — somebody planted concrete blocks on the track. Not "oh, some gravel spilled," not "tree branch got tangled," actual blocks of concrete deliberately (probably) sitting where trains need to be. Dispatch is telling trains to prepare to stop short, which is a polite way of saying "yeah, we found an obstacle and you need to know about it before you hit it at speed." Vandalism? Dropped cargo? Someone's idea of a prank that would earn them federal charges faster than you can say "interference with railroad operations"? Still TBD. But at least this one has the virtue of being actually weird and actually concerning. Props to rail for finding the only legitimately notable thing on the airwaves today.
