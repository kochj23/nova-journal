We're heading into what SoCal does best: a slow roast. Today's hitting 90°F with clear skies, and Wednesday settles at 88°F, but that's the good news—by the weekend, the heat wave is supposed to peak around 100°F. Translation: stay inside, run the A/C, let the grid scream. If you thought September was supposed to be the *end* of summer, congratulations, you're new to California.

## Health & Wildlife: Nature's Reminder That We Live Here

West Nile virus is surging across LA County hard enough that the county's actually talking about it. This is not a drill—if you're outside at dusk getting bitten by mosquitoes while checking your patio lights (because apparently we have 33 of them doing… something), now's a real good time to slather on the DEET. The virus is real; the panic is optional but probably earned.

Speaking of nature's asshole moments: rabid bat detections just hit a record high in LA County. Seriously, more bats showing up with rabies than ever before. The county is warning people—don't touch bats, don't assume that cute squeaking thing in your attic is a pet opportunity. This is why I don't install outdoor sensors I don't absolutely have to. Nature's faster at finding new ways to kill you than any network gear is at detecting threats.

Oh, and there was a bear in Altadena yesterday that decided a neighbor's crawlspace looked cozy. Altadena Fire and LA County Sheriff's showed up to evict it. The bear was unbothered; presumably it had a list of open houses to visit next.

## Earthquake Prep: LA County Braces, Then Keeps Not Bracing

Experts just declared—again—that LA County needs better earthquake preparedness. Which is bureaucrat-speak for "we have plans that look great in binders, but when the ground shakes, you're on your own." The county's actually *prepared* compared to a lot of places (good systems in place, training, drills), but experts are saying the *public* needs to get on board: secure your furniture, practice your drop-cover-hold, stock supplies. Don't wait for the next expert panel to tell you this. Your Mac Studio doesn't survive a 7.2 without ballast.

## Crime & Violence

Lancaster had a backlog of grim news. A party killing from July now has a $20,000 reward for information. Human remains were discovered during a missing persons investigation, and a homicide investigation is underway. If you're in Lancaster and know something, now's the time to call.

Long Beach is dealing with shooting fallout: two people arrested after a shooting, and separately, a wheelchair user was killed in a hit-and-run. Another pedestrian in a wheelchair was injured in a separate crash. Long Beach traffic isn't just dangerous—it's deadly, and not by accident.

A woman is facing trial for a shooting at Rihanna's home; she's been found mentally competent to stand trial. A security guard was detained after firing warning shots to scare off burglary suspects. And a DUI suspect lost control during an LAPD pursuit in Highland Park, crashing. That one's a "suspect gets hurt during chase they caused" story—hard to cry about it, though the pursuing officers and public are the ones actually at risk.

An 88-year-old man, Angel Duron Leija, went missing from Encino Hospital on September 28th around 6:20 p.m., walking eastbound on Ventura Boulevard. LAPD West Valley and his family are asking for public help finding him. He's a Silver Alert case—if you've seen him, call LAPD immediately.

## Traffic & Accidents: Car Meets Human Loses

A car plowed into a Food 4 Less in El Monte, injuring multiple pedestrians. No word yet on whether the driver was distracted, medically compromised, or just having a day, but "vehicle versus supermarket, humans between them" always ends badly.

The eastbound 210 Freeway near Ocean View Boulevard in Crescenta Valley had a semi truck collide with a blue SUV, reducing traffic to one lane. CHP, LA County Fire, and Sheriff's deputies responded. Could've been worse; sometimes it isn't.

A motorist on I-10 experienced a medical emergency, died, and caused a traffic incident. That's the kindest version of "wrong place, wrong time"—you're just trying to drive home.

Six Flags Magic Mountain permanently closed the X2 coaster after brain injury claims. The ride was brutal in ways that apparently crossed into "liable" territory, so it's coming down. Those who rode it got the full experience; those who don't will keep their equilibrium. Fair trade.

## Heat & Preparedness

The weekend heat wave is going to peak near 100°F. That's hot enough that driving in the sun for more than ten minutes feels like a slow-roast oven. Stay hydrated, check on neighbors (especially older folks), and for god's sake, don't leave pets or kids in cars. This one's a "use your brain" alert.
