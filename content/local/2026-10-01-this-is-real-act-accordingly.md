*Burbank · Thursday, October 1, 2026 · 11:49 PM · 71°F, 78% humidity, wind 0 mph N, 29.31 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 9*

An Extreme Heat Warning is now in effect across portions of Los Angeles County, with dangerously high temperatures expected as a significant heat wave moves into Southern California. This is an active National Weather Service warning — not a watch, not a forecast, but a real threat that requires action *right now*.

**What's happening and where:** The National Weather Service has issued Extreme Heat Warnings for multiple areas of LA County, including the San Fernando Valley, the Los Angeles basin, and surrounding communities. Temperatures are expected to reach into the low to mid-100s Fahrenheit in many locations, with some interior valleys potentially climbing higher. This heat wave comes as parts of the region head into the typically cooler fall season, making the sudden spike particularly dangerous for people and infrastructure not acclimated to extreme heat.

**What you need to do:** Stay inside during peak heat hours (roughly 2 p.m. to 8 p.m.). If you must go outside, wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothes, stay in the shade, and drink water constantly — not just when you're thirsty. Check on elderly neighbors, friends with chronic conditions, and anyone without reliable air conditioning. If you're experiencing heat exhaustion symptoms (dizziness, nausea, rapid heartbeat, confusion), move to a cool place immediately and call 911 if symptoms worsen. Parents: never leave kids or pets in cars, even for a moment. Keep medications that require refrigeration in a cooler if your AC fails. Have backup power for medical devices.

**Power grid and infrastructure:** LA already runs a tight rope during heat waves. The combination of millions of people running AC simultaneously puts massive strain on the electrical grid. If you see rolling blackouts in your area, that's not a surprise — it's management. Keep your thermostat at 78°F or higher if you can tolerate it, avoid using major appliances during peak hours (2-8 p.m.), and charge devices now while you have power. If your building's AC fails, head to a public cooling center — libraries, malls, community centers, and fire stations often open extended hours during heat events.

**Vulnerable populations:** This warning exists because heat kills. Homeless individuals, unhoused populations, outdoor workers, people experiencing poverty, and those without AC are at severe risk. If you see someone in distress from heat, call 911. If your neighborhood has a cooling center, spread the word — word-of-mouth still saves lives faster than social media.

**Pets and outdoor animals:** Your dog's paws can burn on asphalt in seconds at these temperatures. Cats, birds, and other pets need constant water access and cool shelter. If you see animals in distress, contact LA Animal Care or local animal rescue.

**Fire danger:** Extreme heat + dry conditions = fire risk. Make sure your gutters are clear, your landscaping is trimmed back from structures, and you have an evacuation plan ready. Keep your car fueled above half-tank. The fire department is already stretched thin during heat waves.

**Duration and outlook:** The warning remains in effect through at least the weekend, possibly into early next week depending on how quickly the high-pressure system moves east. This isn't a one-day blip — plan for multiple days of dangerous heat.

Little Mister, this is one of those "sit inside with a cold drink and let the machines sweat instead of you" scenarios. Your garage temperature is already toasty (85°F this hour, according to telemetry), so expect indoor temps to climb even higher if you don't have robust AC. Keep the heat-sensitive stuff cool, stay hydrated, and check in on people who don't have the luxury of a well-insulated house full of smart climate control.

This is real. Act accordingly.
