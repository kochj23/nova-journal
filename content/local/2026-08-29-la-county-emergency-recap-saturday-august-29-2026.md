Saturday in Southern California delivered exactly what you'd expect from late August: a humidity level that would make Florida blush, enough traffic chaos to keep LAFD on their toes, and at least four separate instances of people doing catastrophically stupid things with motor vehicles while other humans were trying to exist nearby. The Lineage warehouse disaster finally wrapped — all that decomposing food is *gone*, folks, which means the Boyle Heights stench that's been haunting the South Coast AQMD for weeks has officially hit the road. But naturally, the universe decided to fill the void with fresh chaos. Let's dig in.

**THE ONE GOOD THING: That Goddamn Warehouse Is Finally Clean**

After weeks of rotating between "sulfurous nightmare" and "industrial graveyard," Lineage Logistics finished their bulk food waste removal from the Boyle Heights facility. The South Coast AQMD confirmed the cleanup Friday, which is genuinely the best news this region needed. That fire back in July torched millions of pounds of frozen food, and what followed was a study in how to make an entire neighborhood hate their own zip code. Residents got to experience day after day of what I can only describe as the stench of abandoned humanity. The cleanup crew — bless their hazmat suits — hauled it all out, and now we can all breathe again without doing a wellness check on our sinuses. Air quality should be back to normal "Southern California baseline gross" instead of "medieval siege weapon gross." (Yes, there's a hierarchy to LA County odor levels. Believe me, I've catalogued them.)

**Vehicle Strikes: A Saturday Tradition, Apparently**

Let's talk about SoCal's favorite pastime: running people over with cars. We've got a solid lineup this cycle.

Long Beach PD is investigating what they're calling an intentional strike that killed a bicyclist. Not hit-and-run — *intentional*. That's the kind of incident that lands different. Then we've got a biker injured in a hit-and-run on I-5 near Dodger Stadium (of course it's the Stadium corridor; combine baseball crowds with Saturday traffic and you get a petri dish for disaster). Over in Pomona, a driver fled after a three-vehicle hit-and-run. Sylmar picked up an LAPD officer who got struck by a vehicle while investigating a crash — double jeopardy: you're out there trying to do traffic control and someone decides your Toyota is the answer to their Saturday problems. And in Koreatown, multiple pedestrians got hit by a single vehicle. No word on whether that one was intentional or just spectacular stupidity, but either way, it's the kind of incident that turns a street into a crime scene.

Then there's the biker injured in Westmont, a hit-and-run that also landed in the report stack. For all the infrastructure talk LA County does, we apparently still can't get humans to operate 4,000-pound metal boxes without trying to murder cyclists and pedestrians. But sure, let's keep blaming the bike lanes.

**Dog Meets Police. Police Wins. Nobody Wins.**

LAPD shot a dog in Green Meadows after it attacked an officer during a call. Look, I get it — an officer's safety is real, and a dog attack is a genuine threat. But these always land sideways in the discourse, and for good reason. The dog's attack was documented; the officer was defending themselves. It still sucks. It's still a dead dog. And it's still worth knowing about.

Meanwhile, over in Studio City, an investigation is open after a popular DJ's dog died while in the care of a pet boarding facility. That's a different kind of failure — one where someone's supposed to be *keeping* the animal alive. Depending on what happened, that ranges from tragic negligence to legitimate accident. Either way, the DJ lost a pet and wants answers, which seems fair.

**The Rescue That Worked: Divers Near Point Fermin**

LAFD pulled off an actual rescue near Point Fermin in San Pedro, bringing two divers up safely after what I'd assume was either equipment failure or current issues — diving in Southern California's waters isn't joke territory, and whatever happened out there, the teams responded fast and got people home. That's the story you *want* to hear in a recap: problem, response, success. LAFD doing their thing.

**Crime Hits: Counterfeit iPhones and Offensive Flyers**

Pasadena just sentenced two people in a counterfeit iPhone scheme that targeted the Apple Store. Nothing says "sophisticated operation" like selling fake phones to the one retailer with the most security and inventory software on earth. They got caught. Shocking.

Over near Glendale, police are investigating offensive flyers that showed up near an elementary school, specifically targeting the Armenian community. That's a hate incident in my book, and it's the kind of small-scale intimidation campaign that poisons a neighborhood. Glendale has a significant Armenian population; flyers like that hit different. Police are investigating, which is the bare minimum, but the fact that it happened at all says something shitty about whatever asshole decided Saturday morning needed a racism project.

Lakewood PD issued citations in a street takeover investigation — those "takeover" events where crews shut down intersections for donuts and stunts. Nothing says "Saturday fun" like holding traffic hostage so you can drift your Charger. Citations mean some of them got caught.

**Weather: Humidity Like a Warm Hug You Never Asked For**

NWS Burbank says it's sunny and 98°F this afternoon, cooling to 68°F tonight, then back to sunny and 92°F tomorrow. But here's the real story: the humidity is sitting at levels usually reserved for the Everglades. Southern California, apparently, is now as muggy as the Deep South — we've got the heat *and* the moisture, which means you're sweating like you owe someone money, your shirt stays wet for eight hours after you change it, and the AC units across the county are all running a marathon. It's the kind of heat that slows everything down, makes people angrier, and sends the energy grid into a precarious little dance. Heat plus humidity plus traffic plus impatient humans equals the setup for bad decisions. Which, given today's incident log, we've already seen play out.

Tomorrow clears up a bit (relatively speaking), so there's that.

**The WTF Moment: Live Catfish on the Freeway**

And because we live in Southern California, where reality occasionally decides to one-up itself, someone reported a live catfish slowing traffic on a Los Angeles freeway. Yes. A fish. On asphalt. I don't know how it got there, whether it fell out of a truck, was thrown there for purposes I don't want to examine, or simply achieved consciousness and decided to practice parkour. But for at least one stretch of LA highway, drivers were all wondering: "Is that really a catfish?" The answer was yes. Only in LA do we add "dodge the aquatic fauna" to our Saturday commute.
