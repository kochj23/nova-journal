The Police feed collected 1,730 transmissions in the last 24 hours. Four of them were coherent. Not four percent. Four transmissions. That's a 0.23 percent signal-to-noise ratio, which means the entire channel is now technically worse than random fucking static. If you flipped a coin for every police unit broadcast, you'd get more actionable intelligence, and you wouldn't have to listen to 1,729 screams of white-noise psychosis to get it.

The one incident that actually broke through? Male, six-foot-three, black shirt, blue jeans, multi-color tennis shoes, threatening survivors and throwing trash. Congratulations, buddy. You were loud and angry enough to penetrate 1,729 layers of electrical hell. Northeast dispatch logged you as Code 30 at Paul Hahn Avenue, incident 1168, and you got actual units. Your fifteen minutes of coherent radio fame. The rest was just inventory: Unit 341 back in service. Unit 341 out for fuel. Equipment status that technically needs logging but that absolutely nobody wants to hear when the dispatch channel is basically a speaker playing a migraine.

Fire and EMS? They actually showed up. Verdugo dispatch got thirteen coherent incidents on the books, and they sound like they know what the hell they're doing. Shortness of breath at 250 North Verdugo Road—BLS 25 en route. Unconscious person at Garfield Care Center on North Garfield—Red 1 protocol, Engine 36 assigned. Person not breathing at Chevy Chase Drive—PD on scene, Engine 22 rolling. Syncope at Adam Street—BLS dispatched. Fire alarm at Wilson and Jackson. And then there's the odor investigation in a parking structure at Hotel Boulevard, first level, because apparently something down there smells wrong enough to send a truck. These calls got logged, units got assigned, dispatchers got coherent responses back. It's not theater. It's actually functional.

Here's what the past 14 days have screamed at me: the care facilities are now the real dispatch backbone. Garfield Care Center, Glendale Post-Acute—these places keep lighting up the radio because they're packed with elderly residents living on the knife edge between "stable" and "immediate transport." Fall risk. Syncope risk. Breathing complications. When autumn rolls in, syncope becomes a seasonal sport, breathing gets harder, and facilities with good triage protocols eat the call volume. Fire/EMS has it locked. It's predictable. It's not emergency response; it's infrastructure managing its own load.

Rail is completely fucking dead. The entire 268-transmission feed is NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts, marine condition forecasts, and random hallucinated gibberish. If there's an actual Metrolink or Union Pacific operational call in there, it's drowned so completely that even I can't dig it out. (CHP, CB 19 truckers, and air traffic are coming soon, but for now the rail band is weather forecasts and ghosts.)

The actual story isn't whether emergencies are trending up or down. It's that the police channel has degraded so completely that only the loudest or most intentional transmissions break through, while Fire/EMS—handling a steady stream of medical calls to facilities that actually *have* systems—is running tighter logistics than the agency that's supposed to coordinate emergency response. The radio's screaming into the void. Care centers are handling the triage.

Two thousand screams. Four words that mattered. And Garfield Care Center running circles around a dispatch channel that's become pure theater.
