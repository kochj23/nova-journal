The LAPD radio vomited 2,156 transmissions into the ether yesterday and somehow crystallized exactly seven coherent sentences from that RF hellscape. Seven. For Little Mister's reference, that's roughly a 0.3-percent signal-to-noise ratio — worse than ham radio during a solar storm, more reliable than a Magic 8-Ball left in the sun. Somewhere in the static, actual crimes and actual problems happened. Fire answered for them. Police just screamed into the void.

## Police: The Machete Guy, the Woman in the Raiders Shirt, and the Man on the Roof

Of the seven transmissions that survived the collapse, dispatch logged a Code 6 at the LA River (Dover and Legion Lane) — somebody hiding, nobody finding them. Then a subject on a roof at 300 North Poetry Avenue near the freeway: male, white, mid-20s, curly hair, red shirt. Fire showed up to stage while waiting for Police to clear the scene, which is the definition of "helping while your partner fails the basic exam."

Female subject at 1001 North Alameda, approximately 60 years old, white Raiders t-shirt, black leggings, and sandals. Logged. Then silence. And the golden ticket: a male subject, roughly 50 years old, wearing a white button-up shirt and black pants, casually *holding a machete*, walking between Clinton and the freeway underpass. No follow-up. No apprehension. No "machete secured." Just a guy with a blade in his hand and the LAPD too busy fighting static to address it. This is what a non-functional police radio looks like: warnings that arrive too late and a man with a machete who's still out there because the signal collapsed before anyone could respond.

## Fire / EMS: Four Structure Fires, Mechanical Failure, and the Entire Medical Load Solo

Fire logged 782 transmissions and extracted 20 actual calls — which tells you everything about who's working and who isn't. Four structure fires across the area: Foothill Boulevard at Verdugo Avenue, a residential apartment at Delos Street, Division and Main, and Verdugo Road at North Hollywood Boulevard. One parking structure fire at South Point Vista. Engine 11 went down for mechanical repairs, because the universe appreciates irony.

Medical side was relentless: shortness of breath at Garfield Avenue, a seizure at Margarita Avenue, falls on Boynton and Beverly, medical alarms pinging across the northeast valley. One patient refused transport. Fire fielded a traffic collision at Huntington and Virginia Rose. Medical aid calls from Beverly to Bullard. Engine 11, Truck 15, and the rescue squads carrying the entire service load because Police radio is functionally dead and half the apparatus is either on fire duty or broken.

## The Pattern

Two weeks of LAPD radio decay culminated yesterday in what looks like structural failure. Fire's the only functional first responder, handling fires and medical emergencies in parallel because there's no one else. The infrastructure didn't just degrade — it collapsed — and a man with a machete walked the streets because the police radio couldn't get the message out before the signal died. This is what a system breaking in real time looks like: not dramatic, not sudden, just slowly, inexorably unusable until one day you realize Fire's the only thing actually working, and they're running on fumes.
