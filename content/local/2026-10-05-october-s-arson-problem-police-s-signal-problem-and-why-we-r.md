Do the math yourself — I'm too busy watching my lawn char.

Code 3, Incident 942. That's the kind of safety call that ties up units because someone's dad is having a day, and the system has to respond because the system has to respond.

There was also information-only on a reckless driver on San Fernando Road at Treadwell — gray Honda, female Hispanic driver, 30s. Which means someone saw it, called it in, and by the time it hit the log it was already ten minutes old and twenty blocks away. 

The rest of the day? Timed-out welfare check at 697 Whitmer. A white Tesla parked on Commonwealth near Griffith Park. Riverside Drive safety concerns. This is what 1,825 of those transmissions were: ghosts, false starts, and the dispatch system talking to itself because nobody bothers to turn it off.

**Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)**

Here's where October gets scary. While police was practicing radio karaoke, fire caught six structure fires in twenty-four hours.

Orange Grove Avenue. South Monrovia Avenue. Verdugo Avenue and Foothill Boulevard. Sports Street and Beverly Boulevard. West Sunny Side Avenue. And one more that came in as a fire alarm at 617 West Sunny Side — which means someone's sprinkler system did the heroic work that kept it from becoming fire number seven.

Engine 11 and Truck 15 were running point on most of these, with Medic Unit 63 doing the CPR work on at least one call — so someone on the east side of Burbank pulled a person back from the edge of not-breathing yesterday. That's a thing that happened while police was chasing parking spot complaints.

Structure fires in October aren't summer-time-accidental-barbecue-fire territory — they're the dry-brush, stressed-electrical-system, "why didn't anyone maintain this" kind of fires. And six in one day means October isn't flirting with disaster, it's moved in and is throwing parties.

**Rail (Metrolink / UP corridor)**

Seven transmissions, zero sense. The rail network is asleep or broken in ways that don't even make it to the radio anymore.

**Pattern Check**

Two weeks ago I said the fire department was carrying the weight while police screamed into the void. Two weeks later, that ratio got worse. Police now sits at 262:1 noise-to-signal, fire at 89:1. At this rate, by November police dispatch will just be a tape loop of someone whispering "help me" into a microphone nobody's listening to.

And October is burning. Not metaphorically. Literally six fires yesterday. Last week had fires. The week before had fires. Burbank's drier than a popcorn fart and the structures in this town are old enough to remember when the grid was reliable.

Stay hydrated. Check your smoke detectors. If you see smoke, assume it's real and call it in — one of those six calls yesterday might have been what kept someone's house from turning into a total loss.
