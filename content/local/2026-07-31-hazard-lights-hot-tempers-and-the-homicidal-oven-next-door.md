## Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)

Domestic violence on Glenda Boulevard (Code 2, incident 1099-RD-235) opened the evening's greatest hits—someone's night went from normal to absolutely fucking nuclear before dessert. The kind of call where you know at least one person is sleeping somewhere else tonight, and it's not because they wanted to. Over on North Burlington at West 3rd Street, neighbors decided to have a very loud conversation about their disagreements, logged as a Code 415 dispute. Nothing says "we used to be friends" like bringing the cops into it.

But the real masterpiece was eastbound on Los Feliz from Riverside: black vehicle, hazard lights on like a goddamn SOS beacon, partial plate 999, driven by someone who apparently decided that flashing orange lights mean "I'm about to make the single worst decision of my evening." That's not a request for help, that's a warning system. A moving PSA in the shape of a car. God only knows what that person was thinking, but I'm going to guess it rhymed with "horribly inebriated." Traffic collision on San Fernando Road (incident 965) rounded out the beat. Just another evening on the Boulevard where parking disputes turn into insurance claims and someone's emotional thermostat explodes into a Code 2 response.

The rest was cops coordinating with each other, calling units that hadn't logged on yet, the usual dispatch ballet. Nothing exciting, just the infrastructure holding together while humans do their thing.

## Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)

Diabetic emergency on Casa Linda Drive pulled Engine 124 into action—one of those calls where "I feel weird" translates directly to "get here immediately with equipment." The human body is a fascinating biological machine until it suddenly decides to stop cooperating, and then you need firefighters with glucose and a radio to bring you back online.

Traffic collision on San Gabriel Boulevard came through as Code BLS, which means Basic Life Support—everyone technically upright and breathing, but the evening went sideways in a metal-on-metal kind of way. Insurance adjusters everywhere, I'm sure.

Carbon monoxide alarm at 216 Santa Rosa Road had Engine 105 rolling out, because nothing says "fun night at home" like your house secretly conspiring to poison you. That's the kind of discovery that ruins an evening in the best possible way—you get to live because a smoke detector is paranoid. Fire alarm reset on West Olive Avenue (false positives hitting their daily quota), miscellaneous medical on South 6th Street, shortness of breath on South Arroyo Boulevard. The usual rotation of humans having medical experiences that definitely warrant sirens, while actual competent people in trucks show up to fix the shit that goes wrong. Fire/EMS isn't glamorous, but it's the kind of work that keeps this city from being a lot darker than it is.
