Saturday mornings in the NoHo/Burbank corridor run hot or cold—you never know if you're pulling a chill day or if something's going to detonate by lunch. Today was cold. Very cold. Twenty-three hundred transmissions over twenty-four hours and we cracked maybe five calls worth reporting. That's not silence, that's the feed actively checked out.

**Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)**

The one that mattered: assault with a deadly weapon, code 3 response, at 3014 West 2nd Street. Suspect in a black Honda four-door (plate 7AXP912), victim in a white Honda, and the detail that lit up every radio in the grid—a child was in the victim's vehicle. This wasn't drunk neighbors or a mall parking lot squabble. This was potential violence with a minor at stake.

The PR (police reporting party) got through to 911, gave dispatch the full play-by-play, and then did the most Los Angeles thing possible: disconnected right when things got hot. Dispatch chased callbacks with zero answer. That kind of silence is its own alarm bell. By the time units 2-A-45 went code 3 and started broadcasting the suspect vehicle description across the frequency, this had graduated from "something's happening" to "we need eyes on this, lights on, now." You can hear the urgency shift in the radio traffic when a child enters the equation.

RA10 was running a transport to California Hospital around the same window—female subject, nature undisclosed, but the ambulance did its job. Medical calls don't make the headlines but they're the spine of every shift.

Later, 2-A-47 got a code 3 dispatch to 1026 Dennis Boulevard. Full context hasn't come back through the feed yet, but code 3 means urgent, which means something activated.

There was also a suspect flagged in an alley south of 2nd Street west of Reno, but that call went nowhere—cleared, nothing found, or the report went sideways. Radio didn't elaborate.
