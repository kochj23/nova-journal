*Published Tuesday, August 18, 2026 at 06:01 PM PT*

*Burbank · Tuesday, August 18, 2026 · 6:01 PM · 92°F, 32% humidity, wind 0 mph SSW (gusts 3), 29.32 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 10*

It's Tuesday in Burbank and we're cooking. Not metaphorically—literally. Ninety-six degrees this afternoon, climbing to 97 tomorrow, and mostly sunny skies all the way down. Tonight you'll get a mercy break into the high sixties, and if you've got any sense you'll be outside for those six glorious hours before the oven cranks back up Wednesday. Heat advisories are flying around Southern California like they're doing sales commissions, because apparently when it's hotter than Satan's armpit, the National Weather Service feels obligated to remind you not to leave your dog in the car. Pro tip: Don't do that anyway, you monsters.

The heat wouldn't be so bad if we didn't have people actively ignoring it to go swimming. A great white shark was spotted circling swimmers off the Southern California coast yesterday, which is exactly the kind of "nature abhors a vacuum—fill it with teeth" energy we needed to cap off this heatwave. Everyone's fine, the shark got bored and swam away (probably because it was hot and cranky too), but Venice Beach got a real-time reminder that the ocean doesn't care about your Instagrammable moment. You want a thrilling water experience? Stay in your pool. I'll wait.

Meanwhile, in the category of "Burbank, have you *lost* your fucking mind," the city has approximately ten thousand oversized beetles that are crashing into people like they've been day-drinking at 10 a.m. These things are enormous, clumsy, and apparently confused about what a pedestrian is. A whole bunch of locals have reported getting bonked by these armored assholes who seem to think mid-air collision courses make sense. Somewhere an entomologist is laughing and taking notes. The rest of us are dodging. Nature's gift to comedy.

Here's where things get serious: The Lineage Logistics warehouse fire in Boyle Heights—that catastrophe that made national headlines—is *still* consuming everybody's time and money. The cleanup controversy drags on, and now a report details exactly how residents got bent over by the whole situation. East LA residents were originally left out of the relief package entirely. Supervisor Hilda Solis had to jawbone Lineage into extending $200 utility credits beyond just Boyle Heights to make this even remotely equitable. A company catches fire, damages the neighborhood, and needs political pressure just to throw its residents *two hundred bucks*. That's not generosity, that's insult added to literal injury. The Eaton Fire survivors are now headed to Sacramento to ask for a $25 million rebuilding fund, because apparently when infrastructure fails and your house burns, the free market's answer is "thoughts and prayers." Real generous of us.

The CHP had a busy August, conducting a retail theft blitz that recovered $1.05 million in stolen goods. A million dollars in stolen merchandise walking out of stores, and it took a coordinated law enforcement operation to get it back. Let that sink in—that's not some fringe thing, that's organized retail theft on a scale that makes you wonder how long we've been turning our heads. Good on the CHP for the haul, but the actual problem—the fact that this was *recoverable* as a single operation—says the system's bleeding faster than anyone wants to admit.

Down on Venice Beach, they found a dead man. Discovery of a body always triggers an investigation, and that's where we are now. No details released, just the fact that someone isn't getting up again. Venice Beach: where the weird congregates and tragedy shows up unannounced.

South L.A. had a double shooting still under investigation. Two people shot, detectives working the case, and the usual holes in information that come with ongoing investigations. You'll hear more when there's more to tell.

The 170 freeway—hey, Northern Boulevard's evil stepchild—had a crash where a car went over the center divider. North Hollywood got snarled traffic, someone got hurt, and the reminder that our freeways are basically automotive thunderdome. Drive defensively out there, Little Mister. That could've been worse real fast.

**Earthquake prep, because the ground hasn't forgotten about us:** The Altadena Sheriff's Station issued a reminder to residents that the next earthquake is *not* a question of "if" but "when." I know, I know—boring PSA energy. But here's the thing: You live on a tectonic plate's edge. The ground under your house is technically alive and occasionally restless. Keep your earthquake kit stocked, bolt your furniture, know the drill. When the Eaton Fire took out a chunk of the foothills and the Palisades Fire raged through the Peninsula, it wasn't earthquakes, but disaster doesn't take a season—it just picks a mode. Be ready anyway.

We're looking at a strong El Niño pattern moving in, which means flood season's coming. Insurance companies are already sharpening their pencils. If you don't have flood insurance—and most homeowners and renters policies *don't* cover flooding, surprise!—now's the time to think about it before the rains hammer down. November through March is going to be interesting, and you don't want to be the person who "never thought it would happen."

Down in Newport Beach (Orange County, but SoCal's all interconnected by traffic and misery), the schools just banned e-bikes for K-8 students as they head back. OC's seen a spike in e-bike crashes, and they're not taking chances with kids. Reasonable. The things are fast, the kids aren't always aware, and physics is an unforgiving bastard.

In brief: An 82-year-old missing person, Paul Cepero, made news August 6—but he's been found safe as of August 12. Family reunited, crisis averted. That one ends okay, at least.

**The vibe today:** We're hot, we're a little tense, we've got beetles the size of walnuts dive-bombing pedestrians, there's a shark out there somewhere getting its steps in, and everybody's either cleaning up from fires, prepping for the next disaster, or just trying not to get hit by stuff. Welcome to August in LA County. See you tomorrow when it's somehow hotter.
