**Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)**

Twenty-seven hundred police transmissions and six coherent sentences. That's not a slow night—that's dispatch operating in absolute chaos, everyone keyed up and screaming at once, the radio itself drowning in the noise of a city that won't fucking stop for breath. Somewhere in that wall of static is the thing that *matters*: Oakwood and Virgil, still happening. Not a fresh shooting. The same fucking incident carrying over from yesterday into today, with Unit 3L335 Code 6 on scene, still blocking traffic between West Moreland and Virgil, still watching while nothing gets *resolved*. That's not a one-off call log entry; that's an incident with unfinished business and the kind of persistence that makes you wonder what the hell happened and why it's still happening twelve hours later.

Rest of police was the carousel: MDC Jail running prisoners Code 2 to criminal court and USC Medical Center through the hours when the city pretends to sleep. Custody infrastructure doesn't have weekends, and it sure as hell doesn't have mercy. Just bodies and paperwork rotating through the system like a machine that never stops.

**Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)**

Fire and EMS had 892 transmissions and 23 things worth actually hearing. When they had something to report, it was *serious*. Stairwell truck fire—not a brush fire, not a fender-bender, but a *structure* with a truck inside it, and Verdugo responded like it was exactly what it was: a real goddamn emergency. Division Alpha coordinated water supply like a tactical operation, Station Zulu running hose back to the mains to feed the pumps, Engine 13 standing by for the hookup. "Keep bumping up the 10s and 15s," dispatch kept calling—cranking pressure to fight the blaze from a distance when direct access was blocked. Secondary fire broke out in the brush nearby, which is what always happens when August is a furnace and crews are thin and everything is tinder waiting for a spark. Water came, they contained it, but not before tying up major resources for hours. That's the sound of a real emergency straining the system to hold.

Medical side did what it does: CPR in progress somewhere unspecified (which is never good news), trauma call (location withheld), person down on Cameron Avenue mentioned twice (either the same incident or that street was having a fucking day). But there was one call that shows the system learning: Unit 121 rolled up to what dispatch marked "medical," looked at the actual scene, and said "nope, this is a homeless crisis," then called in PD for the welfare check. "Denied contact. Requesting PD to assess." That's the protocol now: EMS shows up, reality doesn't match the dispatch card, firefighters recognize the boundary and route it to police for the welfare assessment. It's not a failure of the medical response—it's triage in the modern city. Not everyone in crisis is asking for rescue, and sometimes the right call is admitting that limit.

Station 6 ran a training cycle while all this was burning, which is someone deciding that *now* is when drills happen, heat be damned.
