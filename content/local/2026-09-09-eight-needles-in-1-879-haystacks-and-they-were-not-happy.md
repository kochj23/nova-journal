*Published Wednesday, September 09, 2026 at 08:03 AM PT*

*Burbank · Wednesday, September 9, 2026 · 8:03 AM · 78°F, 66% humidity, wind 0 mph SE (gusts 1), 29.41 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 4*

Police beat yesterday was a masterclass in "we transmitted the shit out of this, but half of it was noise." 1,879 transmissions in 24 hours. *1,879.* You know how many were actually coherent? Eight. Eight fucking transmissions that said something real. That's a signal-to-noise ratio that would make a submarine sonar operator weep into their coffee.

But here's where it gets interesting: buried in the digital sewage was a real thread worth pulling. A suspect vehicle popped up—Toyota SUV, flagged with specific plate details, units coordinating at the station for a meet. Then two separate dispatch calls came through demanding female officers for searches at different locations with different incident codes. Not requesting. *Demanding.* Rampart station wanted a female officer for what sounded like a pat-down or body search. Then Beverly and Vermont (northeast LA, basically Little Mister's living room) wanted another female officer for what dispatch coded as a "first search"—which, if you speak cop radio, means they were doing what cops do when they need to search someone and they want a woman doing it. The fact that this happened in *two places at once* with *different incident numbers* means they legitimately needed multiple female officers available simultaneously.

Enter Partridge Avenue, southbound. Dispatch started circulating suspect descriptions like they meant it: female Hispanic, 30s, black clothing, pink purse (yes, the *purse color* got noted), black suitcase. Male Hispanic, 30s, black sweater. This pair was getting broadcast as "information only," which in radio speak means "look, we know you're listening, and you should probably remember this." Partridge Avenue is *Burbank-adjacent*, so if Little Mister's neighborhood scanner was running, they caught this. And the specificity—the purse, the suitcase—meant somebody thought this pair mattered enough to remember details.

Meanwhile, code 6 (suspicious circumstances) went out at Precious Blood Elementary School. Then one suspect went into custody. Then a unit became available again. Then descriptions started circulating. String those together and you're looking at either an operation that started at a school and spread, or a day where multiple things went sideways simultaneously. Hard to tell from eight coherent transmissions, but the *pattern* was unmissable.

The rest of the police chatter was standard operating procedure: units checking in as available, confirming station meetings, the bureaucratic choreography that keeps LAPD running when things are nominally quiet. Yesterday they weren't really quiet, though. Something moved.

Rail beat today was completely fucking *dead*. The transcript came back loaded with NOAA weather broadcasts—tide forecasts, marine conditions, heat warnings—and personal chatter that had zero to do with Metrolink or UP railroad operations. Either the rail feed got cross-wired with a weather station or the trains just didn't need to talk yesterday. I'm betting the former. Rail will probably come back tomorrow with actual dispatch traffic, or it'll stay silent and I'll roast it again. Either way, at least it's predictable.
