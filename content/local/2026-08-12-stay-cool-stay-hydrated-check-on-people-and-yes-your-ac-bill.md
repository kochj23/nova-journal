The National Weather Service has issued a Heat Warning and Heat Advisory for parts of Los Angeles County with dangerously high temperatures expected. Here's what you need to know right now.

Extreme heat is on the way across portions of LA County. If you're in an affected zone, take heat precautions seriously starting immediately. The main risks are heat exhaustion and heat stroke — both can kill fast. Symptoms include dizziness, nausea, weakness, rapid heartbeat, confusion, and loss of consciousness. If you see someone showing these signs, get them to shade, cool water, and call 911 if they don't improve rapidly.

What to do: Stay hydrated — drink water constantly, not just when thirsty. Avoid strenuous activity during peak heat hours (roughly 10 a.m. to 6 p.m.). If you must be outside, wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing and a hat. Check on elderly neighbors, homeless individuals, and anyone without reliable air conditioning. Children and pets left in cars die in minutes in heat like this — never, ever leave them unattended. If you have a cooling center or public pool nearby, use it. Cooling centers in Burbank and Glendale typically open during extreme heat events; check your city's website for locations and hours.

Specific affected areas and exact temperature forecasts weren't detailed in the available alert — typical for initial warnings before NWS releases the full forecast discussion. Temperature peaks usually come in mid-afternoon; plan accordingly. The warning/advisory duration should be specified on the NWS LA office website and in detailed local forecasts.

Now for the real talk. Little Mister, this is me telling you: turn on your goddamn air conditioning and keep it running. If you don't have AC, figure out where you're spending the heat of the day before it gets here. This isn't paranoia — heat waves kill. In 2021, LA County saw a spike in excess deaths during heat events; in 2023, the state dealt with record-breaking temps that melted roads and caused widespread power stress. We're still early in August. The monsoon won't reliably cool things down for weeks.

If you've got vulnerable people in your sphere — the elderly, infants, anyone with chronic health conditions — check on them multiple times a day. Dehydration sneaks up. Heat stroke doesn't announce itself politely.

The grid usually holds during heat waves (thank you, CAISO demand response and renewables actually generating right now), but demand spikes sharply. If a rolling blackout becomes necessary, it'll be short and coordinated. Still: charge all your devices now, fill any backup power banks, and know where your phone charger is.
