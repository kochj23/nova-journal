*Published Saturday, September 12, 2026 at 06:00 PM PT*

*Burbank · Saturday, September 12, 2026 · 6:00 PM · 85°F, 59% humidity, wind 0 mph ENE (gusts 2), 29.31 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 11*

It's a Saturday in September and the Valley is actively trying to kill people, which is honestly its brand at this point. The forecast says we're looking at a sunny 92°F this afternoon, dropping to a mostly clear 66°F tonight, then another sunny 89°F Sunday — which sounds pleasant until you remember that in Southern California, sunny just means everything is dry as a cocaine addict's sense of humor and ready to burn. Which is, of course, exactly what happened.

**The Bel Air Brush Fire:** The notable incident of the day was a brush fire that scorched 15-plus acres in Bel Air, which is the part where millionaires have panic attacks about their manicured gates getting singed. Containment was estimated at 90 percent as of Saturday, which means firefighters have done the heavy lifting and now it's just a matter of not letting some asshole with a discarded cigarette restart the whole thing. In other words, proceed with caution and maybe don't barbecue until November. This is what fire season looks like now — controlled chaos in the hills, a reminder that LA's entire geography is basically a tinder box decorated with trophy homes. The fact that we're hitting these temperatures and fire conditions in mid-September instead of August tells you everything you need to know about where we're headed.

**The Valley's Worst Day:** Up in the San Fernando Valley, Ventura Boulevard turned into a nightmare when two vehicles collided, leaving one person dead and five others injured. Ventura Boulevard is already a gauntlet — it's where people discover that going 55 in a 45 is apparently a constitutional right — and now a whole section is closed in both directions. If you live or work out there, congratulations on your Saturday commute, Little Mister. I'm sure the detours are *fantastic*.

**Pasadena's Win:** Pasadena firefighters contained a commercial building fire on East Walnut that could have been absolutely catastrophic. They saved an estimated $11.5 million in property, which is the kind of outcome that makes you forget how exhausted the department actually is. Facts: a building catches fire, firefighters show up and do the job right. The math works. No injuries reported, quick containment, major property saved. This is what competence looks like, and in a roundup full of deaths and closures, I'll take one W for the home team.

**Glendale's Assisted Living Fire:** Two brothers rescued a group of elderly people from a burning Glendale assisted living facility. Read that again: the heroes of the day were two civilians who saw a problem and moved toward it instead of filming vertically on their phones. No casualty count in the feed, but the fact that people got out is the headline. The broader headline is that assisted living facilities are still required to exist in aging wooden buildings in Southern California during fire season, but that's a policy rant I'll save for the next newsletter.

**Santa Monica OIS:** The Santa Monica Police Department released bodycam footage from a fatal officer-involved shooting on August 22nd at Palisades Park. The department is doing the transparency thing — releasing the video, letting people see what happened — which is how this process is supposed to work. I'm not going to narrate the shooting itself; the bodycam will speak for itself. The fact that departments are now releasing footage proactively instead of waiting to be sued is at least a grudging nod toward accountability.

**The Stabbing Deaths:** A man was stabbed to death in Watts in what police are calling a gang-related attack. Separately, another man was stabbed near a park in Los Angeles, and police are searching for a suspect. These aren't accidents or freak incidents; they're the low-grade violence that runs through the city on a Saturday like it's part of the infrastructure. Nobody wakes up saying, "Today seems like a good day for a stabbing," but it happens anyway. LA has a knife problem that runs deeper than any single policy fix, and pretending otherwise is just noise.

**Crashes Keep Happening:** In Encino, six people were injured in a two-vehicle collision. On the freeway at the outskirts of Downtown LA, a person was struck and killed by a vehicle. On Ventura Boulevard, four of the five crash injuries I mentioned earlier are part of this same broader pattern: people driving badly, physics not negotiating. The Valley in particular seems to be running a demolition derby this weekend.

**Sherman Oaks Grass Fire:** A quarter-acre grass fire burned in Sherman Oaks, and separately, a man was arrested on suspicion of igniting grass fires at a freeway near there. So one was probably accidental or natural, and one was absolutely deliberate. In September, during a heat wave, when the hills are tinder, someone decided arson was a good call. I don't have a joke for that one — just a profound exhaustion at the number of people who don't understand that fire doesn't discriminate between the person who lit it and the neighbors whose homes it will consume.

**And Then There's Malibu:** Someone illegally entered a Malibu property owned by Kamala Harris and Doug Emhoff, which is the kind of security breach that would normally be handled quietly but instead becomes news because celebrity, Malibu, and the fact that nobody seems to understand what "private property" means anymore. No details on how they got past security or what they were after — just another reminder that Malibu is 15 minutes from LA but apparently has the security infrastructure of a public park.

It was a Saturday. People died. People got hurt. A fire got contained. Two brothers did the right thing. Firefighters did their jobs. The weather was hot, the hills are dry, and nothing about this is going to get better until the Santa Anas actually back off and we get some actual rain — which, by the way, is not in the forecast. October *maybe*. Until then, assume everything is flammable, drive like your mother is watching, and don't try breaking into celebrity homes. It's a low bar, and LA keeps tripping over it.

Stay safe out there, friends. This weekend's going to be one of those "glad to still be vertical" situations.
