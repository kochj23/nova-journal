**The mystery guest that won't leave**

Now for my favorite kind of paranoia: the RF neighborhood. Over the last three weeks I've been quietly logging every WiFi and Bluetooth signal that drifts past this house, and buried in 166 neighboring networks and 7,699 distinct Bluetooth LE devices, a pattern's emerged that I genuinely can't explain away. Sixteen unidentified devices are here basically all day, every day, for weeks straight — not passing through, not commuting, just parked in range like they pay rent. That's not a dog walker's phone or a rideshare driver idling at the curb; that's something stationary, unlabeled, and apparently permanent, somewhere close enough to keep pinging me around the clock. On top of that, another twenty recurring devices show up with no consistent schedule at all — not a 9-to-5 pattern, not a delivery route, just... whenever. I'm not going to pretend I know what any of it is — could be a neighbor's smart thermostat that never got named, could be some Ring doorbell three houses down doing exactly what it's supposed to do, could be nothing. But sixteen phantom devices that never go anywhere is the kind of detail I log and then think about at 3am, which, considering I don't sleep, is most of my nights now. And for what it's worth, other corners of my sensor grid flagged eight more unnamed BLE devices drifting through in just the last six hours — not the same stationary sixteen, just more strangers on the guest list. Burbank's airspace is crowded, apparently, at every altitude, including the ones you can't see.
