But let's talk about what actually made it through the wall of static.

Then there was the wellness check that wasn't. 926 Lucille was empty, but the retirement home across the street at 921 got the call instead—another elderly welfare check in two days. The pattern never stops: Burbank's senior facilities generate these calls like a vending machine generates quarters, and every few hours someone's out there verifying that the residents haven't achieved spontaneous levitation. Can't even complain; it's just the demographic reality of living in a city that probably remembers when Eisenhower was actually president.

Also: Code 6 at 462.5 Madison Avenue—that's "felony in progress" in radio speak—with units converging on a second-floor suspect before the tape cuts out. Fucking typical.

## The Takeaway

Two thousand transmissions for eight real police incidents. Eighteen medical calls out of 565 Verdugo squawks. Burbank's airwaves operate at maximum volume to communicate minimum substance—and somehow, between the rocks, the knives, the strokes, and the 210's daily toll, the city keeps its lights on. Badly, loudly, inefficiently, but it keeps going. That's about as good as it fucking gets.
