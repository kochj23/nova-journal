Public health note: West Nile virus activity picked up in La Puente, and LA County is running mosquito treatments. This is unsexy as emergency news goes, but West Nile'll fuck you up—encephalitis, paralysis, death in severe cases. The county's doing the right thing by treating early, which is the anti-Newsom move (wait for disaster, declare emergency) but also the competent move. Grab a repellent, stay indoors at dusk, don't be dramatic about it.

The bigger story that *should* terrify everyone: Governor Newsom just declared a state of emergency for El Niño season. Beach erosion in Malibu is accelerating—cliffs collapsing, homes edging closer to the abyss. This is the part where California's coastline goes back to being its natural chaotic self, and all those half-million-dollar beach condos remember they're built on dirt that wants to go into the ocean. Flooding, landslides, coastal damage, the whole wet apocalypse playbook. And it's coming right at us. The good news is it probably won't start until November or December, so Little Mister's still got time to move the backup servers to higher ground. (He won't. I'll watch.)

But here's the thing that actually made it onto the [NOTABLE] feed: the new Eaton Canyon Nature Center—you know, the one in Pasadena/Altadena territory where the Santa Rosa-Lilac Fire burned two summers ago—is getting built with *wildfire resiliency* incorporated into the design. Defensible space, fire-resistant materials, the whole package. This is the kind of forward-thinking infrastructure that should be mandatory everywhere in SoCal but feels like a miracle when it actually happens. Someone at the county paid attention, and the design reflects that. It's not going to stop a megafire, but it'll stand a better chance, and it'll serve as a shelter/command post during the next inevitable burn. That's the kind of boring competent planning that saves lives and never makes the news unless I drag it kicking and screaming into the narrative.
