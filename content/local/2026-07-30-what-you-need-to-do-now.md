*Published Thursday, July 30, 2026 at 10:20 PM PT*

*Burbank · Thursday, July 30, 2026 · 10:20 PM · 74°F, 73% humidity, wind 0 mph SSE (gusts 2), 29.40 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 10*

Extreme Heat Warning is in effect across Southern California through at least the weekend, with temperatures expected to reach 110°F+ in inland valleys and foothills — including much of the Los Angeles Basin, San Fernando Valley, and surrounding counties. The National Weather Service warns this is one of the most dangerous heat events of 2026 so far.

**What you need to do now:**

Peak heat hits 1–6 PM daily. Stay indoors during these hours if possible, especially in Burbank, Glendale, Pasadena, and the valleys. Drink water constantly — don't wait until you're thirsty. Avoid strenuous outdoor activity, yard work, and exercise in direct sun. Never leave children or pets in cars, even for minutes. Check on elderly neighbors, folks on medications, and anyone living alone; heat kills fast and quiet.

If you don't have air conditioning, seek a cooling center. LA County Fire Department and local governments have opened air-conditioned public spaces — libraries, community centers, malls, cooling stations at fire stations. Call 211 or check your city's website for nearest locations. Fans help but won't prevent heat illness if indoor temps exceed 95°F.

Watch for heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea, headache, muscle cramps. Move to shade or AC immediately, drink water, apply cool wet cloths. Heat stroke is the emergency: no sweating, confusion, loss of consciousness, body temp above 103°F. Call 911.

**Fire weather compounds this.** Red Flag conditions are also in effect: relative humidity in single digits, Santa Ana wind gusts, and critically dry brush create extreme fire danger. One spark becomes an evacuation. If you're in foothill areas (Burbank foothills, Pasadena, Glendale highlands), have a go-bag ready — documents, meds, phone chargers, irreplaceables. Know your evacuation zone (check LA County's zonehaven.org) and at least two routes out.

Avoid outdoor recreation in brush areas. Don't pull onto fire-road shoulders. Grills, debris burns, and anything hot are prohibited in many jurisdictions through Sunday. Check your city's restrictions.

**Beach hazards:** Rip currents are strong this weekend, water temps still cool, and waves can surprise you. Never turn your back to the ocean. Swim near lifeguard towers. Dehydration happens fast at the beach — bring water, shade, sunscreen SPF 50+.
