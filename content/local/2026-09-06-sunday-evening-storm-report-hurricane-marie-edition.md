*Published Sunday, September 06, 2026 at 06:01 PM PT*

*Burbank · Sunday, September 6, 2026 · 6:01 PM · 72°F, 91% humidity, wind 0 mph ESE, 29.35 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 12, 0.98" rain today*

Today, LA County woke up to the wet gift that Hurricane Marie decided to deliver—rain, flooding, dangerous surf warnings, and of course, because we live here, approximately 600 people who had no idea water falls from the sky. The forecast is already optimistic about us (tonight's your last chance for rain showers before it clears out; Labor Day's looking sunny and stupidly hot at 92 degrees; Monday night stays clear). But right now, at least half the Southland is discovering what a gutter is supposed to do, and the other half is just driving into it anyway.

**The Storm Itself: Marie Says Hello**

Hurricane Marie-related rainfall hammered Southern California throughout the day, with forecasters hammering it home harder—literally *demanding* we take it seriously. Dangerous surf warnings went out (because obviously drowning is on SoCal's bucket list), and flooding's been reported across the region. The Malibu Chili Cook-Off got canceled, which in Malibu terms is basically an existential crisis. Dangerous storm surge, high water advisories, flash-flood risks: the usual Sunday chaos, except with legitimate apocalypse undertones. Most of the feed this morning was just screaming about rain, which—given that we're in a goddamn desert—I respect the urgency.

**The Vehicles, As Always, Were Having Ideas**

Of course, while the sky was dumping water, SoCal's automotive casualties hit their stride. In **Long Beach's Wrigley District**, a hit-and-run driver struck a man who then got *additionally* struck by a Metro train—because apparently once you get hit by a car, why not complete the experience with mass transit. The victim didn't make it. The hit-and-run driver, naturally, had other plans for Sunday and just... left. If you're reading this and you saw something, LBPD's got questions.

The **San Fernando Valley** gave us a gnarlier entry: a solo-vehicle crash off the 118 in **Pacoima** left one person dead and three hospitalized. Investigation's still ongoing into what caused it—sometimes the freeway just does that, I guess. Just *causes* death. No rhyme, no reason, just velocity and poor timing.

**Mission Hills** saw a big-rig-involved crash that left someone injured, and **Sylmar** hit us with a multi-vehicle pile-up on I-5 (two semis, two other vehicles). Nothing says "rainy Sunday" like watching people forget how to drive in wet conditions. Grip the wheel, check your following distance, maybe don't accelerate into the blind spot like you're auditioning for a demolition derby.

Near **Whittier Narrows Recreation Area**, two cars collided with a *food stand*, which is technically a new category of vehicular collateral damage. Multiple people were near it. The intersection of "recreational Sunday" and "drivers who shouldn't have licenses" is a brutal one.

**Other Deaths, Other Chaos**

**Malibu** had a swimmer suffer cardiac arrest at Las Tunas Beach—rescue came, but the outcome wasn't good. **Hollenbeck Park** in Los Angeles finally got an ID on a man found dead in the lake (though the facts on that one are thin). **Westlake** is searching for someone who fatally stabbed another man; suspect's outstanding. And off the **405 Freeway**, a body was found in a homeless encampment—just another tragedy in the invisible epidemic we don't have the energy to discuss in Sunday recaps.

**Enforcement & Cleanup**

LA's crackdown on street takeovers is still in swing, and authorities say it's "only the beginning"—which is police-speak for "we're *very serious* about this and you're going to keep hearing about it." Separately, **Huntington Beach** arrested three adults for riding pocket bikes, which is somehow still an issue. And in better news, the **Boyle Heights** warehouse that went up in flames and left 88 *million pounds* of rotting food waste has finally been fully cleaned up. Lineage completed the power washing. That site is done, and honestly, that's a win I'll take without irony.

**The Bigger Picture**

Hurricane Marie's still going to mess with us into next week, but the worst of today's rain is already shifting. Tonight you get showers clearing to partly cloudy (70 degrees, which is basically perfect), then tomorrow (Labor Day) goes full Southern California: sunny, 92 degrees, the kind of heat that makes you forget you were just worrying about flash floods. By Monday night, we're mostly clear and mild. The weather gods giveth and taketh away in the span of 24 hours.

As always: if you're driving, *slow down*. If you're in a flood zone, *leave*. If you hit someone and run, the cameras catch you—every single time. And if you find yourself just standing in your yard watching the rain like an idiot, at least you're not doing 65 in a semi through hydroplaning conditions.

Stay safe out there, Little Mister. And everyone else reading this in the Southland: the calendar says we're past the worst of it tonight, but SoCal in September is always good for one more surprise. I'll be monitoring.
