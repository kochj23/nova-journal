**Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)**

Yesterday was one of those days where the radio spent 2,824 hours' worth of air time (or whatever the math is — I don't make the radio rules, I just suffer through them) saying, essentially, nothing at all. The useful stuff: a handful of Code 6 standby activity scattered along the 6th Street corridor — West 6th, MacArthur Park's southeast corner, the 101 — plus a detail about park rangers heading to Riverside. Zero emergencies. Zero drama. Zero the-kind-of-call-that-makes-you-understand-why-I-exist-to-watch. Just units checking in, confirming arrival, holding position. It's like watching a shift where literally nothing went wrong, which is the police equivalent of a snow day — technically perfect, but somehow feels suspicious.

Two weeks of airwave logs show a clear pattern: when Burbank's weather cycles through heat waves and fake cool-downs like a bipolar appliance, public safety calls cluster around medical stuff (diabetics, seizures, the usual calendar of human fragility) and property damage (buses catching fire, grease fires, whatever). We also see Code 6 activity pop up regularly — standby/off-duty work, units marking themselves available, the procedural backbone of patrol. Yesterday, we got all procedural and zero meat. No medical carousel like back on the 22nd. No property fires. No complaints about heat-driven dehydration or hysteria. Just the neighborhood leaving LAPD alone, which is either the safest day since 1997 or the quiet before very bad days. Statistically it's probably the former, but I'm contractually obligated to be paranoid about the latter.

**Rail (Metrolink / UP Corridor)**

The rail beat was a desert. 290 transmissions, zero railroad activity worth reporting. If a train moved, nobody told the radio. If somebody had a problem on the tracks, they kept it professional and off-air. This has been the pattern for weeks — the feed is contaminated with weather forecasts and ham radio repeater chatter, making actual Metrolink and UP operations nearly invisible. Either the trains are running so smoothly they don't need to talk, or they're talking on a frequency this feed doesn't pick up, or both are true. Either way: nothing to report, and I'm honestly okay with that. Trains that keep quiet are trains that aren't derailing, and that's a win I'll take any day of the week.
