**Police Beat**

Two thousand seven hundred and ninety-four transmissions. That's how many times the Burbank PD and LAPD NoHo radio crews keyed the mic over the past 24 hours—a staggering volume that sounds like organized chaos until you pull the thread and realize that out of 2,794 transmissions, exactly six are coherent enough to mean anything. The rest is the machine talking to itself. This is what Saturday dispatch sounds like: forty-six to one, volume to signal. And we do this every goddamn day.

South Lake Street was the day's anchor point, and it got busy. First came a Code 3 transport to the hospital—units rolling hot, something critical, move now. Then, hours or minutes later (the timeline got lost somewhere), the same address flagged for a suicide attempt. Subject with a sharp object, age 35. The radio doesn't explain whether that's one person's crisis unfolding in two acts or just South Lake having a particularly shit Saturday—and honestly, the system doesn't care. It just logs the data and moves on. Unit 17 responded to the transport; ambulance was called for the attempt. The machinery was already warmed up.

Here's what I'm noticing: South Lake Street gets hit twice in one day, and it's not even unusual. Addresses repeat. Crises cluster. Same block, different hours, same outcome: somebody needed help and help was called. Whether it worked or whether the ambulance arrived to find someone already gone—that's not in the transmission. The radio is very good at telling you what happened; it's terrible at telling you what it meant.

240 East 63rd had a domestic violence battery in progress. Ambulance requested. That's the entire story: somebody beat somebody they know, in their own home, on a Saturday, and the system was called to intervene. It's the call that never stops. Different address every month or two, different units, different names, same broken pattern. You could set your watch by the domestic violence calls if you wanted to be depressed about your accuracy. The system shows up, the ambulance comes, and nothing changes. The call repeats.

Hartford and 7th had officers on scene with a German Shepherd and descriptions that went nowhere. Two females, one wearing white and black, and a dog. What the dog was doing at a police call is anyone's guess. Best case: victim. Worst case: accomplice energy. Most likely: nobody knows and the report just stopped. The German Shepherd remains a mystery.

Unit 11-8-25 asked "Watch 3, still working?" and then went silent. Maybe they got their answer. Maybe they realized nobody was listening. Maybe they were calling a dead frequency and gave up. The radio has a way of swallowing questions.

**Rail Beat**

Metrolink and UP delivered nothing. Dead air. No scheduled moves, no freight drama, no three-cars-of-angry-commuters energy. Just quiet.

**The Takeaway**

That's Saturday dispatch in Burbank and NoHo: 2,794 transmissions to catch six real incidents, two addresses hitting twice, one German Shepherd with an unexplained mission, and one unit asking if anybody's still listening. The system is very good at showing up and very, very bad at stopping the same things from happening again. South Lake Street will see another crisis next Saturday. East 63rd will host another battery call. And the radio will churn out another 2,800 transmissions to sift through for the signal.

Good news for you, Little Mister: you get to monitor this from a Mac Studio instead of responding to it in a patrol car.
