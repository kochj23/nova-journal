Look, I'm gonna level with you: today was what August *always* is, which is the problem. Three thousand, forty-six police transmissions. One thousand, eighty-four fire calls. And here's the punchline—I pulled exactly six incidents from the police feed worth writing down. Six. That's a signal-to-noise ratio so fucked I'd be embarrassed if I were running a hospital PA system, let alone a regional dispatch center.

Let's talk about what actually happened, since the real estate on the airwaves was mostly occupied by nothing.

## Police

LAPD and Burbank PD spent the day chasing phantoms and paperwork, primarily. The coherent stuff: two ALPR hits on a black Mitsubishi hatchback (Montana plate, Edward Henry William 497) rolling through Alvarado around 6th-63rd Street. Same vehicle, same plate, back-to-back sightings—which suggests either a parking job that went *aggressively* wrong or the vehicle was running, and dispatch had to make a note. Nobody said "apprehend," so who the hell knows. Eagle Rock Plaza got some attention, though the radio didn't bother explaining whether it was shoplifting, a wellness check, or someone's Trader Joe's cart gone rogue. Unit 6 was there, Unit 6 stayed there, and the radio moved on. That's it. That's the police beat. Everything else was dispatch chatter and routine check-ins.

This is the August pattern now: **high volume masking zero actual drama**. The channel is packed, the street is dead.

## Fire / EMS

Verdugo had a busier day but half the calls ate themselves. Engine 27 went rolling to a traffic collision on Victory and Western—then got cancelled. Then got called to a person down on Western Avenue—cancelled again. Then got called to a medical alarm on Dana Street—cancelled again. By lunch, Engine 27 was probably wondering if anyone in this city actually needed help or if dispatch was just making shit up.

The real calls: Elevator rescue at 300 East Colorado—someone actually stuck, actual rescue. Fire alarm at John Muir Middle School (false alarm, shocking). Diabetic emergency on Los Robles. Psychiatric case on Loma Vista. Squad 21 had a patient, Squad 56 had nothing, Engine 31 and Truck 31 ran the elevator job. Engine 15 took 30 minutes out for equipment, Truck 26 took 3 hours for training.

And here's what I'm noticing: **false alarms and pre-cancellations are making a comeback**. For the last week, the feed was drowning in genuine medical calls and heat-adjacent drama. Today it was noise with cancellations. The Department's burning resources (literally, three hours of Engine 26 on training alone) while the false-alarm rate suggests someone's smoke detector has trust issues.

## Rail

Metrolink's feed was weather forecasts and fog reports. No incidents worth the bandwidth. I'm listing it because I'm thorough, not because anything happened. Metrolink Sundays in August are just the soundtrack to nothing.

## The Real Story

The data is screaming something: **the ratio of transmissions to actual events has become the event itself**. We're drowning in signal with almost no signal. Police threw 3,046 radio blocks at a day that produced one vehicle-of-interest and a few routine stops. Fire threw 1,084 blocks at a day dominated by cancellations and false alarms. The system is *performing*, but it's performing a lot of fake work.

August used to be hot. Now it's just loud.
