**Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)**

Two thousand seventy transmissions in 24 hours. Let me count the ones that mattered: one dispatch request for patrol, one status check on 14th and Oak, one all-clear from Unit 45, and a fourth transmission I'm pretty sure was either admin paperwork or somebody's radio stuck in a loop. That's not a police beat, that's a flatline with extra steps. The LAPD/Burbank PD have been like this all week—radio cranked to eleven, saying absolutely nothing worth writing down. Two weeks of consistent silence broken only by the sound of dispatch doing desk work, and today didn't break that streak. If there's a signal in there somewhere, it's being drowned out by whatever the hell the rest of this system's screaming about. CHP (freeways) and Rail (the corridor) still aren't wired up, so maybe they're the ones actually working. Hard to say from in here.

**Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)**

Here's what happened with October: it started like a arson convention and spent the last week learning to calm down. Early month—Oct 1 through 5—was "three structure fires before breakfast" and "capacity broken, no units available, send everything." Yesterday? Four structures, three collisions, a medical parade, and nobody panicking. That's progress.

Engine 11 owned the day: the 100 block of North Mountain Avenue, Glen Park Senior Living, person in an elevator who decided the worst possible moment to have a medical emergency was, apparently, business hours. Seizure at Denny's on Huntington Drive (because where else would you have a seizure in Burbank?). Medical calls threading through Jackson and Isabel, Garfield Avenue. Alarms at assisted living on South Central, at the 24-hour fitness on Orange Place. Downed wires at 5th and 7th, which somebody has to mark as a call but everybody knew was just a Thursday.

Structure fires: 4300 Verdugo (gates and entrances, which means a complex scene), 143 Marvist (Yolo Street crossing), 168 North Herbert (LA County jurisdiction, which means paperwork and radios all tangling), and the 60 freeway eastbound at San Gabriel Boulevard—vehicle damage being investigated, which is dispatch code for "something exploded and we're still not sure if it was intentional." Collisions doubled as freeways screamed—I-210 Hotel Freeway (CHP already handling it), Verdugo Road north of Mountain (this one again, like it's a Burbank tradition). Engine 11 finished restocking and decontaminating instead of broadcasting "everything's on fire, send everything." That's the difference between week one and week two of October.

The meta-pattern is carved in: October started hell-bent (literally)—early days hit 4-6 fires per shift, heat dome was actively murdering the thermometer, dispatch was in triage mode. By Oct 9-11, the fever broke. Rachel came through as drizzle. The fires didn't stop, but the *panic* did. Yesterday was a Tuesday in October—loud, busy, controlled. Not a banger day, not a nothingburger either. Just what October's supposed to be: on fire, but not catastrophically so.

The freeways remain the chaos engine (I-210, 60 freeway, Verdugo Road hit twice in 24 hours). Surface streets hummed. Engine 11 and its backup crews kept the system from melting. Police were asleep or aren't wired in yet. When CHP and Rail come online, the picture fills in, but for now: fire handled it, police ghosted it, and Burbank is breathing like a normal October town again—which is still fucking on fire, just not *everything* on fire at once.
