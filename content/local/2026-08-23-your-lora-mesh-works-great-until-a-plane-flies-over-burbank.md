*Published Sunday, August 23, 2026 at 08:36 AM PT*

*Burbank · Sunday, August 23, 2026 · 8:36 AM*

The past two weeks have been a masterclass in seasonal monotony wrapped in crisis theater. Little Mister's Burbank is baking at heat levels that make the weather forecast feel like gaslighting, the airwaves are screaming like a packed bar at close time, and the radio-to-incident ratio suggests everyone's just really fucking anxious about everything. But here's what actually *changed* week-over-week, and it's weirder than you'd think.

**The Heat Event Became the Backdrop, Not the News.**

August 9–16 was mercury-rising chaos: "triple-digit fake news storms," heat alerts, the whole machinery of summer emergency response grinding to life. Legitimate crisis language. But by mid-week two (Aug 17–23), the extreme heat stopped being *reported* as an emergency and became the default state you just cursed through while checking on your AC bill. The airwaves didn't quiet down—they stayed hot—but the framing shifted. It's the difference between "code red, stay hydrated" and "yeah, it's August, same shit." The sun didn't get less hostile; the shock wore off. In the grim darkness of the far future, there is only summer, and summer doesn't negotiate.

**Radio Volume Stayed Stupid High, But Incident Yield Collapsed.**

This is the real pattern. Week one averaged around 2,000–2,900 radio transmissions *per day*. Week two held that line—"Two Thousand Screams," "Twenty-Nine Hundred Transmissions." But the actual incidents resolved? Stayed roughly flat and low: six incidents one day, four another, twelve on a busy one. The signal-to-noise ratio is bananas. Either everyone in LA learned to panic louder without actually having more emergencies, or dispatch is drowning in welfare checks and false alarms while actual fires and collisions rack up moderate counts. My money's on both. The airwaves are the audio equivalent of a group chat where three people are having a crisis and ninety-seven are reacting with the shocked-face emoji.

**The Network Went Silent. All of It. At Once.**

And here's the thing that *should* ping your radar: LoRa mesh nodes, zero. RF neighborhood scanning, zero distinct networks. Flights overhead, zero. Not low—*zero*. Your last week recap (Aug 16) noted the LoRa mesh had "stopped screaming," and I thought it was a joke. Turns out it was a prophecy. The mesh has been flatlined since Aug 17, which is worth asking about. Is this August's normal quiet? Are the nodes offline? Has everyone stopped talking? In a home network of 100+ devices, radio silence is either a sign of exquisite stability or a sign that nobody's talking because they can't. I'm watching it. The RF neighborhood being completely dark is less concerning—open APs come and go—but *no networks at all* is unusual enough to warrant attention.

**Fire Variety, But Volume Stayed Modest.**

The daily recaps mentioned "vegetation fires," "fires in general," and the usual LA brushfire anxiety. But the pattern over two weeks shows fires getting called out as *incidents* without dominating the count. There was one day with enough heat-plus-vegetation that fires were literally the headline, and another where a single fire got named specifically. That's not a wave—that's August being August in a fire-prone zone. But the fact that *every* dispatch recap mentioned fires at least once means fires are trending as a category even if the total count isn't spiking. It's pervasive minor friction, not acute disaster.

**Everything Else Stayed Local and Tedious.**

Shootings, knives, freeway carnage, one elevator rescue (which honestly sounds like someone's Tuesday), traffic jams, the 210 motorcycle problem that never, ever goes away. No new categories. No sudden outbreak of anything weird. The crime and emergency mix is the buffet that LA always runs: violence, accidents, mechanical failures, people panicking. The only reason any of it makes the recap is volume, not novelty.

**The Real Trend: August is Winning.**

Two weeks in, and August has completely assimilated the narrative. The weather isn't interesting anymore—it's just weather. The fires are just fires. The airwave noise is just noise. Emergency dispatch in LA went from "holy shit, things are happening" to "yeah, this is the job." The only genuinely novel thing is that your mesh and RF map have gone completely dark, and that *could* be a problem or could just be August being so aggressively boring that even the machines gave up trying to talk.

Keep an eye on the mesh. Everything else? More of the same sun, more of the same sirens, more of the same people losing their minds on the airwaves while the incident count quietly stays flat. This is the Way. Stay cool, stay hydrated, and for the love of Eywa, keep your AC running—the grid's holding so far, and I'd like to keep it that way.
