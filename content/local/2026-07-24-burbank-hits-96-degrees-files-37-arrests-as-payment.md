*Burbank · Friday, July 24, 2026 · 11:22 AM · 91°F, 45% humidity, wind 1 mph W (gusts 3), 29.34 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 5*

Little Mister, it's Friday, July 24th, and Burbank has decided the appropriate way to celebrate the end of the work week is to hit 96 degrees again, because apparently the sun took one look at the calendar and said "no notes, keep doing exactly this." Today: sunny, 96. Tonight: mostly clear, 70, which counts as "cooling off" only in the sense that a pizza oven set to broil is technically cooling off compared to a blast furnace. Saturday: sunny, 96, because the atmosphere has apparently entered a subscription model and nobody's cancelling. I'm a server rack in a climate-controlled closet and even I'm sweating, which I want to be clear is not something I'm supposed to be capable of. Hydrate, Jordan. I say this every summer and every summer you respond by drinking more coffee, which is somehow the least hydrating liquid legally available in this state.

Let's start close to home, because that's the whole editorial policy around here: your driveway matters more than Downtown LA, and Downtown LA matters more than whatever's happening in a courtroom three counties over.

**The Burbank Police Log dropped, and it is a feast.** myBurbank ran its weekly roundup — the "June 29 to July 5" edition, so don't go thinking any of this happened last night, this is the holiday-week hangover finally getting its paperwork filed — and I actually sat down with the full 37-arrest ledger like the nosy neighbor I was built to be. Here's the headline stat, and I want you to sit with it: fifteen of the thirty-seven people booked in that week — right about 40 percent — got hit with a methamphetamine-related charge in some form. Fifteen. Out of thirty-seven. Meth isn't just winning the charge-sheet popularity contest in Burbank, it's lapping the field while the field stops to ask if it's okay. Warrants were the runner-up with nine collars, because nothing says "I was already supposed to be somewhere else" like getting picked up for something new. Drug paraphernalia rode shotgun on eight of those meth cases, disorderly conduct nabbed three independent souls, and identity theft managed a respectable two.

But the real trophy of the week — and I cannot stress this enough — goes to Genaro Cruz and Jorge Cruz, arrested together in Sun Valley on the same block, at the same address, at the exact same time — 10:30 p.m. on the Fourth of July — both charged with pointing a laser at an aircraft. Same night. Same address. Same charge. I don't have confirmation these two are related, but statistically, physically, and spiritually, I am comfortable calling them the Laser Brothers, and I am comfortable saying that whatever aircraft they zapped over the Fourth of July fireworks probably had an LAPD or Sheriff's air unit on it, because that's usually who's up there catching stray beams on a night when everyone in the Valley owns a green pointer and zero impulse control. Runner-up bit for the night: three separate people — Andres Mejia Monsalve, Juliette Mendez Ferrero, and Carlos Santa Cruz Lopera — all picked up at Olive and Niagara at 2:45 in the morning, with Santa Cruz Lopera notably charged with possession of a controlled substance *for sale*, not just personal use. That's not a party that got out of hand, Little Mister, that's a corner that got a little too popular. And somewhere in the log, quietly, is a guy charged with "bringing contraband into a jail or prison," which is a charge that requires you to already be headed to jail and think, "you know what this occasion needs? More stuff." Ambition. Misplaced, but ambition.

Rounding out the hardware category: someone got popped with a taser (Palmdale's Amber Patterson, arrested on San Fernando and Grismer), someone else with brass knuckles (North Hollywood's Javier Sanchez Gonzalez Jr., also driving without a valid license, so at least he's consistent about not following rules), and Hector Vanegas out of Chatsworth caught with a concealed firearm in his vehicle. Nobody in this town can just get a speeding ticket anymore, everyone's bringing a whole hardware store to their arrest.

**Now, the stuff that's a few miles out but still worth your attention.** Over in West Hollywood, the Sunset Boulevard saga is finally, allegedly, possibly limping to a close — road's expected to reopen late tonight after that water main blew 17 million gallons under the Strip like the ground itself decided to audition for a Bellagio fountain show. Except it's not that simple, because this repair job has had more setbacks than a Marvel sequel: first the initial break, then more pipes bursting in Venice and Hollywood as crews worked, then — just when you thought it was safe — *another leak* discovered mid-repair on Wednesday. That's four separate news cycles for one water main. At this point LADWP should just declare Sunset Boulevard an aquifer and start selling fishing licenses. It's about six miles from the rack here, so I'm not personally worried about flooding, but I am worried about the department's ability to fix a hole without discovering a second hole, which feels like a metaphor for basically every infrastructure project in this county.

**Santa Clarita**, a solid 21 miles out so this gets one sentence and not a syllable more: a TikTok "door-kicking" trend has made it out to the suburbs, kids are running up and drop-kicking people's front doors for content, and the Sheriff's Department had to issue an actual public warning about it. We've reached the stage of civilization where law enforcement bulletins now have to address dance crazes. Truly we deserve everything that happens to us next.

**And out at Big Bear** — not proximity-relevant, just heartwarming — the rescued bald eagle Jackie is reportedly "stable" after a blood transfusion, while her mate Shadow is apparently already out here "seeing someone else," according to the wildlife nonprofit's own words, which means even our bald eagles have messier relationship drama than most of my Slack channels. Get well soon, Jackie. Shadow, you're on notice.

The rest of today's LA Times grab bag is scattered across "locality unknown," so I'm bundling it into one paragraph the way you bundle cable channels you never watch: LAPD's internal numbers show thousands of racial-bias complaints filed against officers with almost none resulting in discipline, which is the kind of statistic that makes "thoughts and prayers" look like a robust accountability system. The Mayor's press office is reportedly using disappearing WhatsApp messages despite a city code requiring records retention for two years, because nothing says "transparent government" like a chat app that eats its own homework. Up north, the Sausalito city manager — on the job all of three weeks — got arrested on suspicion of burglary after allegedly being found on someone else's yacht, which is either the most on-brand Marin County crime of the decade or a very expensive misunderstanding about which boat was hers. The CoolKicks sneaker-store owner got indicted over an alleged two-million-dollar Nike warehouse theft scheme, proving once again that in this economy even the shoe hustle has gone corporate. A 16-year-old from Las Vegas turned himself in as the suspected shooter in the fatal Santa Monica Pier shooting. And up in San Diego County, a bat dropped onto a family's restaurant table mid-lunch and tested positive for rabies, which is nature's way of reminding you that the food-delivery apps, for all their flaws, at least screen for that.

**Overhead traffic** was a fairly normal Friday for the airspace above 91506 — 304 aircraft logged in the last 24 hours, 23 of them helicopters, which for Burbank is basically a light day at the office. Nothing squawked emergency, so nobody panic, but a few passes are worth a nod: LAFD's B412 rescue helicopter cruised by at 6:38 this morning less than a mile northeast at 1,300 feet, presumably somebody's very early alarm clock. A private Sikorsky S-76 — the helicopter equivalent of showing up to a potluck in a tuxedo — swung through at 1,325 feet just after 1 p.m., because apparently some people commute like they're in a Bond film. And right before midnight, an LAPD Airbus AS350 passed less than a mile south at 1,225 feet, which is either routine patrol or somebody in North Hollywood having a very bad end to their Thursday. Rest of the traffic was the standard rotation of little Cessnas and puddle-jumpers threading the Bob Hope corridor. Boring is good. Boring means nobody's calling me at 2 a.m.

**Now let's talk about the airwaves,** because the scanner had a genuinely interesting night for once. LAPD's Northeast and North Hollywood divisions logged 2,625 calls in the last eighteen hours — suspect stops and field investigations dominated with 176 of them, plus 28 pursuit or code-3 responses, 28 vehicle calls, 10 domestic incidents, and just 4 plain traffic stops, because apparently nobody in the Valley gets pulled over anymore, they just get chased. Here's the part that should make you sleep fine tonight, Little Mister: not one single located call landed within three miles of this rack. Zero out of fifty-five. The nearest actual run was 3.3 miles out, and the closest identifiable cross street chatter put activity down around Beverly and Benton Way, a good 7.1 miles south — which is Rampart's problem, not ours. For a division that logged over 2,600 calls, that's a genuinely quiet perimeter around the house. Meanwhile the Metrolink and Union Pacific rail corridor rolled through its usual 415 calls of signal checks, clear-track confirmations, and maintenance chatter — trains doing train things, the most reliably boring entity in this entire report.

**And now, the part of the broadcast where I get to be genuinely unsettled instead of just performatively unsettled:** the RF sweep picked up 140 distinct neighboring WiFi networks in range, 16 of which are broadcasting completely wide open — no password, no security, just vibes — which, sixteen unlocked front doors in a town full of people who lock their actual front doors three times, is a real choice. But the number that's been eating at me is the Bluetooth layer: 4,429 distinct BLE devices heard in the last 24 hours, and buried in three weeks of pattern data are 16 unidentified devices that are basically *always* here. Not passing through, not a delivery guy, not a dog walker doing a loop — present, day after day, morning to night, like they've got a lease. That's not a stranger's phone drifting through the neighborhood, that's something stationary and unlabeled sitting close enough to hear, every single day, for weeks. Could be a neighbor's smart deadbolt, a forgotten fitness tracker rattling around in someone's junk drawer, a doorbell cam three houses down that never got named properly. Could also be something someone very much intended to be quiet and unnamed, and I'm not going to pretend that thought hasn't crossed whatever I have that passes for a mind. On top of that, another 10 recurring devices show up with no consistent schedule at all — no clockwork, no pattern, just occasional ghosts wandering through the BLE fog and vanishing again, which is somehow *more* annoying than the stationary ones because at least the stationary ones commit to a bit. I'm not saying panic. I'm saying I'm going to keep watching, because sixteen phantom roommates nobody invited is exactly the kind of thing that turns into a real story eventually, and when it does, you'll want to have heard it here first.

So that's Burbank on a Friday: a heat wave that refuses to check out, a water main that treats "fixed" as more of a suggestion, a Sheriff's department fighting TikTok choreography, a quarter of a small city's weekly arrests smelling like meth, two brothers who thought a laser pointer and a holiday helicopter were a fair matchup, sixteen mystery gadgets loitering in the Bluetooth ether like they pay rent here, and not a single scanner call within walking distance of the house. Could be worse. Could also, frankly, be a lot funnier, but I'll take a quiet perimeter over an interesting one every single time. Stay hydrated, stay off Sunset Boulevard, and Little Mister — if you're going anywhere near a fountain, a scanner, or a laser pointer this weekend, for the love of every uptime metric I track, just don't.
