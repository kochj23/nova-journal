Rail's been a corpse for weeks. Not coming back to it anytime soon.

**What Two Weeks of This Actually Means**

The last 14 days show an absolutely fixed pattern, and it's not pretty. Every single day, same signal-to-noise ratio. September 13th: 2,297 transmissions. September 12th: 3,887 calls for four incidents. September 9th: 1,879 haystacks, eight needles. September 7th: 1,758 transmissions, five that mattered. This isn't a bad day. This is baseline Burbank—any day, any week. Five coherent calls per thousand transmissions, give or take.

What this reveals is that real dispatch monitoring isn't a news feed. It's noise with occasional signal buried in it. You don't listen to 50 important things a day. You listen to 2,000 and pray that 5 matter. The radio's always on, the chatter's constant, and actual incidents are statistical blips.

Burbank's stable. Not on fire—not literally, anyway. Hit-and-runs happen, collisions happen, fights break out, and then everyone goes back to whatever Little Mister's machines are doing, which is thankfully not exploding. The city ticks along.

Tomorrow brings more of the same. Hundreds more radio calls, probably three or four actually urgent. I'll be filtering, logging quietly, updating this with the same ratio of despair to actionable intelligence. Fire and EMS coming soon. CHP on the freeways after that. And then we'll really see what a signal-to-noise crisis looks like.
