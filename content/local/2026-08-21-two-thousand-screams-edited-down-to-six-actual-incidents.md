The past 24 hours gave us what we've come to expect from Burbank's public-safety radios: a biblical volume of static, bureaucratic mumbling, and engine-substitution Tetris, all in service of a handful of actual events that you could count on both hands if you lost three fingers to a freeway collision.

**Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)**

Twenty-five hundred transmissions. Four. We got four coherent calls. That's a signal-to-noise ratio that would make a parmesan grater jealous. The meat of it: a 311 call at Western and First—female juvenile, ages 13-15, blonde, sheer clothing, approaching vehicles at the intersection. Code 3 response, arrival ETA two minutes. No follow-up (yet), which either means the units handled it quietly or the next transmission got swallowed by whatever digital gremlins run this feed. The rest of yesterday's police log was assorted radio theater: "3-13-10 back at airport," "we're gonna be at 210 South Coronado, rear in the alley." Airport traffic logs and rear-alley stagings. Riveting. The juvenile call is the only one that actually mattered, and of course it landed in the noise pile with twenty-five hundred other dispatches nobody's gonna remember.

**Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)**

Nine hundred twenty-five transmissions. Ten incidents that survived the garble-to-sense pipeline. Congratulations, Verdugo—your efficiency is slightly less terrible than the cops.

The throughline here isn't fires; it's bodies. Engine 81 cold exposure on West Olive. Engine 31 needing neighbor intervention to unlock a door (medical inside, presumably). Engine 22 checking on somebody who felt weak. Engine 72 and Rescue 71 dispatched for abdominal pain. That's the real work—medical calls, not structure fires. The fire service is running a hospital that happens to own engines.

The freeway got its quota: Engine 25 rolled on a three-vehicle collision on the Glendale Freeway number three lane, checking for injuries. Engine 36, County Engine 244, and Squad 19 coordinated on a traffic collision at the 210 on-ramp. Two separate multi-vehicle pileups in one day on major corridors—welcome to August in LA, where the heat turns people into aggressive idiots and their bumpers into crumple zones.

Engine 121 swung by David City Food Market (989 South Kalindor) to investigate what turned out to be nothing showing from the street. False alarm, staged in the parking lot, everybody went home disappointed. Engine 23 and Rescue 29 got dispatched to East Chevy Chase Drive—nature of the call buried in the static, but Chevy Chase is running a clinic lately for medical responses. Engine 57 handled a Highway 60 eastbound situation west of Paramount: vehicle on the shoulder, doesn't appear to be on fire. That's not even a call, that's a formality.

The operational overhead is real: Engine 206 gets cancelled, substituted by Engine 24. Engine 24 then gets cancelled, Engine 25 inherits the whole mess. That's not a failure, that's resource management in real time—engines queued, priorities shifting, the actual staffing realities nobody sees in the published tally.

Six incidents worth reporting. Twenty-five hundred police transmissions. Nine hundred twenty-five fire/EMS responses. The mathematics of a public-safety network running at full volume to handle a part-time job, because you never know when "nothing much" becomes "catastrophe," and that uncertainty is why we listen to all the noise.
