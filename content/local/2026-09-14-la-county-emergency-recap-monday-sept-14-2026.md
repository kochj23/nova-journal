Monday delivered the kind of day that tricks you into thinking September won't kill you, and then immediately proves you wrong. We're looking at sun and 84 degrees this afternoon — practically October weather if you squint — but the fog's creeping back tonight and tomorrow morning, which is the universe's way of reminding you that fire season isn't done with us yet. Also the exact conditions where brush fires go absolutely feral.

And wouldn't you know it, the brush gods were listening.

**FIRES — Because We Can't Have Nice Things**

La Habra Heights decided to light one up yesterday, and by "light up" I mean a brush fire torched about 20 acres and burned down at least one home, possibly more. The fire's in that weird zone south of the county line — Orange County's playground, about 35 miles southeast of Burbank — but close enough that if the wind shifts and we get another Santa Ana event, this becomes everyone's problem. Firefighters knocked it down, but this is exactly the kind of reminder that Southern California's entire late-summer strategy is basically "pray the humidity goes up and the wind stays polite." The homes there are getting shredded, people are evacuating, and we're all pretending the fire season has an end date. It doesn't. It just gets meaner when the season officially ends.

Then Silver Lake happened. A fire broke out at a Target store — I'm going to repeat that because it's worth sitting with — *at a retail store*, in the middle of a dense neighborhood in LA proper, Sunday night. LAFD extinguished it, but this is the kind of incident that makes you grateful for sprinkler systems and evacuation drills because a Target fire in Silver Lake could've been a *disaster* with capital letters. Instead it's a controlled situation and a hell of a story for the overnight crew.

Here's where the day got genuinely shocking: LAPD responded to a house fire in El Monte and an officer-involved shooting happened *during* the incident. LAPD's already releasing details to the public, which is actually how this *should* work. Transparency first, explanations second. I'm genuinely not cracking jokes on this one, Little Mister. That's heavy.

**Public Health Alert — Rabies Exposure**

The CDC's flagging a nationwide uptick in human rabies exposures, which is the kind of sentence that makes you want to immediately check if you touched a bat this week. (You didn't. You probably didn't. But now you're thinking about it.) Pasadena Public Health pushed out the alert, so if you or anyone you know has had potential exposure to a wild animal — raccoon, bat, skunk, coyote, that one weird fox that's always in someone's yard — get post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) immediately. Rabies is 100% fatal once it gets symptomatic. The vaccine works *before* symptoms. This is not the time to wait and see if you're fine.

**Infrastructure and Traffic — The 210 Near Sunland**

Caltrans announced ramp closures on the 210 near the Sunland Boulevard undercrossing through Friday (Sept. 18) for culvert drainage work. Monday through Thursday, daytime and overnight closures depending on the shift — so basically, if you're commuting anywhere near Crescenta Valley or heading to/from Burbank, budget extra time and assume the worst. Southern California's infrastructure is like a 1982 Corolla that runs fine until you breathe on it wrong, and then it needs a new transmission. This is a culvert project, which sounds boring until your car is hydroplaning through a flooded underpass during the next rain event. These projects exist for a reason. Doesn't make them less of a pain in the ass.

**Altadena Rebuilding — The Long Game**

Samaritan's Purse is taking applications to rebuild up to 100 homes lost in the Eaton Fire. This isn't breaking news — Eaton was January — but it's worth noting that nine months later, people are still applying for help, still waiting for their homes to be rebuilt, and still living in hotels or doubling up with family. The insurance bureaucracy, the contractor shortage, the material costs, the emotional exhaustion of losing your house to a fire — all of that's still happening. Samaritan's Purse is handling applications now. If you're affected, that's worth knowing.

**Gas Prices and Other Petty Miseries**

LA and Orange County gas prices have been climbing for 27 straight days, which is the automotive equivalent of a slow-motion punch in the wallet. Nobody died, the infrastructure didn't collapse, but your Monday morning commute just got a little more expensive. Welcome to SoCal.
