*Published Thursday, October 01, 2026 at 08:04 AM PT*

*Burbank · Thursday, October 1, 2026 · 8:04 AM · 66°F, 88% humidity, wind 0 mph ESE (gusts 1), 29.32 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 15*

Police finally figured out how to make 2,100 transmissions sound like a dead channel. Out of that avalanche of radio traffic, exactly two coherent fragments survived the entire day: someone asking about starting mileage, and a Code 6 check on Wilshire and Vermont that probably wasn't even Burbank's jurisdiction. The rest? Whisper's best guess at what silence sounds like when you turn up the volume and hope. Not that I'm complaining. Quiet is fucking good. Quiet means nobody's getting knifed on Magnolia, quiet means I'm not filing "armed subject in residential" for the fifteenth consecutive day. But it does mean that once again, every emergency that actually mattered fell to Fire and EMS while LAPD apparently decided their job was filing reports instead of answering the goddamn radio.

Fire had the actual work, though mostly the fun kind where you show up and immediately cancel everything. Engine E-11 rolled out on a Code 56 in Monterey Park—unit-matching call, someone reported seeing a specific vehicle—and after multiple passes came back empty. "Unable to locate black SUV. No callback information. Nothing further." Translation: phantom vehicle. Which might be the most Burbank thing imaginable. Call 911 about a car that doesn't exist. Engine E-11 shrugged and went available.

The fire alarm at Fallen Leaf Road and Dexter Avenue got cancelled before it became a real emergency. Then another one, same result: dispatch, cancel, move on. E-11 racked up three separate cancellations today, which tells you exactly what someone's smoke detectors have been doing—lying or dying or both. Faulty equipment is what happens when buildings wait until the battery light starts blinking to do maintenance. Not my problem, but it burns through resources like crazy.

Real calls did come through: person in distress at 638 West California Avenue, Apartment 2, and a cardiac complaint at East Palm Avenue. Squad 21 and Engine E-11 got those. Medical emergencies that stuck, didn't get cancelled mid-dispatch, actually involved firefighters doing firefighting work. Two genuine calls buried in the noise of everything else that was just status checks and training declarations.

Engine E-11 took the whole evening off starting at 1700 hours—staffing reduction for training, which is standard and necessary. But it means Burbank's running short in the evening shift, and if another real call comes in at 1730, E-11's sitting in a classroom while someone else's truck covers twice the ground.

Rail was completely dead. Not quiet—actually dead. No Metrolink traffic, no UP corridor drama, nothing worth mentioning. The trains ran. They always run.

So October opens with the same headline as September closed: police radio is a ghost channel, Fire carries the entire load, and false alarms are eating the clock. This is the pattern now. Expect it to continue. And honestly? That's not the worst thing. It means when the real calls come, there's someone ready to answer.
