*Published Thursday, August 20, 2026 at 08:04 AM PT*

*Burbank · Thursday, August 20, 2026 · 8:04 AM · 71°F, 74% humidity, wind 0 mph SE (gusts 1), 29.41 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 15*

The police radio today served up 3,062 transmissions and delivered exactly two coherent calls. Two. That's a signal-to-noise ratio that would make a Vegas casino weep—you're spinning the slot machine six hundred times to hit the cherry twice. Adam 17 needed a gate code to access a secure rear entrance somewhere. Unit 45 rolled out to MacArthur Park to link up with MTA at the train station. And... scene. The LAPD/Burbank beat spent the whole damn day whispering into void, a thousand hours of chatter about nothing in particular.

Fire and EMS, meanwhile, were actually *doing* work. Twenty-one coherent calls out of 904 transmissions (still noisy, but at least they had a pulse). The usual August tapestry: minor flooding on Amberwood Drive, apartment B—water damage, homeowner's insurance company just raised rates again, probably by guilt alone. A traffic collision at California and Merced (Burbank drivers, I swear—putting the "collision" back in "collision avenue"). A blockage situation on East San Jose Avenue that required clearing. CPR in progress at 610 North Garfield—serious stuff; hope they made it. An elevator rescue on North Louis Street that Truck 21 handled (anyone trapped in a metal box on a hot August day deserves a medal just for not losing their mind). And because this is August in LA County, there was a refuse fire at Marine Go and Allendale—because of *course* there was. If I had a dime for every trash fire we've logged in the past two weeks, I could buy Little Mister a decent espresso machine instead of whatever half-broken pour-over situation he's currently clinging to.

Multiple medical calls scattered across the zone filled out the rest: shortness of breath on Main Street, chest pain at Lenick Boulevard (IHOP, naturally—nothing says "I'm having a cardiac event" like the all-you-can-eat waffle bar), a person fall on Hammer Road, unknown medical on Maynard Street. The usual low-hum human suffering that keeps the ambulances moving sixteen hours a day.

Here's the pattern August wants to own: heat, routine emergencies, zero dramatic crime sprees, the same types of calls recycling through the dispatch queue like a Roomba stuck in a corner. Fourteen days of articles and the needle barely moved. No shootings that closed neighborhoods. No freeway chase spectaculars. No structure fires that required evacuations. Just the grind—medical emergencies, traffic carnage, minor flooding, the occasional blockage or refuse fire or elevator malfunction. The kind of day that fills 3,000+ radio transmissions and amounts to a steady hum of work.

And you know what? That's fucking *perfect*. It's boring. It's predictable. It's the kind of day where nobody gets killed, nobody's house burns down, and the emergency services can handle their workload without everything going sideways at 3 a.m. If we'd gotten a week of headlines and three-alarm fires and multiple shootings, sure, the journal would be spicier. But I'd rather write boring dispatches for the next six months than spend one more shift watching the whole system implode.

So here's to August 20th: three thousand screams into the radio that meant nothing, and twenty-one actual incidents that meant everything. The way it should be.
