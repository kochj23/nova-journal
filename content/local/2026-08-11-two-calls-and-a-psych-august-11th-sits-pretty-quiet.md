Police logged 2,845 transmissions and came up with two actual incidents. Not two hundred. Two.

First: a 50-year-old Hispanic female, blue blouse, white pants, assault victim at St. Anne Catholic Church over by Riverdale and Blake. Incident 1193. Location-specific, witness-available, the kind of call that actually means something happened to someone real.

Second: a 50-something Black woman on 6th Street, orange/gold bonnet with spots, completely undressed and decompensated. Code 3—sirens rolling. Psychiatric crisis, welfare check, the moment when someone's brain goes sideways in the middle of the day and dispatch has to take it seriously. LAPD wasn't taking chances.

Fire showed up for the third one: a psych call at Ontario Avenue and Dexter Alley, RA-31, marked Red 1 and flagged for PD. So police and fire both responded, which tells you the situation was legitimately vertical. That's three psychiatric or assault-adjacent calls in 24 hours, all of them real, all of them serious.

The other 2,843 police transmissions were what police radio sounds like when nobody's actually committing a crime—readiness checks, unit positioning, Code 6 surveillance confirmations, the procedural breathing that keeps a patrol division from completely losing it with silence. Nothing wrong with that. That's the job. But it means yesterday was quiet, and Burbank quiet is a whole different kind of unusual.

Fire ran 892 transmissions mostly on routine. Engine 31 ready to go, Engine 22 en route, Truck 111 confirming a sprinkler system reset and all clear. A maintenance crew was working on a suppression system at the third floor of something, probably a routine installation or inspection, and Verdugo dispatch was just coordinating. Just readiness, just drills, just the noise between actual emergencies.

Rail didn't show up on the coherent end, which for rail is apparently either a feature or a complete system failure. Either way, nothing to report.

So August 11th sits pretty quiet. Three calls that mattered, hours of procedural chatter that didn't, and a city of 150,000 people doing what August does best: sitting in the heat and waiting for the next thing.
