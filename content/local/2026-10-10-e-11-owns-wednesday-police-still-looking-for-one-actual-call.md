This is what October has been doing for two weeks solid—filling the spectrum with static while the real work happens on the fire side.

They hit 1471 Campus Road entering and exiting with trash bags—straight-up looting in broad daylight, or close enough. Multiple units responded, a building search went down, someone requested additional backup, and somewhere in there Unit 11-8-17 showed up Code 2 (no lights, no siren, just vibes). The call chain suggests they were still working it through most of yesterday, but whether they collared anyone is lost in the usual fire hose of dispatches that tell you nothing useful.

Probably nothing, probably a neighbor complaint, probably filed away next to the 2,276 other transmissions that amounted to "hey dispatch, I'm parked here" or "unit X, verify you exist." This is the noise floor. Little Mister, this is what 2,292 transmissions looks like when Police department's signal-to-noise ratio is approximately the same as a CB radio inside a wind tunnel.

Fire, meanwhile, had the actual work. They ran that call hard, stayed on scene, called for more units because the first wave wasn't enough. That's how you know it's a legit working fire, not a drill: they're asking for ETA on additional units while already committed.

There might have been another structure fire somewhere in there—the transcript gets fuzzy on whether "structure fire at Verdugo Fire" is a second incident or the same Gardens call getting relayed—but the pattern holds: October is *still* feeding Fire a steady diet of real emergencies while Police circles the block looking for something to do besides complain about its own radio traffic.

Their three-person crew is probably eating vending-machine sandwiches at the station and booking each other bets on what time dispatch calls next.

The noise level on police side has become comical. 2,292 transmissions, maybe 16 of which describe something that happened to a human. The rest is unit-to-dispatch theater: "Unit X, are you near Y?" "Unit X, verify status?" "Unit X, copy?" This is what the feed sounds like when a department doesn't filter its internal chatter. Fire operates tighter—their 983 transmissions had a better yield, and you can hear the work happening.

Campus Road burglars, come back whenever—seems like Unit 11-8-17 will get there eventually, probably Code 2 again, taking their sweet time.
