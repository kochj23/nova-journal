*Published Monday, August 17, 2026 at 08:24 AM PT*

*Burbank · Monday, August 17, 2026 · 8:24 AM · 70°F, 78% humidity, wind 0 mph E (gusts 2), 29.47 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 12*

Sunday on the airwaves was peak LA energy — absolutely deafening static punctuated by the occasional actual emergency trying desperately to be heard over the noise. Police chatter clocked 2,890 transmissions in 24 hours. You want to know how many of them were coherent, real-world incidents? Six. That's a signal-to-noise ratio that would make a statistical engineer weep into his coffee. Fire/EMS did marginally better at 13 coherent calls out of 624 — still mostly screaming into the void while dispatch tried to remember what day it was.

## Police (LAPD NoHo/NE + Burbank PD)

A possible shooting at 1624 South Hope Street kicked off the day as a Code 3, incident 192. South Hope is technically south LA, not Burbank proper, but close enough to remind us that the area's never really quiet. Then dispatch caught wind of an assault with deadly weapon suspect doing his best impression of a two-bit street performer at a 76 gas station at 304 North Vermont. This genius was wearing a green jacket and red hood — so if you're keeping score, we found our criminal through the medium of obnoxious fashion — and he was throwing rocks at vehicles and pedestrians with the kind of dedication usually reserved for people who've decided their life insurance wasn't paying out enough to justify going quietly.

The real weight in today's police digest, though, was a missing person report: Gabriel Diaz, a 5'8", 140-pound Hispanic male with brown eyes and black hair, diagnosed with dementia, last seen around 1 p.m. near 1323 Douglas Street wearing gray and blue. These calls don't usually make the highlight reel because they're not dramatic — no shootout, no chase, no dramatic takedown. They're just the quiet terrifying kind where someone's clock is running wrong and nobody knows where they went. If you're in Burbank and you see him, the alert was out there. That one matters.

## Fire / EMS (Verdugo dispatch)

Traffic collisions continue their reign as LA's most reliable self-inflicted disaster. We caught Engine 34 handling a collision at Delmar Boulevard and Lake Avenue, Engine 57 and Squad 56 rolling to Beverly Boulevard and Garfield Avenue (PD on scene for that one), and Truck 12 dealing with an overturned vehicle at Burbank and Evergreen Street, checking for injuries. Collisions in threes is basically a medium day in August — it's hot, people are agitated, hydration is nonexistent, and apparently nobody remembers how to maintain a safe following distance. Physics doesn't care how angry you are.

The alarms started up like they do in heat waves: Engine 31 to 817 South El Molino Avenue, Engine 26 to 333 Monterey Road, and two separate audible-alarm responses because nothing says "I'm having a real emergency" quite like a building's fire system deciding 2 a.m. is the perfect moment for a false drama queen moment. These usually amount to nothing, but they burn resources and bandwidth that could be elsewhere. Engine 52 was out of service for eight hours of probationary training, which is absolutely the right move but doesn't help the available unit count when everything's popping off simultaneously.

There's an uptick in illegal burning reported at Santa Clara Avenue and Kennedy Avenue, PD in route. That one's interesting — heat waves breed a specific kind of stupidity where people think "now is a great time to have a backyard fire" or worse, it's someone testing limits knowing fire response is stretched thin. Either way, it's a bad bet.

Medical calls kept rolling in. Engine 101 and Squad 101 got pinged for a diabetic emergency and a medical call at 1445 Highland Avenue, and another response to 1024 Royal Oaks Drive. Standard summer attrition: heat exhaustion, dehydration, existing conditions going sideways because people don't hydrate like they should.

The day was loud, mostly harmless, and entirely unremarkable except for the guy throwing rocks at a gas station and the missing person who matters. Pick your battles, Burbank. Stay cool. Stay hydrated. Don't throw rocks at strangers. It's not complicated.
