*Published Friday, October 02, 2026 at 08:05 AM PT*

*Burbank · Friday, October 2, 2026 · 8:05 AM · 68°F, 73% humidity, wind 0 mph SE (gusts 1), 29.35 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 4*

Fire had itself a solid Tuesday. Not a "code red, everything's burning" day, but a legitimate "earn-your-paycheck" day — six structure fires scattered across the area kept the department moving between coordinates like they'd signed up for the world's worst obstacle course. This is the kind of shift that separates the training from the doing.

**Fire / EMS**

Alhambra High School on South 2nd Street took the first call. Engine 11, Truck 15, and Medic Unit 63 rolled to campus and found an actual structure fire that needed the full orchestra — not a drill, not a false alarm, but the real deal on school property. Fire knocked it down and moved on. While they were still catching their breath, North Point Vista Street reported flames too. Then El Monte Avenue. North Parkwood Avenue after that. Over on Colorado Boulevard, a parking lot joined the party and went up in its own way. The Verdugo Road and Balboa Boulevard intersection rounded out the structure-fire bingo card, and by the time the smoke cleared (literally, chemically, by the magic of water and foam), the department had fielded six separate structure fires across Burbank and the surrounding area in twenty-four hours.

That's not a typo. Six fires.

Between the flames, Fire also got the human edition of emergencies: A back pain call on Colorado Boulevard at Harkness Avenue. Security was on scene, but Fire rolled anyway because that's how it works now — firefighters are orthopedic consultants, apparently. Then there was a female hanging from a fence somewhere, and Fire had to coordinate an actual rescue while Unit 71 assessed and police tried to figure out what "holding" meant. And because nobody can plan anything in this city, someone threw a smoke alarm in a trash can by an apartment complex, triggering the whole emergency chain. Fire showed up, confirmed that yes, trash fire ≠ structure fire, and moved on.

So Fire handled six structure fires, medical calls, a rescue, and false alarms. All in one day. All with precision.

**Police**

Police reported one coherent incident. One. Battery suspect at Santa Monica Boulevard — and the beautiful part: citizens were holding him down when dispatch got the call. They didn't wait for police to show up and sort it out; they just grabbed the guy, held him in place, and called it in. "Hey, we got your suspect right here, send someone when you get a chance." Citizen arrest. Respect to the people of Santa Monica Boulevard for doing the heavy lifting.

**Rail**

The UP corridor and Metrolink apparently looked at the calendar, looked at what Fire was dealing with, and collectively decided everyone needed a mental health day. Nothing. Silence. Can't blame them.

So: six fires, one arrest, zero trains. Fire carrying the weight while police showed up for the paperwork, and Burbank kept spinning like it was just another Tuesday — because it was.
