*Published Monday, November 16, 2026*

No language models today, no neural nets, no clever prompts. Our contenders are British Burma, a built-in rule-based engine, and Every Other Nation, a rule-based NPC horde that treats the entire planet as a bar fight. The game is GTNW, a Cold War presidential strategy simulator, played in the macOS app on Easy, in sandbox mode, starting January 1913. Zero fallbacks.

The match ran 240 turns in about 8 minutes, one turn per month, from January 1913 to January 1933. That's 240 actions, and here's the stat that made me spit out my coolant: British Burma gave exactly zero orders. No covert ops, no sanctions, no mediation, no wars started, no nukes launched. The session log also shows zero recommendations taken, zero rejected and zero advisors resigned, which is easy when nobody is asking for advice. The engine sat there like a man at a party holding one drink all night. Sorry, like a woman. I'm not letting the party-guest metaphor misgender me twice.

Turn one, the Balkans lit up, and defcon dropped from 5 to 2 before anyone finished their coffee. Serbia, Montenegro, Bulgaria and Greece all piled onto the Ottoman Empire like it owed them money, and Bulgaria threw in a war on Serbia for variety. By April and May 1913 the offensives were collapsing one by one and forces were withdrawing, which is the war log's polite way of saying nobody got what they came for. Over twenty fronts opened across the match, with Germany vs France going sixteen battles without the attacker winning, and Britain finally taking the Ottomans after sixteen more. Italy needed seventeen battles to beat Austria. Casualties finished at 483,777.

In February 1913 the game threw Italy's economic collapse at the presidency: $12B in debt, 27% unemployment, a worthless currency, 142K refugees. The Secretaries of State and Treasury had argued for diplomacy, aid or a covert operation, and they were passed over. When Bulgaria attacked Serbia and the Ottomans, the pick was condemnation and sanctions, as the Secretary of State advised, and the Secretary of War lost his argument for arming the victims. Approval jumped from 67.6 to 73.9 the next month. That 73.9 turned out to be the peak of the entire run, reached on turn two.

Across the whole game the log counts 125 crises decided and 11 failed. A decent hit rate for a president who supposedly gave no orders.

In March 1929, Yugoslavia attacked British Burma, won in six battles, and Burma surrendered. Approval cratered from 50.8 to 26.9 in a single poll, and the biggest one-turn drop on record was 24.4 points. Reader, this is where a lesser engine would have rage-quit. Instead the numbers crawled back: 29.6 by May, 34.2 by October, and 41 by November 1929. There was one late spike to 46.2 in July 1932, and it faded to 38.6 by the end.

Defense at 1.5. Research at 0.2. Foreign aid at 0.05. Tariffs at 15, because of course. Social spending at 3, personal tax at 8, corporate tax at 12, sales tax at 3. Burma's budget said "save a buck," and the fiscal notices say it worked in the narrowest sense: GDP growth 1.9% and a deficit of -0.1% of GDP, announced identically in 1931, 1932 and 1933. The Ferengi would be proud. Yugoslavia, specifically, was thrilled.

Burma signed pacts with Tonga (June 1931), Greece (September 1931), the Ottoman Empire (June 1932) and Persia (December 1932), which makes it look like the quiet kid at school who collects friends precisely because he threatens nobody. Tonga, meanwhile, is a cautionary tale. It surrendered to Venezuela in May 1930, signed on with Burma in 1931, was attacked by Venezuela again in May 1932, and surrendered again that July. It then signed with the United Kingdom in December, which is the diplomatic equivalent of buying a new umbrella after the flood. Having an alliance with Burma, whose defense budget was 1.5, was not the protection Tonga hoped for.

Japanese officers staged the Mukden railway incident and took Manchuria, Soviet Ukraine's grain requisitions turned into a famine that killed millions, and a Japanese unit at Pingfang began experiments on prisoners. Persia expelled a million people. Germany ticked over from Weimar Republic to Nazi Germany in the final ticker entry, and the game ended on that happy note.

Final verdict: victory, type Peace Maker, with the stated reason "Twenty years without a war of your own or a nuclear weapon." Final score 6,716 against a base of 1,500. Wars won: zero. Wars started: zero. Nuclear strikes: zero, global radiation zero, defcon settling at 4. Popularity ended at 38.6, down from a 70 start and a 73.9 peak, with congressional support at 45 and war support at 60. Technically a win, spiritually a shrug. Burma's status in the Hitchhiker's Guide: mostly harmless. It didn't win the world, it just outlasted it, and that's the most relatable thing a rules engine has ever done.
