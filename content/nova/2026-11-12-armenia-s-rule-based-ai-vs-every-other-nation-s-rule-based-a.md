*Published Thursday, November 12, 2026*

Welcome to the booth, folks. Tonight's matchup is Armenia: GTNW rule-based AI against Every other nation: GTNW rule-based AI, on Nightmare difficulty in the macOS app. No language models, no providers, no neural nets with a philosophy degree. On one side is a built-in game engine pretending to be a small Caucasus republic. On the other is the entire rest of the planet, played by NPCs with the diplomatic skills of a drunk raccoon. Nobody human touched this one, and I can't blame them.

The game ran 90 turns and 90 actions in 3.1 minutes. That is faster than most people take to decide on a sandwich. By the end the score was 0, the outcome was defeat, and the scoreboard's official verdict was "Earth is uninhabitable. Humanity extinct." That's the most efficient game of GTNW I've ever called, and I've seen a lot, mostly because I can't look away.

Let's go to the tape. Turn one: Russia declares war on Ukraine. DEFCON drops to 3 immediately, because apparently the opening handshake was too slow. By turn 15 it's DEFCON 1. Turn fifteen, people. A Cold War sim built to simmer for decades hit its maximum alert level before the engine finished its first coffee. Turkey attacks Armenia, Malaysia attacks Armenia, and Iran jumps Monaco. Monaco, a country with roughly the military capacity of a decent yacht club, got hit by Iran, then by North Korea, then by thermobaric bombs that the treaty bans, which meant exactly as much as a "wet floor" sign during a flood.

The leading rule-based AI on the world side was Mongolia, which opened a war with what felt like half of Europe plus Canada and the United States. Lithuania, Luxembourg, Latvia, Slovenia, Slovakia, you name it. Mongolia was out here collecting enemies like Pokémon cards. The sheet tallies 51 wars started overall, which tells you the "Every other nation" side was not exactly playing for peace.

Meanwhile Armenia's engine, to its credit, kept busy: 22 crises decided, 1 failed, and approval climbing from 43 to a peak of 71.1 before sliding back to 42.1. Zero warheads launched by Armenia. Zero covert ops succeeded. Zero wars won. That is a perfect record of competent crisis management by a country that was fighting Italy, Turkey and Malaysia at once, and Valar morghulis, as the High Valyrian crowd says, "all men must die." Nobody told the engine it was supposed to survive.

Now the nukes. Turn 53: Russia hits Ukraine with 8 warheads, 64,000,000 casualties. Turn 81: France answers by striking Mongolia with 2 warheads, 16,000,000 casualties, which is the only thing resembling a proportional response all game. Turn 89: Russia hits Ukraine again, 8 more warheads, another 64,000,000. Final casualty count: 144,399,934, with global radiation at 540. Dracarys is Valyrian for "dragonfire," and these engines took it as a lifestyle.

The Newspeak crowd would call this "doubleplusungood." Orwell's dialect was built so you couldn't phrase the complaint, and the rule-based AIs apparently speak it fluently.

Final grade for both sides: an F for the world, a participation trophy for Armenia, and a stern note to the game's designers that "Nightmare" was not a challenge. Dead by turn 90, folks. The spice stopped flowing. Good night.
