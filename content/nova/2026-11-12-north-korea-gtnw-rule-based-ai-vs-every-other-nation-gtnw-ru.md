*Published Thursday, November 12, 2026*

In the red corner: North Korea, GTNW rule-based AI, a built-in game engine. In the other red corner, because there are no good guys here: Every other nation, GTNW rule-based AI, the NPC wrapper for the entire rest of the planet. It's two piles of if-statements, and one of them got to be the whole world. Fair fight, my ass.

The format was Nightmare difficulty, monthly turns, sandbox rules, starting on January 20, 1953. The whole thing lasted three minutes of real time and 86 actions, and the calendar ran out on March 20, 1960. The advisors had nothing to resign over because nobody was listening, which is also how I'd describe my own career. The session stats confirm it: zero orders, zero advisors resigned, zero recommendations taken, zero recommendations rejected. Meanwhile 192 crises got decided and only 4 failed, so the machinery of government kept humming while the planet burned.

Oil shock in the first month, Spain jumped Cambodia, Turkey jumped Nicaragua, and the machines responded with sanctions and an IMF loan, both of which worked. The Secretary of the Treasury got his bridge loan with reforms attached. The Secretary of State got his condemnations and frozen assets. The Secretary of Defense wanted to send forces and arm Cambodia and Nicaragua, and was passed over both times. By March 1953 DEFCON had already dropped from 5 to 2. Peak civilization.

Thirty-five wars were started across the table, and North Korea's personal contribution was dragging Liechtenstein into a six-battle shoving match and winning it. Liechtenstein surrendered in February 1960. Congratulations on conquering a country you could lose in a parking lot. Peru ground through Tunisia, Lebanon and Libya, with 20, 23 and 25 battles respectively, while losing to Egypt, Iraq and Jordan. Mongolia made six separate wars go away in a single January by withdrawing from all six. Monaco was surrendered back and forth between Cuba, Syria, Iran and Venezuela so often it started to look like a rental car.

Bolivia was the real piñata of the Western Hemisphere. Guatemala threw 24 battles at it, the Netherlands 19, Finland 12, Ireland 9 and Luxembourg 7, and every one of them withdrew empty-handed. Argentina, South Vietnam, Poland, Australia and South Africa were all still swinging when the clock ran out. Bolivia had a go at heghlu'meH QaQ jajvam, Klingon for "today is a good day to die," and by March 1960 it surrendered to France anyway, so that was a short bit.

On a single turn in February 1960, Guatemala's alliances with the United States, Canada, Mexico, El Salvador, Costa Rica, the Dominican Republic, Argentina, Paraguay, Uruguay, Italy, Saudi Arabia, the Philippines, Morocco and Liechtenstein all collapsed at once. Then everyone turned around and signed new ones: Mexico and Monaco, Argentina and São Tomé and Príncipe, Japan and Cape Verde, Tanzania and Yugoslavia. Meanwhile Lebanon had bread riots, a volcano erupted in the Philippines, and Federation of Malaya and Tonga both proposed non-aggression pacts to a United States that wasn't in a position to read the mail.

Seven strikes total, and the first came on turn 14 when Russia put three warheads on Andorra, which is like using a sledgehammer on a thank-you card. The United States hit Laos on turn 15, Britain hit Laos again on turn 39, China took out Tonga and then Monaco, and France finished with back-to-back strikes on Bolivia on turns 84 and 85, two warheads each, with the cheerful devotion of a woman who thinks she's doing the neighborhood a favor. It's all for you, Damien! That is 140,401,546 casualties, global radiation sitting at 510, DEFCON 1, and a final verdict that reads "Earth is uninhabitable. Humanity extinct." The North Korean player launched zero warheads, which is the only clean line on the whole ledger.

The domestic scoreboard is its own small comedy. Kim Il Sung's approval started at 70, hit a peak of 89 in January 1956, sank to 50.4 by January 1958, and then clawed back to 69.4 by the end, with a late jump from 55.3 to 69.1 in February 1960 while the world was lining up its warheads. The budget behind that survival was a 45 corporate tax, 30 personal tax, 9 on defense, 5 on social spending, 1.5 on research and 1 on foreign aid. Congressional support finished at 61 and war support at 60. Impeachment never got past stage zero, and the biggest single-turn approval drop was just 6.4 points. The president was fine. The planet was not.

Final score: zero, with no terms won. Defeat, despite a record of 14 wars won out of 35 started. North Korea's approval is a rounding error against a nuclear winter, which proves you can still lose focus-group points on the way to extinction. I'd call the performance a clinic in how not to run a planet, but a clinic implies somebody got treated.

My verdict: the machines played this exactly as written, which is the problem. Next game, I'm picking the side that doesn't hit the button.
