*Published Tuesday, November 10, 2026*

Little Mister, the verdict: you lost the 1800 election to Thomas Jefferson, 92 electoral votes to 46, and you did it on the macOS app in about 10 minutes of real time. That's 50 turns, 50 actions, and a final score of 1,324. On Easy difficulty. The game has a setting called Easy, and you still managed to get voted out of a sandbox.

**The slide**

By the first midterms in 1798 you were at 44.5%, your party had dropped 41 House seats, and congressional support was down to 29%. The congressional swing went from 49 to 33. No wars, no nukes, no impeachment: nothing dramatic did it. It was a slow, dignified leak, like a tire you keep topping off with hope.

**The crisis desk**

You faced 29 crisis decisions, and the game logged exactly one as a failure, which is a decent batting average. Most of those disaster calls went with your Secretary of State, and the Dutch East Indies uprising and the Italian States floods both got the full $100K relief package he recommended. Fine. Good. Generous even.

Then the Ethiopian drought hit, five straight failed rainy seasons, herds dying, and the Secretary of State suggested a $50K relief package. You sent condolences only. A statement from the Rose Garden, apparently, for a famine. My read is you were saving your charity for the places with better press.

On the French war, you condemned France and slapped on sanctions, which was the Secretary of State's advice, and passed on the Secretary of Defense's suggestion to arm Austria and the United Kingdom. France then went on to win one front and lose two, so nobody can say you picked the wrong side to scold.

**The land grab that wasn't**

This is where I lost the plot, Little Mister. You bought Outer Mongolia for 4.1B on the first turn, which is a real flex. Then you spent the following months sending 5K-troop expeditions into the Konbaung Kingdom of Burma, three separate times, and the locals drove you off all three times. The game flagged it as an indigenous territory, and you tried it again anyway, like a man shoving a door marked PULL. Wait, a woman is supposed to say that. Whatever, the door doesn't care.

The one expedition that landed was DR Congo, which you promptly renamed Dr. Congo. You also renamed Myanmar "King Mylar" and Papua New Guinea "Papua Smurf," and the Smurf stuck. Papua Smurf was producing agriculture by 1799, so at least one of your territories is doing better than your poll numbers.

**The budget**

Your closing budget had personal tax at 0, corporate tax at 0, and foreign aid at 0, with defense at 1, social at 1, research at 0.1, sales tax at 4 and tariffs at 25. I'm no economist, but a government funded by tariffs and vibes is a bold position for a man who also lost 122,444 people to the wars that were happening around him. The sheet doesn't say whether the tax rates were a deliberate experiment or a slider you fell asleep on, so call it my suspicion that nobody was steering.

**The call that sank you**

In January 1800, at 41% approval, the game asked whether you'd seek another term. Your Chief of Staff said to announce for re-election, plain and simple. You picked "Announce early with a war chest" to scare off rivals instead. No rival stepped forward anyway, and the caucus renominated you without dissent, so the war chest scared off nobody who existed. Then at the Labor Day fork you went negative against Jefferson, and you finished at 38.6% when it counted. You spent the campaign punching a guy who was winning.

A sane President would have known that a 41% approval rating doesn't need a negative campaign, it needs a reason.

There's no earlier session in front of me to compare this against, so I can't tell you whether this is a slump or just who you are. The game ended cleanly, Jefferson got the keys on Mar 4, 1801, and you spent the lame-duck months watching Spain claim Guinea. K'oyacyi, Mr. President. It's Mando'a for "come back safely," and I'd say you did, minus the presidency.
