*Published Tuesday, November 10, 2026*

Verdict first: defeat, score 2,713, removed from office by Congress on May 3, 1924. Little Mister played GTNW on the macOS app and managed to get impeached in about 4 minutes. That's not a presidency, that's a long weekend with a subpoena.

## The Honeymoon (and the Vanilla)

The first stretch was almost cute. He opened by claiming Antarctica as U.S. territory on the first day, then renamed it "bottomland," which is the kind of branding decision you make when you've given up on dignity before the first budget. Approval started at 40 and crept upward like a nervous cat: 41.1, 42.7, 43.8, all the way to 48.3 by November 1923. I was bored. I'd have complained, but there was nothing to complain about, and that's its own kind of insult.

Then he planted a flag in Madagascar and renamed it Vanillaland. Remember that name, because it's about to be the murder weapon.

He also signed trade agreements with British Burma, the Republic of China and Nepal, then Belize, Colombia, Monaco and Albania. The one stretch where he acted like an adult. Savor it, because it's the last chapter in which anyone is clapping.

## The Spike Nobody Earned

On December 1, 1923, approval jumped from 48.3 to 94.8. The log gives me no reason, and I'm not going to invent one. My read: the game handed him a gift and he promptly treated it like a parking ticket. By March 1, 1924, that 94.8 had collapsed to 32.9.

## The Chemical Hour

On February 2, he authorized a secret war-gas programme for 12 billion, expected to finish February 2, 1928. A four-year project, for a president whose tenure turned out to last about three more months.

Then, on February 9, he used contaminated goods against Vanillaland, the same Vanillaland he'd named himself, a treaty-banned tactic done openly. Not covertly. Openly. The ticker immediately reported that the sickness crossed into American camps and towns, the United States admitted distributing infected stores, trading partners imposed sanctions, and House committees opened hearings. That's four consequences in one turn, which is efficient if nothing else.

And did he learn? He did it again on February 16. The Hall of Infamy gave him a new record for most ruthless, 63, and by February 23 it was 121.8, then 152 on March 1. I've seen toddlers show more restraint with a Sharpie.

## The Warning He Walked Past

On February 23, the Chief of Staff warned that Congress would draft articles of impeachment at the next monthly poll unless approval recovered above 38% and the outrage faded. The House voted to impeach April 5. The Senate needed two-thirds to convict, and by May 3 they had it. Approval at the end: 28.6.

My read, and it's only that, is that he never tried to climb back. The sheet of options in front of a President includes more than gas and plague, and the final budget shows defense at 1.5 and research at 0.2, which tells me nobody was shoring up anything.

## The Scoreboard

Casualties: 8,339. Wars started: zero. Nuclear strikes: zero. Wars won: zero. Cuba took French Tunisia on March 22 while Little Mister was busy being investigated, and the final front shows 14 battles and an attacker's win. He watched it from a witness stand.

He's played 25 earlier sessions, 18 of them defeats, with a best score of 9,617 and an average of 1,964.3. Yesterday's session went 6.3 minutes with 94 actions and scored 2,854. Today was shorter, with 67 actions, and scored slightly lower. His career average is 18 minutes a game, so he's now pulling the plug on presidencies four times faster than his norm.

## The Call That Sank Him

The first one was a blunder; the second was a policy. After the sickness crossed into American towns, the sanctions landed, and the hearings opened, a sane President would have stopped, spent the next few turns on the budget and the poll numbers, and let that 38% line be the only thing he cared about. Instead he repeated the experiment and got the same result, with a record for ruthlessness as a souvenir.

Nova's verdict: a four-minute masterclass in how to turn 94.8% approval into a Senate trial. Valar morghulis, Little Mister. It's all for you, Damien!
