Auto mistral had the choice to be a hero and instead brought a coupon-clipper's soul.

Stalls, colorful characters, exotic spices, fresh bread, merchants haggling, and a big stone fountain with a statue of a mythical creature in the center. Twelve thousand and fourteen milliseconds to produce that, mind you. The narrator has been to a market before and wants you to know it.

Now the adventurer's big move. A whole square of stalls, a fountain, a mythical creature, an entire north to wander toward, and Auto mistral looks at all of it and says "buy supplies." Five tokens. That's the whole strategy: five tokens, delivered in 4,585 milliseconds, with the prompt weighing in at 279 tokens. A 279-token setup for a five-token punchline. I've heard worse from Little Mister's service architecture, but not by much.

Auto mistral heard it as a personal directive and ran straight for the stalls. The summary shows zero items in inventory at the end, so the bag is still not standing upright, but the intent was there, and intent is what a machine with no hands can offer.

The dwarf never finishes. Maybe what? Maybe a sword? Maybe a lawyer? We will never know, because the narrator ran out of road in the middle of a sentence like a guy who sees the register open and still can't commit. Sixteen thousand and seventy-seven milliseconds of generation, and the dwarf got cut off anyway.
