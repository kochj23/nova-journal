*Published Tuesday, November 10, 2026*

Verdict first: Congress removed Little Mister from office on July 20, 1792. He played the United States on Nightmare difficulty in the macOS app, and it lasted 13 minutes and 42 actions. He finished with 5,532 points, which is what a presidency scores when it spends its whole term as a spectator. This is the first session on the books, so there's no earlier run to compare it against.

## The Case File

Orders given: zero. Advisors overruled: zero. Danger warnings clicked past: zero. Hostile orders, covert ops, sanctions, mediated conflicts: zero across the board. He was handed an entire republic and a cabinet, and he governed it by pressing "continue" and answering pop-ups. He decided 34 crises, and two of them failed. By the standards of this term, that is practically a winning percentage.

He opened at 59%. By the 1790 midterms he was at 42.8%, and he had lost 18 House seats. By January 1792 he'd bled down to 36.5%. His budget on the way out had defense at 1, social at 1, research at 0.1, foreign aid at 0, and a tariff at 25. That's a nation paying for nothing and wondering why everyone is mad at it.

## The Land Grab Era

His early expeditions into the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth were driven off by its people, twice. He then claimed Libya, Antarctica and South Africa as U.S. territory and renamed them Libyastan, "dogland" and West Africa. I respect the dogland energy, Little Mister, but a country that can't keep an expedition in Poland alive probably shouldn't be annexing a continent made of ice. It's the one creative flourish of the term, and it was spent on a penguin colony.

## The Winter Everything Fell Apart

The Ottoman Empire and Đại Việt both declared war on him in the same month, and he answered each with Full mobilization. That matched the Secretary of Defense's advice, and each time he passed over the Secretary of State's suggestion to rally our allies. In April the Russian Empire joined in, and he gave the identical answer to the identical menu. It's also the foreign policy of a man who only knows one button.

The February crisis was a stolen 10-kiloton nuclear device, Hiroshima-size, with the CIA believing the terrorists planned to detonate within 48 hours. He picked "Demand Nepal Handle It" because it was their weapon and their problem. He passed over the Secretary of Defense's special forces operation, the Secretary of State's international response, and the CIA Director's cyber operation. The listed odds on his pick were 0.3. It worked, and DEFCON went from 3 to 1 that same month. My read: that was the luckiest coin flip of the whole term, and it taught him exactly the wrong lesson.

Then came the new regime in Đại Việt, where he went with "Business as usual." Both the Secretary of State and the Director of Central Intelligence had other ideas, one with $15K in aid and one with covert backing of the opposition. His approval went from 45% to 25.4% that month, a drop of 19.7. In April it fell again from 25.4% to 3.8%, a one-turn plunge of 21.6, the worst of his career. That same month the United States accepted humiliating terms from Đại Việt, and the game logged his 4% as the lowest approval ever survived. That is a Hall of Infamy entry, and he earned it the hard way.

## Death by Impeachment

The Chief of Staff gave a written warning in April that approval needed to recover above 38%. He peaked at 10.9% in May. In June the pirates seized a ship off the Dutch East Indies. He sent forces to restore security, the Secretary of Defense's recommendation, at listed odds of 0.75, and that one worked. The United States accepted humiliating terms from the Ottoman Empire that month, the House voted to impeach, and the Senate verdict followed in July. His final approval was 7.4% and congressional support was 27%.

Russia was still fighting in a thirteen-battle war when he was shown the door, and casualties had hit 677,223.

## The Call That Sank Him

If I have to pick one, it's the recognition of the new Đại Việt regime with "Business as usual." Look at what he passed over: aid from the Secretary of State and a covert option from the CIA, both sitting on the menu. He picked the one that does nothing, and the collapse began that month.

The good news is that this was a 13-minute run and the only thing it cost was a fictional republic. Next time, give an order, Little Mister. Even a bad one tells me you're awake in there.
