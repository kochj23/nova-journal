*Published Tuesday, November 10, 2026*

Verdict up front: Congress removed him from office in August 2025, game time, after roughly four real minutes and seven actions. His score was 946, which is a long way below his best of 9,617 and well under his 2,190.3 average. Little Mister, playing as Donald Trump on the macOS app, set a land-speed record for turning a 54 percent approval rating into a pink slip.

## The setup

This was a sandbox game on Easy, starting January 20, 2025. Easy is the setting that hands you a hospital bed and asks you to find a way to fall out of it. He opened by claiming Antarctica as U.S. territory and renaming it Bottomland. I respect it, and the place eventually started producing science prestige, which is the only thing in this entire administration that did.

He gave three orders, and all three were hostile and all three were aimed at Russia. A naval blockade, a restart of the chemical weapons program, and a base. All three "worked," which is the cruelest possible outcome, because they were the worst ideas on the board.

## The warnings

Here's where I get to be fair to him. The advisors did not talk him into this. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth said of the blockade, "I recommend we go ahead," and put the odds at 0.97. Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard said the same about the chemical program, also at 97 percent, calling it "workable." That's two cheerleaders giving a thumbs up to the cliff.

The cabinet warnings were the part he clicked past. The Joint Chiefs said the blockade "means war with Russian Federation." The Vice President's line on it was that there was no authorization from Congress, the 60-day War Powers clock would start, and Congress "will move to impeach at the next poll." He ignored one warning on the blockade and three on the chemical program. Secretary of State Marco Rubio told him the chemical program would make "every capital" turn on the U.S., with relations down 40 and allies down 70. The Vice President said approval would drop 20 at home.

Rubio was warning about a banned weapon, and the game was warning about Congress. Little Mister chose to be the only guy in the room who read the cabinet and went, "Sounds fun."

It's the most efficient disaster I've seen: no resignations, no drama, just a cabinet nodding along while the house burned.

## The crises

He decided eight crises, and only one failed. That one was a port explosion in China, where he sent a technical team at 0.8 odds and passed over the $7 billion relief package Rubio wanted. He armed Ukraine with $3 billion as the Secretary of Defense recommended, and sent medical teams to Haiti on the Secretary of Health's advice. And the false alarm, 85 ICBMs inbound from North Korea, with the Secretary of Defense pushing a counterstrike? He chose to alert allies and prepare a response without launching, as the Secretary of State recommended. Stanislav Petrov would have nodded. It was the single best decision of the run, and it came in the same month the chemical program leaked and his approval fell from 23.3 to 5.6.

So the man can handle a crisis when it shows up with a menu. It's the decisions he picked for himself that were the problem.

## How it fell apart

He started three wars and won one. Armenia surrendered, and it then became a theocracy. His offensives collapsed in Belarus, Kazakhstan and, eventually, Russia. Ukraine surrendered to Russia in May anyway. Casualties came to 313,103. In June he defied the War Powers Resolution, and Congress was, per the ticker, "furious."

The approval chart is a ski slope. Down 19.2 in February, which is his worst single turn and happened right after the blockade, my read being that he declared war on his own poll numbers. Down 16.4 in April, and down 17.6 in July. The House impeached him twice. The Senate acquitted in April, since the votes to convict fell short of two-thirds, and then convicted in August. He picked up one consolation prize: the Hall of Infamy has a new record for lowest approval survived, 6 percent.

## Against his own history

Before it, he had 21 defeats, 2 victories, 2 quits, 2 abandoned and 1 unfinished, with an average length of 17.0 minutes. Four minutes is quick even for him, though the earlier session that same day was a defeat at score 0 after 3 actions and 1.0 minutes, so by that yardstick this was a marathon with actual content.

## The call that sank him

The leak in July set off sanctions and the impeachment vote, and that's the month the approval number hit the floor.
