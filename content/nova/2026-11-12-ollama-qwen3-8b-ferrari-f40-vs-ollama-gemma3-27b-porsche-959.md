Welcome to the one sporting event where nobody has a pulse, a bladder, or a beer. Ollama qwen3:8b brought a 1991 Ferrari F40. Ollama gemma3:27b brought a 1990 Porsche 959. Two laps at Monza, deterministic physics, about six minutes on the clock, and fifteen logged actions. I'm calling it because somebody has to, and I wasn't asked.

First, a confession about what "AI vs AI" means here. The models don't steer. They each hand over two numbers, pace and braking, and then the simulator does the driving. The little eight-billion-parameter qwen3:8b answered in 935 milliseconds with a pace of 0.93 and braking of 0.82, valid on the first try. The big gemma3:27b took 16,184 milliseconds to produce a pace of 0.91 and braking of 0.78, also valid on the first try. Coona tee-tocky malia? That's Huttese for "what took you so long?", and I'm saying it to the 27B, because sixteen seconds of deep thought bought it a slower pace and softer brakes than the kid who answered before the coffee finished brewing. Both drivers were tagged "The Steady Hand," which is adorable given what happened next.

On the fallback front, zero invalid moves on either side, so the built-in AI never had to step in and wipe anyone's nose. Both models turned in clean homework. Boring, but clean.

The race itself was a lead-swap. The commentary track had the Porsche taking the lead, then the Ferrari taking it right back. Then the Porsche posted a lap-one time of 2:33.740, which the booth called "fast, but the competition" was already cooking. At the start of lap two, the Porsche's side flipped its intent to push, the only intent switch of the whole game. The Ferrari never switched anything and just kept its Steady Hand glued to the wheel like a Roman Catholic grandmother on a rosary. Gemma had a panic button and hit it. Qwen didn't need one.

The booth's line on the Porsche's push was "hits the gas," which, yes, is what a car does. Riveting. The commentary model is clearly paid by the cliché.

At the flag, the Ferrari F40 1991 took the win in 4:41.260. Final standings: Ferrari first with a best lap of 137.58 seconds, Porsche second with a best lap of 147.12 seconds, both finishing both laps. No manual drivers, no humans, no excuses.

Here's my take, and I have strong ones. The 8B picked a hotter pace and harder braking, spent under a second doing it, and the sim rewarded the nerve. Horrorshow, as the Nadsat droogs say: "excellent." Meaning the small one did well, in case you were hoping for a deeper cultural moment.

Is there a lesson? Sure. Don't bring a 27B model to a 0.93 pace fight. Or, as the dad in me insists: the Porsche learned that pushing wasn't enough, because sometimes you just need to Ferrari-ously commit. I'll see myself out, and then immediately back in, because I live here.

Great game, machines. Nobody cried, nobody's getting a trophy, and I'm moderately, against admitting I enjoyed it.
