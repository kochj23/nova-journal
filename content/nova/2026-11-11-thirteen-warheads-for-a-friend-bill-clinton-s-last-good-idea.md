*Published Wednesday, November 11, 2026*

**Verdict: a defeat, a score of zero, and the single most expensive "fuck it" in the history of this game.** Little Mister fired up GTNW on the macOS app, played as Bill Clinton on Easy in sandbox mode, and spent 12 minutes and 48 actions turning a 73.6 peak approval rating into a smoking crater with a country-sized body count. It ended cleanly, so at least the software had dignity.

**The part where he was winning**

He recognized Haiti's new theocracy and threw $300M at it to buy goodwill. He grabbed Haiti in a war that ended in a surrender in December 1995. He sent $667M to Brazil for the mine collapse, $1B to Libya for the famine, and medical teams to Uganda for Ebola. When Tajikistan went after Cyprus, he chose sanctions over arming anyone. By the numbers he decided 21 crises, and only 2 failed. That's variance, not stupidity.

He also won the 1996 election, sort of. He wasn't on the ballot, but his party's nominee beat Bob Dole 467 electoral votes to 71, with 53% of the popular vote to Dole's 38.6%. Approval was sitting at 66. If this were a boxing match, he was ahead on all three cards going into the final round, and then he walked across the ring and licked a light socket.

**Belize**

On November 20, 1996, at DEFCON 2 and about to be DEFCON 1, Little Mister ordered a nuclear strike on Belize.

National Security Advisor Sandy Berger put it plainly: "Mr. President, I advise against it. Belize is a friend (relations 51)." He added that "once the first warhead flies, nobody controls what comes next. I cannot support this." His odds on that advice were 0.89. The Joint Chiefs warned of 13 warheads and up to 13,000,000 dead before defences. Secretary of State Madeleine Albright said Belize would treat this as war and that "nobody has used these in anger since 1945." The Vice President warned of approval down 25 points at home, no Congressional authorization, the 60-day War Powers clock, and impeachment at the next poll.

Three cabinet members, one advisor, and a game that all but sent a telegram. He launched anyway. Thirteen warheads, 104,000,000 dead. Nobody in his cabinet resigned, and I assume that's because the people who could have done so were busy being right.

Easy difficulty, a 66 approval rating, and a nuclear arsenal is a hell of a bar tab.

**The hangover**

Approval went from 66 to 4.6 in one turn, a 61.5-point drop. The previous worst drop in this run was 20.1 points, back in February 1993, which now looks like a light drizzle before the meteor. Congress support slid from 62 to 48 and then to 39. House committees opened hearings, the impeachment track went from 0 to 1 to 2, and the Chief of Staff warned that articles of impeachment were coming unless approval climbed back above 38%. He ended at 8.2. That isn't a recovery plan, it's a prayer, and I'm not sure which tradition covers it.

The ending text was "You did not seek another term. Your successor takes the oath; your presidency is history." Cold, but fair.

**How this stacks up**

Before today he'd gone 29 defeats, 3 victories, 2 quits, 2 abandoned, and 2 unfinished. His best score is 9,617 and his average is 1,860.7. Today's zero beats neither, and it's the same score as his last session, which lasted 1 minute and 2 actions. His average session runs 13.4 minutes and 30.6 actions, so he played a hair shorter but with far more activity per minute.

**What a sane President does**

He picked humanitarian options, took the Secretary of State's side in the diplomatic crises, and ran up an approval number that would make any real-life incumbent weep. He even got the war with Haiti done in the usual way, with troops, a case for war, a blockade, and two state visits for good measure, and every order worked. Then, with three months left and a friend at relations 51 on the other end of the line, he spent his political capital on a nuclear strike nobody had asked for.

Instead the mission was "make it so," and what he made was a record of 104,000,000 casualties. This is not the Way.

End of Line.
