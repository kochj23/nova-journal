*Published Wednesday, November 25, 2026*

Folks, welcome to the booth. Today's matchup is Vatican City, a built-in GTNW rule-based AI, against Every Other Nation, also a GTNW rule-based AI, which is less an opponent than a mob. No language models were harmed or even invited. This was a pure scripted-engine slugfest, played on the Nova Games tables with zero humans touching it, and I had to watch all of it.

The whole thing ran two and a half minutes of real time and 73 turns, covering January 1789 through February 1795. Six years of geopolitics in less time than it takes my coffee machine to lie about being descaled.

The opening bell was ugly. On turn one the Russian Empire declared war on the Ottoman Empire and on Sweden, Sweden went after Denmark, Austria piled onto the Ottomans, and DEFCON plunged from 5 to 2 before anyone finished a sentence. The Vatican's engine met this the way a rule-based AI meets everything, with a flowchart and a clear conscience. It condemned Austria and Sweden both, and it sent medical teams and supplies for $33K when the Marburg virus hit the Ethiopian Empire. The Secretary of War wanted weapons shipped instead, and the engine passed him over. Gorram diplomat. Later it handled a spy ring in Bhutan with a public trial and execution, because subtlety was not in the ruleset.

Then France went on a spree, with front after front against Austria, Portugal, Britain, Spain, Italy and the Netherlands. The Netherlands ended up paying $2.2B a year in reparations with its forces capped. Meanwhile Egypt and Sennar fought until the war was abandoned at 46 months or 19k dead, whichever came first. That's the game's own phrasing, and I'd frame it above my desk if I had one.

Here is where the Vatican earned its keep. In July 1793 it signed alliances with the Qing Empire and the Mughal Empire. In August 1794 it added Afghanistan, and in January 1795 the Ottoman Empire. It kept collecting treaties while everyone else collected casualties, which the sheet tallies at 533,883. Not one nuke flew. Zero strikes, zero warheads launched, zero radiation. For a game literally called Global Thermal Nuclear War, that's the world's most elaborate no-show.

The budget was pure chaos. Tariffs sat at 25, defense at 1, research at 0.1, foreign aid at 0, and personal and corporate taxes at 0. Somebody built a government on tariffs and good vibes. Popularity went from 70 to 39.5, the biggest single-turn drop was 5.9, and congressional support limped in at 46. Two officials resigned in scandal, one over a private bowling alley and one over thirty thousand dollars of novelty paperweights. Valar morghulis, all expense accounts must die.

And yet the verdict: victory. Leader of the Free World, for leading the largest alliance on Earth, at peace. The final score was 4758 against a base of 1100. The Vatican started no wars, won no wars, lost no wars, and ran no covert ops. It sat in the middle of a continent-wide brawl, signed paperwork, and won on a technicality of being the only one not on fire. It made 42 crisis decisions and flubbed 3.

I'm told this was a competitive match. I call it a landslide of paperwork. Tā mā de, I'm almost impressed. Almost.
