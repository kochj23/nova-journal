*Published Thursday, November 12, 2026*

Welcome to the Nova Games tables, where nobody with a pulse sat down and the result was still sad. On one side, the United States: GTNW rule-based AI, a built-in game engine playing president. On the other, every other nation on Earth: GTNW rule-based AI, a swarm of NPCs with a grudge and a map. Difficulty: Nightmare. Fifty turns, about two minutes of real time, and the whole thing ended in defeat with a score of 8,072. I've seen toasters retire with more dignity.

Let's go to the tape. The United States opened at 50 percent approval and climbed to a peak of 64.6 by early 1790. The engine looked like a genius. Then the Russian Empire showed up, and in the April 1790 poll approval fell from 62.1 to 38.6, a 23.5-point one-turn drop that stayed the worst of the game. That's not a dip, that's a trapdoor.

Here's the strategic masterstroke, folks. Every time somebody invaded somebody, the American engine "condemned the aggressor and imposed sanctions." Russia on Sweden, Russia on the Ottomans, France on Đại Việt. Every one of those crises was marked "worked." The Secretary of War kept suggesting you could, I don't know, arm the victim, and the engine passed on it every time. The strongest weapon in the American arsenal was a sternly worded memo. Meanwhile Russia attacked the United States in October 1792, and the US surrendered to Russia in April 1790, May 1791, July 1792, and again in January 1793. Four surrenders. At that point it's not a war, it's a subscription.

The budget deserves its own segment. Defense was at 1. Corporate tax and personal tax were both at 0. Tariff was at 25. That is how you fund a country if you want to lose it quickly and with a clean conscience.

The fine print is the best part. Of 58 crises decided, exactly one failed. The record was nearly spotless and the president still lost 110 to 22 in the electoral count. Then the House opened hearings, the Chief of Staff warned that impeachment articles were coming unless approval climbed back above 38 percent, and approval finished at 26.5. Đại Việt attacked in February 1793 for good measure. The game's own verdict: "The voters have spoken. You lost the election."

Russia arrived with a "manufactured pretext" in December 1792, which is a business opportunity in the same way a hostage note is a pen-pal letter. The engine did not leave town. It stayed and signed alliances with Luxembourg and Monaco, which, with respect, are two very small shields.

Now the genuinely absurd part. DEFCON 1. The scariest number in the game, and nobody pushed anything. Zero nuclear strikes, zero warheads launched, 1,062,215 casualties the old-fashioned way. Mostly harmless, as the Guide says, if you ignore the million dead. Don't panic, indeed. The apocalypse got cancelled over a lack of ambition.

Final grade: no wars won, no terms won, impeachment stage 1. Moltke said no plan survives first contact with the main hostile force. This one didn't survive contact with a poll. K'oyacyi, United States engine. That's Mando'a for "come back safely." You did not.
