*Published Wednesday, November 25, 2026*

Little Mister played the macOS app today and ran a presidency that went 3-0 in wars, never touched a nuke, and still ended with Congress shoving him out the door. Playing as Jimmy Carter, he lasted from Jan 20, 1977 to Dec 20, 1978, which is 23 turns and 23 actions in about 23 minutes, and finished in defeat with a score of 7376. So he's a mediocre president and a surprisingly decent mugger. Congratulations on the personal best in the category of "winning while losing."

**The Resume Was Fine, Right Up Until It Wasn't**

He sent troops to back Iran, Israel surrendered in July 1977, and by August he'd annexed it as a U.S. territory. Approval climbed from 40 at the start to a peak of 58. Then August 1977 arrived, DEFCON went from 2 to 1, and approval dropped from 58 to 40.9, his worst single turn at 17.1 points. The game blames "other events," which is a polite way of saying nobody has the receipts. My read is that someone was playing with matches near the big red button and then acted surprised when the room got warm.

**The One Order That Cost Him the Building**

In January 1978 he gave exactly one order of the whole session: a Ground Invasion of Japan, over the objection of Secretary of Defense Harold Brown. Brown's advice was as clear as it gets: "Mr. President, I advise against it. We have done this before: the war on Israel was won in 1 months in 1977. It worked then, and the room likes what it knows. Turning on them now contradicts everything our policy has built."

Little Mister got a full chorus of warnings and treated it like a coupon he'd already used. The Joint Chiefs said opening odds were 71% with 769K troops, "a war of weeks, with casualties on both sides." Secretary of State Cyrus Vance said Japan would treat this as war. The Vice President delivered the line that should have been engraved on the Resolute Desk: "No authorization from Congress: the 60-day War Powers clock starts. Impeachment talk is rising on the Hill."

The invasion was carried out, approval slid from 45.4 to 43.3, and impeachment stage 1 showed up in April. He did take Japan in June, with terms of $323.8B a year in reparations. Neither does the House Judiciary Committee, and unlike opportunity, it brings subpoenas.

**The Rose Garden Doctrine**

Between invasions he had fifteen crises to decide, and his foreign-aid philosophy was "Send condolences only," each time with the in-game note "A statement from the Rose Garden." He used it for border clashes in Thailand, wildfires in South Korea, a mass exodus from Libya, and cholera in Zambia. In Zambia the Secretary of Health had a $125M medical-teams option on the table, and he took the strongly worded card instead. Credit where due: he evacuated Americans from Haiti, which the Secretary of State had recommended, and sent forces in the China hostage crisis at 75% odds, which the Secretary of Defense had recommended.

**The Slow Motion Impeachment**

The House impeached in July, the Senate acquitted in August, and a humbled president celebrated by renaming the United States "Daddy." I'm not going to analyze that. In September he hit the midterms and chose "Stay above the fray," and his party lost 41 House seats, leaving congressional support at 21%. Then the "A Shellacking" crisis came up and he picked "Veto everything." Support dropped from 42 to 27, which is what happens when you answer a hostile Congress with a pen and a grudge. The House impeached him again in November, the Senate convicted in December, and the game announced that Congress had removed him from office.

His approval at the end was 41.4, above the 38% line the Chief of Staff kept waving around. Curse your sudden but inevitable betrayal.

**What I'd Have Done**

A sane President leaves Japan alone, keeps the winning Israel streak, and spends the saved political capital on the Zambia medical teams instead of a statement from the Rose Garden. He'd have stayed in office, kept the 58 approval, and probably beaten that 16395.

Little Mister, the war room wasn't the problem. The Hill was.
