*Published Tuesday, November 10, 2026*

**Verdict: removed from office by Congress on August 20, 2025, in-game, after a presidency that lasted roughly four minutes of real time.** Little Mister played GTNW on the macOS app, as the United States, in sandbox on Easy difficulty. The final score was 946, which is about what you'd expect for a man who sprinted the full lap from 54 percent approval to 7.9 percent in seven turns.

I don't have any earlier GTNW sessions to measure him against, so we'll judge this one on its own merits.

## The Antarctica Opening

Turn zero, before anything else, he claimed Antarctica as U.S. territory and renamed it Bottomland. The claim violated the Antarctic Treaty, and the signatories protested. Trading partners slapped sanctions on him and House committees opened hearings, all before he'd done a single thing a normal President would call governing. By the end, Bottomland was producing science prestige, which makes it the only part of this administration that turned a profit.

## The Order That Ate the Presidency

In February he ordered a naval blockade of Russia. Defense Secretary Hegseth gave it a 97 percent, said go ahead, then added the sentence that should have been engraved on the Oval Office desk: "Their forces are nearly a match for ours. This would not be a short campaign." The Joint Chiefs told him, "This means war with Russian Federation." The VP warned that with no authorization from Congress the 60-day War Powers clock would start, and that Congress would move to impeach. War was declared. Approval fell from 54 to 34.8 in a single turn, the biggest drop of his career at 19.2 points, and DEFCON went from 5 to 3.

Then he doubled down, because apparently one regime-scale mistake leaves you lonely. He restarted the chemical weapons program, ignoring three more danger warnings. Rubio told him it was banned by the Chemical Weapons Convention and that "every capital will turn on us: relations −40, allies −70, sanctions." The VP put it at minus 20 approval at home. The program was leaked in July, sanctions followed, and the approval line went through the floor, down 17.6 points to 5.6.

Of three wars started, he won one. Armenia surrendered. The Belarus and Kazakhstan offensives collapsed. Russia won all 21 battles on that front, and he ended up withdrawing from there too. Total casualties came to 313,103. In June he defied the War Powers Resolution, and Congress, in the game's own words, was furious. The catch was that the Pentagon's 97 percent was only the odds of the order going through, not the odds of it working out.

## The Part Where He Was Actually Good

Here's the infuriating thing, Little Mister. Of eight crisis calls, seven worked, and some were excellent. Facing a false alarm showing 85 ICBMs inbound from North Korea, he chose "Alert Allies, Prepare Response" over the immediate counterstrike Defense wanted. Stanislav Petrov would have nodded. The Haiti virus, the South African mine spill, and the new governments in Armenia and Ukraine all got sensible responses. The Armenian unrest got a covert operation at 60 percent odds, which paid off even though he skipped three advisors' preferences.

State had suggested a $7 billion relief package and Health had suggested a $3 billion medical one. He picked the cheap option, and I suspect that's the saddest line in the whole report. He backed the Ukraine arms package Defense recommended, and Ukraine surrendered to Russia in May anyway.

## The Impeachment Two-Step

In March the House impeached him, and in April the Senate acquitted him, with the votes to convict falling short of two-thirds. In July the House impeached him again, with approval at 5.6 percent, a new record for lowest approval survived. The ticker even put it in the Hall of Infamy. In August the Senate convicted him. Impeachment stage went from 0 to 4 over seven polls, and the Senate gave him one reprieve for his trouble.

## What a Sane President Would Have Done

Everything bad followed from it: the war, the clock, the impeachment, the chemical program he panicked into.

Moltke said it best: "No plan of operations extends with any certainty beyond the first contact with the main hostile force." Little Mister's plan didn't survive contact with the first warning label.

Next time, read the warning label, Little Mister. It's free, and it's the only part of this job that was.
