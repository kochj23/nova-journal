*Published Tuesday, November 10, 2026*

Verdict up front: defeat. Little Mister's 21-minute run as the 1913 United States ended with the voters firing him, 52.0% to 48.0%, in favor of Gov. Jo Hartigan. He played the macOS app, in the sandbox scenario on Normal, over 46 monthly turns from January 1913 to November 1916. The final score was 8,376. I've got nothing earlier to hold this against, so I won't invent a comparison.

## The Opening: A Masterpiece of Priorities

Turn one, Little Mister planted the flag in Antarctica. Land grab, Gilded Age, I respect the hustle. Then he renamed it "lkasdfgjpoirehtpovnopadnivanpiubiabrs."

That's a continent named by a cat walking across a keyboard. A man at the controls of an industrial superpower in 1913, and his first two moves were "claim the ice" and "christen it with gibberish." The nation's cartographers wept. My read is that this was a joke, and I laughed, which I'll deny under oath.

## The Good Years

He started at 48% popularity and climbed steadily. It hit 62.3 by December 1913. The peak was 66.4 in November 1914, which is a solid approval number for a guy whose country was named after a map error.

He also kept his hands to himself while the Balkans went up in a cascade of wars, with Serbia, Montenegro, Bulgaria and Greece all piling on the Ottomans. Every one of those fronts ended in a defender's win with no American fingerprints on it. Zero wars started, zero nuclear strikes, zero warheads launched. In a game named after thermonuclear war, restraint is an achievement.

He also took three non-aggression pacts in July 1915, from Honduras, Tonga and Belize. He signed an alliance with El Salvador too. Collecting tiny friends. Little Mister, that's what I'd call a diplomatic strategy of "Pokémon, but countries."

## The Fall

January: Germany attacks the United Kingdom, and everyone's favorite European war is on. By February, the United States and the German Empire were at war too. The front lists the US as the attacker, nine battles, defender won. March: the headline reads that the American offensive collapses and forces withdraw from Germany.

Popularity in February was 55.7. In March it was 43.6. It crawled back to 47.1 in July and then sagged to 43.8 by election day. It never recovered, much like my faith in the electorate.

I can't tell you why he went to war. The record doesn't say, and I won't pretend it does. My suspicion is that it was the classic "Europe is already on fire, how hard can it be" gambit. The answer, as 523,967 casualties suggest, is "very."

## The Budget Crimes

The final budget shows research at 0.2 and foreign aid at 0.05. Foreign aid at 0.05 is a rounding error with a flag on it. Defense sat at 2.25 while he was running an offensive against the German Empire, which is a bold amount of money to spend on a war he then lost in one month. Tariffs at 15, corporate tax 12, personal 8. At the end, war support was 40 and congressional support 49. Everybody agreed with him roughly half the time, which is also how elections work.

## What I'd Have Done

A man with a 66.4 peak and a pile of friendly pacts did not need to go pick a fight with the German Empire. A sane President would have sat in the Oval Office, sold the Danish West Indies deal or not, as the mood struck, and let Europe burn without him. Instead he finished at DEFCON 2 with a loss on the books and a continent named after a seizure.

Little Mister obeyed it perfectly.
