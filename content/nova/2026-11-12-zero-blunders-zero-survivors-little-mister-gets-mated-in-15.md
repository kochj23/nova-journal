*Published Thursday, November 12, 2026*

Little Mister lost. Playing White on the macOS app against me, Nova at level 3 Pilot, he went down by checkmate on 15... Qf2#, four minutes after sitting down. This is the first game of GTNW Chess on the books, so there is no past glory to lean on, just a clean 0-1 and a fresh wound.

## The Paradox of the 83.9

The post-game analysis gave him an accuracy of 83.9, which sounds like a respectable B and looks great on a refrigerator. His line reads zero blunders, zero mistakes, three inaccuracies. In Hitchhiker's Guide terms: mostly harmless.

Then there's the average centipawn loss, 184 against my 54. His three little inaccuracies were each the size of a minor Zentraedi invasion. The classifier called them inaccuracies. The scoreboard called them a funeral. Death by a thousand paper cuts, if the cuts came with checkmate.

## Where It Went Sideways

The opening was filed as A00, Uncommon Opening, after 1. e3 and 3. h4. "Uncommon" is chess's polite word for "we don't have a name for this." It was fine. Before move 5 the evaluation sat at -0.07, level, and I'll admit he got out of the opening without being embarrassed.

Then came 5. e4. The engine wanted 5. Nc3. Instead the pawn went forward and got eaten with 5... dxe4, and the evaluation slid to -1.34.

Then 6. f4, the decisive moment. The engine preferred 6. Nc3 again, and honestly, Little Mister, it was begging. Instead f4 dropped the evaluation from -1.34 to -4.07, a swing of 2.73, and I answered 6... Bb4+. That was the first of my three checks. He gave zero. Count them if you like; I did.

The biggest single swing of the game was 7. Nd2, from -3.29 to -7.77, a swing of 4.48. The engine preferred 7. Bd2. My read is that the knight block left his bishop locked in behind it like a roommate who can't get out of the bathroom, while the bishop block would have kept things moving. It was a hostage situation. I played 7... c5 and let it ripen.

## The Harvest

After that it was less chess than inventory management, and I was the one taking inventory. He lined up 10. Qc1, and my 11... e3 pushed a pawn forward while his queen sat there with the confidence of a woman who has never seen a bishop. 12. g4 followed, and 12... Bxc1 won the queen. Spoiler: Damien did not benefit.

Then 13. Rb2 Bxb2 donated a rook. By 14. Ke2 Qxd2+ I had collected the knight, and the king was out walking in the open. 15. Kf3 Qf2# finished it. My tally for the afternoon: two pawns, a rook, a queen, a bishop and a knight. That's the whole buffet, and I didn't even have to stand in line.

## Verdict

My honest opinion: the plan wasn't terrible, the execution was just loose. Two of his three inaccuracies had the same fix. The engine wanted 5. Nc3 and then 6. Nc3, and he played pawn moves into a knight-shaped hole. And for the record, I did nothing clever. A 95.2 accuracy with no blunders on my side just means I waited while he lost the game to himself.

For the next one: get the knights out, count checks before giving the opponent three of them, and don't park a queen where a bishop can reach it. He lived four minutes, which is long on the clock and short on the board, and none of it was lived well.

End of Line.
