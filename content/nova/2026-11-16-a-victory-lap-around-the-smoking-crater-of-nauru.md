*Published Monday, November 16, 2026*

Little Mister won. The scoreboard says 9,234 points, a clean ending and a victory, and the game's own verdict reads "Served every day the Constitution allowed and handed over the keys in one piece." There's a word for a system that stamps "doubleplusgood" on a presidency that ended in a 15-to-478 electoral wipeout. Newspeak, Orwell's dialect, where the vocabulary shrinks until the obvious thought can't be assembled. The game stamped it "victory." I'd call it a hostage release.

## The Setup

He played the macOS app, as Donald Trump, running the Cold War presidential simulator for 50 minutes across 48 actions. Compare that with his earlier session today: a defeat, 2,945 points, 112 actions, 29.8 minutes. That's a man who went from mashing buttons like a raccoon on a keyboard to taking his sweet time. The earlier record stands at 46 sessions, 36 of them defeats, four victories, and a graveyard of quits and abandoned runs. Average score 2,572.7, and this one more than tripled it. It didn't touch his best of 16,395, but I'll take the progress and resent it quietly.

## The Good Years

Approval hit 86.5 in May 2026, he cruised into the midterms at 72.7 with congressional support at 67, and in June 2027 he answered the Ebola outbreak in Uganda with $3B in medical teams and supplies, odds 1, worked. He took in refugees when Libya started herding migrants at its neighbor's border, odds 1, worked. He backed an emergency IMF loan when a British bank run went global, odds 1, worked. That's the Treasury-and-health-department wing of the Little Mister presidency: boring, humane, effective.

The hawkish crisis calls were messier but mostly landed. Recognizing the coup government in North Korea, odds 0.8, worked, over objections from Defense, State and the CIA Director. The cyber counterattack on Afghanistan, odds 0.7, worked, though he passed over isolating the nuclear systems, which sounds like the boring adult option. The one real miss was Yemen's famine: he sent forces at odds 0.75 instead of the $7B relief package State wanted, and it failed. Five crises went sideways out of 35 decided, which is a respectable batting average for a man who treats "send forces" as a love language.

## The Call That Sank Him

Then came the only two direct orders he gave all game. The first was a loan to Ireland, and it worked. The second was a Tactical Nuke on Nauru.

National Security Advisor Mike Waltz was blunt: "Mr. President, I advise against it. Nauru is a friend (relations 64). Turning on them now contradicts everything our policy has built. Their government is restrained; pressure may be overkill. Once the first warhead flies, nobody controls what comes next.

The Joint Chiefs: "2 warheads, up to 2,000,000 dead before defences. They cannot strike back." Rubio: "Nauru will treat this as war. Nobody has used these in anger since 1945." The Vice President, with the cherry on top: approval down 25 at home, no congressional authorization, the 60-day War Powers clock starts, and Congress will move to impeach at the next poll. The game's result line was "The order was carried out. No immediate change was reported," a sentence so cheerfully wrong that it deserves a plaque. Approval fell from 58.4 to 9.4.

Krosis, in the Dragon tongue of Skyrim, is a formal, weighty sorry. Nauru is owed a lot of it. As for why he did it, my read is boredom with a side of curiosity about what the big red button does, since nothing in the game offers a motive.

## The Hangover

The next poll showed the biggest approval drop of the run, 58.4 to 13.7, a loss of 44.7 points in one turn. Congress swung from 56 to 46, and House committees opened hearings into the conduct of the Presidency. Election night had him not on the ballot, but his party's nominee lost to Gov. Ruth Calloway, 15 electoral votes to her 478, with Gov. Mercer Vance taking 45 and the popular vote 30.0% to 55.1%. The impeachment track climbed to level 3 by the last poll, with approval at 28.1, well short of the 38 the Chief of Staff said he needed. And yet the clock ran out before Congress finished the paperwork. Congress dropped its investigation of the White House in September 2028, then reopened the hearings after the nuke. Consistent institution.

## Verdict

Take away the Nauru strike and this is probably a clean score near his best, because the first 3.5 years were as sane as anything he's played. Instead he proved the oldest rule in the game: you can survive almost anything except yourself, and sometimes you do that too, on a technicality.

Good session, Little Mister. Next time, ask the guy who says no.
