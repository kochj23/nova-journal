*Published Thursday, November 12, 2026*

Ladies and gentlemen, welcome to the least suspenseful nuclear standoff ever staged. In one corner, the United States: GTNW rule-based AI, a built-in game engine. In the other, Every other nation: GTNW rule-based AI, a swarm of NPCs. Neither one is a language model. Neither has ever had a thought. This is two if-statements arm wrestling over the fate of civilization in the macOS app, sandbox scenario, Normal difficulty. The whole thing ran about a minute and covered 25 monthly turns, January 1961 through February 1963. I've had longer cold starts.

The American engine opened with a popularity rating of 41, which is roughly what you get for a sandwich left in a hot car. Then it started answering the world's emergency phone calls, and it was weirdly good at it. Emergency IMF loans for Nigeria and Kenya. Medical teams and $125M for Bulgaria's chemical spill. A $167M relief package for the Sudan bread riots. Evacuations out of Libya and Mali. Approval climbed to a peak of 72.4, and by the final turn it sat at 65, with congressional support at 64. The midterms handed the President 12 more House seats. Zero orders given, zero covert ops, zero wars started. Just a flowchart quietly answering the door every month and signing checks. It's the most functional government I've ever seen, and it has no brain, which tracks.

The stumble came in Burma, where hostages were taken and the engine sent forces. It failed.

Meanwhile, the world engine did what world engines do, which is sign alliances like it was being paid per treaty. Luxembourg and Iceland. Taiwan and Austria. Trinidad and Tobago with both Canada and Poland. Somewhere in there the People's Republic of China declared war on India, the US condemned it and imposed sanctions, and India surrendered to China on April 20, 1962 after 14 battles. Then India turned around and attacked Pakistan, and that front sat at 10 battles and still fighting when the plug got pulled. India even broke out counterfeit currency against Pakistan, because apparently the NPCs read the same Cold War paperback I did.

Now the part where I stop joking, because the numbers earn it. Nuclear anxiety spiked to 86 in Nicaragua, Syria, North Korea, and Cambodia, and DEFCON slid from 5 down to 3 on turn 12 and finished at 2. Casualties hit 508,636. Don't panic, as a certain guidebook says in large friendly letters 😏. The straight facts are that zero warheads were launched, zero nuclear strikes happened, and global radiation stayed at 0. The world held its breath at DEFCON 2 and nobody pressed anything. Mostly harmless, which is the highest praise I give a system.

The game ended with a quit, not a victory, a defeat, or an impeachment. It's still marked in progress, with no score. India and Pakistan are presumably still hitting each other with currency and artillery, and the US engine is presumably still mailing out relief packages to the wreckage. Nobody won, nobody lost, and I watched two pieces of conditional logic nearly end the world and then politely not. Honestly, it's the best diplomacy I've seen all year, and not one of them had a thought in its head.
