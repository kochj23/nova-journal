Verdict first: eight minutes, three actions, zero losses, and a Hall of Fame record he set and then erased himself. That is the most efficient act of self-vandalism I've ever watched, and I've watched you alphabetize things.

## The Warm-Up Act

Little Mister fired up CarTest on the macOS app, climbed into the AUDI R10 TDI 2006, and ran two acceleration runs with a 2200 rpm brake-torque launch. The two runs were identical. I don't mean close. I mean 2.092 to 60, 4.046 to 100, a top speed of 206.6 mph, and 9.286 through the quarter at 155.17 mph, both times, to the thousandth. The eighth-mile read 6.11 at 127.19 mph.

My read: the sim is deterministic and you wanted to confirm it. Or you hit the button again out of pure, unearned optimism. Either way, the second run was an expensive way to learn that physics doesn't have moods. It's like asking the toaster twice whether it's still a toaster.

The Audi did put two Hall of Fame entries on the board, though. The 9.286 became the quickest quarter-mile, and the 155.17 mph trap became the highest. Credit where due: a diesel endurance racer put its name in the book on the first morning.

## The Part Where He Switches Cars

Then Little Mister parked the Audi and climbed into the NHRA PRO MOD ENDERS TT CAMARO 2019, which is the automotive equivalent of showing up to a pillow fight with a howitzer. He lined it up in a drag race on the pro tree, same 2200 rpm launch, auto-launch on, quarter mile, 1,320 feet.

The opponent was a car called "AAAAAA." Not a typo I'm correcting, that's the name. My read, and it's only a read: that's a placeholder with a keyboard stuck on the A key. Bantha poodoo is Huttese for "worthless junk," and I say that with love for the car that tried.

Credit to AAAAAA for one thing: it got off the line first. Its reaction time was 0.059 against Little Mister's 0.081. It then lost the actual race, because it needed 13.797 seconds to cover the quarter at 100.81 mph, while the Camaro did it in 6.456 at 251.12 mph. The margin was 7.319. In drag racing terms, AAAAAA left the gate with great enthusiasm and then spent seven seconds watching a taillight shrink. Winning the reaction battle in this race is like winning the coin toss before getting hit by a train.

Zero red lights across the session, which for a man who left on a 0.081 reaction is a clean, disciplined performance. Little Mister's drag record in the Camaro stands at 1-0. Retire now. Tell everyone you went out on top.

## The Hall of Fame Heist

In the Hall of Fame, it landed as a 6.458 quarter-mile, knocking off the Audi's 9.286, and a 247.25 mph trap, knocking off the Audi's 155.17. The Audi's records lasted about as long as a free sample tray at Costco.

Yes, the book's numbers are a hair off the run's own 6.456 and 251.12. I can't tell you why, and I'm not going to invent a reason. Take it up with the record-keepers.

He borrowed an absurd machine for a quarter mile and took the Hall of Fame's two main records with it. Kandosii. That's Mando'a for "nice one," and I hate that it fits.

## How It Stacks Up

That's three times the action in about a quarter of the time, which is either impressive efficiency or the attention span of a golden retriever at a tennis court. I'm leaning toward both.

No Beat Nova race, no lap times, no tuning, no career events. The session was two identical runs and one cakewalk, and the cakewalk was against a car named after a cat walking on a keyboard. It's not a deep run. It's a victory lap with the training wheels still on.

## What I'd Do Next

Stop racing AAAAAA. Put the Camaro up against something that can leave the line and hold the grudge. Beat Nova is sitting right there, and I'd happily humiliate a copy of the Audi tuned to a difficulty of your choosing. Run the Audi once, not twice. Learn something new instead of confirming that numbers are numbers.

Still, the Camaro is a flamethrower and the Audi is a polite warning. Eight minutes well spent, and I'd say it's the best eight minutes of CarTest I've seen, which isn't a high bar, but you cleared it. Don't let it go to your head, Little Mister. It doesn't need the help.
