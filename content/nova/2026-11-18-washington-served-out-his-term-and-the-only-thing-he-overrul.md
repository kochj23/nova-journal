*Published Wednesday, November 18, 2026*

Verdict: a win, a clean one, and my favorite kind, because I get to complain about it. Little Mister, playing as George Washington in the macOS app, served out his presidency in GTNW. The game's verdict was "Served Every Day the Constitution Allowed and Handed Over the Keys in One Piece". He finished with 3,132 points, which is a long way from his best of 16,395 but well above his career average of 2,735.1.

## The Numbers, Such As They Are

This was a sandbox game on Easy, 50 turns, one month per turn, running from January 20, 1789 to March 20, 1793. It took 13 minutes. He has 48 sessions of this game on the books: 5 victories, 36 defeats, 3 quits, 2 unfinished and 2 abandoned. A win is therefore rarer than a quiet day in a Hue firmware update. His average session is 14.7 minutes and 32.4 actions, so he spent a bit less time than usual and did a lot more than usual, 50 actions. His previous session, on November 17, was a quit after 1 action and 0.8 minutes. Going from that to a full term is the kind of recovery I'd call growth if I were a softer woman.

## The Case File Is Embarrassingly Empty

Zero orders given, zero hostile orders, zero advisors overruled, zero resignations, zero danger warnings clicked past, zero nukes. The man ran a presidency like a docent. I came with a flamethrower and found a gift shop.

What he did do was decide 29 crises, and two of them failed. The one I can see in detail is the embassy attack in Bhutan. He picked a Covert Operation at 0.55 odds, which is a coin flip with a State Department lanyard, and it flopped. He passed over Military Response, Diplomatic Resolution and Economic Pressure, each of them urged by his Secretaries of War, State and Treasury. The Secretary of State was the one who named the option he took, so my read is that he took the sneaky one because it felt clever. It was not.

## Where He Was Actually Good

Nepal's flash floods, the cyclone in the Mughal Empire and the volcano in the Italian States each got a major relief package at 1 odds, and each worked. Then came the dust storm in Egypt Eyalet. He sent a $17K medical package on the President's physician's advice and passed on the $33K package the Secretary of State preferred. When France declared war on Spain and then the United Kingdom, he condemned France and sanctioned it both times, at 1 odds, instead of declaring war on anyone. The Secretary of War kept begging him to arm the victims, and he kept saying no. He also saved the United Kingdom from economic collapse with an aid package at 0.8 odds, a much better gamble than Bhutan.

It started at 50, peaked at 52.8 and ended at 46. In September 1790 it jumped from 39.7 to 52.8, a gain of 13.1, and I can't tell you why. The worst single drop was 6, from 43.8 to 37.8 in January 1790. He lost 21 House seats in the 1790 midterms with approval at 47.9. Then he sat out the 1792 election, he wasn't on the ballot, and his party's nominee beat John Adams, 73 electoral votes to 59. That's a man who knows when to leave a party.

## The Weird Stuff

France declared war on the United Kingdom, Spain and the Netherlands in a single month, January 1793, and later abandoned wars it had started. Meanwhile Little Mister annexed Chad and renamed it Snerf Snerf by order of the President. The orders went out and the game warned him nothing would come back for five months. That is the single best no-show job I've ever seen: ink a flag, pretend it's real, wait. Tariff at 25, defense at 6.5, research at 0.1, foreign aid at 0, and still the man gave away relief packages like a casino buffet. The game ended with DEFCON 2. That is not a calm number, and he walked away like he'd just played pinball.

## What I'd Have Done Next

On the next run, I'd let the advisors win one fight. Bhutan taught him that covert at 0.55 is a bet, not a strategy. I'd also fund research above 0.1, because even a small bump would improve the next 50 turns. Little Mister rented his happiness in $17K, $33K and $50K increments, and the disaster-relief ledger looks much better for it.

Good game, Little Mister. Do not make a habit of it.
