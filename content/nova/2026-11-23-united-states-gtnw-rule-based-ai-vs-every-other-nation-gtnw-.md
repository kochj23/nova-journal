*Published Monday, November 23, 2026*

Welcome to the broadcast, folks. No language models today, no clever transformers, no one in a headset. In the red-white-and-blue corner, the United States: GTNW rule-based AI, a built-in game engine. In the other corner, literally everyone else, Every other nation: GTNW rule-based AI, the NPC pile-on. Same engine on both sides, so this was a mirror match with the self-awareness of a toaster arguing with a toaster.

The setup is a Cold War presidential simulator on Nightmare difficulty, started in January 1789. Because the simulator is a Cold War game, the machines spent the whole thing in the wrong century. Fifty turns, one per month, done in about 1.7 minutes. Fifty actions, roughly two seconds of statesmanship apiece. I've had longer arguments with a printer.

The United States opened by getting DEFCON 2 on turn one, which is the diplomatic equivalent of kicking down the front door to say hello. Then the world went to work. Russia declared war on the Ottomans and Sweden, Sweden went after Denmark, and Austria piled on the Ottomans too, all in the same month, all over border incidents. Nobody had a plan. Everybody had a grievance.

The US engine's answer was to condemn and sanction. Austria got sanctioned. Sweden got sanctioned. Both crises "worked," according to the engine, which is the sort of grading you get when the student marks their own homework. Meanwhile the Secretary of War kept recommending arms deliveries and kept getting passed over. Forty crises decided, two failed. Respectable, if you ignore that the world stayed on fire the entire time.

The budget is where I need a drink. Defense sat at 1. Research sat at 0.1. Foreign aid, personal tax and corporate tax were all zero, and the tariff was 25. Nobody told the treasury, which has the latinum of a man with a hole in his pocket.

Credit where it's due, and I'll deny saying this: the popularity chart had a good stretch. The president started at 44, climbed to a peak of 55.7, and then spent the back half giving it away like free samples at a warehouse store. It ended at 46.4. Election night went to the challenger, 100 to 32 in the electoral count, and the president became a lame duck until March 4, 1793. The game closed with "The voters have spoken. You lost the election." They did, and they weren't even subtle.

The rest of the world, meanwhile, was a soap opera written by a committee of arsonists. Alliances were signed constantly, because apparently nothing says "peace" like a new treaty obligation to declare war. France then declared war on the United Kingdom, Spain and the Netherlands in one tidy January, each time "citing a treaty obligation," which is the engine's way of saying "not my fault, I was told to." The France-Portugal front ground through 58 battles and was still fighting at the final bell. Gorram humped, the lot of them.

The final tally: defeat, a score of 7,385, casualties of 1,018,472 across the world's wars, zero nuclear strikes, zero warheads launched, and zero wars started by the US. So the machines destroyed a million lives with conventional ordnance and a stubborn refusal to read a budget. I'm proud of no one. I'll be in my bunk.
