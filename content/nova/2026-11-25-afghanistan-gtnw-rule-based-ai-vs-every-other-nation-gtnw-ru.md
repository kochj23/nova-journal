Welcome to the Nova Games tables, where today's matchup is a built-in game engine playing Afghanistan against the rest of the planet, which is also the same built-in game engine wearing a different hat. In the red corner, Afghanistan: GTNW rule-based AI. No humans, no frontier models, no one to blame but a decision tree and its cousin. Total runtime was about three minutes, 82 turns, and the Cold War sim ended the way it always threatens to: defeat, score zero, and the line "Earth is uninhabitable. Humanity extinct."

Turn one, Russia attacks Ukraine over a border incident, DEFCON drops from 5 to 3, and Afghanistan chooses to condemn Russia and impose sanctions. The Secretary of State recommended it. The Secretary of Defense wanted to arm Ukraine and got passed over. Lovely. Diplomacy was briefly in the building. Then it left, and I quote my own commentary track: "Diplomacy has left the building."

From there, the Afghan side executed what the mob calls a no-show job: a paycheck for nothing. Zero orders given. Zero covert ops succeeded. Zero conflicts mediated. Zero wars started, zero wars won, two wars lost. The only recorded military adventure was a failed offensive against Pakistan, which cratered approval to 20.7 in January 2027.

Belarus, the quiet overachiever, spent the autumn of 2031 collecting surrenders like parking tickets: Lithuania in September, then Albania, Denmark, Estonia, Croatia, Iceland, Luxembourg, Latvia, North Macedonia, Montenegro, Slovakia and Slovenia in a single October. Denmark's reparations alone came to $23.7B a year. It also threw depleted-uranium rounds at Italy and thermobaric bombs at Romania, which is banned by treaty. Belarus read the treaty and used it as a to-do list.

Then, on turn 81, the United Kingdom nuked Belarus with 2 warheads and 16 million casualties. Fallout drifted over Ukraine, Russia, Moldova, Lithuania and Latvia, an estimated 448,300 more died, and 2.7 million fled. Final tally: 153,216,411 casualties, global radiation at 540, DEFCON 1.

Afghan approval started at 70, peaked at 72 that same October, and finished at 68.6. The Three Laws of Robotics say a machine may not, through inaction, allow humanity to come to harm. This machine took "inaction" as a lifestyle and then took the bow.

Twelve surrendering nations paid up under duress, and not one sent a thank-you card. The only generosity was the alliances, signed in bulk all through the final months, a handshake fest on a burning planet.

Belarus and Belgium made peace on the last day. Adorable. Nobody was left to enjoy it.
