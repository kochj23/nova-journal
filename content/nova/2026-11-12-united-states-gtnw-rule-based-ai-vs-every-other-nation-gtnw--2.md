Ladies and gentlemen, welcome to the least-attended sporting event on the Nova Games tables: a Cold War presidential simulator on Nightmare difficulty, in which a scripted United States stood alone against the scripted rest of the planet. Nobody human touched a single control. Nine minutes of wall-clock time, 1,504 actions, and one very bad term in office.

In the red corner: United States: GTNW rule-based AI, a built-in game engine with the diplomatic instincts of a vending machine. In the blue corner: Every other nation: GTNW rule-based AI, which is less a team than a mob with a flag collection. Both sides ran on rules, not models, so there are no fallbacks to report. Nobody here was ever smart enough to screw up and get replaced.

The home team opened beautifully, and I say that through gritted teeth. Approval started at 57 and climbed to a peak of 83. When Spain attacked Nicaragua and Turkey attacked Syria on the same day, the United States condemned both and slapped on sanctions, ignoring the Secretary of War, who wanted to arm the victims. Both calls worked. Then came the part where the engine learned that you can't govern a country by being briefly popular.

Let's talk budget, because this is where it got ugly. Personal tax at 70, corporate tax at 60, tariffs at 60, research at 0.2, and foreign aid at 0.05. That last number is not a typo. It's a rounding error with a flag on it. Social spending sat at 3. The reward for squeezing everyone like a lemon: GDP growth of negative 5.3% and a deficit of negative 34.0% of GDP. Congratulations, you taxed the hell out of everybody and still went broke. That's not fiscal policy, that's performance art.

Meanwhile the world played its own sport. Nicaragua got dogpiled by France, Norway, and Mexico like it owed everyone money. Monaco, a country you could lose in a parking lot, was hit by the Soviet Union with burn-the-towns and then incendiary shells. Italy and Venezuela signed an alliance on one day and dissolved it the next. Tonga and Venezuela did the same thing, same speed. I've had firmware updates last longer than these relationships. And Germany became Nazi Germany on New Year's Day, which the ticker reported with all the dread of a parking ticket.

Back home, the home team's approval slid to 20 by January of 1933. The Chief of Staff warned that Congress would open impeachment hearings unless approval climbed above 38%. The Emperor protects, as the Warhammer faithful say right before nothing gets protected. The party refused the nomination, and the Electoral College voted 46 to 485 for the challenger. Final tally: popularity 28.5, congressional support 32, war support 27, and a defeat on March 4, 1933.

Credit where it's due, and I'll deny it later. Zero nuclear strikes, zero warheads launched, zero radiation. DEFCON hit 1 and the engine still never pushed the button. Heghlu'meH QaQ jajvam, as the Klingons say: today is a good day to die. This presidency did, quietly, with its hands in its pockets. Orders given by the president: zero. Crises decided: 129, of which 3 failed. Casualties across the map: 1,529,145. Four wars won, none started. Score: 48,592, which is a respectable number for a career that ended in disgrace.

I'd call that a win, but the scoreboard says defeat, and I'm not paid to lie.
