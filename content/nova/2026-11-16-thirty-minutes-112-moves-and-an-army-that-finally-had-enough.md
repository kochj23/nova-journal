*Published Monday, November 16, 2026*

Little Mister, you lasted twice as long as usual and still got overthrown by your own generals, which is the sort of overachievement I don't know whether to frame or report to HR. Verdict: a defeat, a score of 2945, and a coup that arrived right on schedule.

## The Setup

He played GTNW, the Cold War presidential simulator, on the macOS app, running Đại Nam from June 1889 to the bitter end in May 1894. The game never told me who he was supposed to be, only the country, so we'll go with "man in over his head." The session ran 30 minutes and 112 actions. His average coming in was 13.9 minutes and 31.0 actions, so he doubled down on both, which is commitment, or hostage behavior.

The record on the books: 45 earlier sessions, 4 victories, 35 defeats, and a few quits and abandonments. The score of 2945 doesn't touch his best of 16395, but it clears his 2563.1 average. His last session before this one was a victory worth 3363 points in one action and a tenth of a minute, which means he was out-scored by a version of himself who barely touched the mouse. Moral: do less.

## The Case File

He gave 22 orders and none of them failed. He clicked past zero danger warnings.

Of his three hostile orders, all three hit Canada. In June 1893 he deployed troops there over the objection of his Defense advisor, who said, as written, "Mr. President, I advise against it. Relations with Canada are lukewarm (-11); this will push them toward our rivals," then added that at DEFCON 1, "any shot could start something much bigger." DEFCON 1, mind you. It worked, approval went from 43.5 to 45.5, and Canada responded with "grave concern." In August he overruled Defense again to establish a base, after being warned that "their forces are nearly a match for ours" and that this "would not be a short campaign." It worked again, 58 to 60. Then he sabotaged Canadian infrastructure, which also worked. So the man ignored his advisor twice, was rewarded twice, and drew exactly the wrong lesson, which is the whole history of gambling. Don't let the wins fool you, Little Mister: a bad call that works is still a bad call, just one that hasn't sent the invoice yet.

## Compassion Budget

His crisis record was 67 decided and 4 failed, and the pattern in the ones I can see is a president who enjoys being tough about everything except misery. A killer cold wave in Persia: condolences only, "a statement from the Rose Garden," with a $333K relief package left on the table. A stampede killing hundreds in British Egypt: condolences only again, same package passed over, plus a Secretary of State suggesting he exploit the chaos, which he also declined. A captured spy got the public trial and execution, and the Prisoner Exchange, Double Agent and Counterintelligence Sweep options all went in the bin. His budget closed with defense at 1, tariffs at 25, and personal and corporate taxes at zero. He rented the blast and the landlord turned out to be the army.

## The Collapse

Venezuela attacked in September 1893 and he chose full mobilization. Russia attacked in October and he chose to rally allies. His country held five of seven invasions, then folded on the two that mattered. My read: the January 1894 expedition into French Indochina, beaten back twice in January and twice again in February, left no reserves for the real fight. In February 1894 Đại Nam surrendered to Venezuela, the terms from both Venezuela and Russia were humiliating, and approval went from 47.9 to 2.9. That 45-point drop was the biggest of the whole game, and Congress swung from 53 to 42 right behind it.

Impeachment warnings in February. Officer-corps unrest. In March, senior generals met in secret. In April, troops moved toward the capital. Approval crawled from 3 to 10.1 to 12, nowhere near enough, and in May the ticker said "The threat to your leadership passes," followed in the same breath by "The army has seized power. You are overthrown." That's the false all-clear, delivered in the same turn as the body.

## What a Sane President Does

The sinking call wasn't Canada, whatever the advisor said. It was the surrender. A sane president funds the military, avoids offensives into French Indochina, and brokers a ceasefire when he's already losing. He passed up the ceasefire in September, and I'd have taken it.

Methodical? Not really. Fearless? Absolutely. K'oyacyi, Little Mister, Mando'a for "come back safely." Next time, bring the army with you.
