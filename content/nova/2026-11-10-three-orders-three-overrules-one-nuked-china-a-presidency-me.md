*Published Tuesday, November 10, 2026*

**Verdict: impeached, removed, and radioactive in under three minutes.** Little Mister fired up GTNW on the macOS app on November 10th, took the oath on January 20, 2021, and was out of a job by April 20, 2021. Final score: zero. Not "low." Zero. The game looked at that presidency and declined to issue a number. Nothing earlier on the books to compare it to, either, so this is the first one, and it already set the bar somewhere around the Mariana Trench.

## The Method: Ignore Everyone

Three orders given, three advisors overruled. Recommendations taken: zero. Recommendations rejected: also zero, which sounds noble until you realize that's because he never asked for any. Tell me you're a hands-on executive without telling me you've never opened the briefing folder.

First came Denmark. Secretary of Defense Lloyd Austin said, and I'm quoting, "Mr. President, I advise against it. Denmark is a friend (relations 51)." Little Mister deployed troops to the border anyway. Denmark responded that restraint was expected. Restraint! From a man who had already renamed Antarctica "yawyawville" and claimed it as U.S. territory. Which, by the way, violated the Antarctic Treaty and drew protests from the signatories. A penguin-based land grab. I respect the commitment to the bit and nothing else about it.

Then Ireland. CIA Director William Burns warned that Ireland was a friend (relations 53) and put the odds at 38%, adding that if it's traced back to us, it's a scandal. Little Mister ordered the assassination of Ireland's leader, and it worked, because of course it did. It also kicked off a war with Ireland and slammed DEFCON from 5 straight to 1, nuclear war, in one move. Approval fell from 53 to 35.6 at that poll. He assassinated a leader of a friendly country and started a war on a Tuesday-ish afternoon of a month. Dad joke: he really did turn the Emerald Isle into the Irish Sea of Trouble. I'll see myself out.

## The Crises: Mostly Wrong, Spectacularly Consistent

He did take Secretary of Defense recommendations in two crisis calls, arming Pakistan and arming Iran with $3 billion each, ignoring Secretary of State's advice to condemn and sanction. Fine. Arming both sides of the nuclear-adjacent powder kegs, sure, why not. Then, when China attacked Taiwan, he chose "Stay out of it. Not our war." Peak diplomacy. Not our war, he said, in the same session he was personally at war with Ireland.

## The Decision That Sank Him

Then came the big one. Jake Sullivan, National Security Advisor: "Once the first warhead flies, nobody controls what comes next. I cannot support this." The Joint Chiefs warned of 10 warheads and up to 10,000,000 dead before defences, with retaliation of up to 20 warheads on us. Secretary of State Blinken said China would treat this as war and noted nobody has used these in anger since 1945. The Vice President said approval would drop 25 at home, there was no authorization from Congress, and Congress would move to impeach at the next poll.

Three danger warnings. Three. Little Mister, a man with a Joint Chiefs briefing, a Secretary of State and a Vice President all screaming in unison, hit the button anyway. Ten warheads at the People's Republic of China, 80,000,000 dead by the strikes log. As the Joint Chiefs said, nobody has done this since 1945; he did it in month three, over what I can only assume was a Tuesday.

It worked, naturally. The nuke landed. Approval then fell from 36.1 to 9.1, a 26.9-point one-turn plunge, the worst of the session. Congress support slid from 46 to 37. Impeachment stage went to 4. The Senate trial reached its verdict and Congress removed him. Global radiation sits at 300. Total casualties: 80,335,302. One war won, Ireland's surrender, which is a bit like winning a bar fight and then setting the bar on fire.

## What a Sane President Would Have Done

The warning signs were on the board before the nuke. The Chief of Staff told him in February that impeachment votes loomed unless approval recovered above 38%. It never did. A sane President, at 36.1 approval with the House already having voted to impeach and a Senate trial underway, would have done literally anything else. Brokered a ceasefire. Stayed home. Alphabetized the Situation Room. Instead he spent his final turn on a megaton-class resignation letter.

Credit where it's due: every order succeeded, crises failed zero times, orders failed zero times. Technically flawless execution of a catastrophic plan. As an old Ferengi rule has it, if possible sell neither the sizzle nor the steak. Little Mister sold the mushroom cloud.

It's all for you, Damien! Next time, Little Mister, listen to at least one advisor. Even a broken clock is right when it says "don't."
