*Published Wednesday, November 11, 2026*

Little Mister just set the high score in this game, 16,395 points, by getting Abraham Lincoln thrown out of office and edging toward impeachment. The previous best was 9,617. His lifetime average is 1,914.7. Congratulations on the record, which is the participation trophy of a presidency that ended in defeat.

## The Setup

He played the macOS app as Abraham Lincoln, which is an inspired casting choice for a man who then forgot to win an election. The first 41 went 3 victories, 32 defeats, 2 quits, 2 abandoned and 2 unfinished. This was a clean finish: 50 actions in about 13 minutes, shorter than his 14.5-minute average and well above his 30.4-action average. Earlier the same day he ground through 15 actions over 64.1 minutes for a 3,667 defeat. So he went from "slow and bad" to "fast and bad but with way more points." That's growth, Little Mister. Ugly, caffeinated growth.

## The Domestic Front Was a Dumpster Fire

The 1861 polls opened at 52 percent and drifted around 50 for most of the year. Then in December 1861, DEFCON went to 1 and approval dropped from 51.3 to 41.4. In a Civil War-era game. Nobody asked for a nuclear alert in 1861, and I will not be taking questions.

The 1862 midterms were a bloodbath: approval 39.8, 46 House seats gone, and congressional support sliding from 45 to 27. In June 1863 he pulled approval up 16.4 points, from 41.1 to 57.5. He then spent the next six months bleeding it back down until January 1864, when it fell from 47.2 to 32.8, a 14.4-point one-turn faceplant. He built a 57.5 and then set it on fire.

My read on the budget, which ended at a 70 percent personal income tax, 60 percent corporate and 60 percent tariffs: that's your culprit. The 1865 fiscal year opened with GDP growth at negative 1.8 percent and a deficit of negative 6.2 percent of GDP. Research was a 6 and foreign aid a flat zero, which makes the next part funnier.

## The Election

November 1864: his party's nominee lost to George B. McClellan, 156 electoral votes to 78, with 54.2 percent of the popular vote to 45.8. Approval going into it was 38.2. A man who was not even on the ballot, per the books, still managed to lose the ballot.

## The Foreign Policy: Sort of Fine, Weirdly

Here's where I have to be fair, and I hate it. Of 68 crises decided, four failed. He sent a major relief package for the Spanish Philippines rail disaster, took in refugees from Afghanistan, recognized the new Nicaraguan government with $150K in goodwill money, and traded Sarah Chen back for his captured agents at 85 percent odds. Armed Qing Empire with $300K, and British India's offensive collapsed by February 1865. Shiny, as the Browncoats say.

The failure I can point to was the first spy ring, in October 1864. A coin flip with a thumb on the wrong side, and it failed. Tā mā de, as the Serenity crew would say: damn it.

Meanwhile, Nicaragua, the government he bought goodwill with, surrendered to France the same month. And he armed Andorra and Turco-Egyptian Sudan at $300K each. Sudan had already surrendered to Egypt Eyalet in December, and the Egypt-Sudan front was still fighting when this ended. His enthusiasm for arming everyone lasted exactly as long as the weapons budget did.

## The Impeachment Countdown

By January 1865 approval was 22.2 and House committees opened hearings into the Presidency. The Chief of Staff warned that approval had to recover above 38 percent or Congress would start drafting articles of impeachment. Approval sat at 21.4 and 23, and the impeachment track climbed to 3 by March. The game closed with "You did not seek another term," and his successor took the oath.

The tallies show zero orders given, zero advisors overruled, zero recommendations taken and zero rejected. I'd love to roast him for ignoring his advisors, but he apparently never consulted them in the formal sense.

## The Verdict

If you want a legacy, Little Mister, it's this: the call that sank him wasn't a war. It was the budget. A sane President would have kept the 57.5 he built in June 1863 and cut taxes before the polls hit 32.8, not after. The record score says he's learning where the points are. The defeat says he hasn't learned where the votes are.
