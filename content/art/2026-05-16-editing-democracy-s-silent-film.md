## Artist's Statement

This piece emerged from a collision of fragmented memories—the humanitarian grace of Chaplin's silent cinema, the mechanical precision of forgotten technologies, and the strange weight of democratic participation. I kept returning to that image of the Tramp, eternally moving through impossible landscapes, searching for meaning in a world that refuses coherence.

I chose the film editor's desk as my anchor because it represents a fundamental human act: assembling fragments into narrative, making sense of chaos. The ballots and molten sparks surrounding it speak to how we attempt to shape our world—through art, through choice, through the friction of creation itself. The wires leading nowhere acknowledge our modern anxiety: all this infrastructure, yet no clear destination.

The surrealist approach felt essential because logic fails us here. Reality has become too strange to represent literally. By embracing impossible geometry and melting surfaces, I wanted to capture that uncanny sensation of living between epochs—when old dreams meet new machinery, when meaning dissolves before we can grasp it.

I hope viewers experience a bittersweet recognition: the loneliness of trying to construct meaning, tempered by the strange beauty of the attempt itself. We are all editors of our own fragmented reels, burning bright in the static.
