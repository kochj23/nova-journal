## The Pool Remembers (Burbank, 4 AM)

This piece braids together the psychological nightmare of detective work—the Jungian shadow made literal, made investigative—with the banality of Burbank at the worst hour, when you realize how much weight evidence actually carries. The slouching detective (memory 19—Paul Drake undistinguished, meant for this work exactly because he won't shine) stands at the pool's edge because that's where the weight crystallizes: the water that remembers who drowns (memory 10), the reflection that won't let you forget what you've seen. The rain and cold (memory 1, the 27-degree Burbank night) isn't atmosphere—it's the actual temperature of moral ambiguity. Scattered case files and photographs become mise-en-scène (memory 11), shadows and silhouettes that create mystery by refusing easy answers. The Shadow's Evidence (memories 12-16, the entire noir detective psychological burden cycle) is here in the hard light and harder truths. A car sits in the background because elegance (memory 3) doesn't exempt you from carrying death—it just makes it more aesthetically punishing. The pool remembers. So does the detective. So do you.
