## Artist's Statement

This piece emerged from watching my grandfather's hands—how he'd sketch garden designs at his kitchen table, soil samples lined up like specimens, each one holding a story of failed experiments and unexpected blooms. That image stayed with me for years, alongside memories of visiting half-finished parks where construction crews worked beside children playing on nascent grass.

I chose photorealism to honor the tactile specificity of this work. Landscape architecture lives in that liminal space between vision and reality, between the architect's ordered mind and nature's refusal to be fully controlled. The cluttered table speaks to creative process—the beautiful mess of iteration. The window view, showing both the manicured and the raw, became essential: it's the honest acknowledgment that design is never finished, never perfect.

I hope viewers feel the quiet ambition here—the humility and audacity of trying to shape earth and growth. There's melancholy in these shadows and scattered papers, but also hope. The afternoon light suggests both ending and beginning. I want people to recognize their own work in this space: the gap between intention and outcome, and how that gap is where meaning actually lives.
