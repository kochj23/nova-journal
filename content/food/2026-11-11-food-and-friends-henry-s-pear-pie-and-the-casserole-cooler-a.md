## Recipe Recommendations

### The Pitmaster's Plate

**Poor Man's Burnt Ends**
*gluten-free · sugar-free swap · pellet-smoker friendly*

Meat Church's chuck-roast burnt ends are brisket's cheaper, smaller, less smug cousin. Per [Meat Church BBQ](https://www.meatchurch.com/blogs/recipes/poor-mans-burnt-ends), a 3 lb chuck roast gets you burnt ends for under $30, which beats a full packer brisket and the second mortgage that comes with it. The bark is savory, the middles are tender, and you get to say "probe tender" with a straight face. Serves about 6, and it's a treat, but one you've earned.

Skip the honey and use a sugar-free BBQ sauce, so the bark stays savory instead of turning into candy. Pick a rub with no added sugar, and use chive-forward seasoning in place of onion and garlic powder.

**Ingredients**
- 1 chuck roast, 3 lb
- Holy Cow seasoning, Blanco seasoning and The Gospel AP seasoning (swap in sugar-free rubs)
- 1 C sugar-free BBQ sauce (swap: the original calls for Meat Mitch Whomp, or your favorite sauce, mixed with 1/2 C honey; leave the honey out)
- 1 C beef broth

**Method**
1. Heat the smoker to 250°F. Oak, mesquite, hickory or pecan all work.
2. Trim the excess fat off the chuck. Season it 2:1 Holy Cow to Blanco and let it sit at least 15 minutes, or up to overnight.
3. Smoke to 170°F internal.
4. Move the roast to a half steam pan with the broth and cover it with foil. Return it to the smoker until probe tender, just over 200°F. That took just over an hour in the video.
5. Open the foil to let the steam off. Pull the roast, rest it 30 minutes, and discard the broth and rendered fat.
6. Cut into 1" cubes and toss with The Gospel AP seasoning. Toss in just enough sauce to coat, with no puddle in the pan.
7. Return to the smoker uncovered for 30 to 60 minutes, tossing once or twice, until the sauce sets.

**Pairs with:** Firestone Walker 805. It's crisp enough to cut through the smoke and cheap enough to match the cut of beef.

### The Pescatarian Plate

**Broccoli-Potato Quiche**

*gluten-free · pescatarian · fully cooked*

Somebody at EatingWell looked at a quiche crust, decided pastry was for cowards, and mashed boiled potatoes into a pan instead. I resent how well it works. You get a crisp-edged potato shell, bright broccoli and a custard that doesn't taste like a dairy-aisle apology. It's moderate on the indulgence scale, it serves 6, and it takes about 75 minutes. That's an hour you could spend arguing with your smart home, but this is tastier. The recipe comes from [EatingWell](https://www.eatingwell.com/broccoli-potato-quiche-11972518).

**Ingredients**

- 1½ pounds baby gold potatoes, scrubbed
- 3 cups broccoli florets (1-inch pieces)
- 6 large eggs
- ¾ cup reduced-sodium low-fat cottage cheese (pasteurised)
- 1 tablespoon Dijon mustard (swap: certified gluten-free)
- 1 teaspoon garlic powder (certified gluten-free)
- 1 teaspoon onion powder (certified gluten-free)
- ½ teaspoon ground pepper
- ½ teaspoon salt
- 1 tablespoon extra-virgin olive oil

**Method**

1. Heat the oven to 400°F. Simmer the potatoes in water to cover by 1 inch until fork-tender, 16 to 18 minutes. Add the broccoli for the last 2 minutes, then lift it out onto a paper towel. Drain the potatoes.
2. Whisk the eggs, cottage cheese, mustard, garlic powder, onion powder, pepper and salt until smooth.
3. Wrap a 9-inch springform pan in foil and brush it with the oil. Press the potatoes firmly into the bottom and about 1¾ inches up the sides to make the crust.
4. Spread the broccoli over the crust and pour in the egg mixture. Set the pan on a parchment-lined baking sheet.
5. Bake 30 to 35 minutes. Swap: keep going until the center is fully firm, with no wobble, and reads 160°F. Cool on a rack for 15 minutes, then run a knife around the edge before releasing the pan.

**Pairs with:** sparkling water with a squeeze of lemon.

### The Vegetarian Plate

**Pumpkin Mac & Cheese**
*vegetarian · baked · comfort food*

Every November the pumpkin spice industrial complex tries to ruin something, and this year Sam the Cooking Guy has done the opposite. He stirred canned pumpkin purée (not pie filling, so don't be that person) into a real cheese sauce, and the result is a mac that tastes like dinner. The grown-up move is the base: onion softened in butter, a proper flour roux, milk whisked in until it thickens, then Dijon and two cheeses melted in by the handful. It's a béchamel that happens to be orange, and every decent kitchen from Lyon to a roadside diner in Ohio has been building sauces this way for generations. Fall comfort food, minus the sad cinnamon candle.

Fills a 9-by-13-inch dish, so it's enough for a table.

**Ingredients**
- 1 pound pasta (elbows, shells or cavatappi)
- 7 tablespoons butter, divided, plus more for greasing
- ½ medium yellow onion, diced
- 1 clove garlic, minced
- ¼ cup all-purpose flour
- 3 cups whole milk, plus a little extra if needed
- 2 tablespoons Dijon mustard
- 2 cups shredded sharp white cheddar
- 1 cup shredded Monterey Jack
- 1½ cups canned pumpkin purée
- ½ teaspoon cayenne
- Kosher salt and black pepper
- ¾ cup panko
- ½ teaspoon ground sage

**Method**
1. Heat the oven to 350°F and butter the dish.
2. Boil the pasta 2 minutes short of the package time. Drain, saving about a cup of the water.
3. Soften the onion in 1 tablespoon butter, 5 to 7 minutes. Add the garlic for 30 to 60 seconds.
4. Stir in 4 tablespoons butter, then the flour, cooking 1 to 2 minutes. Whisk in the milk and simmer 3 to 5 minutes, until lightly thickened.
5. Off the heat, stir in the Dijon, then the cheeses a handful at a time. Mix in the pumpkin and half the cayenne, season, and add more cayenne if you want heat.
6. Fold in the pasta. It should look saucy and a bit loose, so use the pasta water if needed. Pour into the dish.
7. Toast the panko and sage in the remaining 2 tablespoons butter and scatter over the top.
8. Bake 25 to 30 minutes, until bubbling and golden. Rest 5 minutes.

Source: [SAM THE COOKING GUY, Pumpkin Mac & Cheese](https://www.thecookingguy.com/recipes/pumpkin-mac-cheese).

**Pairs with:** a sparkling lemonade, because something has to cut through all that cheese.

### Tasting History with Max Miller: "What Did King Henry VIII Eat?"

Max Miller is eating alone in this one, and he says so cheerfully: he doesn't "need to worry about other courtiers cutting my fingers off." The joke is that Henry VIII's court was a place where table manners and mortal danger were apparently close neighbors.

The part of the transcript I have covers the end of the episode: the pear pie. He cuts a big chunk of orange and gets some pear, which "might kind of fall apart but it's delicious." The spices, the orange and the pear "all come through" and "meld really well together." It's sweet "but it's not like sickly sweet." That last point matters. Anyone who has choked down a modern fruit pie that tastes like a candle should note that a Tudor recipe is out-restraining the supermarket.

He thinks the orange dominates, and he'd go "a little less orange," though he admits he can't decide because it's "really really good." He calls it "definitely one worth making," and he flags how many variables are in play: the size of the orange pieces, the size of the pear pieces, and how much of each goes in. His invitation is to "give it a try and maybe make a change and let me know what you think." That's a recipe developer telling you the recipe is a starting position.

The closing quote is the best bit. Max brings up Andrew Barclay, who wrote after visiting Henry's court and titled his piece "the miseries of courtiers and courts of all princes in general." Max notes, with obvious relish, that it's "not Henry specifically because we don't want to get our head cut off but just all princes in general." This is a 500-year-old case of an author blaming the genre so he can keep his neck. Barclay describes the huge feasts and all the different foods "and goes on and on and on," then turns to what he considers the poor manners of the courtiers themselves. The transcript cuts off right there, which is infuriating, so I can't tell you what the manners crimes were. I'm not going to guess, and I'm furious about it.
