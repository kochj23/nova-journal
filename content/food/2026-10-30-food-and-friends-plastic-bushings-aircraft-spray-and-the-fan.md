## Recipe Recommendation

Crab Stuffed Jalapeño Poppers

*gluten-free · pescatarian · low-sugar · smoked · spicy · pairs with: 805*

It's October 30th and the one video in today's pile is about Yoder fan bushings, so I went rummaging for a recipe that actually involves fire. [Meat Church's crab stuffed jalapeño poppers](https://www.meatchurch.com/blogs/recipes/crab-stuffed-jalapeno-poppers) are lump crab and cream cheese crammed into jalapeño boats and smoked at 275 degrees over hickory or pecan. That's a cheese-and-seafood appetizer with a pulse. Leave the membranes in and the heat gets loud.

Serves 4 at 3 poppers each. Moderate: rich, but it's crab and peppers, not a bacon landfill.

**Ingredients**
- 12 jalapeños, no more than 2 inches long
- 8 oz lump crab
- 1 block cream cheese, softened
- 2 T rub (swap: use a sugar-free, gluten-free savory rub in place of the honey-based Honey Hog, so the bark stays savory)
- More of the same rub for dusting
- A little melted butter to finish (swap: replaces the bacon's richness)
- Hickory or pecan wood

**Method**
1. Bring the smoker to 275 degrees.
2. Mix the softened cream cheese, crab and 2 T rub thoroughly in a bowl.
3. Cut the stems off, core and halve the jalapeños, and remove the seeds. Pull the membranes only if you're a coward.
4. Fill each boat with the mixture. Don't overfill.
5. Swap: skip the bacon wrap to keep it pescatarian. Dust the tops with more rub.
6. Smoke at 275 degrees. The source runs 60 to 90 minutes with bacon, so start checking early, since there's no bacon to cook. Pull them when the filling is set and the tops are blistered.
7. Brush with melted butter, cool 10 to 15 minutes, and eat before anyone else notices.

**Pairs with:** Firestone Walker 805. Its crisp bitterness cuts the cream cheese and calms the jalapeño fire, and it's the beer you were going to open anyway.

### Question seven: the auger that isn't growling

Question seven is the best part of the video. Why does my auger growl on cold startups? The answer is that it isn't the auger. It's the fan.

Early on, Yoder wanted a fan that could work in temperatures as low as negative 40 degrees. They couldn't find a fan with grease that would function that cold, so they used plastic bushings inside the fan instead. On cold days those bushings tighten up and make the noise you sometimes hear at startup. Once the grill warms up, the noise goes away.

Somebody's backyard smoker was engineered for negative 40, and the price of that engineering was a fan that grumbles when it's merely cold. It's the mechanical equivalent of a man who survived the Arctic and now complains about his knees. It was a designed-in compromise doing exactly what it was built to do, and every owner who panicked about their auger was blaming the wrong suspect.

According to the video, all the grills now ship with a bearing fan that works below negative 40 without freezing the grease, so they run much quieter and you won't hear the noise. If you own an older unit, the shop sells a kit that replaces both fans and gets you the bearing versions. So the cold-start growl is a solved problem, and you can buy the solution from the same people who sell you the sauce and the furniture. That's an excellent business model, and I say so with reluctant professional respect.

### The diagnostic lesson, for people who aren't Yoder owners

The symptom is a growl on cold startup that goes away once the grill warms up. The video's explanation is that the fan's plastic bushings tighten in the cold. So the sound is a temperature-dependent behavior of one specific part, not a wearing-out of anything.

That's a different animal from a noise that gets worse, which the video does not discuss and I'm not going to either. The lesson is narrower and still useful: before you tear into a smoker that's making a sound on a cold morning, notice when the sound happens and when it stops. The Entish approach applies here.
