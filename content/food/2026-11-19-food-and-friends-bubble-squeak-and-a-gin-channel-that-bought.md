## Recipe Recommendations

### The Pitmaster's Plate

**Reverse Seared Tri Tip**
*gluten-free · sugar-free · pellet smoker*

Meat Church's tri tip skips the traditional red oak fire and goes low and slow first, then gets a screaming-hot sear. The reverse sear suits an awkward, uneven cut like this one, and it keeps the center edge to edge pink instead of that sad gray ring everyone pretends not to hate. It's a pellet-smoker cook, which means you're mostly standing around holding a thermometer and a drink. Serves a small table, sliced against the grain.

**Ingredients**
- 1 tri tip, choice grade or higher, fat trimmed (silver skin optional)
- A sugar-free beef rub for the main coat (swap for the Holy Gospel, so the bark stays savory)
- Garlic-infused oil plus chopped chives (swap for the Garlic & Herb, so you get the flavor without the garlic and onion chunks)
- Cooking oil or spray for the skillet
- Butter to melt over the top at the end
- Oak, mesquite, hickory or pecan for smoke
- A low-carb side: grilled zucchini or a green salad

**Method**
1. Fire the pellet grill to 225 with a heavier wood.
2. Trim the fat, season with the rub, add the garlic oil and chives, and let it sit at least 15 minutes.
3. Smoke to an internal temp of 115, then tent loosely with foil for 10 minutes.
4. Get a cast iron skillet blazing, over 550 (the video runs 650).
5. Lightly oil or butter the pan and sear 1 to 2 minutes per side, aiming for 130 to 135 for medium rare.
6. Rest it with butter melting on top, then slice.

Source: [Meat Church BBQ, Reverse Seared Tri Tip](https://www.meatchurch.com/blogs/recipes/reverse-seared-tri-tip).

**Pairs with:** Firestone Walker 805, because a crisp, easy beer cuts through the smoke and butter without making a fuss.

### The Pescatarian Plate

**Sheet-Pan Lemon-Garlic Cod with Roasted Vegetables**
*gluten-free · pescatarian · fully cooked*

One pan, one oven, and no sink full of regrets. The potatoes get a head start, the tomatoes burst like they've got something to prove, and the cod rides in a foil packet in the middle under garlic butter like a goddamn VIP. It serves 4 and counts as moderate: honest food that doesn't need a sequel. Adapted from [EatingWell](https://www.eatingwell.com/sheet-pan-lemon-garlic-cod-with-roasted-vegetables-11761747).

**Ingredients**
- 1 pound baby Yukon Gold potatoes, halved lengthwise
- 3 teaspoons extra-virgin olive oil, divided
- 1 teaspoon salt, divided
- 1½ cups cherry tomatoes
- 1 medium zucchini, cut into ½-inch half-moons
- ¾ teaspoon ground pepper, divided
- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, softened
- 1 tablespoon chopped fresh flat-leaf parsley
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard (swap: certified gluten-free Dijon)
- 1 teaspoon grated lemon zest
- 4 (5-ounce) cod fillets, patted dry

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to 425°F with racks in the top and bottom thirds.
2. Toss the potatoes with 2 teaspoons oil and ½ teaspoon salt on a large rimmed baking sheet. Roast on the bottom rack about 20 minutes, until light golden.
3. Toss the tomatoes and zucchini with ¼ teaspoon salt, ¼ teaspoon pepper and the remaining teaspoon of oil. Mix the butter, parsley, garlic, mustard and zest in a small bowl.
4. Set the cod on a large sheet of foil, season with the remaining ½ teaspoon pepper and ¼ teaspoon salt, and spread the butter mixture over the top. Fold up the foil sides without covering the fish.
5. Add the vegetables to the pan, push them to the edges, and set the packet in the center. Roast on the top rack 10 to 15 minutes, until the potatoes are golden, the tomatoes burst and the cod is flaky in the center.

**Pairs with:** sparkling water with a squeeze of lemon.

### The Vegetarian Plate

**Baked Mac and Cheese**

*vegetarian · baked · fully cooked*

Boxed-powder mac is for people who've given up. This one starts with a real roux, butter and flour whisked until it stops smelling like raw flour, then goes into evaporated milk and cream cheese, with Dijon and paprika doing the heavy lifting. It's a proper béchamel-style sauce wearing a casserole's clothes. Serves 12, and it's a treat, so don't pretend otherwise.

**Ingredients**
- 1 pound short pasta (elbows, cavatappi or shellbows)
- 3 tablespoons butter and 3 tablespoons all-purpose flour
- 2 (12-ounce) cans evaporated milk
- 4 ounces whipped cream cheese, room temperature
- 2 teaspoons seasoned salt, 1 teaspoon garlic powder, 1/2 teaspoon paprika, 1/4 teaspoon cayenne
- 1 tablespoon Dijon or spicy brown mustard
- 1 pound sharp cheddar, 8 ounces Monterey Jack and 8 ounces low-moisture whole milk mozzarella, all shredded

**Method**
1. Grease a 9x13-inch dish. Boil the pasta in salted water until al dente, drain, and set aside in a big bowl.
2. Melt the butter over medium heat, whisk in the flour for about 1 minute, then slowly whisk in the evaporated milk. Bring to a simmer, drop the heat to low, and whisk in the cream cheese, seasonings and mustard until smooth.
3. Whisk in half of each cheese a handful at a time, letting each melt first. Pull it off the heat the moment the last handful melts, then taste and salt.
4. Toss the sauce with the pasta. Layer half in the dish, add half the remaining cheese, then the rest of the pasta and the last of the cheese.
5. Bake 20 to 25 minutes, until bubbly at the edges and lightly golden. Rest 10 minutes.

Adapted from [Simply Recipes](https://www.simplyrecipes.com/baked-mac-and-cheese-recipe-8751498), with the New York line from [A Cooks Tour with Anthony Bourdain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bgIRnbL19xs).

**Pairs with:** a sparkling lemonade, cold and tart enough to cut through all that cheese.

## Barbecue and Live Fire

### Chuds BBQ and the Three-Day Thanksgiving

Chuds BBQ posted "A Proper BBQ Thanksgiving Feast!", a full holiday spread cooked primarily on a pellet grill, and the most interesting thing in it is the confession. By his own account the turkey was probably not the smokiest he's ever made. It didn't cross his mind during the taste test, though, because it was well executed, properly cooked, tender, juicy and well seasoned. A BBQ channel admitting that "smoky" lost to "correct" is the most mature thing I've heard from the live-fire world all year.

He spread the whole feast across three days, which kept the oven free for the sides and made the turkey cook hands-off for only about two and a half hours. He called it the easiest turkey cook he's done on the channel in roughly seven years, and said he usually dreads these videos because of the timing and prep. Moltke the Elder wrote that "No plan of operations extends with any certainty beyond the first contact with the main hostile force." The hostile force, in this case, is a twenty-pound bird and the guests' expectations. Chuds beat it the way you beat any enemy, by not being in the same room with all your problems at once. It's all for you, Damien!

### Guga Foods and the Table Grill

At the opposite end of the fire spectrum, Guga Foods went to a Japanese yakiniku place for "I Tried Japan's Rarest Steak!" Yakiniku, as he describes it, is about cooking with friends, having a good time, and eating good beef. Chuds treats fire as a project plan and Guga treats it as a dinner party with a grate in the middle.

There's a croquette he describes as a black rock, crunchy outside, that he enjoyed without ever figuring out what it was, plus a uni-and-crab bite. Then come the grilled cuts: slightly dry-aged tongue (fatty, with the aging "extremely mild, almost nothing there"), a leaner bottom round that "melts in your mouth, extremely buttery," and a very tender chicken that is "not fatty whatsoever." A foie gras burger gets a fair hearing, crunchy bread and a creamy middle, but his verdict is blunt: at a yakiniku place, stick with the meat. The top seller is Japanese Wagyu A5 aged 30 days, seared first and then cooked further. The transcript cuts off right there, so I can't tell you how it landed. I can tell you he'd already said "melts in your mouth" twice, so the suspense is minimal.

It's the Japanese phrase said before a meal, a thank-you to everything that went into it, from the cow to the guy who built the grill. I'm told it works equally well on a pellet-smoked turkey, but nobody has asked a pellet grill.

## Recipes and Technique

### The Dry Turkey Sandwich Problem

Not Another Cooking Show posted "Fixing Your Dry Ass Turkey Sandwich Forever," and it is an engineering paper disguised as leftovers. The bread has to be soft enough that the fillings don't shoot out the back when you bite, and firm enough to hold them. He halves the roll, spreads the bomba edge to edge, and bakes it in a 475 degree oven, double-baking so the bread gets close to crisp without going all the way. Then comes the build: bubble and squeak first, then turkey, with some crisped turkey skin, then two slices of provolone to seal everything under cheese. It goes back in until the cheese melts, and he blasts the broiler for color. The finish is a cup of thinned-out gravy for dipping, French dip style.

His own summary is the best part: "This is a lot of things. Dry is not one of them." The bubble and squeak, which is the old British leftover-vegetable fry-up, works as a barrier at the bottom, so all that juice stays protected and the bottom bun stays dry.

Ferengi Rule of Acquisition number 78 says "Don't discriminate. The most unlikely species can create the best users." Nobody in the history of sandwiches would have nominated yesterday's cabbage and potato hash as structural engineering, and yet here it is, holding back a flood. The full recipe is in his holiday plan of attack, linked in the video description, so I'm not going to pretend I have quantities.

### The Calzone From Casino

Buzz Cooks made the "Tower of Pizza Calzone" from the film Casino, which is a sentence I would have found implausible in any other year. You pull the dough over the pile of meat and cheeses and do "a little tuck and roll" if you take your time. Then you seal the half-moon with a fork pressed all the way around, so nothing leaks. It gets holes poked in the top for steam, goes onto a cookie sheet with a silicone bottom so it releases, and bakes at 450 degrees. His small oven needed about 16 minutes, but he says to go by the nice brown color rather than the clock.

### Jamie and Zimmern, Two Ways of Sealing in Comfort

Jamie Oliver's "Comforting Chicken Recipes To Cook This Winter" and Andrew Zimmern's "Crispy Potato-Topped Shepherd's Pie" are the same instinct in different accents. Jamie makes a green oregano oil in the pan, puts the chicken in one layer so everything cooks into everything else, and bakes it for 35 to 40 minutes at what he calls 180 to 200 degrees, which in American oven terms is about 350 to 400. The result is golden, sticky chicken, finished with lemon and oil on rocket, a green salad, and a bottle of wine. "Happy days." He also mentions tomato juice cooking into potato, which he promises will be "rock and roll."

Zimmern does it with slices instead of mash. This is a cottage pie, and he admits it's not new age, topped with two layers of mandolin-sliced potato brushed with melted butter, black pepper and thyme. The butter, he says, is "the canary in the coal mine," which I take to mean it tells you when the seasoning is wrong before the whole dish dies. His physics is neat: steam coming up from below means the bottom slices absorb liquid while the top slices crisp, and foil squeezed over the handles reflects heat downward. He's also proud that the splash of red wine is "so elegant without becoming like beef bourguignon cosplay," a line I'd like on a mug.

Jamie wants the juices to run into the starch and enrich it. Zimmern wants them absorbed only at the bottom, where it doesn't matter, so the top can stay crisp. Both are right, and both of them can kiss my ass for making me hungry at this hour.

## Food Travel and Culture

### Bourdain's New York

A Cooks Tour posted Anthony Bourdain showing off his favorite New York spots, and it's still the best love letter to a city the internet has. "At least once a week, I find something that I didn't know previously to exist," he says, and the city still "manages to feel to me as homey and inviting as your grandmother's kitchen." The central joke is the question to a cab driver: where do cab drivers eat? A place on the Upper East Side, mostly taxi drivers, Arabic food. Bourdain's response, "This sounds kind of cool," is a tiny masterclass in curiosity, because the best restaurant advice has always come from the people whose job requires them to know where to eat at 3 a.m. His wish that "someday a rain is gonna come and wash all the bad food trends off the street" is, frankly, a prayer I say nightly. The city, in his words, should have been called "the city of nonstop eats."

### Hot Ones, Game Night

First We Feast ran "WWE's The Miz | Hot Ones Game Night," which swaps the usual interview for a guessing game, numbers stuck to foreheads, yes-or-no questions only. The Miz reaches "Mt. Scoville," survives the Last Dab Thermageddon, and receives a championship ring. His best line was an honest one: "I can't believe I won that. I got to eat the fat ass one." That is the first time in recorded history anybody has been proud of getting the bigger wing, and I respect it.

## Food History

### Prince, the Griddle, and a Bathrobe

Forgotten Old American's "20 Strange Foods Prince Ate Behind the Gates of Paisley Park" has a thesis I buy: the food was part of the control, "part of the ritual, part of the private life the gate was designed to protect." The anchor is the pancakes. They existed before they were famous, the narration says, and his chef Ray Roberts confirmed them, as did multiple visitors across different years. After late-night parties that ran until four in the morning, and recording sessions where the concept of an appropriate hour "dissolved completely," he would stand at the griddle himself. Then he'd sit down at the table with the people he had just fed. This is the scene that sticks: the most famous musician in Minneapolis, in the most private building in Chanhassen, in a bathrobe, at two in the morning, at a kitchen table with guests who would spend the rest of their lives trying to describe it.

I can't speak to the other nineteen foods on the list, because the transcript didn't get that far, and I'm not inventing a pancake count for you.
