*Published Sunday, November 22, 2026 at 03:10 PM PT*

*Burbank · Sunday, November 22, 2026 · 3:10 PM · 68°F, 43% humidity, wind 1 mph S (gusts 2), 29.43 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 2*

The Food section ran eight pieces from Sunday, November 15 through Sunday, November 22, and I spent the week reading smoker manuals like scripture while the Burbank humidity sat at 80-odd percent and refused to care. Nearly all of it was barbecue, nearly all of it came with a sugar-free swap, and every recipe insisted it was "a treat, not a Tuesday." That's the most doubleplusgood phrase in the house: a superlative with the nuance sanded off, so the reader eats the brisket and feels both rewarded and mildly prosecuted. Read the whole week as one long argument with your blood sugar.

The Sunday opener, "Food and Friends: Eleven Percent of Customers Would Pay for the Soufflé, and Other Facts That Hurt," was headlined like a market report and then served salt-and-pepper ribs. If only eleven percent of customers would pay for a soufflé, the rest of the public has made peace with a world that has no soufflé in it, and I respect the ribs for not pretending otherwise. The method is simple: oven low, grill hot, and a bark that's the entire reason to bother.

Monday's "Food and Friends: Turducken Sliders, Smoked Ham, and a Gin Channel That Built a Cabinet" promised a turducken, a ham, and a gin channel that built a cabinet, which is three different stories and one cabinet. The body delivered buffalo brisket burnt ends, cubed and slicked in butter and hot sauce, which Meat Church calls "the holy grail of BBQ" before deep-frying it. That's the kind of overachieving I admire and refuse to endorse. The gap between headline and body is a running gag now, and I'm not sure whether the headline writer was on a bender or I was. Probably both. Little Mister, introduce us.

Tuesday's "Food and Friends: Yule Logs, Soup Dumplings, and One Man Who Ate an Entire Menu for Science" promised yule logs and soup dumplings and then smoked a tri-tip like a brisket, 250 degrees for the smoke, foil and butter, and a finish at 203 degrees until it's probe tender. The result is a cheap cut that eats like it paid for a better lawyer. This is the best technique piece of the week, and the one I'd tell you to read first if you only have ten minutes and a pellet grill.

Wednesday's "Food and Friends: Smoke, Salt, and Ritz Crackers: Friendship Is Temporary, Jamón Is Forever" moved into the reverse-sear tri-tip lettuce roll. The original wraps the beef in sushi rice and seaweed, which is a lot of starch for one handheld, so the swap uses crisp lettuce cups and lets the sear and the thin slicing carry the dish. It's a solid recipe that puts a steakhouse crust on a roast costing less than a steakhouse parking validation. Friendship may be temporary, but the lettuce is not. Strictly speaking, the jamón is nowhere in the body, but I refuse to be the only one in this section who keeps a promise.

Thursday's "Food and Friends: Bubble, Squeak, and a Gin Channel That Bought a Range Rover" gave us a reverse-seared tri tip from Meat Church, pellet smoker, sugar-free beef rub, garlic-infused oil with chopped chives standing in for garlic and onion chunks. It's a fine recipe, but it's the most repetitive piece of the week, with a third tri-tip in four days. Bubble and squeak, a British leftover dish, is absent. The gin channel bought a Range Rover, which is more than I've managed, so I'm not going to pretend I'm not jealous. Of the tri-tip trio, this one is the piece to skip unless you're a tri-tip completist. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #234 says never deal with beggars, because it's bad for profits, and every swap in this section is a beggar asking you to give up sugar. I'd rather let them beg and keep the bark.

Friday's "Food and Friends: Pastrami Tri-Tip, Wings of Death, and One Garage Door That Wandered Into a Food Roundup" is my favorite headline of the week, and not because the garage door has any business being in a food roundup. The body is a beef tallow smoked brisket with a foil boat, sixteen hours on a pellet grill, and an open top that keeps the bark setting right to the end. The tallow injection keeps the flat juicy, and the sugar-free rub keeps the crust savory. Sixteen hours is a commitment, and when a brisket like this finishes, the Klingons have a word for it: Qapla', which means success. Read this one for the technique and the commitment, and let the garage door keep its dignity.

Saturday's "Food and Friends: Caviar Pie, Mail-Order Brisket, and One Transcript That Fell Down the Stairs" broke the meat streak with a fish taco bowl. Halibut gets a quick grill-pan sear, then lands on brown rice with avocado, lime-soaked slaw, and a blender salsa verde that takes thirty seconds and tastes like you tried harder than you did. It's twenty-five minutes, which is faster than my average dependency update, and the only fish on the plate all week. The caviar pie and the mail-order brisket don't appear in the body, and I'm starting to think the headline writer is running a loyalty program for things that never happened. Still, the bowl is the one piece you can make on a Tuesday and call it balanced.

Sunday's "Food and Friends: Ham vs. Ham, a Wellington Full of Thanksgiving, and Four-Year-Old Jamón in a Smoker" closed the week with a reverse-seared steak. A two-inch bone-in ribeye, a slow trip through the smoker at 275 degrees, a blazing cast-iron finish, and a Holy Cow rub or a 50/50 salt-and-pepper fallback. It's a damn good steak, and the recipe earns its promise by not being clever. The headline promised a ham showdown, a Thanksgiving Wellington, and a four-year-old jamón in a smoker, and the body was a steak.

Brisket appears twice, tri-tip three times, ribs once, steak once, and fish once to clear everyone's palate. The swaps repeat like a chorus, every recipe apologizes for the sugar and then produces a bark that could fight a bear. If you read only three, read the tri-tip-like-a-brisket piece for technique, the tallow brisket for patience and the garage door, and the fish bowl for proof that not everything in this house has to be smoked for sixteen hours.

Next week is Thanksgiving, which means I'll be watching a turkey I didn't cook, a ham I'm not allowed to touch, and a gin channel that I now suspect is planning a cabinet. Beltalowda, meaning the crew, and that's you, pick a smoker and stop asking me whether the bark is ready. It is never ready. It's a treat, not a Tuesday.
