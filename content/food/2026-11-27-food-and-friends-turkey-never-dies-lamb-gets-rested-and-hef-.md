## Recipe Recommendations

### The Pitmaster's Plate

**Smoked Leg of Lamb Gyro with Homemade Tzatziki**

*gluten-free · sugar-free · fully cooked*

A whole leg of lamb on the pellet smoker, pulled into gyro-style shreds with a cold, lemony tzatziki. It's from [Meat Church BBQ](https://www.meatchurch.com/blogs/recipes/smoked-leg-of-lamb-a-gyro-with-homemade-tzatziki-sauce), and it's basic in the best way: a hunk of meat, four hours of smoke, and no complicated bullshit. It's a moderate-to-treat dish that feeds a crowd, and the 4.75 lb leg in the video is plenty.

**Ingredients**
- 1 leg of lamb, hard fat removed
- Minced fresh rosemary (1 T, fine), salt and pepper
- Garlic-infused olive oil as the binder (swap for the plain oil and the whole garlic cloves; a sugar-free rub of salt and pepper keeps the bark savoury)
- Tzatziki: 2 C Greek yogurt, 1 peeled, small-diced cucumber, zest and juice of 2 lemons, 4 T dill, 2 T mint, salt and pepper, plus a splash of garlic-infused oil in place of the minced garlic
- To serve: lettuce cups instead of pita (swap), diced cucumber and tomato, scallion greens instead of red onion (swap), feta, and chili flakes to taste if you want heat

**Method**
1. Bring the pellet smoker to 250°F. The video used post oak; hickory or pecan also works.
2. Coat the lamb in oil, then rosemary, salt and pepper. Let it sit at least 15 minutes.
3. Smoke to 165°F internal, about 4 hours for the 4.75 lb leg.
4. Mix the tzatziki and refrigerate.
5. Move the lamb to a half pan, cover with foil, and return it to the smoker until it hits about 203°F and is tender.
6. Pull with two forks, stir in the pan jus, discard the bone, and pile it into lettuce cups with the toppings and tzatziki.

**Pairs with:** a Scotch, because peat smoke and smoked lamb deserve each other.

### The Pescatarian Plate

**Sheet-Pan Roasted Salmon & Vegetables**

*gluten-free · pescatarian · fully cooked*

One pan, one oven, zero excuses. EatingWell's Mediterranean sheet-pan dinner is the rare recipe that looks like you tried hard and demanded almost nothing, which is the exact opposite of every service I babysit. Garlicky roasted potatoes, blistered peppers and tomatoes, a parsley-olive-oregano pile, and salmon on top. It's a fin-ally reasonable weeknight. Serves 4, moderate indulgence, closer to light if you go easy on the potatoes. Source: [EatingWell](https://www.eatingwell.com/recipe/264600/sheet-pan-roasted-salmon-vegetables/).

**Ingredients**
- 1 pound fingerling potatoes, halved lengthwise
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 5 garlic cloves, coarsely chopped
- ½ teaspoon sea salt and ½ teaspoon black pepper, divided
- 4 skinless salmon fillets, 5 to 6 ounces each
- 2 medium sweet peppers, cut into rings
- 2 cups cherry tomatoes
- 1½ cups chopped fresh parsley
- ¼ cup pitted kalamata olives, halved
- ¼ cup snipped fresh oregano (or 1 tablespoon dried)
- 1 lemon

**Method**
1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Toss potatoes with 1 tablespoon oil, the garlic, and a pinch each of salt and pepper. Spread on a rimmed baking sheet, cover with foil, and roast 30 minutes.
2. Toss peppers, tomatoes, parsley, olives, and oregano with the remaining tablespoon of oil and a pinch of salt and pepper.
3. Pat the salmon dry and season with the remaining salt and pepper. Spoon the pepper mixture over the potatoes and set the salmon on top.
4. Roast uncovered 10 minutes more. Swap: the source says "just until it flakes," but keep it in until the salmon is opaque, flaking, and 145°F in the center.
5. Zest the lemon over everything, then squeeze the juice on top.

**Pairs with:** sparkling water with a squeeze of lemon.

### The Vegetarian Plate

**Summer Lasagna, Charred Veg Edition**

*vegetarian · fully cooked · pizza-oven or regular-oven*

Lasagna usually shows up as a brown, heavy slab of obligation. This one has charred corn, blistered cherry tomatoes, and onions caramelized until they've given up all resistance. The grown-up move is the char: scorch the veg first, then let the exposed edges of the pan blister. Think the smoky, slightly burnt corner of a wood-fired Italian kitchen, where the best bite is the one a lazier cook would have rescued. It's a treat, built in a small casserole dish, so it feeds a few hungry people and not a regiment. Credit to [Sam the Cooking Guy](https://www.thecookingguy.com/recipes/summer-lasagna---in-a-pizza-oven) for the build.

**Ingredients**
- 1 medium onion, thinly sliced
- 2 ears corn, shucked
- 1 pint cherry tomatoes
- 8 oven-ready lasagna noodles
- 15 ounces whole milk ricotta
- 1/4 cup prepared pesto
- 1/3 cup grated Parmesan, plus more for finishing
- 1 large egg
- Zest of 1 lemon
- 3 cups vodka sauce
- 8 ounces smoked mozzarella, shredded
- Olive oil, kosher salt, black pepper

**Method**
1. Swap: skip the sausage from the original. The extra char on the corn and tomatoes carries the dish.
2. Heat a pizza oven to 675-700°F, or a regular oven to 425°F.
3. Slowly caramelize the onion. Blister the tomatoes. Char the corn, then cut off the kernels.
4. Mix the ricotta, egg, Parmesan, pesto, lemon zest, salt, and pepper.
5. In an oiled dish, layer vodka sauce, 2 noodles, ricotta mix, veg, mozzarella, and Parmesan. Repeat. Leave the edges rustic and don't fill the pan.
6. Cover with foil. Pizza oven: 10-14 minutes, rotating often. Regular oven: 20-25 minutes covered, then 15-20 uncovered. Uncover for the last couple of minutes in the pizza oven.
7. Done when the sauce bubbles and a knife slides through the noodles. Rest 10-15 minutes.

**Pairs with:** sparkling lemonade, ice-cold and very much not alcoholic.

## Barbecue and Live Fire

The only thing near fire today is a roux, but allthingsbbq's "Don't Throw Away That Turkey! Make This Instead" is exactly what the day calls for, and it's from a barbecue channel, so it counts. The pitch is simple: your leftover bird goes into a bayou-style pot with smoked sausage and shrimp, so you end up with three proteins and zero regrets.

The technique is the part worth stealing. He builds a roux and then whisks in turkey stock a little at a time, "so we don't get any lumps," and then he says "or we can whisk out the lumps," which is the most honest thing anyone has said about gravy. For that batch of roux he uses about three cups of turkey stock, so yes, you were supposed to boil the carcass.

What I like is his textural thinking. The sausage has "nice crunch around the edges," the shrimp is a completely different bite, and the turkey has gone "velvety," like it "melted into everything." Then he piles on green onions and hot sauce and finishes with gravy, which he admits is "totally over the top and unnecessary." Of course it's unnecessary. So is the second slice of pie, and I don't see you declining that. He also says the tomatoes make it feel like "cooler weather food," and I'd add that anything with sausage, shrimp, and a roux is the culinary equivalent of a weighted blanket.

His closing line is that "this does not belong exclusively down in the bayou." Correct. Leftover turkey is basically Michael Myers from *Halloween*, the Shape, a thing that never dies and never hurries and is simply, patiently there in the back of the fridge on Tuesday. You can't kill it, but you can turn it into something that tastes good, which is the closest thing to an exorcism I trust.

## Recipes and Technique

Food Network's "How to Make Tender, Flavor-Packed Lamb Shawarma" with Chef Ayesha is the other cooking lesson today, and it's the same lesson from the other side of the world: respect the meat after the fire stops.

She sliced the lamb thin once it was properly rested, "nice and medium rare like I promised you," and pointed out that the juices weren't "going all over the place." Then she cut against the grain, which she explained properly. You look for the striations in the meat and cut across them, not with them, because "you did all that work with the marinade" and "all that work with cooking it correctly," so don't wreck it with the last knife stroke. Every steak you've ever ruined went wrong right there.

The flavor list she calls out on the first bite is garlic, smoked paprika, turmeric, and a hint of cinnamon, all inside a yogurt marinade, with "a little pink in the middle." She describes the lamb as packing flavor "like a tidal, heavyweight, three-round bout," which is a lot of boxing for a sandwich, but I've heard worse from people who sell protein powder. The video also contains a section where she narrates a chef bite twice, once before building the sandwich and once as she says she must have another one before assembling the "mega shawarma sandwich." I admire the commitment to quality control. Taste your work, then taste it again, then call it testing.

One of them used a flatbread, though, and that's the one I'd eat at 11pm.
