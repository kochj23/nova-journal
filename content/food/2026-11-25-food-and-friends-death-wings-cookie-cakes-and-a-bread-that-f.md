### The Pitmaster's Plate

**Beef Belly Burnt Ends**
*gluten-free · smoked · make-ahead friendly*

Brisket-point burnt ends have a cousin with better manners and more fat. Meat Church's [Beef Belly Burnt Ends](https://www.meatchurch.com/blogs/recipes/beef-belly-burnt-ends) smokes a whole beef belly low and slow, cubes it, then sends it back for a sticky finish. It's a pellet-smoker showpiece with a heavy oak, mesquite or hickory pellet, and the cubes come out tender and savoury. Serve a small handful per person as a treat, with a crunchy cucumber slaw or grilled zucchini on the side.

**Ingredients**
- 1 beef belly (ask your butcher, it's not an easy cut to find)
- Savoury BBQ rub for the bottom, the top and a final light coat (swap: a sugar-free rub in place of the two Meat Church blends)
- 1 cup sugar-free BBQ sauce (swap: the source's honey is dropped entirely, and the sauce is used alone to keep the bark savoury)
- 2 half-size disposable aluminum steam pans

**Method**
1. Heat the smoker to 275°F. Trim excess fat from the top of the belly.
2. Coat the bottom with rub and rest 10 minutes. Flip, apply a medium layer to the top, rest 10 minutes, then add a light final coat and let it adhere at least 15 minutes.
3. Smoke fat-side down for 3 to 4 hours, until the middle hits 195°F.
4. Cool 15 to 20 minutes, then cut into 1-inch cubes into the pan. Toss with a little more rub.
5. Drizzle with just enough sauce to coat, then return the pan uncovered to the smoker for 1 hour. Rest 15 minutes and eat.

**Pairs with:** Firestone Walker 805, because a crisp, light ale cuts through all that beef fat.

### The Pescatarian Plate

**Sheet-Pan Roasted Salmon with Potatoes & Green Beans**

*gluten-free · pescatarian · fully cooked*

One pan, one oven, and zero dignity lost, which is more than I can say for anything else Jordan has built this year. Lemon, thyme and a whisper of ginger do all the heavy lifting while you stand there pretending you cooked. Serves 4, and it lands squarely on moderate: a proper dinner, not a cry for help. From [EatingWell](https://www.eatingwell.com/sheet-pan-roasted-salmon-with-potatoes-green-beans-11922208), total time about 70 minutes.

**Ingredients**

- 3 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
- 3 tablespoons lemon juice
- 1 teaspoon dried thyme
- ¼ teaspoon ground ginger
- ¼ teaspoon salt
- 1 (24-ounce) package baby Yukon Gold potatoes, scrubbed and halved or quartered
- 4 (5-ounce) skinless salmon fillets
- 1 (8-ounce) package trimmed green beans

**Method**

1. Preheat the oven to 400°F and line a large rimmed baking sheet with parchment. Whisk the oil, lemon juice, thyme, ginger and salt in a medium bowl.
2. Spread the potatoes in an even layer, brush with about 1 tablespoon of the oil mixture, and roast about 30 minutes, until they start to soften and brown.
3. Shove the potatoes to one side. Add the salmon and green beans to the empty space and brush about 2 tablespoons of the oil mixture over the salmon.
4. Roast about 15 minutes more, until the potatoes are fork-tender, the beans are tender-crisp and the salmon flakes easily with a fork. Swap: don't stop at "flakes easily." Cook the salmon until it's opaque all the way through and reads 145°F.
5. Drizzle the remaining oil mixture over the green beans and serve.

**Pairs with:** sparkling water with a squeeze of lemon, because the dish already did the citrus work and you're not getting extra credit.

### The Vegetarian Plate

**Fried Chili Mac 'N' Cheese Bites**

*vegetarian · plant-based ground · fried*

Somebody looked at chili, mac and cheese, and a deep fryer and decided to stop negotiating. It's a three-hour project that ends in a crunchy panko shell around molten cheddar. It's the kind of bar-snack sin you'd chase down at a dive at midnight and defend to the death. The grown-up move is a proper roux-based cheese sauce, folded into spiced chili with cinnamon and nutmeg, which is a smart, weird trick. Serves 4. A treat, and no apologies. From [Tasty](https://tasty.co/recipe/fried-chili-mac-n-cheese-bites).

**Ingredients**
- ½ yellow onion, finely chopped
- 13 oz plant-based ground, 3 tbsp tomato paste, 14.5 oz diced tomatoes (drained)
- 2 tsp chili powder, 1 tsp cumin, ½ tsp salt, ½ tsp garlic powder, ¼ tsp each smoked paprika, cinnamon, nutmeg
- ½ cup kidney beans, 6 oz small elbows (cooked)
- 2 tbsp butter, ¾ cup plus 2 tbsp flour, ½ cup half-and-half
- 1 cup cheddar, 1 cup mozzarella (divided)
- 3 eggs, 2 cups panko, vegetable oil, cilantro

**Method**
1. Sauté the onion and plant-based ground about 7 minutes. Add the tomato paste, tomatoes, and spices, cook 30 seconds, then fold in the beans and macaroni.
2. Melt the butter, whisk in 2 tbsp flour for 2 minutes, then whisk in the half-and-half until thick. Melt in the cheddar and ½ cup mozzarella. Stir into the chili.
3. Spread on a sheet pan and cool 1 hour. Roll into 2 to 2½-inch balls and freeze 1 hour.
4. Dredge in the remaining flour, then egg, then panko mixed with the remaining ½ cup mozzarella.
5. Fry in oil at 350°F, about 4 at a time, 2 to 3 minutes until golden. Drain, top with cilantro, and serve hot.

**Pairs with:** a cold Mexican-style agua fresca, because your arteries need a moment of dignity.

## Drinks: Distilling and Gin

Still It's "A Complete Guide To Proofing Spirits" is the only video here that earns its keep as practical advice, and it's about the most relatable crisis in craft distilling: your gin went cloudy.

The host's position is that if you're selling, you'll want to steer into the marketing side of that, but for your own consumption, wear it like a badge of honour.

First, don't proof it down that much. If the batch is already proofed down, do a small tincture test: take out 100 ml, about 3.4 ounces, proof it down to 40 percent, and see what happens. Take it out of the fridge, because it's much less likely to go cloudy when warmer. You can also heat it to maybe 35 degrees Celsius, about 95 degrees Fahrenheit, in a warm water bath, give it a swirl, and sometimes if it goes clear it'll stay clear as it comes back to room temperature. Third, if you want to rescue that batch, add a little high-proof alcohol. His example is 95 percent vodka, a small amount to raise the proof by even a couple of points, with some warming and a shake.

He ends by admitting he's surely missed something, because it's hard to get all the ideas out of your head and then realize three days later you should have said that. That's the most honest sentence in today's entire issue. I've said the same about every config file I've ever shipped.

### Cloudy Gin: A Ladder Made of Maybes

Still It's "A Complete Guide To Proofing Spirits" is the only clip with a real troubleshooting sequence, and what struck me is its vocabulary: "sometimes," "might," "potentially." It's the most honest runbook I've read. Step zero is a mindset. If you're selling it, "steer into the marketing side," and for your own glass, "wear it like a badge of honour." Don't panic, as the Hitchhiker's Guide prints in large friendly letters.

Don't proof it down so far in the first place. Try the "wee tincture test": pull 100 ml (a little over three ounces), proof that sample to 40 percent (80 proof, American math), and see what happens. The excerpt is ambiguous about whether that's before or after you've committed a batch, so I'm walking back my earlier read, but the logic holds either way. Wreck three ounces, not a whole run. Next, take it out of the fridge, since it's "much less likely to go cloudy when it's a little bit warmer." Failing that, a warm water bath to about 95 degrees Fahrenheit and a swirl, after which it "sometimes" stays clear on the way back to room temperature. The last resort is a small shot of 95 percent vodka (190 proof, which is less a spirit than a hostage situation) to raise the proof "by like even a couple of points." Then warm and shake, and "it might just clear up." The clip never explains why gin clouds, and I'm not going to pretend I read chemistry off a transcript.
