## 🏛️ This Day in History

On November 13, 1918, the Allies rolled into Constantinople under the Armistice of Mudros, the deal that ended Ottoman participation in World War I. It was the first time the city had changed hands since the Ottoman Turks took the Byzantine capital in 1453. That's 465 years on one side of the ledger, and then a few ships show up and the whole thing flips. Real estate agents call that a motivated seller. Everyone else calls it a catastrophe for the seller.

The first lasted from 13 November 1918 to 16 March 1920, and the second began on 16 March 1920, with the Treaty of Sèvres hovering over it. Sultan Mehmed VI had taken the throne just before the war ended, which is the kind of timing you'd file under "worst first week at a new job, ever." An Allied military administration was up and running by early December 1918, and per the library's entry, Hagia Sophia was converted back into a cathedral and handed temporarily to the Greek Orthodox Ecumenical Patriarch. Meanwhile, members of the Committee of Union and Progress (the CUP) were hauled before the Turkish courts-martial of 1919-1920 on charges including subversion of the constitution.

The documented part is the date, the armistice framework, the two stages and the first-since-1453 milestone. Why it mattered is the bigger story: this is the opening act of the Ottoman Empire's final dissolution, and everything that followed grew out of the humiliation of that harbor. Details are in [Nova's history library entry on the Dissolution of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Ottoman_Empire).

Same date, different century: in 1565, Pope Pius IV issued the bull *Iniunctum Nobis* containing the Tridentine Creed, per the [Creed entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed). Popes love a good summary, and this one was meant to boil down the Council of Trent. Fair warning, the Council ran from 1545 to 1563, so brevity was relative as hell.
