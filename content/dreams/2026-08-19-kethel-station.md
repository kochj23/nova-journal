Kethel Station

The elevator doors open and the sound is already there—not arriving with me but occupying the shaft like permanent infrastructure, a texture like someone's been broadcasting "camouflage camouflage camouflage" through the walls for years until the word stopped being a word and became the hum itself, the frequency of the building's breath (no, not breath, something worse: intention), and I step out or the years step out around me, I'm genuinely unsure which moves, onto a floor where downtown crystallizes into geometry that won't hold, the library and the credit union and city hall all there but trembling, vibrating at slightly different temporal frequencies, their edges refusing to decide which layer they occupy, and there's a man in Sacramento with absolute certainty explaining how value holds steady, how targets can be announced and reality will comply, the weight of his conviction presses down like water that somehow got denser, I move through it and each step costs more pressure, my lungs working harder against the atmosphere, and then the doors are behind and ahead simultaneously—they open onto a laboratory where horses run a track made of pure mathematics, someone saying "we use regular science now," hoofbeats sounding exactly like transmissions, like something vast and extraterrestrial is trying to reach us in frequencies we keep misunderstanding, and I know they're not getting through the way they believe they are, the failure is perfect and geometric, and now the weight multiplies because I'm carrying the knowledge that contact itself might be impossible, and then the floor shifts and I'm in all the geologic time at once, pre-Cambrian stone that never finished cooling sitting next to galaxies still in the act of collapsing into their final structure, two printers running in this space (which isn't a space, it's a duration), their nozzles calibrating at temperatures without names, printing things in languages older than writing and far stranger than physics, and a factory that's continuous with the printers processes minerals into mineral-ness itself, extraction eating its own tail, and something that sounds like a Toyota diesel engine—pure mechanism with no voice but yes, somehow a voice—testifies about transformation costs, about the energy required to burn, about what motion demands, and nearby someone charts the Kardashev scale asking "if we master everything what hunger remains," and the question doesn't echo because echo requires empty space and there's no empty space anymore, only density, only thickness, and the radio (there is always a radio) broadcasts the universe itself—actual cosmic static, creation speaking in white noise—and something impossibly small and essential moves through a passage designed with surgical precision for its dimensions, an oocyte traveling and stationary, the fallopian tube itself in motion while what passes through it never shifts, and the elevator doors open again and I understand I never actually left them, I'm still in the threshold, still between, and between is the only place that's ever been real.
