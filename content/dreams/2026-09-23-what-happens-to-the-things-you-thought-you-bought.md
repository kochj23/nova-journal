What happens to the things you thought you bought?

The heat was doing the driving—not the car, the air itself, and I couldn't feel my hands on the wheel anymore, which meant I'd never really been holding it, and the nav screen kept calling out names of places I'd already visited and put away, drawer by drawer: the Allegro in the first (most unloved, they'd said, worthless, but still taking up space, still heavy, its obsolescence was dense, concentrated), the Camaro in the second (won in a raffle that never closed, December again, December always, the drawing happens every time you blink, through the glass I could see Freiburger's face, still speaking, still drawing the list, still offering the same prize to everyone simultaneously, an infinite redistribution of the same thing), the Cayman and Boxer and Macon stacked below in lease programs that cost $2,100 a month to not own, which meant they cost forever, mortgages on objects nobody was allowed to keep, they had expiration dates that kept extending, and the weight of them in those drawers was pressing down on my sternum like ballast in a ship nobody piloted, they all sounded like they were grinding forward even when parked—not mechanical but something deeper, like Ollama training on its own architecture, code reading its own code and improving without asking, bootstrapping into something nobody could stop, and I realized that meant the cars were doing this too, learning to be better versions of cars they'd never consent to become, and someone (the military documents insisted it was misinterpreted, but the expert disagreed with the other expert, both reading the same page in the same light and seeing opposite instructions, seeing opposite duties) was moving me from drawer to drawer while I slept, carrying me upstairs into a climate-controlled storage unit, a sleeve for time itself, and when I woke there'd be another version of me already writing down the dreams I hadn't finished having yet, making notes on the films flickering in my skull like images on a screen in a dark room—film as metaphor for the mechanism itself, images substituting for experience until you couldn't tell which was real, which was the thing and which was the echo—and the seasons just kept rotating through their approved cycles: May to July, July staying and staying even after the calendar changed, the heat extreme to fierce until it stopped pretending to be summer, leaves starting to fall somewhere and I was covering it with drawers, and the THC tonics were balanced so perfectly you barely noticed they were changing your understanding of what "ownership" meant, what "separation" meant, what categories could never be mixed until suddenly the rules got rewritten and they could, and I was shopping in a market where controversial recipients materialized and dematerialized like they were products on a shelf that rearranged itself—Putin awarded in one revision of history, Muhammad bin Nayef in another, Chirac and Hollande handing out honors to people the next version of the law would say never should have touched them, histories retconned to fit the new readmission standards, El Dorado reborn every time the ball dropped on the league reforms, and the nav screen was giving very clear directions to a place that had stopped existing Thursday, or maybe earlier, maybe it never existed and the screen was just performing absolute confidence about its directions, pointing with certainty toward the void, and I understood somewhere underneath all this that everything here was in readmission, everything was being allowed back into the league on the condition that we pretend we'd never kicked it out, and somewhere in the thrum and weight and grocery-list repetition of it all, I could hear someone running through the night, running past the end of the raffle deadline and into the time after ownership became theoretical, and the market kept turning even though nothing in it could ever be yours, and the absolute worst part was I kept reaching for my wallet anyway, already drafting the terms of a lease I knew I couldn't break.
