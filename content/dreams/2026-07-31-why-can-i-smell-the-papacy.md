**Why can I smell the papacy?**

The catalog lists seventeen galleries, which matters because I need to prove I've been here exactly as long as the attendant says—no longer, no shorter. She keeps checking her clipboard, but the order keeps reversing itself. Gallery Four smells of sovereign independence and archival dust. Gallery Six smells of fire and metal and some chemical potency I can't name, and the attendant insists it comes before Gallery Four, though I walked past Four first. "You're confused about the sequence," she says. "The sequence is about the observer, not the walking."

I try to leave.

The exits all smell identical: institutional authority mixed with outdated pride, like someone's million-person decision archive left open to rot. The weight of that smell presses against my sternum. Every door I open, the scent gets heavier, not less. The attendant follows me with the clipboard. "You need to verify the causes," she says. "Which smell made which smell come next?"

"Smells don't cause each other," I tell her.

"Exactly," she says, and writes something down. "So you admit you don't know."

I keep trying to leave through different galleries. Gallery Eleven is all pharmaceutical comparisons—this one is seven percent more potent than that one, this one is nearly as potent as something I've never encountered. The weights feel wrong here; the smells press down from above instead of rising from the floor. I'm not smelling them. Something is smelling me through them. I can taste the distinction trying to collapse.

Gallery Nine repeats itself. Fire, fire, fire. But not the object—the insistence. Someone singing it, or the walls singing it, or the smell itself vocalizing. The attendant catches up. "You were supposed to know the first smell," she says. "Before you entered. That was the deal. You were supposed to come in already knowing which one started the chain."

"I wasn't told there was a chain."

"No," she agrees, writing again. "But you double down and insist there isn't one, because that's easier than admitting you forgot. Isn't it? That's what they all do. You heard it differently somewhere else, and now you're protecting that first memory."

The museum has three exits, or seventeen exits, or exits that only exist if I'm not looking at them. The weight increases when I walk toward them. My sternum is now the consistency of ancient lead. In Gallery Twelve, I can see through to other galleries beyond the walls—a Harvard classroom, a war archive, something institutional and stacked with layers of access I no longer have permission for. The attendant is still behind me, or ahead of me, or she's become the clipboard itself now, a flat voice reading smells aloud as if they're facts that need verification.

"The effect has to come before the cause here," she says, pointing to something I can't locate. "That's the policy."

"That's not how causality works."

"Right. It's a construct. So you made it up, and now you're bound by it. Everyone does this."

I step into Gallery Fifteen, which smells like perception instead of objects. Like the idea of papacy instead of papacy itself. Like fire remembered by someone who never saw fire. The weight on my chest becomes weight I'm not feeling externally in my body—I know Hume said this was the problem. Power and necessity are qualities of perceptions, not things. So the suffocation is subjective. So the inability to leave is something I'm perceiving, not something the museum is doing.

The attendant's voice comes from inside the smell: "You've been here seventeen minutes and also weeks. Both observers are correct. You can leave and also remain. The catalog is complete and also impossible to fill."

I push toward what was an entrance. The smell reaches me before the door does, which means I'm walking through it backward, or it's walking through me. The attendant calls out something kind—a permission I didn't know I needed—but her voice sounds like seventeen different people saying contradictory things, and I can't verify which one is the original complaint.

When I finally step outside, the air smells exactly like the papacy again, which means I never left at all, or the museum follows, or leaving and staying were never distinct categories to begin with.
