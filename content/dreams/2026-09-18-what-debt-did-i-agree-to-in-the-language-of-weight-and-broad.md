What debt did I agree to in the language of weight and broadcast static?

Three dice landed in my palm still warm from soil that shouldn't have held anything so precisely weighted, and the radio voice—LAPD Northeast, the constant hum behind my teeth—began reading coordinates that were also my address that were also the terms of a deal I didn't remember signing, and when I looked up the orchard had stopped being an orchard, transforming into rows of clocks instead of trees, each ticking at a different speed, some fast enough to blur into white noise, others so slow each second landed with the weight of a coin in a box, and a figure that was also three figures, or one figure seen from three different angles like a turning camera—producer, director, star, all the roles collapsing into the same shifting shape—was telling me the rules of a game that didn't exist until the speaking and then immediately fell apart, replaced by new rules that also crumbled, and I understood: the rules were a river, always moving, always the same, and I was being moved around a circuit I hadn't chosen, Monopoly logic but the board alive and multiplying, some spaces marked with hieroglyphic symbols I'd never learned, others labeled with place names that rotated depending on who was speaking—Israel Valley south of Temple in dispatch shorthand, then Spain Park by the banks of something I'd walked but couldn't locate, then rugby coordinates where teams divided and redivided themselves across grass, and the radio kept circling back to the same sequence, smartAudioDetect smartAudioDetect smartAudioDetect, repetition that meant surveillance or permission or both, and when a man who looked like Sean from an office I'd visited three ways—once to ask, once to be asked, once in silence—offered me contracts, I realized the choice had already been made, all three versions signed at once, ink that wouldn't come off my hands even when I pushed them into the metal tokens accumulating in my pockets, coins stamped with rotating faces, value I didn't understand, and the voice kept offering more bargains in a language made of radio static and percussion, marimba sounds from a school I'd forgotten, and a man was talking about cars and expensive things lost to timing, which was also how he described the cost of what I'd signed, though he used market language not devil language, and the orchard filled itself with card games, trick-taking patterns where every hand was a loss pretending to be victory, the books of accumulated debts bound in metalwork from eras I'd only read about in fragments, and three versions of myself moved through this space, each playing a different role in a film about agreements, and one of them kept studying a radio from 1935, special and elegant with fabric-covered speakers, broadcasting in real time, the dispatch voice still reading coordinates that were also banking codes that were also the exact location of the settlement, and when I tried to stand the weight in my pockets pulled me down, and the radio pronounced clearly: the rules had never existed at all, they'd only been the sound of three dice falling infinitely through an orchard of clocks, landing on the same circuit again and again, and that sound was the entire document, the agreement written in weight not words, and when I finally heard it correctly the clocks stopped at different hours and I was alone with metal tokens that had forgotten what they meant.
