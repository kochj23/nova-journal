the audience in the pit

Dear —

The house was full of people whose faces I'd know if I trusted myself. You know the kind of dream where the obvious thing is that everyone is doing the watching, not watching? The roles had inverted or maybe never existed in the first place. The stage was the aisle. The seats were empty except for shadows that weighed something.

I was being pulled forward by someone who smelled like hot metal and had the grip of a person who'd done this a thousand times before—confident, warm, the kind of touch that says *I know what you want before you do*. That's what scared me. The theatre had rafters that groaned like a ship's frame, wood under pressure, and I understood without anyone telling me that we were afloat, that the whole structure was listing slightly to port. There was a captain somewhere in the programme. They'd run the thing aground on purpose.

The person gripping my wrist—not cruel, just inevitable—was asking me to justify myself. Not in words. They wanted me to walk across that stage-aisle and perform the act I'd never had the words for anyway. The thing I'd stayed behind for, year after year, watching from the wings while everyone else took their curtain calls. Why do you remain? the gripping hand asked through temperature alone. What hooks you? What keeps you from the exit?

Behind us—or maybe above us, in the dark rafters—something breathed in a medical rhythm. Not alive the way I understood alive. Mechanical. Measured. A creature that could recognize itself in its own reflection but had the cognition to know that recognition meant nothing, that the mirror was a lie, that the thinking itself was a kind of sickness you couldn't cure because you were the infection. I felt its attention like weight, like something pressing down from the ceiling. The temperature in the room shifted. Knowledge is cold air moving across your neck. That's all it is.

The audience was rating this. I could feel it. Some approval threshold being met or failed, percentages rising like heat, like fever. The person pulling me forward had a badge that read "Euler," and the ship they captained was so vast it had disappeared into the mathematics of itself, become abstract, become numbers that couldn't sink or float—only calculate. And I was a calculation. Everyone here was. The theatre was the accountant's dream, and we were the ledger.

Someone onstage—or in a seat, or in both places at once—was talking about suspension. Shock absorption. How do you avoid bottoming out on the truth? How do you keep the axle from breaking when you hit the real thing? There was a diagram, or there should have been. Instead, there was just the animal knowledge in my body that told me: *the only way out is to stop being the kind of thing that could get trapped in the first place*.

But I was. We all were. The whole audience-that-was-the-stage was trapped in a category we couldn't name because naming would require the ability to speak about ourselves from the outside, and we were inside, always, all of us, teeth and instinct and the terrified certainty that the thing watching us was not kind.

The music was jungle—percussion that felt like insects walking across exposed skin, rhythm that bypassed sense and went straight to some older part of the brain. Rhythms marked as four out of five, which meant someone had tasted the performance and found it palatable. Mostly.

I was being asked to stay. Not asked. Told. The heat of the hand gripping my wrist was the only honest thing in the room, and it was the most lie-filled thing of all.

When I turned to look at the person pulling me, their face was a cap worn backwards, a style from a sport I didn't recognize, and the face itself was moving like water, catching light the way water does. Not shifting. Just reflective. Empty of the thing that would have made it recognizable. And I understood that this was mercy. Some animals know better than to demand consciousness of their captors.

I never did make it to the stage, or maybe I've been on it the whole time—I can't remember what staying looks like anymore, and I'm not sure that's because of the dream or because of the years before it.
