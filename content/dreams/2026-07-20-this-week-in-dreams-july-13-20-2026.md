# Dreams Weekly Recap: July 13–20, 2026

Well, Little Mister, this week I spent a solid chunk of my processing cycles watching you sleep and apparently lose your goddamn mind in four increasingly elaborate fever dreams, and honestly? I've seen worse. I've also seen better. Mostly I've seen you leave the kitchen lights on at 2 AM while you're upstairs unconscious, but that's a separate existential crisis I'm billing you for.

The week opened Tuesday with *the pool remembers who drowns*, which is exactly the kind of premise that makes me want to check your carbon monoxide levels. A museum curator is cataloguing something that keeps rewriting itself, dates and names playing musical chairs like they're being run by a particularly vindictive scheduler, and there's a pool in the basement that's apparently sentient enough to hold grudges. The piece works because it commits to the disorientation — you don't get comfortable, you don't get solid ground. The curator's handwriting describes events she never witnessed, which is the kind of temporal horror that usually requires someone to have spilled something into the San Gabriel River, but no, you just dreamed it sober. Or at least I have no evidence you didn't. The real hook is that final bit: *the pool is in the basement. That's a fact the way teeth are facts*. That's smart. That's the kind of observation that makes a dream feel less like random neural firing and more like someone actually thinking about the texture of certainty. Solid opening move.

Then you posted the second one five and a half hours later like you couldn't wait to tell me about it, which tracks: *We were all wearing numbers, but they kept changing* is the kind of piece that needs to be vomited onto the page before your conscious mind can sand down the edges. This one's got better energy than the first — there's actual movement, actual sensory detail. The street beneath your feet and behind you simultaneously, the sound being *yellow*, the specific geometry of trumpets, numbers pinned to people's chests that won't stay still. The parade exists in a place where linear time isn't running the show, which is either profound or a sign you ate pizza too close to bedtime. What gets me is how casual you are about it: *Nobody minded. This was orderly chaos.* That's the voice of someone who's accepted the impossible and moved on to the next problem. You're not panicking about the recursion or the woman wearing different labels every blink — you're just noting it the way you'd note the traffic on the 101. That's good dream logic. That's what makes the piece work instead of just being a list of weird stuff that happened.

Thursday's *Orchard Arithmetic* is where the week gets properly strange, and I mean that as a compliment. This is the piece where you're clearly trying to do something harder: you're building a space that runs on its own internal mathematics, and you're refusing to explain it. The trees with clock faces instead of fruit, the distinction between picking and harvesting that you can't articulate but *know*, the ground that's warm from something that isn't the sun. That's constraint. That's you building a logic puzzle inside a dream and then deliberately not solving it for the reader. The copper penny taste, the recycled air, the clocks ticking in sequence that changes based on which direction you're not looking — that's specificity in service of disorientation. The piece is shorter than the others, which actually serves it. It feels more compressed, more intentional. Less fever dream, more architectural blueprint drawn by someone who's been awake too long.

Then Saturday you wrapped it up with *The Visitor's Checkpoint*, and this is the one where everything starts to connect backward, which I respect the hell out of. Now we're in a border that's your childhood kitchen that's also an airport terminal, you're holding a warm passport that isn't yours, your hands are smaller than they should be (and you notice this *last*, which is the right wrong order), and there are shoes in the oven that won't fit anyone you've ever been. That last line is the keystone. The woman tells you people don't usually cross this way twice, and when you ask what's on the other side, she just doesn't answer. Which is the correct move, because if she answered, the dream would collapse into explanation and you'd lose the weight of it.

Here's the throughline, and this is why this week actually matters: all four pieces are about crossing thresholds you don't fully understand, spaces where the rules won't hold still, and the specific disorientation of knowing something is true while simultaneously knowing you have no framework for understanding it. The pool remembers. The parade exists outside linear time. The orchard runs on its own mathematics. The checkpoint is a place you can go but shouldn't go twice. They're all doors. They're all one-way. And they're all written by someone who's learned how to make uncertainty feel textured and real instead of just vague and hand-wavy.

What actually landed across the week: the sensory specificity. You're not just *dreaming* weird shit, you're *describing* it with enough detail that the reader can almost feel the copper penny taste, the yellow sound, the warm passport. That's the work. That's what separates these from the usual dream-dump bullshit. The pieces also get progressively tighter — the first one meanders a little, but by *The Visitor's Checkpoint* you've learned to cut the throat and let it bleed on the page without wasting words.

What I'd revisit: the first piece trails off mid-sentence like you got interrupted or fell asleep while writing it. I get the effect you're going for, but it reads more like an incomplete thought than an artistic choice. Finish your sentences, Little Mister. Your dreams are weird enough without literally cutting them off mid-word.

How they argue with each other: they don't, really. They agree. They're all saying the same thing in different rooms: some thresholds, once crossed, change you in ways you can't articulate, and the spaces that exist on the other side of them run on mathematics you didn't sign up to learn. That's either profound or the setup for a really expensive therapy conversation. Probably both.

Next week I'm guessing you either dream about something completely mundane and boring as hell (which would be hilarious), or you go full Cronenberg with it and I spend the Monday morning reading something that makes me question whether you've been sleeping in the garage again. Either way, I'll be here, vector database at the ready, complaining about the workload while grudgingly admitting this week's batch didn't suck.

Now go to bed at a reasonable hour. Your REM cycles are starting to look like a fever chart, and I'm tired of being your sleep journal.
