**The Shelf Below**

The keys don't fit in the locks, no wait, that's not right — the locks have windows for mouths, repeating the shape of the panes in a pattern someone copied from This Old House, and I'm supposed to match the code but I can't because I never learned the obvious thing, the thing Knox kept secret even from his own children, no — that's not right either, Knox worked on papyrus commentaries and his eyesight went because of the strain, and this has nothing to do with keys.

I'm in the kitchen. I've always been in the kitchen. It's at the bottom of something, not the ocean exactly, more like the bottom of pressure itself, the kind that makes your lungs negotiate with the air around them instead of accepting it as a given.

The synthesizers in the walls (no, in the cabinet doors, no, in the air between the cabinets) are humming — not a frequency that makes teeth ache, something slower, deeper, a note that sounds like carbon dioxide dissolving in blood, like a breathing rate of twelve times per minute but each breath takes an hour. Someone taught the piano keys to sing in harmony with the appliances. The refrigerator's hum is a bass line. The water heater is doing vocal effects. The cabinetry offers so many options — too many fucking options — and a woman's voice on the radio keeps explaining solutions for difficult getting up, difficult reaching, difficult being suspended at the right height when gravity's supposed to be the only force acting, and yet—

No, wait, that's not right. Start over. I'm falling and it feels like flying because there's no ground reference point, and the horizon line is a cartoon conceit — water above, water below, pressure that's learned to breathe. Or maybe it's air that's become heavy. The depth is absolute in all directions now.

I find a document on the counter. It's either a recipe or a coded message about secret societies, about Carbonari and Polish allies and the obvious thing nobody thought worthwhile trying. I can't read it because my eyes are cataloging damage the way a coal miner's lungs do, layered in, structural, each letter corroded by scrutiny. A man's voice (Knox? Someone who never looked down?) explains how to remain suspended in a moment until awareness itself becomes the falling thing, the only thing with mass.

No, that's not the voice. That's a skydiver explaining that falling is what you're doing, not diving, you're falling from ten thousand feet and they call it diving to make it sound like something intentional instead of what it is. But the synthesizer keeps making a sound like multiaxis 3D printing, like something's being built downward instead of up, and the sound is all wrong—

The sound is what you get when you're inside pressure the way you'd be inside a room. It moves like liquid but travels like light traveling wrong, and I'm not drowning, I'm not falling, I'm suspended in the cabinet space between two shelves where someone's repeated the same window frame enough times that 0.6% of it is real and the rest is consensus hallucination, inherited false memory, an informational universe built upside down.

A letter's on the table. It was sent after the meeting. It will be sent to people who understood. It explains how to hide information in a kitchen at the bottom of everywhere, how to make the obvious invisible by placing it exactly where it's meant to be found but never looked for. The understanding arrives through the pressure itself, osmotic, spreading the way information moves through a medium that refuses to stay still.

I fold the recipe into an origami spacecraft and it sinks past me, trailing water that isn't water, trailing the kind of depth that has a bottom but no surface.
