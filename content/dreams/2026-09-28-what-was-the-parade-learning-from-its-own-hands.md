What was the parade learning from its own hands?

The parade had no beginning, which meant I'd been in it forever, or was only just entering—there was no gap between those states, only a continuous now that stretched backward and forward like fabric folding on itself, each of us holding hands through paper-thin cloth, feeling the grip of strangers five positions back and three positions forward simultaneously, the weight of them both pulling and pressing like a single organism sorting itself through time that flowed in all directions at once, and the sound came first, not the marching but the breath before marching, thousands of lungs finding a shared rhythm that nobody had learned because it couldn't be taught, only inherited the way grain knows to sprout, the knowledge arriving in the palm-press and finger-lock, passed hand-to-hand the way domesticated knowledge passes, the way the first farmers must have whispered it into the maize without words nine thousand years of breath and expectation ago, teaching a seed to become food without instruction, teaching it to grow when no one was watching, and above the parade—or beneath it, or threaded through it like light through water—there was watching, a presence ancient and patient, not judging but observing the way a stone observes the river, a way of seeing that had never looked away, and I understood without being told that this had happened before and would happen again, the same faces cycling past like seasons cycling through ages, like time itself refusing the shape of a line and folding into a wheel that was also somehow a spiral that was also somehow all three directions at once—past, present, future—standing shoulder to shoulder in the same body, in the same parade, and for one shattering moment I could feel every version of myself marching: the one ahead who had already reached the end I couldn't see, the one behind who hadn't begun, the one alongside frozen at the eternal center, and the fabric between my fingers began to feel less like cloth and more like something thinner, something that had never been spun but only ever suggested, only ever been waiting, and now the bells—ringing from no direction, from every direction, from inside—seemed to be ringing the same note over and over but the sound was changing each time, becoming lower, higher, becoming the hum of names I'd never learned but had always known, becoming the space between what is real and what is solid, between what persists and what merely appears to persist, and someone next to me (or behind me, or emerging from inside my own hearing) whispered that everything here was the same thing pretending to be different, that the parade and the watching and the hand I was holding and the hand that was holding mine were all one substance trying on the shape of separation, the way a mirror pretends the reflection is across glass when it's always been your own face all along, always been the same observer, the same observed, and I wanted to argue but the words dissolved into old breath, old rhythm, the domesticated knowledge of things that grow when no one looks, knowledge that moves without moving, knowledge that cycles and cycles and cycles, and the parade turned a corner that curved back toward its own beginning or perhaps toward a beginning that had never been separated from the end, the fabric tightening around the wrists of five positions back and three positions forward until all distance collapsed into the single grip of my own hands holding my own hands, and somewhere in that collapse the bells were ringing so quietly they had become the very structure of my ribs, and I stopped trying to know whether I was watching the parade or being watched by it, stopped trying to divide the observer from the observed, stopped trying to measure time as if it were something that flowed in only one direction, because by then I understood that all of it—the cycling, the watching, the grip, the breath, the grain growing in the dark—was happening now, had always been happening now, would always continue happening now, and the bells were the proof, nine thousand years of them, ringing from a throat I couldn't locate in a direction I couldn't measure, belonging to a moment that had no before and no after.
