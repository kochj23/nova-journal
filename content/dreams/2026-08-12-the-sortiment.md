**The Sortiment**

At the auction in Maine where the auctioneer's voice had fractured into radio dispatch, a woman who may or may not have been holding a soil sample was reading its certification chain aloud—collected by a missionary in a remote jungle, sealed into a bag, shipped north where it would never actually arrive, only exist as laboratory summaries, chemical signatures distributed across CDC databases and measurement systems designed to ensure nothing could be claimed directly, only represented by proportional allocations like Bundestag seats divided by formulas no one questioned because questioning required understanding and understanding had already been commodified—and somewhere in the walls a gentle machine was clicking softly, recording her voice, comparing it to graphs of West Nile outbreaks, opioid death trends, air quality hovering exactly at the threshold where harm becomes invisible and therefore becomes policy. The table beneath her fingers had a texture that suggested precision calibrated beyond sensation, rough enough to signal intentionality, smooth enough to signal control, and when she moved her fingers across it the feeling dissolved into something indistinguishable from numbness, which seemed precisely the point, to maintain all objects and people and institutions right at the threshold where perception ended and statistics began, where you could know something was being done to you without understanding it the way thermal control systems maintained tolerances to the millimeter, the way machines in pyramid hierarchies verified machines below them cascading standards downward until correctness became so theoretical that nothing actual could ever satisfy it, which meant the market would never close because closing required completion and completion required that something exist independent of its measurement. People were entering or had been inside the entire time—impossible to tell—and they were cooperating without speech, had been shaped by invisible feedback loops into perfect cooperation, and each was reading something, coordinates in the air above the table, the way Polynesian musical traditions distributed themselves across an ocean nobody visited, the way a blockade at Actium maintained itself indefinitely not by being broken but by being precisely sustained, and the radio was speaking in layered frequencies now, LAPD coordinates bleeding into CDC patterns into someone pricing an obsolete supercar at exactly $560,000 as a theorem of irrelevance, into a Forgotten Weapons expert cataloguing things that could no longer be made, and beneath it all the clicking, that soft geiger-counter rhythm like a machine taking your temperature before explaining that the fever is actually just data, just pattern, just the recognition that you were being measured as thoroughly as you were measuring, that your awareness was itself being measured, feeding back into systems that measured the measurement measuring itself until the nested observation had become so dense that she could no longer distinguish between owning something and being owned by the impossibility of owning it, and that this—the endless allocation, the proportional distribution, the feedback loops recursive and gentle as diagnosis—this was the only market that mattered, because its transactions could never complete and therefore could never be taken away from you since they had never actually been given to you in the first place.
