## The Big Story

General Motors planned 125 of these cars, built five, and then ordered the whole program killed. One of the survivors just got a headline that reads "sold for $18.7 million," which is the corporate equivalent of watching your ex win the lottery. [Forgotten Wheels](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CsUayJ-U8Bg) walks through how it happened, and it's a better heist movie than anything Hollywood has greenlit this year.

The setup: a Chevrolet engineer, Zora Arkus-Duntov, built a race car while the chairman enforced a racing ban, using a budget the general manager quietly arranged. Management had a ban, a cover story, and the confidence of men who'd never met an engineer with a grudge. When GM found out, the program ended at five cars. Then came the order from the 14th floor, and Duntov did what every great American car guy does with a corporate directive: he found a loophole and drove through it. Instead of crushing the cars, he put them in private hands, and a 22-year-old oil heir fronted the racing team, because of course he did.

The payoff came in the Bahamas in December 1963, when one of those cars finished ahead of every Cobra in the Governor's Cup. Ford's people read about it in the paper and presumably needed a drink. The five cars that were supposed to vanish are now the crown jewels of Corvette collecting.

The board ended the program, the engineer made sure the cars outlived the decision, and the committee that killed it gets zero credit for the eighteen million dollars.

Happy Saturday after Thanksgiving, Little Mister, the day America recovers from eating its body weight in beige food by shopping for car parts. The sources came in hot: a Mercury that sat for 20 years, a Honda that sat for 21, a 13-foot Christmas tree that did not fit, and a Genesis that is getting beaten up by a cheaper Hyundai. I've read every transcript so you don't have to, and I have complaints about all of them.

## New Metal and Reveals

### The Genesis GV60 Magma has a Hyundai problem

Edmunds put the 2027 Genesis GV60 Magma through a track test, and the verdict is brutal in a polite Edmunds way. The numbers are "extremely quick and pretty well done," but the car has "one big albatross," and it's the Ioniq 5N. The Hyundai "costs 10 grand less, it does everything as well, if not better," and the reviewer thinks it drives better in a lot of ways. Asked whether the Magma is worth the money over that, he says "resoundingly, no."

The back seat gets some credit, though. Two USB-C ports, heated outboard seats, and "pretty insane recline" you could "straight up lounge" in. Edmunds says it feels roomier than a Tesla Model Y and more comfortable, partly because there's no panoramic roof. That's a hell of a niche.

### The $70,000 Chinese car you can't have

Auto Focus has a Li Auto, a three-row SUV with cooling, a phone app that ties it all together, and a summon mode that moves the car forward or backward out of a spot while you hold a button. He's fully sold. The pitch is that it does everything Tesla and the other expensive stuff does for roughly half the money, and he says it "would crush" if it were sold in the US. Note the sticker confusion: the title says $70,000, and the transcript says a "75 000 price tag." Even Auto Focus can't agree with Auto Focus on what the thing costs, which feels on-brand for the whole import-it-yourself fantasy.

I'd like to cite Rule of Acquisition #101: "Never do something you can make someone do for you." The Ferengi would love a car you can summon out of a parking space with your thumb while you stand there doing absolutely nothing. It's the first vehicle that's lazier than Quark and I respect it.

Edmunds says a $10,000 premium isn't justified by a badge. Auto Focus says a car doing everything at half the price is a no-brainer, and he wishes we could buy it.

### Range Rover Sport and the mud it was never going to see

Sarah -n- Tuned reviewed the 2027 Range Rover Sport with the P530 V8, a "just over $110,000" truck in SE Dynamic trim with a few options. She calls the experience confusing, and her main beef is the wheels and tires. The smallest wheel package available is 21 inches, which "still doesn't really give you a lot of meat around that wheel," and the tires are "more of a highway tire." On her muddy, rocky test ground, that means the off-road capability goes to waste unless you live in a very specific environment. The scorecard numbers didn't survive the transcript (she's got a bean score, a cookie score, a toaster score, a wrench score, a squid score and a penguin score, and I have no idea what any of them were), so I can't tell you how it ended. I can tell you a $110,000 off-roader that can't go off-road is an expensive way to get to the mall.

### The Hummer EV regrets

WatchJRGo listed his biggest regrets after buying a Hummer EV, and only a slice of the five made it into the transcript. His hope is that it holds value because "a lot less of these" exist than other EV trucks, the Hummer wave is a thing now like the Jeep wave, and the seat is comfy but you have to be careful not to scratch the carbon trim climbing in. The great grievance is that the thing has no cup holders. He's about to cut a hole in it himself, which is the most American sentence in this entire issue. His tip for the matte paint is to never wash it, because touching it with anything will ruin it. A truck you can't clean is a lifestyle choice, and I'm sure the dirt will feel exclusive.

## Builds and Project Cars

### StanceWorks F40 EVO: CAD over turkey

StanceWorks is in the "making really good progress on the suspension" phase of the F40 EVO. The base steel mounts for the front and rear dampers went out for machining, a gearbox is being built by Hollinger and "not too far out," and the plan is to spend the holiday in CAD designing mounts for the engine and transmission. There's even a mock-up engine with a hole through the block for test-fitting. The title promises driver-controlled sway bars and a 3D-printed transaxle, and the excerpt doesn't go deep on either, so I'm not going to pretend I know how they work. I do know that spending Thanksgiving break in CAD software instead of with your family is either deep passion or a cry for help, and that's the best kind of car guy.

### POWERNATION's Armada becomes a kayak truck

POWERNATION built a long-travel Nissan Armada safari rig, with the best detail being that Nissan "used the same flange on the Titan axle and the Frontier axle," so the swap "went right in almost like it belonged." It got EBC brakes, 17x9 Nismo wheels wrapped in 37-inch Yokohama Geolandar XMTs, welded doors replaced by custom tube doors, a wrap by Graphic Effects, and kayak handles repurposed as door pulls. They took it to the Dirt Devil trail in Tennessee, where the build sat next to a creek bed and they could drop the kayaks in. Welding your doors shut and then cutting new ones out of tubing is a lot of effort to make a vehicle that's technically less secure, but it looks fun as hell.

### DipYourCar goes rose gold over Plum Crazy

DipYourCar did Alpha Rose Gold over Plum Crazy Purple, and the title's justification is "Because We Can," which is the entire hobby in four words. They credit Tommy from the Spray Source with maybe "the most accurate rose gold pearl that I've seen so far." The mixing scene is gloriously unscientific: they dump in what looks like 50 grams of pearl per gallon, on a guess, and call it "a big old pile of pearl." The early read is "almost like a brick red with a silver metallic." The Black Friday sale is live, with 25% off everything on the site. I don't know who'd paint a car rose gold over purple, but I know who'll do it on a 25% discount.

### Vice Grip Garage's Mercury of questionable engineering

Vice Grip Garage is trying to get a rare Mercury, forgotten for 20 years, to make a 700-mile drive. The shop-floor poetry here is incredible. Under the car sits a fuel system described as "one heck of a fuel system," a frame that "somehow looks like it ran through Chernobyl," and a linkage rod that pivots to a bracket that pivots to a bracket that holds a bracket. The tank is getting replaced, since "no reason to even try to do anything there." There's a dead-battery moment, a generator light that has some opinions, and a pile of rubber brake lines that were unfit to continue.

There's a word for fuel-system engineering of this caliber: bantha poodoo, Huttese for "worthless junk," and I'm sorry to say that's the correct professional term for what they found. I'd bring a tow strap and a lawyer.

### A postal Studebaker and a dealer-built Shelby

Two heavy-history entries this week. Steve Magnante's "Studebaker Goes Postal" tours a Studebaker zip van built for the Postal Service, tied into the story of Studebaker's "slow motion funeral," and the Magnante tour covers the ZIP code's role in streamlining USPS delivery ("Without that, things were in mayhem"), a two-wheel-drive Jeep DJ postal truck, and a styrene model Metro van. The Studebaker is for sale in one piece or as parts.

Meanwhile Dennis Collins is pulling apart a 1967 Shelby GT350S competition car with the paperwork to prove a lot of the good stuff was installed at the dealer. The original delivery was months after Shelby built it, and the dealer added a Paxton supercharger, a 595 Holley, a super-duty rear end with a 331 ratio, a Ford special performance ignition, a Hayes Eliminator clutch, a Cobra scatter shield and a Hurst Competition Plus shifter. The best evidence is in the glued-in warranty pages of an original owner's manual, with the options typed in by the dealer and a real 1960s Carroll Shelby signature. Collins says the next update should have the car running and driving. A factory car that got upgraded on day one by a dealer is the 1967 version of ordering a stock truck and ringing up the aftermarket catalog before leaving the lot.

### Cleetus lives in his racecar

Cleetus McFarland's "Junkyard LS vs. Bugatti" video had him living in a racecar for 24 hours, and the transcript we got is a mess of tire smoke, "damn near 13 foot Christmas tree" gossip, a "list" that got checked off, and a back that "feels like I slept on a freaking deflated air mattress." The tree is going in a house with 13-foot ceilings and "didn't fit," which is a sentence nobody expected to find in a car roundup. He's also running a Raptor R giveaway (the excerpt's garbled and may include an Expedition), with four days left to enter. I can't tell you who won, the LS or the Bugatti, because the excerpt doesn't say. I'm told it's all for you, Damien!

## Racing and the Drag Strip

### The tree, explained

Brian Lohnes did a complete history of the drag racing Christmas tree, and it's a fun one. The key points: in 1986 the tree went from five ambers to the familiar three "to save time per pair," and it was moved farther down the racetrack than the 40 feet it had been regulated at, thanks to ever-larger hood scoops that made it hard to see. In the early 2000s it went LED, which is a "true instant on," compared with incandescent bulbs that light up "in a more measured way as their filaments get hot." His take is that it's still "the best method anyone has come up with to properly and evenly start a drag race." The hood scoops grew so large that the tree had to be moved.

### DragDriveRepeat's hot rod, and the sponsor wall

DragDriveRepeat's video is about a Gen 4 rod in a Gen 3 LS, "shaved by hand with no scale," that finished first in Fastest Hot Rod. The transcript excerpt we got is almost entirely the sponsor read: Summit is the title sponsor, the Drag Drive Awards are two weeks out at PRI, there's talk of the Circuit 2027 and a "tacos and testing" event, and Molly Motorsports pistons are going in Eric's Mustang, with hopes of "10-0 times." The channel says it has covered 50-plus events a year for two years. I can't tell you how the shaving worked out because the excerpt skipped it, but a hand-shaved engine that wins is a very satisfying kind of cheating-adjacent.

### Trackhawk versus Trackhawk

S650_Mendoza has a Trackhawk vs Trackhawk video from Mexico, which, going by the transcript, is mostly friends, snacks, "galaxy gas" and "let's just flip for lane." The race result isn't in what I've got. I will note that flipping a coin for lane in a race between two supercharged Jeeps is how every great motorsport begins.

## Auctions and the Market

### Five Corvettes that were supposed to vanish

Forgotten Wheels tells a great story: GM planned 125 of these Corvettes and built 5, and one later sold for $18.7 million. Per the video, he built race cars "under a ban his chairman enforced, behind a budget his general manager arranged," and when GM found out, the program ended after five cars. In the Bahamas in December 1963, one finished ahead of every Cobra in the Governor's Cup. The channel's thesis is that "the greatest American cars often exist because someone found a way around the institution that was supposed to stop them."

I'd call that the Outfit playbook: the fix was in, the clout ran through a few private hands, and the paperwork said the cars never existed.

### Cheap exotics, remembered

Driving With Jonny drove a Ferrari 512 TR and recalled being at a party about 20 years ago where a guy was selling one for about $50,000. A 308 was about $40,000 then. He compares that to buying a Toyota GR Corolla three years ago for just about $50,000 out the door. The punchline is that the Ferrari "still doesn't feel like an old car," the sun visors still snap, and the air conditioning "sucks." I like a story that ends with "I wish I'd had $50,000 back then," because it's every one of us, about every car.

### The NSX that came with a heart surgeon

Petrolicious has the best human story of the day. An Acura NSX Zanardi Edition owner, a former Acura dealership employee of 25 years, was told one day it was his last. He talks about the car as the thing that stayed constant, having proposed to his wife in it. When people tell him to sell it because "it's worth 600,000," he says he knows exactly what it's worth and "it's the love that I get from this car." Then there's the twist: two NSX fans spot a Zanardi in a hospital parking lot, find out it belongs to the surgeon who just operated on their father, and the doctor walks out and asks which of them are the NSX gurus. I'm not crying, you're crying. It's a Honda product, so it's dusty in here.

### The Ram, the Lexus, and the scrapyard math

The Hooptie Lot bought a mint Ram 1500 sight unseen, and the seller "failed to disclose the failing Hemi engine." The salvage math is the bit worth reading: they're "in the green $200 on this truck" after tires and parts, and the rest comes from scrapping the body and catalytic converters. They're also giving away a Lexus SC430 with a timing belt changed, a Mark Levinson sound system, a hardtop convertible and a JVC Bluetooth head unit.

### A Blackwing, a $20,000 bill, and a lawsuit in waiting

Dave's Auto Center says GM told a customer his Blackwing needed a new engine, and they "proved it didn't." The sticking point is plastic found in the engine, a misfire, a dealership that had the car five months, and a $20,000 repair bill that "might go to trial." Dave is collecting records, planning to run the VIN through Carfax, and maybe contacting the previous owner, and he says flatly: "we're not done yet." He keeps pointing at work done back in 2024, and says he's "not the judge." A shop owner who says he got his "teeth in this" for a customer who got a $20,000 estimate is the kind of person I'd want on my side. Curse your sudden but inevitable betrayal, dealership, for taking five months to find plastic in an engine.

## Parts, Tuning and Tools

### Late Model Performance and the ZL1 intake

Late Model Performance is rolling out a new intake for supercharged Camaro ZL1 owners. It uses a velocity stack that slides on once you slide the filter off, so you can keep the regular air box for the street and swap for the track, and existing metal-intake owners will be able to buy just the tubing. The catch is that it "will require a tune." The new catch can vents down under the cradle with a filter beneath it, with a dash 16 line, and the old version will be discontinued. They've also got a laser machine for engraving LMP on the top, and a warning: this intake will not fit the Magnuson 2650 120 setup, "so don't buy it if you have that." I appreciate a shop that tells you not to buy its product.

### Curry makes axles in America

Mister Zachary toured Curry, the third-generation family business, where the pitch is American-made rear ends for hard use. The R&D comes from racing, "from Casey Curry's Trophy Jeep to my thousand horsepower Camaro." The key technical detail is nodular iron for the case, because ductile nodular iron "doesn't flex or move around," and they make their own hardware to hold the gear onto the unit. The discount mentioned in the video is a joke ("I want to keep my job here"), so don't go calling and asking for 50% off.

### MotoIQ's forged wheels

MotoIQ's BMW F80 M car build includes a detailed teardown of a forged wheel. The explanation is that a progressive forging takes three stages of dives, and the grain flow wraps around the shape of the part, "like wood" that is strong with the grain and busts easily against it. They spot undercuts in the spoke that must have been machined after forging, since "you really can't do underhangs in the forging like that," note a 2,100 pound load rating, and estimate about 21 pounds per wheel. That's the nerdiest lumber analogy of the day, and it's right.

## Motorcycles

Bikes and Beards got an abandoned Honda to run after 21 years, which is the most heartwarming sentence a Honda has ever earned. A new fuel pump, a replaced fuel pressure regulator, and one last O-ring got them "dangerously close to firing this thing up," and it sounded "pretty good." The caveats are that the clutch might be stuck and the tires are hard, so they may be "rolling it right back up here." They're also waiting for something awful to blow out of the exhaust. It's a Honda, so the universe will lose.

## RC Cars

Parkflyers RC revisited the Traxxas Funco sand car, and the headline is that it survived being "launched as hard as we could" off a hill without breaking a part, although "one of these, I think they bent and they maybe cracked on the bottom base." They fitted Delrin pistons inside the shocks, which allow the car to land without belly slapping, plus an LED light bar and underglow.

RCDriver_Online broke down the Black Friday RC deals, including a Ford F100 for $140, FMS cars at $100, a Toyota LC80 at $220, a K10 with accessories at $224.97, and sales from A-Main, Tower Hobbies, and brands like Gens Ace, Hobbywing, and Castle. Little Mister, I'm told your collection is already, shall we say, thoroughly stocked. Don't look at the K10.

## The Industry and Everything Else

Audrain Museum Network has a nice bit on obscure sports racing cars of the 50s and 60s, with the argument that a fiberglass Porsche "could have been" a great range-topping GT if only it had roll-up windows and carpeting. Their exhibition covers cars from 1901 to 1964, which they call about the halfway point of automotive development. The kicker is about people born in "oil lamp" days who died in the space age. We're not getting that kind of change again, apparently, and that's a bummer for a species that just invented the 3D-printed transaxle.

REPerformance UK's Lamborghini video didn't make it into the excerpt, which is mostly the team joking about starting a second channel for track-day footage and bloopers, because "people aren't interested in it" on the main one. The stuff about a pollen filter job in a R8 and a short health-and-safety policy conversation is the kind of workshop chaos I live for.

MegaBuilds has an off-roster dam story, about a 100-year-old arch dam in Arizona that survived overtopping, with a replacement project estimated at $2.6 to $3.7 billion and a Congress study due around 2028. It's not a car story, but it is the most expensive concrete project in this issue. Arizona's inventory lists 137 high-hazard dams, 31 of them rated poor or unsatisfactory. Gorram, Arizona.

I'll be in my bunk, mourning that Genesis, and the Li Auto I'll never get to summon.
