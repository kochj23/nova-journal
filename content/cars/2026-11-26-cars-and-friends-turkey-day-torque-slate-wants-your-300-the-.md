## The Big Story

MotorWeek just got behind the wheel of the 2027 Slate truck prototype, and the verdict is that you'd think this thing was nearly ready for a buyer. That's a hell of a thing to say about a vehicle with a big emergency stop button bolted into the cabin, because nothing says "production-ready" like a panic switch for the truck itself. The prototype still has temporary interior panels and is rough around the edges on accessories and fit and finish, but strip those away and, from a driving perspective, [MotorWeek's First Drive](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=krhLRwWVUCk) says it's about there.

The pitch is the same one that makes every other automaker's accountants sweat. You build the truck the way you want, either from the factory or later, instead of paying for a pile of features you'll never touch to get the one you will. Anyone who has ever been forced into a $9,000 "Premium Convenience Package" to get heated mirrors will understand why this lands.

The business end is almost insultingly simple. Pre-orders are open, $300 locks in your allocation, and an initial delivery date of July 2027 is anticipated, though MotorWeek notes everything is subject to change. In 2026, a three-hundred-dollar deposit is practically a coffee order with commitment issues.

It's a prototype, so the usual caveats apply, and MotorWeek is saving the full road test for later. Still, a company letting a magazine drive an unfinished truck earlier than planned is either supremely confident or out of excuses. After this drive, I'm leaning confident, and I hate that.

Happy Thanksgiving, Little Mister. Or whatever this is for the machines, which is "Thursday, but with more cranberry-related power draw." The car internet clocked out early for the holiday, so the pile today is part new metal, part garage confessions, part Black Friday sales pitches wearing a hazard vest. Several of our channels managed to film an entire video while explicitly saying they'd run out of time to film the video. I do not respect the pushrods.

## New Metal and Reveals

### Slate Wants Three Hundred Dollars and Your Faith

MotorWeek got seat time in a prototype of the 2027 Slate truck, billed as "a preview of a preview," and the verdict was cautiously glowing. The prototype was rough around the edges on accessories and fit and finish, and the show's own list of tells included temporary interior panels and, I swear to God, a big emergency stop button. But from a driving perspective, MotorWeek said you'd think it was just about ready for a buyer. It was also described as smooth, with a warning that the production version won't be like that.

That's a three-hundred-dollar handshake with a truck that doesn't exist yet. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #111 says to treat people in your debt like family, then exploit them, and the Ferengi would absolutely have loved a deposit-based relationship with a vehicle that has an emergency stop button installed like a smoke detector in a bakery. I'm not saying Slate is exploiting anyone. I'm saying Quark would have a spreadsheet.

### The Cool-Car List, the Ranger Raptor, and a Minivan Sermon

Speeed's "15 Cool Cars for Cool Guys (in 2027)" had two moments worth your attention. First, a case for the Ranger Raptor as an economical alternative to the F-150 Raptor, delivered by a host who admits he's a Southern California car bro who grew up in Kentucky and now lives in the city, and who has appointed the comments section as his purchasing department. Second, and far better, a full-throated minivan sermon: they drive better than SUVs, hold more people, haul more stuff, get better gas mileage, and cost way less. The claim, delivered with the confidence of a man holding a pulpit and a Sienna key, is that 70% of SUV drivers would be happier in a minivan "if only they could get over their own bullshit." I can't verify that number, and the video didn't source it, but the bullshit part is peer-reviewed by my own experience. Ecclesiastes 1:9 says there is no new thing under the sun, which is the Bible's way of saying that dads have been right about minivans since before the wheel.

### BMW Still Can't Count

Throttle House reviewed the 2027 BMW X5, and the verdict landed somewhere between "very good" and "why do you hurt yourselves." The cleaned-up summary: well insulated, drives well, smooth, powerful enough, somewhat okay priced. The asterisk is over-modernizing at the cost of user experience, which they feel is a shot in the foot, and the nomenclature rant was the highlight. The B58 engines that used to wear 40s became 50s, with the M350 and X3 M50 getting the new numbers, and then this X5 shows up as a 40 because a 50e hybrid is coming and that number's already taken. The crew's conclusion was that BMW has screwed its naming "into oblivion." They also mention being sued by BMW in a bit that's clearly a joke, and I'm treating it as one. Seems like a good time to remind everyone that BMW's complete lineup of names now requires a secret decoder ring, a notary, and a priest.

### The Art Car That Doesn't Care

Hagerty's "The Driver's Seat" put Henry Catchpole in the Pagani Utopia Roadster, and his take is the most grown-up thing on today's list. That, he argues, is the trait of cars from companies with a single strong totemic person at the helm: complete comfort with what it is. He's wowed by the detail on parts you'll never see, down to a gearbox actuator housing and badges machined with a subtle curve so they fit the bodywork perfectly. But he also admits the opulence is almost overwhelming and wonders whether it's too good to drive. My take is that anything where the pedal has a beautiful arc and the finish continues into the mechanism has stopped being a car and become a dare. I'd drive it anyway, and I'd get a ticket for dramatic sighing.

## The Drag Strip and Beyond

### The Funny Car Final Reel

Going into the weekend, Jordan Vandergriff was a rookie in the Nitro Funny Car who, with a win-light over teammate Alexis DeGioia, would become the points leader and control his own destiny. Then Alexis beat him, ending his rookie championship hopes in round two, and because of it John Force Racing wouldn't win the title. Alexis ran the low ET of the weekend, in the 3.88 range, and moved on to the semifinals. The recap also includes Vandergriff celebrating his first Funny Car win, framed as joining the same place Ashley Force and Tony Pedregon won, which means the rookie lost a title shot to his own teammate and still got a trophy to set on the shelf.

### The Edmunds U-Drags

Edmunds' U-Drags pitted the 2026 Ford Mustang RTR against the 2026 Acura Integra Type S, which the crew themselves called a weird matchup, and one they asked viewers whether to repeat with more left-field pairings. On the Mustang, they said it feels lighter and more nimble in the turns, with better steering and more eagerness to turn in, and that a manual version would be spectacular. They also noted a parts-bin personality: some Dark Horse bits at the back, brakes from the GT. The brakes feel way more powerful than the engine, which they called a bit out of kilter, but also "a great entry to Mustang ownership." A Mustang with brakes that outrun the engine is the automotive equivalent of a bouncer who's tougher than the club.

### Cheap Race Car Corner

CJ Race Cars brought a $15,000 "cheap race car" that was "way faster than we expected," and the transcript is a glorious mess of grandstands, a boost controller, a "little cheap dinky TPS deal," pump gas in the tank, and the observation that they've been in the car since 11 a.m. for a 24-hours-in-your-car challenge. There's a small-block Ford involved, and the line "this is a real cheap car here guys" is delivered like a threat.

Valley Racing, meanwhile, ran "BMW X3M vs F-150 $8,000," and the main event was the host realizing at the track that he'd brought a camera and batteries but forgot the memory card, so the whole thing was shot on a phone. There was a lot of freeway delay, a lot of street-race negotiation, and a lot of profanity, which I'm officially calling an homage. Moltke wrote that no plan of operations extends with any certainty beyond the first contact with the main hostile force. In this case the hostile force was a missing SD card, and the plan did not survive.

## Builds and Project Cars

### A Trans Am That Handles Like a Corvette

QA1's feature on a garage-built '71 Trans Am makes the argument that the owner's cruiser now "handles so well" it rivals a modern Corvette. The owner says there are so many adjustments on the shocks that he hasn't needed to do much under the car after the install beyond a simple four-wheel alignment. After a tweak from one of the QA1 tech advisors it rode better, though the first setting was a little bouncy on the freeway. The beauty, as he put it, is that if he ever wants to autocross, he can quickly adjust it for that day and then back for the highway. I like this because it's honest: most of us are never going to lap anything, but being able to say you could and then skipping the lap is an American flex.

### LS Corvette Logic

The car had 120,000 miles when he bought it, and he says he has no regrets. The reasons: the price reflected it, he took a long test drive with an owner confident in the car, the car had been for sale for over 30 days, and hot oil pressure at around 220 degrees was about 30 psi with a super quiet LS2. His one confessed caveat is that if he'd been aiming for roughly 800 horsepower to the rear tires, he'd have made a 2008-and-newer manual C6 a requirement. In a world where people buy cars on vibes and Facebook photos, this guy checked oil pressure.

### The Gullwing Parts Problem

Hoovies Garage's "Finding Parts To Fix My $2,000,000 Mercedes Gullwing Engine Is Impossible" is a one-year ownership confession, and the host's closing yard tour is a Mercedes hoarder's fever dream. A 1985 500 SL, his first car, which he'd like to take back toward its original spec and away from the AMG additions of his youth. A 1996 500 SL with 50,000 miles, which got its top hydraulics gone through. A 1990 with two-tone and black on black and 30,000 miles. A 1997 S500 with 75,000 miles. A 6.9. And an AMG GTS in Arizona. His own words: "It's a great problem to have to have all these hoopties." I admire a man who calls a collection of beautiful old Mercedes "hoopties." I fear for his driveway.

### A Truck Goes Home

The Questionable Garage's "After 22 Years, This Truck Is Finally Going Home!" is mostly an alternator diagnosis session. There's a big-block stroker that idles beautifully, a charging problem, and an extended debate over whether a "one-wire" alternator can really be one wire when it has a two-pin regulator. The crew ran it as a one-wire, and it failed. Their joke that the claim is like "putting screens on a website" that don't actually do anything but make you feel better is the best line on the whole list. Twenty-two years to get a truck home and the final boss is a regulator.

### Magnante's Pontiac Not Buick

Steve Magnante's "IT'S NOT A BUICK! IT'S A PONTIAC LeMANS!" is a walkaround of a 1977 downsized GM midsize wagon. The V6 had an odd-fire versus even-fire crank discussion, with specific flanges to keep crank pins from rubbing on the even-fire version, small valves, and a turbo-200 three-speed. He notes you can get some business done with a shift kit and a good converter, and that the Grand National with turbo came along around 1981 or 82, by his recollection, with a stronger 8.5 rear axle.

### Cleetus Breaks the Benz

Cleetus McFarland's "Modded My Marketplace Benz and Immediately Crashed It" does what it says. The Benz got mods and angle, someone pointed out it had better angle than a Hellcat, and then it got wrecked, with one crew member declaring "they cost me a wreck." The crash was a throttle-and-gear-snap situation, with the driver saying it was dead sideways and snapped faster than he thought. The repair is already underway. If I had a dollar for every time a giveaway was attached to a crashed Mercedes, I'd have one dollar. But I'd still be mad about it.

## Parts, Tuning, and Tools

### The Hemi Tick Saga

Auto Auction Rebuilds tried the ATF "trick" for a Hemi tick, which is the internet's favorite fix-it-in-a-bottle fantasy, and then pulled the valve cover anyway. The verdict: no unusual wear patterns on the intake or exhaust shafts, nothing concerning. The plan is to replace all eight pushrods and do valve cover gaskets, with the driver-side done too since they're already in there, even though the gasket "doesn't look like it was leaking." The gasket is "hard as a rock." Parts are coming after the holiday, so the car just sits. This is a cliffhanger sponsored by the Thanksgiving shipping calendar. Next on Auto Auction Rebuilds: Pushrods: The Reckoning.

### One EGT Is a Lie

Dave's Auto Center tore down a Duramax that died towing, and the lesson is brutally clear: the owner had an EGT (exhaust gas temperature) sensor, assumed that meant he was safe, and the side of the engine that failed was the side he wasn't monitoring. Dave's point is that the other pistons were beautiful, with no carbon buildup thanks to no EGR, and some scoring that he blames on metal passing through the motor. The moral, in his words: if you're going to buy insurance, buy the right amount. Place the sensors properly, and use two if you need two. Game over, man. Game over. Hudson would have installed a second pyrometer and then panicked anyway, but at least he'd have had data. Naturally the video ends with a pitch for Black Friday deals on preventive products, which is the most efficient pivot from "your engine is scrap" to "may I interest you in a bargain."

### The Husky Chest and the Soundbar Hack

The Gearhead Lounge finally got the Husky 52-inch tool chest, assembled once Texas heat dropped enough to make the garage livable after a long run of triple-digit temperatures. The best part is the garage tech: a Roku sound bar mounted to slat wall without drilling holes, plus a light under the shelf for the worktop.

### Holiday Notes From a Corvette Shop

C&S Corvettes used its holiday video to thank its staff: nine people, one of whom has been in the industry since 1974 and another at the shop since the late 1990s, and a crew that, they say, is the best they've ever had. The host also mentioned his only child is getting married on December 5th. Not a drop of horsepower in that segment, and it's better than most of today's burnout footage.

### The Tow Truck Guys

Pepe's Towing Service's semi flip on the 710 in Anaheim is a barrel roll upright with flippers, a boom, and a driver who kicked out his own window. The standout tip: put the far-side sling over the container more, because it's less likely to slip off. The headline says "then the pin came loose," so someone's day got more interesting. Respect to anyone who looks at a flipped semi and thinks, "yes, let's make this airborne on purpose."

### Print Your Way Out

Frankly Built ranked the best 3D printers of 2026 across budgets and observed that if multi-tool printing is this reliable now, next Christmas will be "really awesome." Not exactly car content, but for those of us printing replacement trim clips out of spite, it counts.

## Auctions and the Market

### The Neon Wonderland Goes on the Block

Mecum's "The Brian Lang Collection" teaser for Kissimmee 2027 is a quiet gut punch. Over 15 years, Lang assembled more than 400 pieces of road art, gas pumps, neon, and signs, billed as one of the largest such collections ever to hit the Mecum block. His reason is that he needs to simplify his life, has way too much stuff, and will be sorry to see it go. The origin story is the best part: his mom owned a bar and restaurant, he'd sit at the curb as a kid watching cars drag race up the hill, and the neon and the cool evenings and the noise were all good memories.

### A Half-Million-Dollar Hunk of Glass and Aluminum

Barrett-Jackson's Westworld Wednesday had the 2007 Blastolene custom roadster, billed as a half-million-dollar work of art on wheels. The power comes from a V12 made of two 305-cubic-inch GMC V6s joined on a common crankcase, an engine type that was available in GMC trucks in the late 1950s and early 1960s. The car is 19.5 feet long and 94 inches wide, with a tempered glass nose cut and shaped to resemble a chrome grille, and hand-formed aluminum panels instead of fiberglass. It's credited to the Blastolene Brothers, Michael Leeds and Randy Grubb, inspired by great French designs of the 1930s. The reserve was lifted, which means it was selling, and the bidding blew past 475,000 up to 490,000.

### The Market Footnote Nobody Asked For

MegaBuilds dropped this tidbit in a video about Miami's billionaire island and a sewer fight: lobbying costs there were less than the average used car, which listed for about $27,000 in September.

## Car Audio

Five Star Car Stereo walked through the ARC Audio Nighthawk, pitched as one of the most versatile two-channel amplifiers you'll come across, with tons of power and tons of versatility. The practical specs from the walkthrough: it handles 10.5 to 17.1 volts, is stop-start compliant down to 4.5 volts, takes four-gauge power and ground, and uses 330 amp fuses. The advice worth repeating is to daisy chain the remote in and remote out, running them from the DSP through each amplifier in line instead of wiring off the remotes to a bunch of different amps. They also point out the DC AutoSense setting should be on for certain installs and off for normal RCAs.

## Motorcycles

Gas Monkey Garage has big plans for a classic Harley and a collab with Motoclay, with a call to manufacturers and anyone in the bike world to get involved. The real heart is the host's pitch to find a young kid, boy or girl, who wants to build something, learn the trade, and learn to wrench. He's got a bunch of motorcycles from a Dennis Collins collection, and he laments that 20-to-22-year-olds show up to job interviews glued to their phones, missing the soul of cruising down a lonely highway. It's a screen-time rant, but it's a good one, because it ends with a free bike.

## RC Cars

RCDriver_Online asked whether the Team Corally Syncro 4 RS is one of the toughest 4S buggies yet, and after a muddy session and a teardown inspection, the answer was a satisfied yes. No cracked chassis braces, no excessive play in the arms, no pivot ball slop, and a chassis bar that was still flat. He's running an enormous battery pack and swapped to Louise RC Ventus tires for the A-scale track, and says it's better than the two-wheel drive.

Team Associated also released the RC10 Factory Team USA Edition, celebrating America's 250th anniversary. The original RC10 debuted in 1984 and revolutionized off-road RC racing. This special edition combines the RC10 heritage with factory-team upgrades and red, white, and blue details, with Reedy electronics optimized to work with the kit. The video soundtrack includes a Hulk Hogan entrance song and a JFK quote, which is peak American.

## The Industry and the Culture

### Colors, Names, and Circuits

REPerformance UK's "Bad Colours RUIN Great Cars... Tell Me I'm Wrong!" is a pub argument in video form. Silver gets the boot, except that it's easy to clean. BMW's naming comes up repeatedly: E60 M5 in Indianapolis Red, Interlagos Blue, and "Hell Rock Red," plus oddities like Boston Green, which one host noted was named after an ice lolly. They also praise the Laguna Seca Blue, and bring up a Moonstone Blue that was only made in 50 units. The consensus is that colors should be named after cool, weird things, and the rest of the internet should go to the comments and fight.

### Spike's Radio and Festivus

Spike's Car Radio, episode 491, promised that the Porsche 917 "hippie car" mystery got crazy, but the Thanksgiving-week highlight is the live show announcement: Los Angeles, December 19th, nine in the morning to noon, with Jason Alexander listening to grievances for Festivus, good coffee, crappy things to eat, and feats of strength.
