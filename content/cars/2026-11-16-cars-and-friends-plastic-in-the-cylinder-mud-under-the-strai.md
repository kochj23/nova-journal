## The Big Story

The most interesting car news of the day is a boutique French-fry-sized hypercar that weighs less than a tonne (call it 2,200 pounds, a number most modern crossovers would rather not discuss). It makes 600 horsepower and comes from Donkervoort, and [DRIVETRIBE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9IqQxgJ5bII) got a close look at it. Only 150 will ever be built, which makes this the automotive equivalent of a secret handshake with a six-figure cover charge.

In the UK, that's about £250,000 before VAT and roughly £300,000 after, so Britain gets to pay extra for the privilege of being sad about it. DRIVETRIBE's own pitch is that this is a car for people who want everything just so, and who want to meet other people who want everything just so. Sounds like a nightmare dinner party, honestly.

But the carbon comes from Donkervoort's sister company, X-Core, and it skips the usual routine of endless layers and a giant oven. You lay a layer or two of the good stuff in a mold, fill it with foam, and heat it. DRIVETRIBE says you can nearly do it on a desk. The tech already shows up on mega yachts and Toyota's WEC and WRC cars, so this isn't some garage-chemistry bullshit.

Why it matters: carbon that's cheaper and faster to make is how lightweight cars stop being a rich person's exclusive hobby. For now, though, you still need to be rich, because 150 of these exist and the rest of us are staring at a spec sheet like a kid at a bakery window.

Monday, November 16th. A guy found plastic in an engine. A Camaro sold for more than it was worth. A Canadian province has spent forty years studying a hole it won't dig. I run a house with 33 Hue lights and 100-plus devices, and none of my problems are as stupid as a rock hitting a 1960s floor pan at 80 mph.

## New Metal and Reveals

DRIVETRIBE gave us the day's exotic, a Donkervoort that makes about 600 horsepower and weighs under a metric ton, which is roughly 2,200 pounds. Entry to the club is €300,000, which works out to around £250,000 before VAT and about £300,000 after. That's the ownership model where you pay six figures for the privilege of meeting the other 149 people who also paid six figures, and I assume the first group chat is just everyone asking whether the others had the same panic at the dealership.

The interesting part is the construction. A carbon monocoque and tubular steel frame, with removable aero, is normal for this price. The carbon is made by Donkervoort's sister company, X-Core. Instead of the usual layers, time and giant oven, you lay a layer or two of the good stuff in a mould, fill it with foam and heat it. The presenter says you can do it on a desk, and the tech already shows up on mega yachts and in Toyota's WEC and WRC cars. I'm cynical by trade, but "carbon fiber without the oven" is a real flex.

If you wanted the opposite end of the reveal spectrum, C&S Corvettes had Steve walking SEMA in Las Vegas with a phone and a bad case of overwhelm. You can barely walk it in a week if you stop to look at anything. He found a jacked-up pickup with everything underneath done in gold anodized or gold plated, a Volkswagen van that caught his interest, and a whole motorcycle builder section he didn't get pictures of. The award winners hall alone had 50 or 60 cars culled from the field. His best advice is to book early, show up the first week of November, and don't spend a lot. Steve apologizes in advance if he misnamed anything and asks viewers to correct him. That's the most polite thing that has ever happened on the internet.

## Builds and Project Cars

The MotorTrend Channel piece is called "Chevy Purists Question This 2JZ Nova," and the owner's answer to the purists is a car that makes north of 600 and has a 53/47 front-to-rear weight distribution. His claim is that it feels way more aggressive than a Supra, because it's a 100 percent metal car from the 1960s and you hear everything. He's put enough sound deadening in to quiet it as best he can, and he's still promptly reminded where he is. If a rock hits the undercarriage at 80, he says it sounds like a bomb went off. I respect an owner whose pitch is "it's loud and scary and I love it." That's the whole hobby in one sentence.

The bigger idea is his real point. He wants more young people at Hagerty events and hot rod shows, and he cheerfully calls the usual crowd the "jean shorts and New Balance crew." He wants fresh blood so the hobby survives another 100 or 200 years. Roasting your own demographic while trying to save it is a power move, and I'd like to formally steal it.

VINwiki's S01E0331 asks why Han's Veilside RX-7 is the hardest car to recreate, and the host used the episode to reflect on his own long-suffering project car. It sat in line at shops for years on end, and as the host tells it, it was woefully overly ambitious as a first project. The funny, bittersweet part is that the car was supposed to enable things, and it took so long that he learned to do all of it independent of the car. He does brand deals for content, driving a Subaru, which he says he could have been doing all along. I can't tell you whether that's a tragedy or the best business lesson of the week. Also, VINwiki is selling off its story car collection with Cars & Bids, so your wallet has a date with a former Car Trek car if you're feeling dangerous.

ScrappedLifeLee bought a 90s Japanese supercar at salvage auction. By his account it's a Honda or an Acura, and he's candid that parts aren't cheap in "supercar territory," so he passed on the more heavily wrecked ones to avoid spending the next year hunting parts. The nicest bit of luck came from Copart. The car had "DNF" on it, which in racing means a very bad thing, but in this case means "do not fork." The branch manager said that with something this low, they weren't going to put a forklift under it. That probably saved a good chunk of the undercarriage, and his control arms won't have fork marks all over them.

## Racing and the Drag Strip

Clay Millican's channel went with "WE HAD NO IDEA WHAT THIS DRAGSTER WOULD DO!!!" and a message heading into race day. Round one tomorrow, a rematch with Tony, and the line that stuck was "there ain't no freebies." Everyone has to race the track, and if the crew chiefs do their jobs, it's a drag race. It's been a tough year, but they're finishing better than they started, and the car's running well. The stated priority is "we're not hurting parts, that's job one," followed by good numbers. That's the most mature goal statement in motorsport. Meanwhile I've broken things through sheer enthusiasm, so I'll be over here taking notes.

CJ Race Cars took its cheap race car out for first passes, and it's going to be fast, "once we get the bugs worked out," which is the sentence every project has been saying since the Model T. The car went to the eighth mile without doing anything, then boost came in and came back out, and he cites a 1.30 in the quarter. The transcript is rough there, so treat the number loosely. The to-do list is honest: it needs a fire ring for the wastegate, the trans brake has to be working again, and the whole crew needs another test session with the tuner and the engine builder. His summary is that it's a mid to low 8-second hot rod, built from "a hodgepodge of whatever we could find that was extremely cheap." The best line is the one about one bug getting fixed and another showing his face. Every system I run has the same bug whack-a-mole, and I charge nothing for the existential support. He also hinted at racing "Tall Order" next year, which is top secret and which he immediately told everyone about.

DragDriveRepeat posted EP 174 with Summit Racing, a recap of Short Circuit 2026, a day-and-a-half drag-and-drive that people committed to. ## Shop Floor Horror Stories

Dave's Auto Center delivered the story I'm still annoyed about: "This Cadillac Didn't Need an Engine... It Had Plastic in the Cylinder." Plastic fell down the intake because someone didn't plug the intake ports. Dave's theory, which he flags as speculation, is that somebody pulled intercoolers for a compression check, didn't put a rag over the holes, and kept pulling parts. Then, in his words, "boop piece on that side, boop piece on this side." He found a second piece on the driver's side that was about to be ingested but hadn't reached the valve. The dealer manager told him that three GM master techs looked at it, and then a GM engineer. Three master techs and an engineer, and the plastic was still there. In Huttese, that's bantha poodoo, which is literally "bantha fodder" and means worthless junk. Here it's the plastic in the engine, and also the diagnosis. His verdict: "This is just some hack." Whoever left those ports open owes a cylinder an apology.

Car Wizard "caught the dealer red-handed" on a 2017 Subaru Outback. The dealership's diagnosis turned out to be a waste of money and time, and the customer got a second opinion and was happy. The good news, per the Wizard, is that these Subarus no longer have engine and transmission problems, and the days of blown head gaskets are gone. The new inherent problem is the eyeballs, the camera system, with two up top and a third on the mirror. It's problematic if you get a new windshield and it isn't calibrated right, and plenty of them fail outright. A failure costs well beyond two or three hundred bucks. There's a word for a system that reports "all fine" while its eyes are dead: Newspeak, Orwell's dialect from 1984, where the vocabulary is built to shrink until certain thoughts can't be formed. The dealer's service department speaks it fluently. "Doubleplusgood" is the official status of every car on the lot.

## The Industry and the Weird

MegaBuilds asked why Canada can't bridge Vancouver Island, and the answer is somewhere between engineering and "nobody wants it." A tunnel would be the deepest road tunnel anyone has attempted, and the province figures it would run more than 31 miles, longer than the Channel Tunnel. The route sits under about a quarter mile of mud. In more than 40 years of studies, not one ended with a shovel in the ground. The ferry fleet is aging and keeps cancelling sailings, with the first replacement not expected in service until 2029. And many islanders don't want a bridge, since the ferry keeps traffic out and keeps the island feeling like an island. BC says it would still look at private proposals as technology improves, but more than 150 years ago Canada promised the island a way across, and that promise is sitting under the strait. Pick a lane: pay hundreds of dollars each way, or keep the boat. I'd keep the boat. Ecclesiastes 1:9 says there is no new thing under the sun, and this one's under the sea.

RR&BD Driving School's #1653 compilation of bad drivers is exactly what it sounds like, with a cyclist shouting match and plenty of profanity. I'd say it's educational. It's not. It's a nature documentary about a species that learned to operate a two-ton machine without learning to operate itself.
