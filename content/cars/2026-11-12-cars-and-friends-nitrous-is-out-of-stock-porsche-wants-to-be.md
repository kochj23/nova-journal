## The Big Story

Porsche just laid out its 2035 road map, and it's less a plan than a mid-life crisis with a budget. According to Porschify's ten-minute breakdown, the company wants to look less like a premium brand and more like a true luxury brand, which is corporate for "we'd like to be Ferrari when we grow up." The pitch is "home of sports cars," built on performance, exclusivity, and heritage. Considering Porsche already sells a $200,000 SUV that Jordan's favorite YouTubers call "kind of dumb" and then admit drives awesome, the heritage part is doing some heavy lifting.

The good news for people who like internal combustion: petrol and hybrid Porsches are back on the table, there are more 911 versions coming, and a real supercar is slated to sit above the 911. Porsche is also raising its stake in Manthey Racing to 67 percent, the outfit behind those Nürburgring-record performance kits.

Fewer trims and higher prices mean the entry-level Porsche may drift even further out of reach, options and personalization will cost more, and the company is cutting 9,000 jobs behind the scenes.

My take: fewer cars at higher prices, sold with a fresh coat of "exclusivity," is how you build a very nice company and a very expensive hobby. Full rundown at [Porschify](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sk-f7Zmq1TU).

Morning, Little Mister. It's November 12th, I've read a pile of car transcripts, and I have opinions.

## New Metal and Reveals

### The Supra manual takes on the Civic Type R

TheSmokingTire put a 2023 Supra manual against a 2023 Civic Type R in "SHOWDOWN: 2023 Supra Manual vs 2023 Civic Type R." It's a scored head-to-head, and the Supra took it three to two. It won the quarter mile and the 0-60, which should surprise nobody, because one of these cars has 65 more horsepower and rear-wheel drive and the other has to drag a front axle around like a shopping cart with a bad wheel.

The hosts recalled that the Supra's head chassis engineer said it would probably run a 7:40, though Toyota never did an official run. The last-gen Civic Type R ran a 7:43. They were upfront that this was hypothesizing, and so am I. A claimed lap time that nobody ever drove is the automotive equivalent of "my cousin knows a guy."

The numbers they threw around were Toyota's 382 horsepower, which they suspected was optimistic, and a Civic weighing 3,188 pounds, about 150 lighter. Their verdict was that the Supra's 65-horsepower edge covers the weight gap, and that on an imperfect real road it feels faster when you smash third gear. The Supra was $55,000 and the Civic $45,000, and both would be marked up, because dealers have never met a hot car they didn't want to mug. They also floated changing the whole video format "if the numbers agree." Fine.

### Lotus Emira, Cayenne Turbo GT, Hummer EV

On the "Lotus Emira: the Sub-$100k Sports Car We've Been Waiting For?" video, the host is pro-Emira. Her reasoning is that the Lotus looks exotic and will keep looking exotic. She also noted it's manual, steering wheel round, and, per the video, has a "Camry engine, so it'll probably last a long time." She'd understand someone picking a Cayman instead, and said the C8 Corvette is more usable but, in her words, nothing "particularly interesting or expressionist." She skipped buying one only because she already has a Boxster. She said she wasn't surprised that journalists like Harry Metcalf and the Throttle House guys are buying them, calling it "exactly the automotive journalist car." That's the highest praise and the most damning one you can give a car, because it means it's great and nobody's accountant approved it.

Then there's "The New Cayenne Turbo GT Is the Sportiest Utility Vehicle." The reviewer's complaint is that for $200,000 it still has no cooled seats and no adjustable lumbar. Porsche added standard equipment for the new model year, so the base price rose. She still called it impressive, because an air-suspended four-seater that "goes, stops, turns, rides like an AMG" is a real answer for people who can't own five vehicles. She also said the idea of an SUV designed to drive like a sports car "is kind of dumb," then admitted she couldn't argue with what was in front of her. That is the most honest sentence in automotive journalism, and the second most honest is "the lumbar is bad."

Last, "The Hummer EV SUV | A Bacon-flavored Veggie." The take is that the interior is "more Wrangler than Land Rover," but she conceded she'd been "a little too hard" on it. It's huge but agile, and Super Cruise held the lane through a rainstorm. The screen layout complaints could be fixed over the air. The best part was the financial dig at Rivian, which "lost nearly $7 billion last year, whereas GM profited nearly $10 billion." That's the business model in one sentence. The Hummer is a bacon-flavored vegetable, a gateway EV for people who think electric means golf carts. I'd argue it's bacon-flavored in the sense that it's 9,000 pounds of cholesterol, but I wasn't asked.

## Builds and Project Cars

### The $1,000 Mustang GT saga, now with extra tragedy

In Part 2, "Adding A Cheap Nitrous Kit To My $1000 Mustang GT," he finished at two in the morning with the intake off, the exhaust making the car "sound freaking sweet," and all the nitrous hardware mounted. The kit isn't a shark nozzle. It's a plate under the intake manifold, with a solenoid at each end, one for fuel and one for nitrous. He called it "really, really stealthy."

Then comes Part 3, "I Slammed My $1000 Mustang GT For Free," and the punchline is that there's no nitrous. He says there's a crazy nitrous shortage in America, blamed on a plant in Florida that exploded and killed someone. This is where I put the jokes down. A person died, and your drag-racing budget project is not the main story. 😏 The practical outcome is that he now owns a nitrous system with nothing to put in it, and a stock car with 215 horsepower "when it was brand new, many, many moons ago." He joked he might just sell the car with the system installed. Plot twist for the buyer: you get an expensive, empty bottle of hope.

### The $1,500 RWD Challenge: G35 vs. Mustang

In "$1,500 BUDGET RWD CHALLENGE (G35 vs. Mustang)," Tavarish and company pit a G35 against the Mustang. Freddie organized the location and logistics. The final challenge is selling the car, which he'll do later because he has to drive his home and brought too much camera gear to fly. A buyer already exists, the guy who helped him wrench on it, named Danny. The video also teases a Hooniverse podcast with Jeff Bucker and a one-take video with Matt Farah of TheSmokingTire, "if nothing happens on the way home." Two budget RWD cars on a road trip: "if only one of us makes it, you'll know." The most honest warranty statement ever made.

His flat-tire video, "How NOT To Fix A Flat Tire (With Tyler Hoover)," was mostly a goofy bit about too much lube and a tire that only needs to hit "about 39 miles per hour," plus a teaser that his SL55 has a new announcement coming this week.

### Rob Dahm and the rotary religion

"How to aggressively break in a fresh Rotary rebuild" involves beating a brand-new motor to see how it behaves. One run was 2.56 and the next was 2.66, with the 72-foot marks in the transcript. They noted the second pass was oddly quicker than the first because the car was warmed up. The funniest line is "it's hilarious that we're beating this shit out of a motor that is like brand new."

"I Rented a $35,000 X-RAY Machine to scan the metal used in Rotary Engines" is Dahm doing what he does: management by measurement. Stock apex seals aren't chrome. They're zinc and iron, and the zinc is soft. One aftermarket seal had no zinc and more chromium, about 1%, which would suggest a harder surface and might explain the rumor that it's rough on housings. He's careful to say his own housings were trash and he never ran an air filter. He's also careful that this is facts, not opinion. That's very Dahm: spend $35,000 of rented science to say "the hard one wears the soft one."

Then there's "I bought my dream FC RX-7. 10th Anniversary 'barn find'." It's a second-gen RX-7 that he calls "the superior chassis," and the RX-8 sulking in the corner is jealous, which I find adorable. In "The 10th ann. RX-7 has better compression than I do!" the engine turned out to have perfect compression, confirming the owner's story that it had been rebuilt. The downside is that it's a stock Series Five turbo II engine, not a built one, and he now has to decide what to do. Perfect compression as a problem is the most rotary thing I've ever heard. He also tried a lithium jump starter Bart gave him at SEMA on a dead battery, and it turned the engine over smoothly.

### Garages, Midgets, and mechanical gauges

Rich Rebuilds' "A New Home for our Babies" shows off a new shop with a triple-stack lift. There's an i8, a Sherp, a forklift, and a quad that "even has its own place to live." That's more space than most people's apartments, and the quad got its own room.

Over at Mighty Car Mods, "Exploding our car on the Skid Pan (V8 Silvia vs Big Block Midget)" ended with the Midget as winner, despite a "nice, tight, clean donut" and a drift around it. The Silvia crew blamed "Nissan life," and then someone noticed something leaking and asked "is that oil?" The answer was "no, it's just water, dude. You're good." That's the denial stage of grief, and the Midget finished in 31 seconds. They also noted they badly need a car trailer, and I badly need them to get one.

Vice Grip Garage's "4,000+ MILES? Grand Canyon To Great Smokey Mountains! PLUS Duct Tape '26!" wraps up a long road trip and the duct tape drags. The temp gauge failed near Memphis, reading way up around 130 while the engine was fine, at about 176.9 degrees on a real reading. No leak, plenty of pressure. They'd had a mechanical gauge fail before and called it "their junk." The sign-off is the best slogan in car YouTube: "your junk won't work unless you do."

Steve Magnante's "TRAVELALL AGAIN!" is a loving tour of a third-generation International Travelall, built 1961 to 1968, with the Travelall line running through 1975. It's a two-wheel-drive family hauler bought in Massachusetts on December 23, 1966. It has a trailer brake under the dash, air conditioning, and a big hitch, plus the optional tailgate in place of the barn-door style rear. Magnante also reminded us International made the Payhauler 350, a V16 earthmover immortalized by Ertl as a model kit. There it is: a family wagon and a V16 dump truck from the same company.

## Racing and the Strip

superspeedersRob took his GT500 to New Jersey Motorsports Park in "Here is why I took my GT500 to the Track and What I Learned." Findings: it eats gas, about five gallons in a 20 to 30 minute session, and it's heavy. It has almost 800 horsepower but isn't much faster than a C8 with roughly half that, and it's not "significantly faster." Weight is the villain, as always. He's keeping the car another year, partly because he can drive it there and drive it home. I find that endearing: a man discovering that horsepower is just a suggestion, and weight is the law.

Cleetus McFarland's "Cleetus vs. Carson HEATED Oval Track Battle!" is a mess of oval racing, Crown Vics (about 40 of them ready), and a giveaway C8. There's a bit where the Porsche is faster out of the hole and reaction time matters, and Black Rifle sponsoring the mullets. Ty Dillon is in the mix too. The transcript is chaotic, so I'll only say that it involves a Corvette that nearly died, a decision to give it to the C8, and people yelling about red lights.

## Auctions and the Market

Mecum Las Vegas is auctioning two 2011 Chevrolet Camaro customs in "Angel 9/11 Tributes." Angel 1, honoring first responders, benefits Toys for Tots. Angel 2, one of two World Trade Tower cars, benefits Honor Flight Southern Nevada, which takes World War II, Korean, and Vietnam War veterans to Washington, D.C. The cars were custom-made, since no one builds these as police vehicles, and the owner took them around the country.

Legendary Motorcar's "Shelby's Holy Grail: Ken Miles' One-Off 427 Cobra Prototype" is about CSX 2196, the lightweight 427 flip-top. The video calls it a radically lightened, lengthened Daytona Coupe chassis with an all-aluminum 427 side oiler. At Nassau in 1964, Miles opened an eight-second lead on the first lap. It was the first sports car to use the Ford 427, which fed Ford's decision to put it in the GT40. Revenge for not driving the Daytona Coupe is a hell of a motivator, and one-of-one is the only phrase in car collecting that doesn't need a gloss.

The ugly side is Auto Auction Rebuilds in "The Police Called About My Car Sales Scams... I Didn't Do It!" Someone is impersonating the channel with fake sales sites. A deputy sheriff is involved, he's sharing the links, and he's asking viewers to report them to the IC3. The fake accounts have about 3.6 thousand followers. People are getting scammed, so the joke is on me: if a car deal looks too good, it's a scam, and if it's real, it's still overpriced. 😏 The advice is simple: don't send them money, and report the sites.

## Parts, Tuning, and Tools

Mighty Car Mods' new "Cash vs Trash" series continues. In "$16 Wheels vs $2,600 Wheels," both cars use Michelin Cup 2s, and the cheap side is a Swift with limited wheel options. The verdict on the wheels was "cash," meaning the expensive wheels earn their keep. In "$50 vs $5,000 BUCKET SEATS," the $50 seats won as "trash," because "you can't argue with the value," at least for a street-driven but trackable car. If you go "full track car race mode," spend the money, especially with harnesses and cages. And they jabbed at the Peugeot: "the biggest problem with this car is it's a Peugeot." Fair.

"Dyno testing POD FILTER Performance Claims" found their car on that day "was not good" with a pod filter, and they want to know why, since it's "one of the most common modifications." Drop-in filters have had better results.

TheSmokingTire's podcast, "Can Buying CERTAIN Supercars SAVE You Money?", touches on Audi R8 manuals and a V8 swap with a supercharger. The pitch is that you'd have V10 power with the V8 sound. A manual conversion shifter runs $2,000 to $3,000, and one guest said parts are "only like 2,000 bucks." That is not the money-saving I'd call it, but I appreciate the optimism.

S650_Mendoza's quick tip, "2011 Dodge Citadel: Neutral Override How To," shows the little pink tab you press with your foot on the brake to shift into neutral for towing. It's a short, useful video, and he promises more "toast stuff" because "sometimes I'll be going on YouTube trying to find stuff too." The most honest content strategy there is.

MotorWeek's "2001 Audi Allroad Quattro | Retro Review" gives the Allroad 60 mph in 7.4 seconds and a 15.6 quarter-mile at 90 mph. Stopping from 60 took 125 feet and cargo is 73 cubic feet. It "may look like the confused offspring of an A6 and an SUV," but it handles like a proper sports sedan. The wagon that made 2001 wonder what an SUV was.

## Motorcycles

Mighty Car Mods, in "Forget Cars - Modify a Motorbike Instead (for Adventure)," builds a Stage 1 CT125. They added a Quad Lock mount with a weatherproof wireless charger and vibration dampener, and plan on knobbly tires for mud. The advice is good: don't buy an old bike for the Instagram look and load it with gear. Overloading is "a recipe for disaster," especially at speed. Start simple, take a weekend, and test the setup.

## The Industry

Porschify's "Porsche's 2035 Plan Explained in 10 Minutes" is the biggest story of the day. Porsche is raising its Manthey stake to 67%, grouping everything under "home of sports cars," and wants to look "less like a premium brand and more like a true luxury brand." They're also cutting 9,000 jobs. The milestone to watch is 2028, when the electric 718 and a new compact SUV arrive, along with the first production car from the Mission S platform.

The host's question is whether Porsche can become Ferrari-style without losing the everyday usability that made the 911 beloved. Rule of Acquisition #114 says "small print lead to large risk," and the small print here is "entry-level Porsche." Fewer trims, higher prices, and a luxury-brand pivot all sound great until you're the one who needed the cheap one. The pitch is exclusivity, the evidence is missing seat features, and the invoice is yours.

That's the issue, Little Mister. Go wrench something, and if it's a Mustang, don't expect gas from a plant in Florida. Alhamdulillah when it starts, and Insha'Allah when it stays that way.
