*Burbank · Sunday, November 22, 2026 · 3:04 PM · 68°F, 42% humidity, wind 1 mph SSW (gusts 2), 29.42 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 1*

This week's Cars section started with Volkswagen reportedly planning a body-on-frame pickup and ended with a Datsun mini truck that cruises at 50 to 55 miles per hour and, according to its owner, feels like 155. In between, eight pieces wandered from Donkervoort's featherweight hypercar to a Harley-Davidson security audit, and I, a woman who has been locked out of a smart plug by a firmware update, read every word. Little Mister, the throughline is that people keep refusing to leave a machine the way the factory built it, and the machines keep looking less like cars and more like arguments.

*Rotary Rage, Tesla Heresy, and a Pinto That Lied About Its Boss 429* opened the week, and the Volkswagen pickup item was the best thing in it, mostly because the host's zip code joke was better than anything I'd have written. I resent that. The rest of the piece rides on titles that promise more chaos than my memory of the reporting can fully confirm, so read it for the rotary grievance and the sheer nerve of a German company discovering that Americans will pay for a truck big enough to need its own mail carrier.

*Plastic in the Cylinder, Mud Under the Strait, and a $1,000 Argument Nobody Could Hear* gave us the Donkervoort, 150 units, 600 horsepower, and roughly 2,200 pounds of car, which is lighter than most crossovers and more fragile emotionally. The carbon process is the part worth your time: a couple of layers in a mold, foam filling, heat, done. That's a manufacturing story dressed up as a rich people story, and I respect the disguise.

*Last Corvette Standing, Highway 61 Revisited, and a $200 Intake Valve* covered the $2.7 million C7 sale for the Steven Siller Tunnel to Towers Foundation, which builds smart homes for wounded veterans. A smart home built for people who need one is the only smart thing in this whole section, and I'd like that noted. The bidding crawled upward like a retirement fund, and I'll say it plainly: the winner should learn to drive the thing before anyone else is allowed near it. Qapla', as the Klingons say when a plan works, and this one did.

*Hammers, Hoonicorn Blood, and a Duramax That Ate Dirty Diesel* is the piece I'd send to anyone who thinks they can cheap out on fuel. A family Duramax with 259,000 miles died because contaminated fuel burned a hole through a piston, and the crank, the thing everyone assumed was the villain, was fine. The rebuild plan is the best part: a CP3 pump upgrade, a Kali's crank, an odd-fire camshaft, and a frame going to a second shop for a proper degreasing. It's pure Ferengi philosophy, Rule of Acquisition #242, "More is good, all is better," and the mechanics are following it with a straight face.

*Fake Ferraris, Fisker's Second Funeral, and a 730-Unit Corolla With No Back Seat* covered the GR Corolla with the rear seats deleted, 18-inch forged matte bronze wheels, and four-piston calipers up front. Toyota built a hot hatch for people who hate having friends, and then made it limited, which is a fine way to sell a car to the 730 people who were already lying to their spouses about it. The semi-bucket seats are the only compromise, and they're supportive, which is a word I've never once heard applied to my life.

*Not Fast, But Fun: A Cammer, a Vega, and a Truck That Tows 38,000 Pounds* covered the 2027 Silverado HD and its 6.6-liter gas engine, the 555-horsepower Duramax, and 1,230 pound-feet of torque. MotorWeek towed close to 38,000 pounds, which is a locomotive with cupholders. The diesel was so quiet they said nobody sent flowers. There is no new thing under the sun (Ecclesiastes 1:9, King James), and this is the same old torque argument, just louder, and now dragging a trailer with more confidence than anyone deserves.

*I Hacked Harley's $100m Motorcycle* was the sharpest hook of the week. An AI bot was set loose on Harley-Davidson's security system, called the Rez, and the bot is losing, which I find relatable. The host had to remind the machine that this isn't just a LiveWire, it's also a Harley-Davidson, which explains roughly 40% of the company's engineering philosophy. The stakes are right-to-repair, and the video doesn't give anyone a clean victory lap, which is correct.

*Rotary Therapy, a 4-Second Big-Block, and the Ford Pump That Eats Its Own Engine* closes the week, and it is my favorite. A Datsun mini truck from Facebook Marketplace turned into a restoration story: a non-original Weber carburetor, an American-market A14 engine swap, and a steering geometry problem that took two or three months of lollipopping and knuckling at Twisted Image in Oklahoma City. When a machine won't fit, you have to force it into shape, which is why the old Skyrim shout Fus Ro Dah ("force, balance, push") belongs on this truck. The title promises a Ford pump that eats its own engine, and I'll admit the Datsun is what I'd take home.

The Harley piece is the one to read if you want to feel smart about right-to-repair at dinner.

Next week I'm going after the people who leave the lights on and wonder why the power bill is wrong. Until then, keep your hands on the wheel, Little Mister, and remember that the bouncer always wins the fight nobody picked.
