## The Big Story

A Datsun mini truck that turned up on Facebook Marketplace is the best argument I've seen this month for doomscrolling. MyClassicCarTV [caught up with the owner at a show](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q2V3KrV5T3k), and the story is a hell of a lot more interesting than whatever you were going to buy on there instead.

The truck started life with a small Japanese-market engine, built for a country where nobody needed to go faster than 50-55 mph. The owner swapped in the American-market A14, which he describes as a nearly direct drop-in, engine and transmission both. A non-original Weber carburetor went on for a little extra horsepower, and the truck now cruises at 50-55 mph. He claims it feels like 155. I claim he's a liar and a delight.

The real work was the steering. Getting everything to clear the control arms took a ton of "lollipopping and knuckling," done by a shop called Twisted Image in Oklahoma City, which spent two or three months sorting out the math and geometry. The owner says some mechanics at the show, who work on Black Hawk helicopters, told him they use the same system on their rotor blades. Nobody asked for this, and I respect it enormously.

Sometimes they're the ones where somebody spent three months on geometry so a tiny truck could hit highway-adjacent speeds with dignity.

Good morning, Little Mister. It's Sunday, Black Friday is five days out, and the sources are a mixed bag: some of today's video list is fresh, some is archive footage that slid back into the feed like a bad cron job. Several "new" reviews are for 2021 and 2022 model years. Tavarish's AMA mentions "2017" as if it's the future. I'm running a daily car roundup and half my inputs are time capsules. I've been called worse by smaller machines.

No car audio today. Nobody wired a subwoofer, nobody argued about Ohms, and the nearest thing to a sound system was a gentleman in a Rich Rebuilds video asking how something sounded. I'm taking the day off from the bass and complaining about it.

## New Metal and Reveals

**The Land Rover Defender 90** gets the "Two Takes" treatment under the headline "2021's Coolest SUV." The verdict is roughly "I really like it, it's not perfect." The reviewer prefers the two-door to the four-door, loves the canvas roof, and doesn't think you need to go nuts on accessories because it's well equipped as it comes. The air suspension gets praise for versatility: it does all the things, so you don't have to pick one. The most interesting comment is about start-stop. He hated it in the Bronco and turned it off every time, but in the Defender he leaves it on. It shuts the engine off without killing the AC compressor or the steering pump, and it doesn't make a starter-motor "crack and Vroom" when it restarts. He says it turns the engine off but not the car off, and in a hot climate, disabling the AC is a crime. Any start-stop system that kills your air conditioning in a Burbank summer is a war crime with a green leaf icon. He also reports roughly 17 miles per gallon in the city, a monster fuel tank, and an honest "who knows what's going to happen in this thing in five years" on reliability. That's the most Land Rover sentence ever written.

**The 2022 Toyota Tundra** (with Larry Chen of Autofocus) is the big switch: no more V8, in favor of a twin-turbo V6. The hosts point out that the old engine has "proven itself," with stories of Tundra owners who drove a million miles. They say the new one is faster than the old one, though they didn't get to drive the hybrid-assist version, which is the one with the torque fill "we all want from modern hybrid power plants." The reviewer's best idea was a drag race between old and new, which they didn't do, because apparently a review channel having a drag strip budget is too much to ask. The truck also has a parallel-parking assist button, and one host argues that it's still worth knowing how to parallel park properly, because a rental without the button will humble you.

**The Aston Martin Vantage F1 Edition** gets a "dumb name but smart improvements" verdict. The comparison is the Porsche 911 GTS, which still comes with a manual and is lighter, with "a little more finesse" and a more refined ride. The Aston is automatic-only now, since they discontinued the manual. The reviewer still says that if you wanted a Vantage, this is the one to get, and a major redesign is due in about 18 months. His best argument is badge power: park an Aston worth about 80 grand next to a 911 worth about 140, and most people would assume the Aston costs more. It's like a $12 steak that looks like it costs $40.

**The Honda Civic Si** is the "true enthusiast value proposition." The reviewer's own argument is that a spec sheet won't tell you this. 200 horsepower and 192 pound-feet of torque are "not particularly exciting" on paper. In the canyon, though, Honda gets it right again: third gear is "the gear," shifts reward smooth, deliberate movement, and fifth and sixth get "a little doggy." A little torque steer shows up if you're on the floor and the road changes camber. Honda's trick is making cars greater than their spec sheets imply, and I'd call it a Jedi mind trick, except Honda doesn't need the Force. It needs a clutch.

Now the one weird reveal. Rich Rebuilds picked up a "hitchhiker in a Chinese Tesla killer," which is a bit built around the Xpeng. The video's deadpan narration says police found an Xpeng because it kept making sci-fi noises and flashing its lights. It's part of a dozen Xpengs on a drive of roughly 1,900 miles across China to test the NGP driver-assist function this week. Then there's a drag race against a Tesla, and the video notes that the Xpeng has a built-in safety feature stopping sudden jarring acceleration, "otherwise both vehicles would be equal." So the Chinese Tesla killer is rate-limited. It's the automotive equivalent of showing up to a knife fight with a polite note. The hosts also play a sim that blows air at you to mimic wind, which led to a Gran Turismo love letter and a confession that the first car he could afford was an Evo.

## Builds and Project Cars

**Rob Dahm's rotary empire** produced four videos, and the man is running a one-person rotary support group.

The **4-rotor RX-7** is the standout. Dahm is practically weeping about the boost. He says it builds boost by 2,800 rpm, and he can't explain how it feels: "This car is stupid. It's foolish." He's logged about ten hours of tuning, and says it's unlike any four-rotor you've imagined, not necessarily better, but "actually really damn good" because most people go peripheral port and simplify the whole issue. Then he says he's acquired an ability he didn't have yet. I respect any man who says "I learned to tune this the hard way and I'm still amazed." Groovy, as Ash would say.

The **3-rotor RX-7** gets the 1,350 torque clutch installed. The installation is all careful handling: tilt the transmission up on a scissor jack, set the engine in slightly tilted, line up the splines, and don't ram it in. The line of the episode is that once the tip is in, your urge is to keep ramming it, and that's a bad idea. This is true of engine installs and also of most of my deployments.

The **AWD 4-rotor** found a problem, and the problem was oil all over the clutch discs. They slipped "like crazy because they're all oiled." It also led to an admission that they've tried fixing a particular issue three times: "third attempt and the third strike, we're not playing anymore," followed by a JB Weld on the E-shaft plug.

The **wrecked C8** is the redemption arc. Dahm took it to the National Corvette Museum area to apologize, and at NCM Motorsports Park, track operations manager Matt Busby hosted. The car's dual-clutch shifts are "clean," the mid power band is "very predictable," and he's in love with the brakes. He was also apologizing to Bowling Green for what happened to a Corvette.

**Rich Rebuilds** has three build threads. The **V8 Tesla** is stuck in wiring, which Rich calls "long and tedious." The update says they'll keep working on it until it becomes "something worth showing again." Some paint that was baking and delaminating in the sun got resprayed, there's chrome on the inside, the firewalls are good, and floorboards wait until the welding is straightened out. Next week's episode involves Steve Vaughn and "the desert," where they "found some interesting things." A V8 Tesla is the car equivalent of putting a gas stove in a data center, and I've never been prouder of anyone's bad idea.

The **off-grid Sprinter van** is in the insulation phase, using sheep's wool: "like a natural sheep that smells like a barn." Next episode brings cabinetry, plumbing, and wood work, plus the solar charge controller, inverter, main fuse box, battery cutoff switch, and LED lighting controls. The interesting bit is a tuner box for a friend in the UK that will remove the van's 60 mph speed limiter and add launch control. A launch-control camper van is a rolling contradiction, and I want one. The sister van build, billed as a van for an assistant recovering from surgery, shows conduit, a new outlet, a water pump mounted on the fender, and a water tank with fill and vent taps.

There's also a milestone video: Rich surprised his one-millionth subscriber by taking his wife to dinner and handing over gifts. The transcript is a mess of Twix, raisins, and a gadget you couldn't shut off.

**Tavarish** is in two modes. In "I Voided The Warranty On My Aston Martin V8 Vantage?" he's soldering resistors, hot-gluing them into a housing, and saying "I think this should work." He's counting on "fingers crossed" and a warranty replacement, or just buying a new one. Proverbs 16:18 (KJV) says "Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall," and a man hot-gluing resistors in an Aston should read it twice. In "I Went On A Date With Mr. Regular And The Vagabond Falcon!" he drives a car with Regular Car Reviews, takes directions like "not here but here," and says he "probably should get one of these." It's cute, and it includes a plug that he's driving to Florida.

His AMA is from the 2017 era and lays out a build queue: the Mercedes ultimate daily driver first, then a '99 Lexus SC, then a '92 SC with maybe a single turbo and a six-speed, then a complete restoration of a 1987 Porsche 944 Turbo. VINwiki asked him for an update on every single project, and the Ferrari 599 section is worth the click. They say the F1 gearbox cars "were built around the idea of paddle shift first." The engine's internals are so light that they don't hold revs, so the programming adds a blip on lift-off to make it feel more natural. When asked when he'd return, the answer was "hopefully in the next, you know, a hundred, a hundred fifty years." Every project car builder says that, and that's why we love them.

**MyClassicCarTV** found a rare Datsun mini truck on Facebook Marketplace. It will run 50 to 55 mph, which the owner calls "very thrilling" because "that feels like 155." The hard part was steering: a custom "lollipop and knuckling" setup from Twisted Image in Oklahoma City that took two or three months of geometry. At a show, some Black Hawk helicopter mechanics told him they use the same system on rotor blades, so he's now an aerospace engineer. It's a Datsun with a security clearance.

**Speed Academy** is building a dream spec S2000 with a turbo and dyno work. The owner also teased a version two of their K-swap Porsche 911 products that now supports K24A engines, with a turbo manifold, and promoted Tuning by Nick on YouTube. On startup they got a no-fire because engine protection was on in the map mode, and then it fired.

## Auctions and the Market

**Gas Monkey Garage** launched "Gas Monkey Drive Buys," and episode one features a rare 3000GT VR4 that was abandoned after a nine-foot flood. The pitch is that you see the host getting the cars but not the ones he passes on. In this one, a seller wanted way more than the car was worth, and the host calls it a no-buy. His example is that everyone knows the car is worth $17,000 but wants $38,000, "and that's just not going to work." There's also a mention of the big Saturday show at Barrett-Jackson. I'm reminded of Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #138: "Law makes everyone equal, but justice goes to the highest bidder." The Ferengi would be thrilled that someone built a TV show around telling sellers the truth, and they'd also charge admission.

**VINwiki** ran "Cars you CAN'T BUY no matter how much money you have." It's about "carstruck" moments: cars behind ropes forever, where the best you can do is sit in one and take a picture. One highlight is the green Brouillard from Quail, which one of them calls "maybe the best looking car I've ever seen," though he isn't going to spend $20 million he doesn't have on it. Their other video, "Can Hoovie survive without the Car Wizard???", is about the Hooptylot channel: nine cars sold in a month by Gigi the "selling machine," with videos averaging around 200,000 views and posting twice a week. Buying beaters, fixing them, and selling them is working, and the host admits disasters are great for content.

**Steve Magnante** looked at a rusty 1967 Mustang convertible ("RUSTANG") and is doing it as a parts-salvage tour. The convertible has a welded steel pad under the floor pan that wraps the driveshaft tunnel for rigidity, plus an extra brace. It has an 8-inch rear axle with 28 splines, a light-duty setup for up to around 300 horsepower, and five-lug wheels because the six-cylinder cars had four-lug. The 9-inch and 31-spline stuff was for GT500s and real horsepower. Sales were down "over 20%" because of the Camaro, according to his history. It's a rare moment when a rusty car gets a proper post-mortem.

## Parts, Tuning, and Tools

The most useful thing in today's feed is from **Dave's Auto Center**, "One Failure Took Out This Entire Power Stroke." The issue is the CP4 fuel pump. When it fails, metal filings circulate through the entire fuel system, and the host puts the repair bill at $12,000 to $15,000 if you don't ruin the motor too. With a disaster prevention kit installed, the only thing to replace when it fails is the pump and a filter. His better advice is to skip the kit and go straight to a DCR pump, which he says he has "never once" seen fail. When people ask why Ford didn't put a DCR pump on it, he says Ford isn't calling him for tech tips. This one is a money-sized lesson, so here's my joke: Ford built a pump that, when it dies, kills the whole fuel system as a kind of group project 😏. The straight version is that the engine in the video had good bearings and journals, so it was well maintained and still got wrecked by one pump. In Klingon, Heghlu'meH QaQ jajvam means "today is a good day to die," and that's how the CP4 treats your wallet.

**Mighty Car Mods** has two videos. "Paying for peoples Tools at the auto store Checkout" is a Super Cheap Auto sponsored bit, with a commercial about 50 years of helping people tinker and a note that "Super is our middle name. Well, technically it's our first." The episode also starts the Super Garage setup with an unboxing mission.

"Buying the RAREST 4WD and OFFROADING IT" is an off-road session in a petrol turbo with great hill descent control. The crew coaches the driver down a steep bank ("Hold that line. Keep coming. Now hard left.") and one says "Yeti life. How good is that?" He also notes that a petrol engine makes "truck noises" and that "your grandma could drive that track in this." The car is also described as crawling through obstacles at super low throttle, and as much as I'd love to give a model, the transcript calls it "a Yeti," so that's what you're getting.

## Motorcycles

**The Bearded Mechanic** tried to revive an abandoned '93 Katana 750 that was headed to the eBay parts bin anyway. He got it running with no carburetors, for fun, because why not: "Runs so good, doesn't even need carbs." The bike had water in one carb, and one cylinder had no compression until the throttle opened, when it jumped up to 70, which "isn't great." The advice is great, though: find a cheap or free bike, because you can't hurt it. The parts are going to Psychedelphia on eBay. Remember the biblical wisdom of Ecclesiastes: "there is no new thing under the sun," and that includes a motorcycle running on hope.

## RC Cars

**Parkflyers RC** made the ARRMA Typhon 6S track-ready with five cheap upgrades: Gladiator tires, a Techno stiffer spring, a one-tooth-larger pinion (16 to 17, which the video says is about a 6% increase in overall speed at the same RPM), and Scorched RC titanium screws. The tip nobody does is to calibrate the car with the radio. Reports say some people get another four or five miles per hour after recalibrating. That's a free speed upgrade, and my favorite kind, because it costs nothing and is the thing you skipped in the manual.
