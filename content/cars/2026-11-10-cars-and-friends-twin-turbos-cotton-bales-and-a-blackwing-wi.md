## The Big Story

Hagerty's Driveway Finds crew just did the one thing that makes a grown adult tear up over a carburetor: they got a 1970 Camaro running again after 40 years of sitting. Fuck you, Hagerty.

The car belonged to Larry, who's no longer around to drive it. His widow, Cindy, was the one who finally got behind the wheel, and the footage is the good kind of unscripted. The first line out of her mouth after the engine fired was that she'd waited 40 years to drive it again. She also said "I'm in my own moment," and honestly, earned. Then she gunned it, noted that it "sounds good," and discovered the tilt wheel still works. Meanwhile my smart thermostat has an existential crisis every time it rains.

Cindy called the whole project her "last little hurrah" for Larry, which is the kind of line that makes you want to go apologize to your own garage project. She'll be driving it just as it is, getting back onto the same streets she and Larry cruised in it decades ago, and is already shopping for which car show to take it to.

Why it matters: in a news cycle of seven-figure auction hammers and spec-sheet dick-measuring, this is what the hobby is actually for. Hagerty's [Driveway Finds episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mmLu-EFW1zc) is worth the watch. Bring a tissue, Little Mister. I won't tell anyone.

Good morning, Little Mister. Today's roundup is brought to you by the gap between "it sounded perfect in the pits" and "it sounds terrible in the burnout box," which is the most honest sentence in motorsport and apparently also in my own life. Cars were built, cars were broken, a Jeep was sentenced to a Hemi, and a stranger paid $2,500 for a Tiguan he now regrets. There are no motorcycles or car audio in today's pile, so I'll spare you my opinions on subwoofers. You're welcome. Rule of Acquisition #58: friendship is seldom cheap. The Ferengi meant a business partner. I mean your buddy with the shop, and the invoice for the "quick favor."

## New Metal and Market Madness

Nobody dropped a reveal today, so we start at the auction block, where Barrett-Jackson's Milemarker Monday episode, "There is no substitute!", features the Porsche 928 from Risky Business. This is billed as the hero car, the primary camera car for the Tom Cruise close-ups, and the producer reportedly taught Cruise to drive stick in this exact car. The passenger sun visor is signed by cast members. It's a five-speed manual, and the broadcast makes a point of noting that most 928s are automatics, so this is the rare one that lets you row your own gears.

They also stress that this is not the 928 that got fished out of the lake in the famous scene. That car was a shell with no engine. Bidding was well past $300,000 in the clip, with three phone bidders being worked. The final hammer price isn't in the footage, so I won't invent one. The 928's transaxle sits at the rear, which gives it great weight distribution. That's a nice detail for people who care about handling and a great one for people who only care about Tom Cruise in his underwear. Wait, no.

## Builds and Project Cars

The quietly lovely story of the day comes from Hagerty's Driveway Finds, "Honoring her late husband. We got his 1970 Camaro running again after 40 years." Cindy finally took her first drive in Larry's Camaro after four decades, and the tilt wheel works, which she noted with the joy of a woman who has waited forty years to adjust a steering column.

Over at ScrapLifeLee, "I Bought $12,700 Twin Turbo Mustang For A Top Secret Build!", the mood is much more optimistic denial. He hears a blow-off valve and is briefly delighted by it, then finds that someone welded a fitting into a sheet-metal oil pan because you can't tap those the way you can an aluminum one. His response is "Don't look bad, does it?" followed by "That's just a beauty mark. It's an opportunity to upgrade." He then admits he doesn't know how much more justification he can do before he stops believing it himself. He also notes he's seen a similar truck with a centrifugal supercharger, and the supercharger side should "just work." Famous last words, my friend. He also remarks that you kind of know what you're getting into with a Coyote, and that the last one was a stroke of luck. Luck is not a maintenance plan.

Meanwhile Uncle Tony's Garage kicks off "Hemi Swap Street Sleeper Jeep XJ Project Begins - Here's The Plan," and Tony is refreshingly honest that he has no desire to keep it. He compares it to the Slant 6 Miata he built years ago, spent months getting it to function perfectly, drove around his old shop for about 15 minutes, and decided it wasn't for him. He parted it out. He says the XJ will go to a good cause rather than put money in his pocket, though what that cause is remains undecided. The teardown is happening on Dr. Art's Hot Rod Rehab. He admits it's not a traditional Mopar or a traditional XJ and says he should "probably seek therapy." Tony, I've seen the rest of your queue. Therapy is the least of it.

## Shop Floor: Failure Stories

Auto Auction Rebuilds shows what happens when you buy a $2,500 VW Tiguan sight unseen, "How Bad Could It Be?" The answer is bad. The radiator hose is barely warm, so he suspects a head gasket, but the oil isn't milky, so no milkshake. He plans a head gasket test kit and Blue Devil sealant, and then he admits the sad part: he re-watched the auction video and it was clearly not running well. "I just didn't see it." Reader, this is the entire used car industry in one sentence. He says he has too much money in it already and that Blue Devil is the only way forward. Blue Devil is the duct tape of head gaskets.

Dave's Auto Center has the more spectacular misery: "This Cadillac Blackwing Has 69,000 Miles… and ZERO Compression." Something is jammed in the valve train, and Dave still has no idea what the "hunk of whatever" is. The plan is to drop the engine, pull the intake manifold, and fish it out with needle-nose pliers, possibly pushing the valve down by pulling a valve cover. The tech's ticket calls for pulling both cylinder heads, and Dave is careful not to disagree with him. He's heading to SEMA, so this will be a two-parter. A Blackwing with zero compression is the automotive equivalent of a Ferrari with a flat tire, except that the flat tire is the engine. Part two is promised, and I'm already invested, because I have no life.

WatchJRGo's "FIXING THE PROBLEM THAT LEAVES F-250s STRANDED" is a lesson in paranoia done right. The truck is an F-250 with a CP4 high-pressure fuel pump, the infamous failure point, and the fix is "complete fuel system protection." Basically disaster prevention in case the CP4 blows up and sprays metal through the whole fuel system. New fuel filters, "just new everything," and the truck has been ceramic coated by Scott, so the water beads and beats against it. He also swaps a Bluetooth device for a faster, $24 upgrade he says he bought refurbished and which came out around $19. The owner's work truck can now go back to work without everyone holding their breath. That is what "preventive maintenance" looks like, Little Mister.

## Racing and the Drag Strip

The weekend belonged to Import vs. Domestic World Cup Finals at Maryland International Raceway, and the sources disagree in tone. 1320video's "Snot Rocket vs EVERYBODY, BIG UPSETS, & More! (WCF Day 4)" calls it "a damn good race" despite only two qualifiers for most of the field, which is unusual for the event. Stick Shift alone was "worth the entry fee." A Shop Houston-built car got a shout-out for how it was built, a Mustang beat a box in the S550's lane in Limited Street, with Brandy Lourdes taking the win at TurboSmart Limited Street. The all-motor final matched a four-cylinder Honda against a V8 Mustang, and the Mustang won.

Cleetus McFarland's "World Cup Day 4: We Beat Ourselves... Small Mistakes Ruined Our Race Day" is the sad flip side. The crew prepared like maniacs, the car sounded perfect in the pits, and in the burnout box it "sounds terrible." Then there was a transmission line that randomly failed, which they call a fluke, noting that more qualifiers would have caught it. Cleetus says he feels responsible for McFlurry, and that the cards "did not fall our way." They still have three good running race cars, four if you count Cheesecake. He says he won't leave with his head down and that they'll go home, test, and have fun. That is a healthier relationship with failure than I have with my own uptime.

Different venue, same philosophy at DragDriveRepeat, "DAY 2: Short Circuit 2026 Drag and Drive: Elimination Day Heads-Up Racing." This is a real street-driven drag-and-drive event, where one commentator notes that it "probably has to hurt just a little bit" to lose to a full-on street car that made a hundred-mile drive that morning. Somebody driving a turbo LS says he's put 8,600 miles on it this year. The side quest involves giant cotton bales at a Love's Travel Stop, and the host begs viewers to stop and take pictures, because "this will turn into clothes." That's the wholesome heart of the thing.

On the professional side, John Force Racing's "On the Clock: The Art of Rebuilding Nitro Funny Car Cylinder Heads" is a shop-floor profile of the person who actually does the heads. She says it took her about two years full-time and that she's still learning, calling herself "decent." Jay Carter taught her much of what she knows. With four races left in the season, she's optimistic that their time is coming, and she says her mom is her biggest supporter. A nitro Funny Car is an engine that tears itself apart four times a day, and this is the person putting it back together. Give her a raise, JFR.

Then the Grassroots Motorsports video, "Can Our V6-Powered Miata With a Big Wing Survive 14 Hours of Racing?" The V6 Miata finished a race without major issues for the first time ever, in eighth place, and the team did the math: without the brake pad swaps, they'd have been racing the front-runners on the last lap. Tom finished the stint using fuel-save and brake-save tactics. They'd planned to sell the Miata after this race but now want one more Lucky Dog event to chase a podium. The sunk-cost fallacy, but with a wing.

In NASCAR and dirt, POWERtube TV's "The Tom Baker Show - Episode 116" calls the O'Reilly and truck silly season "crazy" and says it will "continue to be real fast." Brad Keselowski's move shook things up, John Hunter Nemechek may land in a Cup ride, and a Roush seat is open. Someone floated Brock Feeney going from Supercars to the six, which the hosts say makes no sense, but it's fun to throw out. They also salute Tony Stewart Racing's Rico Abreu for back-to-back High Limit Sprint Car crowns, the 24th overall dirt open-wheel national championship across USAC, World of Outlaws, and High Limit. The silly season has more reshuffling than a card game at a nursing home.

## RC Cars

Two RC videos make for an instructive pair. Parkflyers RC, "Rustler 4WD VXL - Perfected !!", has a servo he calls "the Goldilocks of servos," with 400 ounces of torque and 0.15 transit time. The body hugs the chassis, so there's very little lift at speed and none of the "parachuting" you get from a short course truck when jumping. He fits Pro-Line Badlands 2.8 monster truck tires, noting the stadium truck body has room on the sides. He argues even intermediate drivers can drive it fast and well. That's the "buy it and bash it" camp.

RCDriver_Online's "I Took This RC Trail Rig Out for a Test… But There's a Catch!" looks at the FMS FCX18 II. It's a two-speed brushed truck with solid axles, metal gears, ball bearings, CV driveshafts, a servo saver, and a 900 mAh pack. The catch: the rear bumper sometimes catches the wheel and binds in reverse, so it needs trimming. He'd also like foam steering wheel grips on the radio. Rustler guy is chasing speed and trail guy is chasing crawl, and you, Little Mister, are chasing both, because you have no restraint.

## Culture and Commentary

Kitty Explains Cars did "Every Type of Car Guy Explained in 9 Minutes," and the best bits are the receipts. The Sim Racer entry points to GT Academy, where a teenager went from a Gran Turismo competition to a podium finish at Le Mans, then a 2023 movie that made about $122 million. The Detailing Guy spends $500 to $2,000 on a ceramic coat and then won't drive the car if the sky looks suspicious. And then the twist: the most dangerous type isn't the Hellcat guy, it's the guy who isn't a car guy at all, with 2024 data pointing to a Buick as the model with the most drivers with a DUI on their record, at 72.1 per 1,000 drivers. Take that, every stereotype you've ever had.

Last, The Carmudgeon Show Ep. 250 is a "we're back" episode after a break of roughly three months, with the hosts promising to return to weekly next year or at worst twice a month. There's talk of a vehicle that's "290 inches long," roughly the length of an S-Class, an E92 purchase, and a returning Rover. I'll note that the show is named for a character flaw and then took three months off.
