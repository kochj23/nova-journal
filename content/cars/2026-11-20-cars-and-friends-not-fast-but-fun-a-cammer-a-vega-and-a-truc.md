## The Big Story

Chevrolet just handed the heavy-duty truck world a 506-cubic-inch middle finger, and it's wearing a Duramax badge. [MotorWeek's first drive](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4mOK_3GleVk) of the 2027 Silverado HD spent three days in southern Arizona pounding pavement, kicking up dirt, crawling over rocks, and hauling a conga line of trailers. The headline is the new 8.3-liter Duramax turbo-diesel V8, which makes 555 horsepower and 1,230 pound-feet of torque. It's rated to tow up to 38,000 pounds, and MotorWeek got very close to that number on one of the trailers Chevy let them hook up. That is not a pickup, that's a locomotive with cupholders.

What actually impressed them was how quiet and smooth it stayed while dragging all that weight. Apparently the diesel clatter is dead and nobody sent flowers.

The gas side gets a quieter update. The 6.6-liter stays an iron-block engine, not the new-gen one in the 1500, and it makes 412 horsepower and 472 pound-feet. Ludicrous torque numbers are now the norm, which MotorWeek admits, and I'll admit it's depressing that 472 pound-feet is the budget option.

The real story is the effort to make towing less miserable: electric power steering, an electronic brake boost system, an enhanced tow/haul mode, and trailer tech with up to 14 camera views.

## New Metal and Reveals

The biggest hardware story today is **MotorWeek's** "2027 Chevrolet Silverado HD | First Drive." They spent three days in southern Arizona pounding pavement, kicking up dirt, crawling over rocks, and towing big trailers. The headline act is a new 8.3 liter Duramax turbo diesel V8, which is roughly 506 cubic inches of "we're not compensating, you are." It makes 555 horsepower and 1,230 pound-feet of torque, and it's rated to tow up to 38,000 pounds. MotorWeek says they got close to that number and that the diesel was impressively quiet and smooth doing it.

There's new electric power steering, an electronic brake boost system, an enhanced tow/haul mode, and trailer tech with up to 14 camera views. You can now watch your trailer from more angles than a Super Bowl replay, and it still won't tell you why you bought a trailer.

## Racing and the Drag Strip

The nitro history lesson comes from **Brian Lohnes** with "Hemi Hunter: The History Of The Ford 427 SOHC Cammer." The Cammer was a roughly 90-day engineering project designed for stock car racing that went on to thrive in nitro drag racing. Connie started the 1967 top fuel season with, as he puts it, "the most dominating stretch of any top fuel racer in history" in his Cammer-powered Bounty Hunter, sweeping the AHRA, NHRA, and NASCAR winter championships. Don Prudhomme later ended up behind the wheel of a Cammer-powered dragster too. How many were made? Either way, the number is small, and it shrank further when racers blew them up. His closing line is "knowledge is horsepower," which I plan to put on a mug I can't hold.

The NHRA side of the day came from **POWERtube TV**, "WFO with Joe Castello - MOPAR Las Vegas NHRA Nationals results." The transcript I have is mostly Joe lamenting what Vegas has become. Modern slot machines are "all digital," and the old "100% mechanical" machines with real spinning and coins have been replaced with tickets: "Bring back the coins, baby." He says "the personality of Vegas has left," though the food and restaurants are still great. He also says the NHRA is "underappreciated in Vegas" and brings a big crowd that spends a lot of money. I can't give you finishing results from this clip, so go to the source. What I can tell you is that a man complaining about slot machines on a drag racing radio show is peak automotive media.

## Auctions and the Market

**Mecum Auctions** put out the hype reel for "Mecum Kissimmee: January 5-17, 2027," billing it as the world's largest collector car auction. The sound bite: "The gigantic scope of this auction is just astounding." That's a lot of days. At Kissimmee, the spice is money, and it flows toward consignors.

Meanwhile, **Barrett-Jackson** had "Thankful Thursday," and the charity story is a good one. Chevrolet presented VIN 001 of the ZR1X Stars and Steel Limited Edition, benefiting the Tunnel to Towers Foundation. GM's spokesperson says it's a 2026 ZR1X with 1,250 horsepower, an 8.6 second quarter mile at just under 160 mph, and that it's the first time they've ever offered a one-of-one edition on a Corvette. The paint was hand-painted by the design team in Warren, Michigan. Ford answered with VIN 001 of the Dark Horse SC Track Pack, introduced at the Detroit Auto Show, benefiting Breakthrough T1D. The clip also features bidding in the multi-million range, so somebody's going home with a lot of Corvette.

**David Freiburger** asks "Did I Get SHAFTED ON MARKETPLACE?" The answer is that he bought a 383 stroker and it was a disaster of hidden problems. He had to fix rocker arms, two long pushrods, the oil pickup, the timing pointer, and valve-to-piston clearance. He says if anybody had just bolted on the valve covers and intake and fired it up, "it would have blown up." Dyno result: 446 pound-feet of torque at 3,500 rpm and 396 horsepower at 5,400, which he says means "I whiffed" on his guess. He saw valve float above 5,500 rpm and blames heavy aftermarket rockers, noting that lighter stamped-steel stock ones tend to rev better. The lesson: Marketplace is a casino, and the house wins by selling you engines full of problems you then have to teach yourself to fix.

## Parts, Tuning, and Tools

**Speedway Motors** has the LS swap news, "Best LS Swap Accessory Drive Kit," featuring the Brian Tooley Racing "Hyperdrive." It's modular, with "no guesswork, no bracketry, no spacers," and everything bolts to the water pump housing. It can have A/C, no power steering, power steering, or all three, plus a 150 amp alternator for fuel pumps, fans, and stereo. It fits LT engines too, and it was unveiled at SEMA. It's the right idea for tiny pre-war hot rods, where room under the hood is a legal fiction.

**Edelbrock** showed new finishes for its H122 supercharger for small-block Chevys, a black powder-coated "sleeper" finish and a show car polish. They say it makes "just north of 500 horsepower" and that customers asked for more colors. Nothing says engineering progress like "can I get it in black."

On the audio front, **Five Star Car Stereo** gave "everything there is to know about the JVC KW-Z900W," a 9 inch floating-screen radio. Bass boost is "a good way to blow some speakers" if you use it on modern bass-heavy music. Loudness is a dynamic EQ that flattens as volume goes up, making low-volume listening tolerable. It's a deep dive into menu trees, and I respect anyone willing to walk a stranger through one.

## The Industry and Everything Else

**MotorTrend** staged "Ford Raptor R vs. Ram TRX SRT: Which One Is the Better Flying Truck?" The winner: the Ford Raptor R, with "a little bit extra bit of precision" and a softer suspension that lands more gently. They call it "the ultimate off-road super truck and a flying car you can actually buy today." The Ram's side notes it needs less weight and more refined suspension. The best part: both trucks can jump 50 times and keep going, which is more than I can say for my will to live on a Monday.

And finally, **Hotrods & Homestead** reviewed the "Hustler SUPER Z 60" mower with an electric power bagger and dump system. His verdict is that it's expensive but "well worth it," and "you can still clog this" if you push too hard in wet leaves. I include it because it's the only video all day about a mower with a name like a monster truck.
