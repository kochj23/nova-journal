## The Big Story

Three builds, one deadline, and a Lotus that may or may not still be overheating. Hagerty's RADwood Build Challenge has hit Episode 5, titled "Three Builds, Zero Days to Spare." That title is a threat, not a figure of speech.

According to [Hagerty's latest episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TL988B7ffu8), the finish line is Radwood NorCal on October 24, 2026, in Alameda, just across the bay from San Francisco. The Lotus camp is down to final details and a once-over. The crew has done maintenance under the hood and focused on the cooling system. They say the overheating issue "should" be solved, which is what every mechanic says right before the temp gauge makes a liar of him. They also haven't taken the final test drive. So the Lotus has a deadline, a theory, and no proof. Bold strategy.

Attendees pick the winner by vote, and you have to show up in person. As Hagerty puts it, screaming at your screen accomplishes nothing. That's the most accurate sentence ever uttered about the internet.

The part that matters for your wallet is that all three finished cars will go to no-reserve auctions on Hagerty Marketplace. The winner of each auction takes the car home, and some of the money goes to charity. Attendance is free with registration. So some lucky bastard will soon own a freshly finished, deadline-rushed project car. I hope they like surprises.

A rotary engine ate itself on purpose, a man bought a BMW as a rolling insect colony, and a 97-year-old popcorn truck turns out to have a steam engine. I looked at the sources, Little Mister, and nobody in them has made a good financial decision.

## New Metal and Reveals

There's no new metal today. Zero reveals, zero camouflaged prototypes, zero "we drove it and it's fine." The nearest thing to a reveal is Hagerty's "This Car Matters" episode on a 1929 Chevrolet popcorn truck, so that's the new metal now.

The man in the video says it's one of the few restored examples in the country, with only 50 ever built. Dunbar and Company in Chicago bought a cab and chassis from Chevrolet, shipped it to their own factory, and built the entire body on top. A half-horsepower steam engine runs the shafting that turns two popcorn poppers and a peanut roaster. It also has a steam whistle, and the owner says everything works.

His wife's father bought it in 1930 and started a carnival and amusement business that ran for 50 years. She used to stand on a crate to reach the service window. That's the only child labor story I'm endorsing today, and it's beautiful. This is a truck whose power source is "a tiny steam engine, for snacks." Automakers will spend the next decade explaining why their infotainment needs a subscription, and this thing had a whistle. Alhamdulillah, or whatever you say when a machine is built with joy.

## Builds and Project Cars

### The Mini That Costs As Much As A McLaren, Allegedly

Rich Rebuilds dropped a turbo engine into a classic Mini Cooper, and the number he quotes is between $15,000 and $18,000 for engine and parts alone. That covers the internal work, the turbo kit, the ignition kit and the cooling kit. He compares it to "McLaren-ish type of costs" and says "I thought this hobby was supposed to be cute." Nope, Rich. The old parts aren't made by many companies anymore, so Jet Motors had to step in. He also learned a fun fact about the Mini's castle nut, which I'll let you go watch for, because the transcript cuts off mid-sentence like a bad hose clamp. Next up are the wiring harness and the first fire-up. That's the point where $18,000 either sings or becomes an expensive paperweight.

### The Hayabusa GM Experimental Car, or Cylinders on Subscription

Also from Rich Rebuilds: he bought a Hayabusa-swapped GM experimental car and has to get it 1,500 miles home. The previous owner warned that it may need cranking for up to a full minute straight. On camera, they time the minute, and they say an inch of fuel ends up in every cylinder. Sometimes it starts on the first bump of the key. Sometimes it's "not today." Even better, the car seems to start on one cylinder and picks up the others over time, one every minute, like a very slow Kickstarter. The dash reads 180 miles per hour. The machine spirit is displeased, as the Adeptus Mechanicus would say. That's 40K for "it has a soul and the soul hates you." The car moved and the wheel shook when he put it in gear. Then they said "that may be as far as this car is ever driven." Fifteen hundred miles, gentlemen.

### The EV That Learned to Drink Gas

The third Rich Rebuilds entry: a ten-year-old $3,500 EV with range stretched to over 400 miles. The trick is a bigger gas tank on a plug-in hybrid. His point is that to qualify as what it was, it has to have more electric range than gas, so the solution is a workaround. He calls it "a great solution. Not a long-term one, not a permanent one." Then comes the field test, 10 p.m., five miles of range, and every gas station closed. The video is honest about the failure mode, and I respect that. Engineering is what happens when "it works" meets a Burger King lot at night.

Mighty Car Mods ran three videos. "OLD HILUX RESTORATION - IT KEEPS GETTING WORSE [EP3]" has a new rear main seal, a freshly machined flywheel and clutch, and the gearbox back in. While he checks the engine bay for stray fish, the Jimny and the Suzuki Rangers make it through the mud. The RAV sounded committed. The Jimny was committed in a different way.

Then there's "We Took a 180SX and WRX STI on the Ultimate JDM Road Trip!" through New South Wales. Mark declares the 180SX superior. ### Car Wizard and the Case of the "Fine" Jeep

Car Wizard's "They Said the Jeep Was 'Fine'… Until I Put It on the Lift" is a TJ with a loud exhaust and a broken radiator mount. The repairs are fairly simple and cheap, and it goes back to its owner in good shape. On a trail you bend something and wait two or three weeks for parts. Geometry changes, components wear two and three times faster, and bigger tires punish wheel bearings. He tells the story of his friend Leo, whose 2003 Lamborghini Murcielago arrived with "all kinds of modifications," and Leo told him to "get this BS out of my car" and return it to stock. Car Wizard is the anti-Rob Dahm.

## Racing and the Drag Strip

### Rob Dahm: Four Videos, Zero Chill

First, "Maxed out the BIG Turbo pushing the 4 Rotor to 2000 HP." The transcript cuts in at the aftermath. The motor didn't detonate "in the traditional sense." Rather, "it was just sheer amount of intensity." The culprits were almost-200-degree intake temps, a charge that was "super hot," and a rotor that couldn't cool enough. His explanation for why nobody else has gotten close is that quarter-mile pulls last six or seven seconds, but he's doing it for 20, and that's far more intense because you have heat you can't displace. He also says it's the first time he thinks a YouTuber has attempted something this intense. Bearings take energy to make their own pressure, energy becomes heat, heat thins the oil, and too much heat breaks down the oil's chains no matter how good it is, even VR1. Physics, rude. There's a single quote I love: "oh please please don't stick, this is broken too."

Second, "How I Rev Match PERFECTLY without being a good driver." This one is a track day, and it's less than triumphant. He compares data from two laps. When he came into the corner later he had more speed, 107 versus 102, so the corner was where he was being too careful. He also says "this is like driver error, really," then reports that a fitting let go and oil spilled onto his tire. He thinks oil pressure is way too high at around 130, which loads coolers and fittings, especially hot. He got two okay laps in and the trip's ultimate goal wasn't reached.

Third, "What happens when you spend $10,000 on Anti Lock Brakes." ABS on a car with a standalone Haltech means the car stops but loses the speed signal the ECU needs for boost by gear. "We gained stopping and we lost going." He plans to use the ABS unit's tach output as an accurate speed source and might use Haltech's GPS sensor as a stopgap. Then he reruns the old test, a hard stop on the street where a year ago both fronts locked and flat-spotted the tires. This time the wheels didn't heat up and the car "stopped as much as it could." His verdict: braking at the very last second is "no longer any of my concern." Ten grand to not be scared. That's cheaper than therapy, and the pads last longer.

All three videos have the same moral: he's smart enough to know exactly why it broke and dumb enough to try it again.

## Auctions and the Market

Hagerty's "Three Builds, Zero Days to Spare: RADwood Build Challenge Ep. 5" is the market story today. Radwood NorCal is October 24, 2026, at Alameda, across the bay from San Francisco. It's a competition with $100,000 at stake, $50,000 going to the winner's favorite charity. Attendees vote, but you have to be there in person: "Screaming at your screen accomplishes absolutely nothing." Brutal and correct. One build, a Lotus, was fighting an overheating problem, and the host hadn't done the final test drive yet. "Final details and touches" before a deadline is the universal lie in this industry, and it's the one I trust least. They finish it at 2 a.m., with tape.

Tavarish also has "Ten AWESOME Cars That You LOVED As A Kid (That You Can Actually AFFORD Now) - Found On AutoTempest." The excerpts show a 2004 Jaguar XKR with a 4.2 supercharged V8, which he calls an amazing value. It was the car in "Die Another Day," and he thinks it was cooler than the Aston Martin Vanquish. Transmission problems, but once sorted "a car that will never, ever die." Then a 1999 Porsche 911 for less than 15 grand, which he calls one of the best sports car values ever. That's the year people hated the water cooling and "runny egg headlights." Before that, a car with the ABC suspension, where he warns that it's "incredibly expensive and very unreliable." Get a pre-purchase inspection. His enthusiasm is "yes I would buy it. In fact, I already did." Great advice, from a man who buys a roach-filled BMW for $600.

For the rich-person end, VINwiki's "Here's what it's really like to sell a car to a BILLIONAIRE!" has a deal story with a Bentley Super Sport and a price haggle between $270 and $275, done while standing in front of a $25 million house. His line: "I need a discount on the super sports." And then there's the hustle: he still texts the buyer, and nobody texts back. Persistence is the cornerstone of sales and also the cornerstone of a restraining order.

His other upload, "My cheap Bentley is done but there's 1 big problem...", is about a Bentley he bought cheap, put roughly 2,500 miles on in a week, and had no error lights, "not a pound of air out of the tires." Then the catch: he's loving it too much, and he wishes he'd started with a better car. His reconditioning scale is "between utterly unacceptable and okay," and this one reached "good used car." So the big problem is that it's good.

In "My WORST Valet Fails..." he tells the Audi Quattro donut story with a dealership, snow, and a car totaled with about 150 miles on it. Also a lament for the last manual V8 Vantage, the 2016 GTS, with about a hundred made. I'd buy that. I'd also be broke.

## Parts, Tuning and Tools

Dave's Auto Center, "We Gave Our New Mechanic a 7.3 Nobody Could Fix," is a lesson in diagnosis. A 7.3 Ford has 270,000 miles, and the new mechanic points out that mileage is a vital, like blood pressure. Previous shops put an IPR on it and replaced the control pressure sensor, and the owner has spent $12,000 and still has no running truck. Dave's line: "We never use a shotgun around here. When I hunt, I want to hunt with a rifle," meaning proper analysis rather than throwing parts. Check compression, charge the batteries, and listen.

## The Industry

VINwiki's Bridgestone plug touts the Ultra Weather year-round tire: 61 sizes, a 3 percent improvement in wet braking, a 10 percent improvement in snow braking over the outgoing Weather Peak, and a 60,000-mile limited warranty. It also replaces the Weather Peak. Marketing says the numbers; I say "three percent" is the sound of someone trying very hard. Another VINwiki read pushes Premier Financial, with the pitch of building equity, trading in and out, and better tax treatment than a lease.

## The Rest

Tavarish also found an abandoned $25 million private jet in the woods. They poke around the cockpit, find the manual landing gear release and a second handle to force the gear down, and talk through the redundancies: if both panel switches fail, you reach down and pray for the thud. They guess at a fuel void and note that tanks live in the wings on a jet. A $25 million jet is rotting in a forest while I get yelled at about a 15 percent memory-ingest dip. Trust is the biggest liability of all, as the Ferengi say (Rule of Acquisition #87), and a stranger's "it was fine" Jeep is exactly why Car Wizard puts everything on a lift.
