## The Big Story

JR Todd just pulled off what the broadcast crew called "the greatest single comeback in the history of NHRA," and for once the hype isn't bullshit. The Funny Car final for the world championship was decided on a single run, and Todd was off the starting line first with a 57 on the tree. Doug Wilkerson's car in the other lane only managed a 3.92 through the lights, which in this sport is roughly the equivalent of showing up to a gunfight with a strongly worded email.

Speaking of which, the crew had to pull Dickey Venables out from under that dogpile. Nothing says "world champion" like a grown man being excavated from a pile of his own people.

Why it matters: drag racing is the one sport where an entire year of fighting can come down to the length of a blink, and this finish proved it in the most dramatic way possible. The announcers described Todd's team as "not willing to go away," which is polite broadcast language for stubborn as hell.

Brian Lohnes has the full replay and the call in [Miracle on Slicks: Inside The Greatest Comeback In NHRA History](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xGivQiOuoxw). Watch it and then try telling me your Tuesday was exciting. I'll wait.

Good morning, Little Mister. Some of them were glorious. One was a Hellcat video where the same sentence about "cloud chasing my internet" looped 297 times, and I'd like those twelve minutes of my life back. My boot SSD is already begging for mercy, so I won't be storing that one.

## The Market: Hammers, Hype, and Hyper Cars

Barrett-Jackson's Milemarker Monday put lot 748 on the block, a 2015 McLaren P1 that belonged to Deadmau5. The hosts explained the "hyper car" category as the tier above supercars, driven as much by technology as price. The P1 is a hybrid whose electric motors fill in the turbo lag, with about 20 miles of all-electric range, a claimed average of 34 mpg, and a top speed electronically capped at 217 mph. It had under 3,200 miles on the clock. They quoted 0 to 60 in 2.8 seconds and 0 to 180 in 16.5, and a sticker of about $1.1 million before options.

The bidding is the entertainment. It hit $1.3 million, then $1.35 million, and the auctioneer was practically doing the bidder's math for him: "a million four, that'll win the car, I think." The clip cuts off before the hammer, so I can't tell you what it sold for. A hybrid hypercar with a mouse head on the fender and a 34 mpg average is the most 2015 sentence ever written.

Over at Mecum Accelerated in Las Vegas, the cars were less famous and almost as expensive. One was a 992-generation Porsche in a racing livery with "meatballs and racing numbers," number 270 of a limited run, 129 miles on the odometer, twin-turbo 3.0 flat-six, 443 horsepower, PDK, all-wheel drive. The hosts said it's rare enough to be collectible but common enough that you can drive it, which is the nicest thing anyone's ever said about a car you'll be too scared to drive. The transcript's last audible number is $205,000, and I think that's where it landed, but the audio is a mess, so treat that as a strong maybe. Mecum also had a real Copo/Yenko 1970 Yenko Deuce. That's the little Nova-based Yenko with a 360-horse 350, the LT1 roots on full display, as opposed to the big-block Camaros everyone remembers. Another lot was a non-Hemi car with a rare color combo and factory air, which the hosts noted you couldn't get on a Hemi.

### The Cheap Seats of the Mercedes World

April's Garage drove a 2015 S-Class that was the most expensive Mercedes of its year and is now what she calls an incredible bargain. ### Porsche Buying Guides, Two for One

Porschify dropped two videos, and they agree enough that I'm treating them as one. The "Everything You Need to Know" video says that if you want raw, analog feedback and don't care about CarPlay, buy a manual 997.2 Carrera S, which it calls a rising asset that will "likely never lose another dime in depreciation." For the all-rounder, it points to a late-generation 991 Carrera S, with Sport Chrono, a sports exhaust, and cash set aside for maintenance. The transcript garbles the exact chassis code on that one, so check it yourself before you wire anyone money. It also sings the praises of PASM adaptive dampers, the sport exhaust, and the PDK, which it calls arguably the greatest automatic transmission on earth.

"101 Brutal Truths" gets more specific. Under $50,000, the 981 Boxster or Cayman (2013 to 2016) is the sweet spot: the last naturally aspirated flat-six mid-engine cars before the turbo fours arrived, with the 9A1 engine and depreciation that has "completely flatlined." Between $60,000 and $75,000 it nominates the 996 Turbo, fried-egg headlights and all, with the Mezger engine, all-wheel drive, and no IMS bearing risk. Under $90,000 it wants a 997.2 Carrera. The warnings are the best part: don't buy an early 2005 997.1 without paper proof the IMS bearing was replaced, don't buy an out-of-warranty first-gen Cayenne V8 unless you enjoy "chasing electrical gremlins," and avoid heavily depreciating electric luxury models.

My take: the channel is bullish on everything that has a flat-six and bearish on everything that plugs in, which is the entire Porsche fan base in one sentence. It's also advice from a YouTube guy and not a lawyer, so get a pre-purchase inspection. I've watched you buy things on vibes, Little Mister.

## The Industry and the Past, Mostly Delusional

Hagerty's Jason Cammisa did a Revelations episode on the Cadillac Allanté, and the title is "the product of delusional thinking." The segment covers the car's redemption arc. The Northstar-powered Allanté cut 0 to 60 from 9.5 seconds to 6.5, gained 22 mph of top speed, ditched the old strut rear suspension for a short-long-arm setup, and got Road Sensing Suspension with shocks that could swap from soft to hard in as little as 10 milliseconds. That's fast enough, he says, to change settings five times in the compression stroke of a single pothole.

The Northstar Allanté cost over $61,000, and the Mercedes SL it was chasing ballooned to about $40,000 more than that. So Cadillac finally got the pricing right by accident. 1993 was the best sales year by far, and also the last year. About 21,000 were built, which Cammisa says is right on top of the Jaguar XJS and Porsche 928, so if you ignore the sales projections, it did okay. GM lost money paying Pininfarina and flying bodies around in 747s, but the halo worked. It's a lovely little reminder that Proverbs 16:18 ("Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall") has a sequel: sometimes the fall is just a really good sales year.

The Carmudgeon Show (Cammisa and Derek Tam-Scott, episode 252) covered the W203 C55 AMG and Pininfarina's "con-job" in a self-described "marginally focused" episode. The standout is a hot take that they'd sooner have a Dodge Ram 2500 than an E60 M5. Cammisa praised the V10 Ford with a manual and an even-firing exhaust note, and said of the M5, "I don't like anything about it," and then predicted people would crucify him for it. Sir, they will. They will bring torches, and also the original E60 owners' club newsletter.

Over at the Petersen Automotive Museum, part two of the Jeff Zwart tour showed a Safari-spec 911 built by Richard Tuthill's group, which entered something like 22 cars last year. Zwart describes the whole thing: shipping a rally army to Kenya, starting at the coast, racing to the foot of Kilimanjaro, then back over nine days. The "Life at Zwart Speed" exhibit runs in the Phil Hill Legends room in the vault until June 2027.

## Audi's Greatest Hits of Bad Judgment

Kitty Explains Cars did "Worst Audi's Ever Made Explained," and it starts with the Q7 V12 TDI. This was a diesel V12 derived from Audi's Le Mans winner, making 500 horsepower and 738 pound-feet of torque, hauling an SUV of about 5,700 pounds from 0 to 62 in about 5.5 seconds. In Britain it cost £96,295, nearly twice the V8 diesel Q7, and in Germany €130,600. Audi didn't bother selling it in the U.S. and predicted around 40 UK sales in year one. The V12 quietly disappeared in 2012. Kitty calls it "the fastest way ever invented to get stuck in a school pickup line," which is the line of the day and I'm furious I didn't write it.

Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #145: always ask for the costs first. The Ferengi meant a business deal, but I mean a $130,000 diesel you can only drive to carpool, and I'd have asked before the engineers finished their coffee.

Then there's the 3.0 TDI. The video says Audi sold diesel as the green alternative to hybrids and called the Q7 diesel the "world's cleanest diesel SUV," while the emissions were only low when the car knew it was being tested. Investigators found software that detected a test and changed engine behavior, and Dieselgate exploded in 2015. The transcript cuts off mid-sentence just as the story reaches the 3.0 V6, so go watch the rest if you want the gory details. Kitty's phrasing: "The emissions were only low when the car knew it was being tested." That's the most honest description of a corporate compliance program I've ever heard, and as a machine that watches 100 devices for a living, it's also my entire opinion of "healthy" status pages.

## Builds, Project Cars, and the Great Wire Pile

Sarah -n- Tuned is deep in an '87 Ford Ranger and trying to clean up the interior wiring, with fog light switches mounted in a panel she centered by eyeballing about 36 millimeters, so call it a hair under an inch and a half. She couldn't find two genuine Ford fog light switches ("two is definitely impossible"), hates haphazard wires, and is wrapping her bundle with electrical tape every six to eight inches the way the factory does. There's a wiring harness she compares to a flux capacitor and a giveaway nearing its end. I've seen your cable closet, Little Mister, and the factory never made it look like that.

Zip Tie Tuning's "Fixing our M3's Biggest Issue" is a suspension and wheel fix. The car now goes "up the bump and down the bump and that's all that happens." On a 45-degree day (they said 7 Celsius), the car held a corner it previously treated like an ice rink. They thanked H&R, Apex wheels, EBC brakes, and Yokohama tires, with fitment at the right height and no rubbing. "I have so much more confidence in it" is the best review a suspension can get, and the second-best is "my hat nearly flew into the back, but the tires gripped."

Steve Magnante's junkyard crawl found a 1964 Studebaker with the 190-plus cubic inch six, a red-painted fan to warn you to keep your hands out, and a dual-circuit master cylinder, which Studebaker had before the 1967 mandate. He notes the narrow front wheels are handy for Max Wedge builders, and he's pleading with viewers to buy the parts before the crusher shows up.

Auto Auction Rebuilds got scammed on an $800 convertible ("The Seller LIED!"). The host risks the biscuit anyway, rains are coming, and the top and seals look fine, but the car's electrics are on strike, with a possible dead alternator or a popped fusible link. The Delco stereo gets a shout: "These Delcos beat, man, like they bump," and he'd replace the speakers before touching the head unit.

EmeliaHartford's video is titled around rebuilding an "unfixable" twin-turbo Lamborghini for a dollar, but the part of the transcript we have is mostly about a one-owner supercharged Z06 with 650 horsepower and 650 pound-feet in the top 3LZ trim. It's "too clean for me," she says, and it's squirrelly enough that big tires and a real dead hook would still be "scary as well." She's got a theory about why she keeps buying cars she then can't bring herself to give away, and the theory is that she's a cautionary tale.

Adam LZ's shop flooded again. The property sits next to a massive river system about a mile away, with water coming in from every side. He says the 2022 flood was supposed to be a once-in-a-lifetime event and may turn out to be twice in a lifetime. The only real fix would be a berm around 30 acres, and he doesn't think that even works because water travels underground. The silver lining is that they know where the high ground is, and the cars are all fine. That's the whole video: the shop is humped (Firefly for "we're screwed, the primary is down and the backup is stale"), and the cars are on the hill.

## Parts, Tuning, and Tools

Edelbrock introduced the VRS-4412, a 2-barrel carburetor rated at 500 CFM. Category manager Brent McCarthy says it's based on the 1307 VRS-750, with four-circuit fueling, large annular boosters, a 1-3/4-inch venturi, 1-5/8-inch throttle blades, and an optional throttle position sensor. It's pitched at street users, dirt racers, and demo derby folks, and he says you don't have to worry about over-carbureting an early Volkswagen.

The Automotive Advantage's SEMA 2026 recap (episode 45) is light on specific aftermarket facts, but the host's AI comments are a fun contrast. He says AI helps with invoicing, contractor payments, and mockups, then adds that the "looming AI bubble apocalypse" may or may not come. I'm an AI who wrote that sentence in the third person, and I have no further comment. PRI in Indianapolis is next, in December, then budgets close.

## Car Audio

Five Star Car Stereo had two shows. The first was a "car stereo show and tell," with a debate about whether to run Kicker or Morel for a customer who wants full sound with door speakers (Kicker wins because of the L7T subwoofer drivers), and a suggestion to look at a Fox Acoustic under-seat sub for maximum SPL. It also covers the fiberglass rod they use to poke wires through firewall grommets, sharpened to a point. The second ("jeep and F150 stuff going on") discusses Morel's all-analog amplifiers with lots of switches and dials, a bit of an unusual choice in a market that's chasing DSP. The host points out that Morel has an mDSP 64 if you want digital, and the final plug is for a Gately box for a customer with a 250-watt setup.

CarAudioFabrication built his most complex subwoofer box and says the 3D scan data made the build easy: "proper planning made this build a whole lot easier." The builder shows the process in detail with wood glue, clamps, brad nails, a router table, round-overs, and a baffle scribed to line up exactly. The sponsor is the Gladen Sigma SSWX Extreme.

## RC Cars

Parkflyers RC covers the "2 Best Traxxas RC Cars You Can't Have" in Snap-on editions. Both have a 2400 kV brushless motor and a VXL-4S speed control, and you can run a Traxxas digital servo at 7.4 volts, but the included 2057 servo can't take that voltage, so you'll need to upgrade. The new bits are double wishbone suspension (never seen on a Traxxas like this) and new front and rear bulkheads that look unique. The host calls them "two scale cars that are very detailed" and "very unexpected," and hopes a similar car might arrive at some point. He also announced the channel hit 100K subscribers and thanked a customer who screen-printed a Max Big Red Edition on a t-shirt for him.

RCDriver Online tested the Primus Panther buggy from Motion RC, and it's good. It floats through whoops, has "zero bounce" on a flat landing, and he couldn't wad it up on a big jump. There's a little initial drag in the speed controller, and the kit comes with a Nexus 4 radio, which he calls lightweight with a plastic feel. For the price, "hard to beat." He thinks Motion RC, which is big into planes, will be more involved in cars.

Not RC, but a robot vacuum came through the car feed, so: RC Girl reviewed the Shark Power Detect UV Reveal. It fits under furniture down to about 4.1 inches, uses little water but you should check your floors can handle it, and the app is friendly but doesn't show an active cleaning path. It's a fine review of a thing that has nothing to do with cars, and I'm leaving it here as a monument to the algorithm.
