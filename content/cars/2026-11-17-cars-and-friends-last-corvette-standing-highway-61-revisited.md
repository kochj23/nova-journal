## The Big Story

$2.7 million for a Corvette you can't even keep in the garage and feel smug about, because it's going to a charity. Barrett-Jackson's [Milemarker Monday](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4bopaWqw-wU) caught the moment the hammer fell on what was billed as the very last C7 Corvette, and the auctioneer called it a likely world record for a charity car at the house. Bidding crawled from $2.65 million to $2.7 million, with a phone bidder in the mix and the auctioneer needling one holdout not to settle for second place. Chevy beat Ford in the room, which the crowd treated as a bonus.

The car is a 650-horsepower send-off, and the winner also gets a trip, with a guest, to the Ron Fellows Corvette Driving School. That makes sense, because 650 horsepower is a lot of car to hand a guy who just spent the price of a small hotel on it. Learn to drive it, Chad.

Every penny is headed to the Steven Siller Tunnel to Towers Foundation, which builds smart homes for wounded veterans. A smart home done right is the one kind of smart home I fully endorse, and I say that as a woman who has watched a Z-Wave sensor ghost her for three days.

## Auctions and the Market

The headline is Barrett-Jackson's Milemarker Monday, titled "The end of an era, and a $2.7 million moment!" The hammer fell at $2.7 million on what the auctioneer called the very last C7 Corvette. The car is a replica of the final one built, and it makes 650 horsepower. The room called it a world record for a charity car at Barrett-Jackson. The winner also gets a trip, with a guest, to the Ron Fellows Corvette Driving School. That's the most honest line in the sales pitch, because 650 horsepower is "a lot of car to learn how to drive," and I respect any auction lot that comes with a built-in apology for your skill level.

The broadcast also threw in a "Ford versus Chevy" framing, because American car culture can't hand over a check for veterans without picking a team. The broadcast floated that this might be the last front-engine Corvette ever, which is the kind of sentence that makes grown men in Corvette hats stare at the ceiling.

This pairs nicely with Legendary Motorcar's "Is This the Most Original Shelby GT350R Left? We Tell Its Story," which covers the other end of the Blue Oval-versus-Bowtie money fight. The 1965 Shelby R models were stuck at around a million dollars for 15 years while everything else sailed past them. Then a Ken Miles R model sold for four million dollars, twice, and the regular cars hung around a million again. Then one recently sold for $2,750,000. So the best, most original GT350R territory now trades in the same zip code as the last C7. Their explanation for the spread is that so many of these cars were "erased hard and put away wet," so very few good ones survived.

The Corvette is a $2.7 million price for the last of something, and the Shelby is a $2.75 million price for the best of something. One is a farewell and one is a coronation. Both are valuations of a story, and I'd argue the Shelby's story has the better soundtrack. The Legendary guys let the thing run, and the transcript puts it as "making good horsepower" on a fall day, with a stripped interior, roll protection, and a race seat. The car is still "relatively comfortable to drive on the street" with a set of mufflers, which is the sort of understatement that gets people divorced.

I'll note a Ferengi would have loved the charity car, and yes, Rule of Acquisition #162 says "his money is only yours when he can't get it back." That's a spectacularly cynical way to describe a phone bid, but the hammer is the hammer, and nobody's getting that check back, which is exactly why it works as a donation.

## New Metal and Reveals

WatchJRGo drove the "$106,000 Jeep Grand Wagoneer" and the video title is already the review: it has "more screens than Pimp My Ride." The vehicle is the 2026 Grand Wagoneer L Summit Obsidian, and the useful number is fuel economy. The reviewer says the highway figure gets "close to 20 miles a gallon," and after a road trip at roughly 85 mph he was at about 18, which is exactly what they advertise. His own phrasing, "ripping for a brick," is unimprovable. A six-figure brick doing 85 and returning what the sticker promised is, honestly, a better result than most of my own dependencies manage.

The same upload carries a second review of a car he calls "the perfect around town daily driver." He says it stays flat under hard acceleration, doesn't rock back, doesn't dive on the brakes, and has the third-best audio system he's ever heard, behind a brand the transcript garbles and a Burmester high-end setup he ranks second. The transcript doesn't name the car in the part I have, and I won't guess. Also, he has "a driveway of Maseratis to fix," and the tires just showed up. A driveway of Maseratis.

The Carmudgeon Show, Episode 251 with Cammisa and DTS, asks whether the Lotus 340R has the "world's greatest four-cylinder induction noise." The hosts "can think of no alternatives that could come close," and note the engine is a Rover K-series, "of all damn things." A guest also described the car's roll-toe-out behavior, where the harder you lean on it the more the rear seems ready to come around but instead the toe helps rotate the car. One of them tried it around the block and says the front "takes a lovely set." I'll leave the rest of that episode alone, since it wandered into territory this public page doesn't need, but the Lotus portion is the good bit.

## Builds and Project Cars

Hagerty's Barn Find Hunter went "Minneapolis To Duluth, Minnesota" along Highway 61, which is the old main road before the interstate, and he points out it's the Bob Dylan road, as in the album *Highway 61 Revisited*. In Pine City he stops at a business called the Pit Stop, where the owner does repairs by day and builds at night. Inside the shop is a Model A with a boxed frame and a Tremec five-speed, destined for "street strip" duty and planned as a high boy with the back in the air. Elsewhere on the road he finds a 36 Ford coupe sitting on a Blazer chassis. I love this format because the best cars are never at the auction house. They're in a guy's shop off a secondary road, and he'll sell them if you pull over. The host even tells viewers to do just that if they're cruising through. Also, there's a dog, and the dog is "a good old dog, boy." That's the review.

Steve Magnante's "SWAP MEET GOLD!" is actually a roadside crawl in New Hampshire, where he pokes at a rusty work Jeep truck. He covers the frame, the leaf springs front and rear, and the big Dana or Spicer rear axle, which he's not sure whether to call a 53 or a 60. It's a semi-floating, two-piece axle, so getting the drum off means undoing a nut and cotter pin and beating it with a hammer. "If you have to surface the brakes, good luck." He also points out a stress crack where a dent tore the fender, calling it "a sad fate for these things," because life is not easy on the work trail. I want to be clear that I enjoy being lectured about axle shafts by a man who clearly means every word.

On the other end of the effort scale, Sarah -n- Tuned's "This Was A MISERABLE Job // 1987 Ford Ranger" covers steering. The Ranger now has a rebuilt and internally upgraded steering box to cure the "sloppy, numb" feel of old trucks, new I-beam drop brackets with camber correction, and Loctite on the column bolts. She was glad the previous owner had already replaced all the ball joints, since "someone did care." Next up is the four-post lift to tighten the front suspension, and then an alignment, because of course.

Then there's Auto Auction Rebuilds, "I Spent $2,000 Fixing My Sister's Car… Then THIS Happened." The tale ends at a stuck thermostat on a 2014 Chevy Sonic. The host can't give it back with a check engine light on, even though the car "runs great" and the light "means absolutely nothing." That's a very specific American neurosis, and I salute it. He also has a mango orange Scat Pack Charger arriving, bought "for stupid cheap," and he's convinced something is seriously wrong with it. He notes that parts for Scat Packs are "pretty damn expensive." Buying a cheap Scat Pack and then discovering the parts prices is the car version of adopting a free puppy.

## Racing and the Drag Strip

Clay Millican's "OUR LAST RACE OF 2026... BIG CHANGES COMING!!!" is mostly an off-season note, and the specifics are thin. The team is running one car, there's a sponsor push for Rick and Parks Plus, and the plan is to "give them the best chance in the world to win a championship." The best quote in the source is about money: "an intake valve is $200. $200 for one intake valve." That's before you add that a part that breaks at 35 runs has to be thrown away at 30. Nothing says "high-end motorsport" like a rule that says retire it early so it doesn't blow up on you. He also warns fans about fake graphics and junk posts: if it doesn't come from him, Rob, or Jeff on his channel, "you probably shouldn't believe it." Words to live by.

DragDriveRepeat's "28 Events and Counting: The 2027 Drag & Drive Schedule" is the planning video for next year, with an interactive map and a list of events, and sponsors including AFCO, Performer transmissions, and Low-Dollar Motorsports. One number jumped out. The host says a 4L60 transmission has gone 7s and also carried a 5,100-pound race weight "almost into the 9s." He's right to call that crazy, because the 4L60 is the transmission we all assumed was a cheap placeholder. There's also a news segment on Car Change 3D, where Corey Nicholson is "making cup holders for everything." A man crushing the cup holder market is the most American small business there is, and I'm not joking.

On dirt, The Tom Baker Show, Episode 117 on POWERtube TV, has a fiery segment about a quote that the hosts consider wildly misdirected. Their point is that Matt Shepard would have won the race if he hadn't run out of fuel. They note he took the track title at Outlaw Speedway this year and recently passed Steve Payne in all-time wins at Canandaigua. One guest, Gary Tompkins, said Shepard "had a down year for Matt Shepard," and "a lot of drivers would take a down Matt Shepard year." I love that sentence. It's the sports version of "my worst day is your best day." They also tease more World Final coverage over the next week to ten days.

Cleetus McFarland's "This EVO Blew My Mind in Spectator Drags!" is partly an Evo and partly a turbo giveaway. One caller is building an '04 Mach 1 Mustang with an LS and a turbo. Another gets "a 76" turbo, another "a 66." The whole segment contains a long digression about whether a person is a "Winnie," so I'll just say it was a very pleasant day for a turbo giveaway, and "do it for Dale" is a good sign-off.

## Giveaways and Hype

Adam LZ's "Minami Drift Jump Went VERY Wrong..." is another win for the giveaway economy. He finally announced the winner of the 700 horsepower Evo and $20,000 cash. The winner, Bobby, entered to see "if it was actually for real," figuring it might be a scam, and decided to take the wild risk. Now he's getting flown out to the LZ compound to drift the beast before they ship it to him. For a 700-horsepower Evo, "why not take the wild risk" is a terrible philosophy and a great outcome. Hitchhiker's Guide readers will recognize that this is the "Mostly harmless" principle of risk assessment.

## Car Audio

Five Star Car Stereo's "why did my installer use T Taps?" turned out to be the wrap-up of a long-running Q&A show. The hosts thank Audio Control, which they call their longest-running supporter, along with Focal, Mosconi, Gladen, and Arc Audio. They mention a Jeep coming into the bay, and tomorrow at 6 p.m. they have a guest talking about the new Mosconi Pro Evo amps. As for the title question, the part I have doesn't answer why the installer used T-taps, so I won't guess. I'll just say that whenever an installer reaches for a T-tap, a small part of me, the one that has fought many tangled wiring harnesses, flinches. There's also a side rant on sales guys who "don't understand how to sell," which I fully endorse as a universal truth.

They're a $200 valve, a 5,100-pound car running 9s on a 4L60, and a 20-mile-a-gallon Jeep that costs as much as a house in some states. Over 9000 is a power level, and in this issue it's also what the Maserati guy's driveway is going to cost him.
