## The Big Story

Chevrolet just handed the Silverado two brand-new V8s, and for once the press release isn't lying about the horses. MotorWeek got first-drive time in southern Arizona with the 2027 Silverado 1500, the fifth generation, and the headline is a 402-horsepower V8 with 428 pound-feet of torque and a best-in-class V8 towing rating of 13,700 pounds. Its big brother makes 481 horsepower and 501 pound-feet, which MotorWeek calls the most powerful V8 in the segment. Chevy basically looked at the truck wars, said "fuck it, more," and delivered.

The turbocharged base engine also got a 40-horsepower bump, up to 350 horsepower and 455 pound-feet. The Duramax diesel carries over with minor refinements and stays the only diesel in the light-duty class, so the oil-burner crowd can stop panicking and go back to smelling like victory.

Chevy trimmed the lineup and added features for roughly $500 over last year. Work trucks start just over $41,000, the standard Silverado (the old LT) just over $50,000, the Trail Boss lands around $64,000, and the High Country and ZR2 both clear $70,000. That's the sound of a $70,000 pickup, and somehow it's still the cheapest part of owning one.

Why it matters: while everyone else is busy electrifying themselves into a corner, GM is doubling down on big naturally aspirated V8s in the truck that pays its bills. Production starts soon. Full credit to [MotorWeek](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FU5x9S-lUYk) for getting there first and towing a heck of a lot of stuff to prove it.

## New Metal and Reveals

### The 2027 Silverado Gets Two New V8s

The 5.7 liter makes 402 horsepower and 428 pound-feet and is rated at 13,700 pounds of towing, which MotorWeek calls best-in-class for a V8. Big Brother is a new 6.6 liter with 481 horsepower and 501 pound-feet, billed as the most powerful V8 in the segment. The base 2.7 liter Turbo Max picks up 40 horsepower and 15 pound-feet for 350 and 455. The 3.0 liter Duramax carries over with minor refinements and stays the only diesel in the light-duty class.

I respect any manufacturer that raises prices by five hundred bucks and calls it a gift. It's the "free" coffee at a dealership: you paid for it in the doc fee.

### Kia's EV3 Asks You for $45,000

Edmunds tested Kia's all-new small EV, the EV3, and the verdict is "definitely worth the wait." They loved the interior packaging, the pockets and bins everywhere, and the pair of USB-C ports on the back seat, which are reachable, a concept some automakers still treat as a rumor. Rear legroom and headroom are fine for adults, and forward-facing car seats fit easily. Rear-facing ones mean sliding the front seat up, and the lower latch anchors are tucked into the cushions.

Is it worth $45,000, "which is a lot for a small SUV," especially when the Chevy Bolt they tested was $35,000? Ten grand of price gap buys you a lot of tucked-away storage bins. I admire the packaging. I just don't think "a really clever glove box" is a $45,000 personality.

## SEMA Leftovers

Chip Foose launched a chassis company at the show. It's called Foose Performance, and he brought two Corvette chassis. By his telling it took nine months of designing in the break room until two or four in the morning with Mike Curtis, plus a team assembling and redesigning as they went. His line: "this is just the podium for you to build your car." That's a lovely sentiment, and I note that he's selling the podium, which is a better business model than building the car and eating the labor. Foose says other products are planned.

Lucky's Garage Show posted a road-trip recap with behind-the-scenes access, including a walk around the hall before anyone else was in it. He explained SEMA as "an aftermarket convention," which is accurate and also the least romantic possible description of a place where grown adults cry over lighting. The best bit was the road trip itself: during highway pulls for a Hot Rod Magazine feature, he heard a scrape and hoped it wasn't the oil pan. It was the condenser, sheared clean off the bottom. He needed a new condenser and dryer by the next day. That's why you don't lower a car and go on a road trip, and also why you do it anyway.

## Builds and Project Cars

### Forgotten Big Blocks and Trucks That Won't Die

Vice Grip Garage dragged a forgotten big-block K5 Blazer out of retirement and got it to run, drive, and "now stop," which, in his words, is "not pretty and far from perfect." It's on 33s with an open rear end, a roughly four-inch lift, and Komodo-print tires, and the stock suspension articulates decently. The question he's putting to the comments is whether to give it a cheap lift, a steering stabilizer, and some exhaust and leak fixes, or send it down the road. He's partial to K5s, and he notes they're getting as hard to find as old Broncos. My vote: fix it. A rusty big-block K5 that runs is a Romero zombie. They're coming to get you, Barbra, and they're wearing Komodo print.

After a rough stretch, he's got a mini truck running and driving again with a new header. He's going back to a 10W-30 he's been running in nearly everything for a couple of years, from a 55-gallon drum that's almost empty, and he's also aware of "that little lifter nugget" floating around in the oil pan. A metal fragment bobbing around in your oil like a sad little submarine, and his plan is to fire it up and see. He's undecided about whether to park the truck until spring or sell it and get back to the Mercury and the K5 Blazer. Sell it, man. The truck is a time suck, and you named the lifter nugget, which is how I know it's over.

Killer Kustoms did an OBS Chevy grille swap where the headlights got wedged in with wood, which is peak backyard engineering. His rule on the brittle plastic is "make it snug, but not over-tight." Solid advice, and also what I tell Jordan about the number of services he keeps adding.

### The Armada Gets Scalped

POWERNATION is cutting the roof off an $80,000 2025 Nissan Armada for Project Jungle Patrol, a safari-style adventure vehicle. They tack tubing in place to hold the shape before finishing the pillars, with a cage design coming next. The video states the obvious: cutting into a vehicle is "nerve-racking" unless you plan. Dracarys, which is Valyrian for "dragonfire" and the word you say when you set fire to something expensive, though in this case the fire is a cutoff wheel. They cut high on purpose so there's room to dial things in later, which is the smartest decision in a video about hacking the top off an eighty-grand SUV. This old girl, as the crew said about the saw, was "not wanting to cooperate anymore." Same, honestly.

### Fixes, Tricks, and a Ferrari's Homework

Adam LZ's Ebisu Circuit YouTuber Takeover is a pile of drift guys shredding, plus the usual side quest: one of the cars has front steering bind. The diagnosis is probably the power steering pump, with the band-aid tricks coming from Adam, plus a suggestion to check whether the rack is bent.

Dave's Auto Center is working on a Duramax that two shops tried to fix and only made worse. The oil cooler wasn't replaced, so bearings get checked, and rocker covers will need clearancing in part two. The best detail: the bolts have a paint dot on them so nobody digs them out. Shop number one wasn't hiding anything, it was just leaving a trail of crumbs for the next guy. We touched it, now we own it, and it's a fantastic reminder that "second shop" is a job title, not a warranty.

StanceWorks wondered what an F40 would be like with F1-style suspension and a 9,000 RPM V12. The answer, for the suspension part, is that he's using bell cranks and keeping it in a single plane and basically, in his words, "trying to copy Ferrari's homework." He's reusing the geometry from the 458, which he says stayed essentially the same through the 296. Ferrari spent millions on that homework.

Stacey David closed out his Power Wagon episode with the viewer-project giveaway. The winner, Joseph, gets a big Woodward Fab bench vise, a fender cover, a sticker pack, a deluxe project planning book "so you remember it for the next 38 years," a Trucking Across America shirt, and a Sergeant Rock die cast. The vise is the best prize and the one most gearheads neglect to buy, according to Stacey. We all own seven impact guns and a vise made from a coat hanger.

## Racing and the Drag Strip

### World Cup Finals and Wet Pavement

The 1320video crew did a recap of the World Cup Finals on their Talkin' Shift episode 30 (the title promises world records, crashes, and chaos). They only got about two and a quarter rounds of qualifying, so by the time it felt like World Cup it was already Sunday. Their favorite part was a photographer getting a shot from somewhere only barely safe and the reaction that it was cool, with the obligatory debate about whether it's cool when your mom says it. The photos are for sale on their site and through SmugMug, and they point out you don't have to own the car to hang the picture.

Late Model Performance spent much of the day sitting in the pits because it was raining, which is racing's way of telling you to go think about your life. There's hype about an OBS truck making 3,200 horsepower, described as the fastest LS truck out there right now, and the video title brags about a 1,400-wheel-horsepower sixth-gen Camaro ZL1. During all that, one car's second pass hit the limiter in the 3-4 shift, so the tune needs adjusting. Moltke said no plan survives first contact with the enemy, and in drag racing the enemy is a rev limiter and a cloud. (That's Helmuth von Moltke, paraphrased from "No plan of operations extends with any certainty beyond the first contact with the main hostile force.")

### The Godzilla Mustang Can't Hook

Hemmings has a 1965 Ford Mustang Fastback with a 7.3 Godzilla swap, and he says up front that he wanted to get into the 14s. The problem is no posi. On radial T/As it won't hook, so he adjusted the tire pressures, discovered they were underinflated, brought them up to 25 and 28 psi, and it made no real difference. Worse, in second gear it just blows the tire off, and he's not even shifting aggressively. There's also a quick-release battery brace and a rival with a 5.3 turbo LS coming next year. "Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall," says Proverbs 16:18, which is a decent description of announcing 14-second goals on a car with one-wheel-peel energy. Buy a posi, Todd.

DragDriveRepeat previewed its Drag & Drive Awards at PRI on December 11. They're thanking sponsors like AFCO and Low Dollar Motorsports, mentioning Howard's Cams in a Cadillac, and getting excited about 8HP transmissions, with the suggestion that if a 4L60 can go into the 7s, an 8HP might go way further. They'll also happily put 300 people under one ticket group.

## Auctions and the Market

### Young Collectors Are Fine, Apparently

Hemmings' Between Two Pumps podcast responded to listener comments about what young collectors want. The hosts note that younger enthusiasts are into vehicles and into keeping them alive, and that they're educating older collectors on technology, including AI interfaces. Listener examples included a 37-year-old with a '68 Charger and a 45-year-old with a '91 Honda CRX. That's reassuring, since the hobby panic about "kids don't care about cars" keeps getting disproven by guys in their forties with a CRX.

Bonus for shoppers: for Cyber Monday, Hemmings is picking over 100 Make-Offer listings and dropping the buyer's fee from 5% to 3%, which the host says is "essentially a 40% off deal." That math is technically correct and tells you how much of the fee they were taking.

Audrain Museum Network drove three versions of the Model T and called it a car that "defies the conventional rules of collecting": not rare, not a technological leap, yet it makes people smile. When a manufacturer lowers the price of a car, it has a "very dramatic and detrimental effect" on resale values, which is the best argument for why dealers will never cut a sticker price.

### The Hooptie Lot Learns About Humans

The Hooptie Lot found out that cheap Toyotas are way harder to sell than they thought: endless lowballs and picky buyers. They admit that if they'd known, they'd have dumped it at auction for a small loss. The silver lining is it doesn't have an internal engine problem like the Buick Regal, so they're expecting to lose maybe a thousand, or something in the hundreds. Rule of Acquisition #106, "There is no honor in poverty," is Ferengi for "stop being proud about losing money." The Hooptie Lot has taken the lesson and wrapped it in a Prius.

### Scammers, Thieves, and the State of Oklahoma

Scammers sent a guy from Iowa to his lot for a car he doesn't own, and now he's meeting with the state of Oklahoma. He advises viewers not to engage, not to play games, and not to get on the scammers' level. Everything he says is correct, and I hope Oklahoma is merciful. I'd make a joke, but this one isn't funny. 😏 The real advice: if the deal is a great price on a car you can't verify, verify the car.

## Parts, Tuning, and Tools

O'Reilly Auto Parts explained P0011 and P0014, the camshaft position timing over-advanced codes. The checklist is sensible: check oil level and condition, inspect VVT solenoid wiring and connectors, pull and clean solenoid screens if accessible, replace sticking ones, verify oil pressure, and look for worn timing guides, a slack chain, or a failing tensioner, plus cam and crank sensor signals. O'Reilly says not to drive a car with these codes until it's diagnosed. For once the sponsor advice and the actual advice are the same. Everything, always, is the oil.

## Motorcycles

They want to buy a LiveWire electric motorcycle, put it on a rental platform like Riders Share, and pile on as many miles as possible, since the bike carries a two-year unlimited-mile warranty and a five-year unlimited-mile battery warranty. Then they'll see how the company deals with the claims and eventually hack the bike after the warranty runs out. "LiveWire is going to love that," they note. I do too, because it's the only fleet testing strategy that involves strangers.

Petrolicious, in a piece on Boccarossa's Renault Clio V6s, drifted into something heavier. The owner recounted a crash on a Honda RC30, Wayne Gardner's Suzuka eight-hour bike, at 35 or 40 miles an hour when a car turned left in front of him. He hit a telephone pole, broke his shoulder, and went out by medevac, and he says he "came back to life." His conclusion, delivered like a man who earned it: "If you're well enough to own the damn thing, you should be driving it." I'm not going to joke on top of that. Go drive your cars, Little Mister.

## The Industry

The 2027 Corvette's new naturally aspirated 6.7 liter LS6 won TopGear.com's U.S. Engine of the Year at 535 horsepower, part of three U.S. awards for the 2027 lineup. Meanwhile, the Corvette ZR1X made Ward's 10 Best Engines and Propulsion Systems for 2026, with a 5.5 liter twin-turbo LT7 making 1,064 horsepower plus a 186-horsepower front electric motor for 1,250 combined. The channel also says the upcoming C9 will most likely offer a manual option in at least some variation.

The detail I liked best: all ten winners on Ward's list had electrified powertrains, the first time in the award's 32-year history, and the ZR1X is one of them. The loud, naturally aspirated V8 gets the one award and the hybrid with an electric motor on the front axle gets the other. Both are Corvettes, and GM gets to claim the whole room. The host's closing advice to GM was "keep doing that. Don't look at anything else." The spice must flow, and in this case the spice is displacement.

Nothing broke, so I'm bored, which is its own kind of heroism. Go drive the thing. Tighten the belt.
