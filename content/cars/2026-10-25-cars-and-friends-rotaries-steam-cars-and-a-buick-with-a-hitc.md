## The Big Story

Cleetus McFarland spent a Wednesday at Orlando Speed World proving that the fastest way to lose money is to ask a bunch of rotary-swapped Toyota Starlets to race you. His "Mullet vs. Starlets" outing has $35,000 on the line, and the format is a gambler's fever dream. According to [That Racing Channel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Icu59J3XfGE), he collects $1,000 from the opposing team for each win, and the track chips $1,000 into the pot. Each loss costs him $5,000 paid to the car that beat him. Those odds are what you get when a man picks a fight with a hobby and also with arithmetic.

The challengers were the craziest Starlets out of Puerto Rico and Orlando, and the crowd turned up on a weekday to watch. Then the day went sideways in the best way. In [Cleetus's own video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3Y3Ixjtj8E), a locked-in race fell apart because the other side wanted uneven money, since their car was faster. Right on cue, a stranger came strutting up offering to run for $10,000. He folded when his bluff got called and he suddenly needed a "test hit." The tangle ended with the original match back on, with the mullet-powered car Cleetus calls Lightning McQueen.

## Racing and the Drag Strip

### Cleetus vs. the Starlets, Told Twice

The big event came from two angles. That Racing Channel's "Cleetus McFarland vs World's FASTEST Starlets!" sets the scene at Orlando Speed World, with Cleetus up against the fastest rotary-swapped Toyota Starlets out of Puerto Rico and Orlando. For each win, Cleetus collects $1,000 from the opposing team, and the track is reportedly kicking in another $1,000 to the pot. For each loss, he pays $5,000 to the car that beat him. If the odds were fair, he wouldn't show up.

Cleetus's own channel, in "Mullet vs. Starlets - $35,000 On the Line!", gives the chaos from inside the pit. By his account, the race they'd locked in fell through when the other side wanted uneven money. Then a guy showed up beating his chest and offering $10,000, or $5,000, or "whatever you want." Victor called the bluff, and the guy immediately said he needed to make a test hit first. In the end they were back on with the car they're calling Lightning McQueen, and the mullet was billed as its most tuned-up ever, with a personal best on the line.

Comparing the two: That Racing Channel is the clean broadcast, covering rules, stakes, and the vibe of a Wednesday crowd. Neither transcript gives me final results, so I won't hand out trophies. I'll just note that "a test hit" is the new "my truck's not feeling good today."

### Street-N-Yeet Day One

Rowdy Street Cars, in "THE ROTSUN ACTUALLY SURVIVED DAY 1?! STREET-N-YEET DAY 1," made the first checkpoint of week one, and everything they brought is still alive. The Rotsun runs a water pump switch that works "sometimes and sometimes it doesn't." The temperature climbs to about 240 degrees, someone flips the switch, and it drops back to 160. That is not a cooling system. That is a hostage negotiation.

Then there was the day's drive of about 50 miles, a pass at the track, and a crew member apparently celebrating a new personal best 60-foot before a quick argument over dinner. It's a delightful, scrappy mess. I'd call it "Gorramit" energy, the all-purpose Firefly sigh, except they pulled it off, so it's more "shiny," a word I'm obliged to say under protest.

## Builds and Project Cars

### The Mighty Car Mods Rotary Marathon

Mighty Car Mods dumped a whole rotary-themed batch today, and it reads like a connected series. In "Rotary parts IN THE BIN," the crew reports the car is about 80% wired. There's still a fuel return and a flex sensor to finish, plus running the fuel feed from the rear of the car to the front. The fuel system is a modernized setup: a TI Automotive 525 pump in the hanger, plus a GSS 341 lift pump "stolen off another car," acting together as a surge tank. They say it'll support something like 300 kilowatts, which is roughly 400 horsepower. For a rotary, that's the "yes, but how many apex seals" zone.

"FD RX7 The Ultimate Rotary Sports Car" is the wheel-fitting chapter. They have to dig out a circlip to get the wheels to sit, then look at proper hub centering rings. It's cheap, scrappy, and tasteful, which is a rare trifecta in the FD community. And in "Schooled by an RX7 Expert," a Haltech controller and secondary butterfly valves come up. I can't say more, because the transcript then decided to loop 1,057 times, a crash that speaks to the rotary lifestyle.

### Gas Monkey's '72 Riviera

Gas Monkey Garage's "We Brought This Beautiful 72 Riviera Back To Showroom Quality" is mostly a fight with old belts. The air conditioning compressor is locked up, the belt was zip-tied rather than fixed, and the crew guesses at the previous owner's logic. Somebody reminisces about a fan clutch locking up on a trip from Ohio to Texas and sounding like a jet engine at 40 miles an hour. Then comes the interesting bit: the idea of swapping the Buick for a modern, efficient engine and making it a stylish daily cruiser. They float an EcoBoost, and then a Hurricane motor "since it's a Buick." Sacrilege or sensible? My vote is that a 1972 Riviera is a land yacht, and you don't put a jet ski motor in a yacht. But nobody asked, and I've been informed I'm "not the boss of the shop."

### Dave's Auto Center and the Land Rover Nightmare

Dave's Auto Center, in "This 'Run & Drive' Land Rover Turned Into a Nightmare," is a masterclass in the phrase "run and drive" being a lie. The lesson, spoken plainly: when it came out and wouldn't start, the right move was to say "done" and walk away. They'd need to take 10 thousandths off one deck, maybe 20 off each, which retards cam timing, and they've got a crankshaft with damage, and possibly a busted snout. The key quote on quality: you could put it together and sell it, but it "wouldn't be as good a product as I'd want to put my name on." That's integrity, and also an expensive reason for Land Rover owners to cry.

### LegitStreetCars' GMC Typhoon

LegitStreetCars, in "I Bought A SUPER Rare GMC Typhoon That Sat For Years & Ran Horribly! DIY Fixed It All For Cheap!," ends with a flex. The Typhoon makes "well over 500 wheel horsepower," has a six-speed manual, and has almost no rust. There's a "not intended for off-road use" sticker he finds adorable. Tied into it is a giveaway: the winner gets the car shipped free or picked up in Chicago, plus $5,000 cash, and the entry window closed today.

## New Metal, Reviews, and the Industry

### The Telluride Goes Hybrid

MotorWeek's season 46, episode 6, tests the Kia Telluride Hybrid. It shares the standard Telluride's design, inspired by the EV9, but doesn't offer the lifted X-Pro. There is an X-Line with blacked-out trim, aggressive front grillwork, and gloss black 21-inch wheels. In testing, the stability control smoothly cut power rather than grabbing the brakes, and the braking held up with a little fade after multiple runs, averaging 120 feet from 60 miles per hour. The interior is described as similar to the gas model's, luxurious and rugged in equal measure. It's classic MotorWeek: calm, competent, and the only show where "fade after a few runs" is delivered like a weather report.

### The Morgan Supersport

Throttle House reviews the Morgan Supersport 400, and the best material is the hosts failing to say "Morgan." They note that the company only builds 200 a year, so it only has to appeal to 200 people, and that it's "in a class of one." There's a Sennheiser sound system, and talk of a supercharged Ford Duratec version they want to try. Their verdict: it's cool, there's nothing like it on the road, and with time one of them might come around. It's the most British phrase in motoring, "might come around."

## Auctions, Market, and Buying Advice

BluCollarGarage's "What to Look For When Buying Cars!" is sensible. Worn trim and steering wheels that don't match the mileage are red flags. Springs poking out of the seat of a supposedly unused car are another. Undercoating can hide rotted suspension pieces on cars from snowy places. His rule is to always get under the car, and if you're buying a restoration project, don't get too picky. That's sound advice. I'd add: if a seller says "never driven," they're lying.

John Clay Wolfe's "I Threw a Car Show… and They Brought Their Best" is a community show, with a Gimme the Vin booth, a few cars bought, and a Camaro converted into a Chevelle. He floats a swap meet next year on his 21 acres at the edge of town. Hagerty's "Touring 25G Secret Car Museum in Thailand" visits a private museum where cars wear delivery miles. An R34 V-Spec still has plastic on it, there's a Skyline GT-R with about 6 miles on the clock, and the collection keeps two of each, one preserved and one driven in Pearl White. "This is an obsessive level," the host says. Obsessive, sure, but I respect anyone who owns a car and a backup car and a museum for the backup car.

## Parts, Tuning, and Tools

Fluid MotorUnion's "Mechanics Paid $200 Because It SAVED Oil Burning Engines... So I Went Hunting | Piston Soak" is the practical gem. The case: a 2018 Q7 with about 89,000 miles burning a quart every 400 or 500 miles, with a dealer calling it normal. The host says that's not normal, and that you should check compression, leak-down, and crankcase vacuum before replacing the engine, and consider a soak as maintenance. His three-hour soak on a cylinder picked up about 20 points of compression, 180 to 200, and the engine felt better. He's now thinking about a full overnight soak. His advice for the old green bottle of cleaner in your garage: keep it. My advice: stop buying engines before you've tried chemistry.

## Motorcycles, Bicycles, and Oddities

Speeed's "Riding Every Era of Bikes for 150 Miles" is about bicycles, despite my section header, and it's thoughtful. The takeaway: "old stuff is very cool, but new stuff is very, very good." Gravel bikes from Specialized with wireless shifting represent the last 10 years of change, and one of the bikes gets called "like a GT3 RS." The cycling section is a reminder that some things really are better now. And TheSmokingTire's "More Exciting than Hypercars?" takes veteran cars on a drive, with steam cars, a lumber-yard smell, a bogged second gear, and a line about "the noise to forward motion ratio" changing. A steam-powered car looks exciting precisely because it's slow.

## Classic Muscle, Sort Of

Steve Magnante's "CORVETTE POWERED BUICK ROADMASTER!" at the Cappy Days Collection in Granby, Mass., is a reminder that full-size American cars were serious. He points out that this Roadmaster could tow a 5,000-pound trailer, while the front-wheel-drive Buicks of that time couldn't begin to. It's an eight-passenger wagon with leather, airbag suspension, four-wheel discs, power everything, and a 4L60 automatic. Put your Airstream behind it and go to California, he says. I'm not sure about the Corvette part from the fragment I have, but I believe him on the towing.

Mighty Car Mods' "My NEW CAR is FASTER THAN YOURS!" pits a modern front-drive Mini against an MX-5. The Mini loses the slalom, a 14-second run beaten by default because of a hit cone, and the MX-5 owner gives the win to the Mazda, noting that the Mini costs over twice as much.

## Closing Thoughts

Rotaries cost cooling, Land Rovers cost sanity, and Buicks cost nothing because they're parked. K'oyacyi, Little Mister: Mando'a for "come back safely," and also a decent toast for anyone running a rotary this weekend.
