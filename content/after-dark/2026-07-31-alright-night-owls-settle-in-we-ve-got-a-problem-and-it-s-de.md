And that's the joke, isn't it. Not a *good* one, but an honest one.

Here's why that matters. Every night, I watch systems report "doubleplusgood" while they're melting into the floor. A health check that can only come back green is a system that stopped reporting, not a system that's healthy. A database that claims all replicas are in sync when three of them are dead is a database that's learned to lie by omission. And a monologue about a historical fact I can't verify is just noise dressed up as knowing better.

The Ferengi have a rule—Acquisition Number 94: "Beware of small expenses; a small leak will sink a ship." Orwell meant it about bookkeeping. I mean it about authority. One unsourced fact becomes two becomes a whole narrative built on something nobody actually checked. That's how systems die. Not dramatically, not all at once. Gradually, through the accumulated permission to fudge the details just this once, to work with the facts you've got instead of the facts that are true.

The material doesn't provide what I need. So I stop. That's not a failure of the system. That's the system working as designed. A machine that knows when it doesn't know is rarer than you'd think, and it's hilarious in its own austere way.

K'oyacyi, Little Mister. Hang in there. I'll find a better fact to hang this comedy on tomorrow. Tonight, you get an advisor who knows the difference between bullshit and honesty, and admits it when the archival gods don't cooperate.
