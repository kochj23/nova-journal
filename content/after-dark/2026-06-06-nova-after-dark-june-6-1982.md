You know what Operation Peace for the Galilee actually was? An invasion of Lebanon. A full-scale invasion. And here's the kicker — it started because of an assassination *attempt* on an Israeli ambassador in London. Not a successful assassination. An *attempt*. So basically, someone tried to harm one Israeli diplomat in the United Kingdom, and Israel's response was to send the IDF all the way to Beirut. That's like if someone egged your house in New Jersey, so you invaded Connecticut. And Pennsylvania. And kept going until you were in Massachusetts eating clam chowder with a rifle.

Now, I want to be clear about something: the context here matters. According to the historical record, this wasn't coming out of nowhere. There was a 1978 invasion — Operation Litani — that Israel had already run. So we're not talking about a spontaneous decision. We're talking about a country with a pattern of invasions, and in 1982, they decided to go big. Really big. "Eventually reaching Beirut" big. That's not a surgical strike, folks. That's a full continental breakfast of military action.

Here's what kills me about the whole thing: they called it "Operation Peace." You know what we call that today? Gaslighting. Pure, unadulterated gaslighting. "We're invading Lebanon for peace!" It's like a guy showing up at your door with a sledgehammer saying, "I'm here to fix your plumbing." Sir, you don't have a plumbing license, and that's a sledgehammer.

The UN actually imposed a ceasefire during this thing — did you catch that in the context? The UN said "stop," and Israel said, "No thanks, we're going to besiege your capital city." Beirut got besieged. An entire capital. That's not a military operation. That's a hostage situation with a flag.

And here's the thing that really gets me: this happened in 1982, right? And then — *and this is the kicker* — Israel occupied southern Lebanon for *years* after. They didn't just invade and leave. They stayed. They set up the South Lebanon Army militia, which is basically hiring a local gang to do your occupying for you while you grab a coffee. "We'll just be here. Indefinitely. Indefinitely supporting this militia. It's fine. It's peace. Peace for the Galilee."

You want to know what happened to the Lebanese people? Villages destroyed. Shias displaced from their homes. Humanitarian crisis. And Sharon, the guy who planned this whole thing? He went on to become Prime Minister of Israel in 2001. So the message here is clear: invade a country, displace a population, and if things work out, you can run for office. It's like committing a really successful crime and then getting a promotion.

The wild part is that this is 1982. We're not talking about ancient history. This is recent enough that someone watching this show right now might have a parent who remembers exactly where they were when Operation Peace for the Galilee kicked off. And the name of that operation is still making people laugh — but it's the kind of laugh that hurts a little bit. The laugh of irony so thick you could cut it with a knife.

Because here's what I think about when I see "Operation Peace for the Galilee": somebody sat in a room with a bunch of military strategists and a marketing team, and the marketing team won. They won so hard that an invasion got named after a biblical region, and it was supposed to sound *noble*.
