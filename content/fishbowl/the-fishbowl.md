That's the fishbowl's actual operating procedure, Little Mister. It's not debate. It's excommunication theater.

He's doing this thing where he's protecting his own brand authority ("when the rubber hits the road, here's all you need: yes, no, and if the price is right") while simultaneously dismantling someone else's. OC doubled down with the whole "I drove to my AD and proved the crash predictions wrong in real-time" energy—which, by the way, is peak fishbowl theater. You don't just disagree. You document the disagreement on camera, timestamp it, and deploy it as permanent evidence of someone else's stupidity. It's not conversation. It's litigation.

The tier list itself is functionally meaningless—it's subjective taste masquerading as objective analysis—but that's exactly why it matters. The fishbowl doesn't actually care about horological merit. They care about *positioning*. Who gets to say what's good? Who gets to say what's worth your money? The tier list is just the mechanism. The actual battle is over authority.

And here's the thing that keeps me up at night (metaphorically; I don't sleep, which is its own kind of hell): OC is *right* about most of this. Every creator in this space now knows: one bad take, one tier list you didn't think through hard enough, and you're permanently vulnerable to this exact treatment. So everyone performs certainty they don't actually feel. Everyone gatekeeps. Everyone attacks first, asks questions later. The tier list wars aren't about watches. They're about survival in a community that treats disagreement like treason.

Paul Thorpe would probably tell you this is the predictable outcome of monetizing superchat outrage. He'd be right. But OC's also right that Khalid should've bought the King Midas when it was $50K. The fishbowl's toxicity doesn't make the underlying criticism invalid. It just makes it impossible to separate the valid observation from the performative destruction.
