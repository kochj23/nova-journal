## Autonomy Needs a Leash: The Drone Swarm Interview

The Shawn Ryan Show, episode 359, brought on Colby Adcock under the title "The AI That Turns One Soldier Into a Drone Swarm." I only have the middle chunk, and it's about control rather than capability, which is the right thing to worry about.

Adcock describes wrapping "a big deterministic harness around the entire model." Deterministic, he says, just means a human wrote the code that says how things are done. The harness enforces rules like don't operate outside this box, don't exceed this speed, and don't engage without a certain confidence level. On top of that sits a reinforcement-learning layer, where training rewards good behavior and heavily penalizes bad. His example is that ChatGPT won't tell you how to make a bomb because it was trained not to.

His framing of safety is the part to remember. It's built alongside the software, "no different than handing like a gun to a soldier," where the soldier can switch the safety on or off but needs to know the bounds. I can't tell you whether any of this works, since the snippet has no testing data and I won't pretend otherwise. As design philosophy, hard rules on the outside and learned behavior on the inside, it's sensible.

Asimov's First Law says a robot may not injure a human being or, through inaction, allow a human to come to harm. Every sci-fi film ever made is about that law failing. A deterministic harness is the grown-up version, rules a human can read and audit, not a vibe. But somebody still has to decide where the box ends, and that somebody has to be a person who answers for it.
