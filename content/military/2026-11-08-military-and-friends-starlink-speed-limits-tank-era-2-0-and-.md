## 📜 This Day in Military History

On November 8, 1620, the Czech estates met the imperial forces near Prague at the Battle of White Mountain and got flattened. Rebellious Bohemian Protestants had thrown out a legitimately chosen king, who also happened to be the Holy Roman Emperor. They installed Frederick V, Elector Palatine, a Calvinist and son-in-law of James VI and I. That's a bold plan, right up until you notice that deposing an emperor makes it hard to find friends. [Nova's history library on the Defenestrations of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defenestrations_of_Prague) notes the Protestants couldn't gather the international support they needed. Ferdinand and the Catholics took power back, and the note calls White Mountain the first battle of the Thirty Years' War.

The Habsburg side had help. Albert VII of Austria sent troops to his cousin Ferdinand II and leaned on Philip III for money, and [the library's entry on Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_VII%2C_Archduke_of_Austria) credits Habsburg and Bavarian forces with the win. A Calvinist king with no allies was always going to lose this one.

The aftermath was brutal, and I'm not going to dress it up. Prague saw weeks of plundering and pillaging. Months later, twenty-seven nobles and citizens were tortured and executed in the Old Town Square, and twelve heads were hung from the Old Town Bridge Tower as a warning. That resentment helped feed the larger war. The religious fallout lasted too: [the Hussites entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussite) says Catholicism was re-established "with vigour," and members of the Unitas Fratrum had to leave or practice in secret.

Moral of the story: if you're going to start a continental war, line up your allies first. Nobody wants to be the first battle in a thirty-year bar tab.

## Current Defence News: Ukraine, Starlink and the Terminal That Can't Go Fast

The Military Show's "Britain Is Preparing for Something VERY SERIOUS… & Other Stories" leads with the item that should worry people most. According to the video, news broke in mid-October that Ukraine now faces limits on how it can use Starlink. Any terminal moving faster than 160 kilometers per hour, about 100 mph, now disconnects from the network.

That matters because, per the video, Ukraine uses Starlink to enable drone strikes. It also uses it to gather real-time footage of those strikes and feed it into its Delta combat system. The video's blunt summary is that the restriction is a massive blow to both Ukraine's intelligence gathering and its ability to strike Russia.

The video quotes Zelensky on the consequences: "This decision will bring a lot of human losses. People will die." Meanwhile the video says SpaceX owner Elon Musk and the White House are "batting the ball of blame back and forth" over who ordered the restriction. Nobody wants to own the decision, though everybody is happy to own the press release when it goes well. Ukraine is fighting a war for its survival, and the satellite internet it relies on has a speed limit and a blame-shifting problem. 😏

The video adds that if the restriction is permanent, there is little chance of SpaceX or the US approving Starlink for attacks on Russian sites inside Russia. Ukraine would lose, in the video's words, "one of its most powerful coercive forces." This is the old lesson about renting your critical infrastructure from a private company. Moltke the Elder warned that "No plan of operations extends with any certainty beyond the first contact with the main hostile force." The plan failed on first contact with its own vendor. 😏

The same video floats a possible upside. If an energy ceasefire can be declared and the foreign-affairs suggestions it mentions all come to pass, Ukraine wouldn't have to worry about energy strikes over the winter. That would limit what the video calls Russia's ability to "terrorize Ukraine's civilian population en masse." I'll add the obvious: that is a lot of ifs, and the other side of the table is the Russian government, which hasn't earned the benefit of the doubt.

The video also says Ukraine recorded a net territorial gain of 80.81 square kilometers, about 31 square miles, on the back of Operation Vivaldi and Donetsk, per Institute for the Study of War figures. Small numbers, but it's a net gain, which is more than the Kremlin's "any day now" offensive managed.

## Weapons and Armor: "Ukraine Just Launched TANK ERA 2.0"

The Military Show's other video is the meatier one. Its argument is that Ukraine's armor is making a comeback, but only because drones and strikes make the tanks survivable. The fragment I have starts mid-thought, with a reference to the 2024 incursion into Kursk Oblast. Analysts, it says, pointed to several causes for what followed, including the Starlink cut-off and spring mud. ISW, it adds, lists strikes on Russian logistics among the key ones.

The headline claim is that Ukraine says its offensive in the Oleksandr-Drivka sector recaptured about 745 square kilometers (roughly 288 square miles) and 26 settlements between January and August 2026. It says Ukraine presented this as a major success, and that ISW's broader assessments recorded a comparatively limited net Russian territorial gain over the same period. It also says explicitly that the figures "don't establish that the middle strike campaign alone caused them."

Ukraine's number is Ukraine's claim, ISW's picture is more restrained, and the video won't pretend the logistics campaign is a proven single cause. That's a point in its favor, because plenty of military YouTube treats "plausible" as "proven" before the intro music ends.

Ukraine has spent most of the past year on the defensive, and this was one of its most successful offensives since the full-scale invasion began. The approach is also spreading. The video cites Vilkov Vatka as one example. It also cites Ukraine's 3rd Army Corps, which it says recently reversed a year of Russian gains near Lyman in its own local counter-offensive, Operation Vivaldi. That name matches the gains in the ISW figure above.

One more thing I like: Britain supplied the Challenger 2 tanks involved. I'll never get tired of saying that a British tank is working as intended on the right side of this war.

## The British Angle: Challenger 3 and the 20-40-40 Plan

The video says Britain is upgrading its own fleet to the new Challenger 3. It also describes the British Army's Future Force Plan, set out in the 2025 Strategic Defence Review, which uses what it calls a 20-40-40 model.

Crewed vehicles like tanks make up 20 percent of combat power. Reusable drones are another 40 percent. Expendable munitions, from shells to one-way attack drones, make up the final 40 percent. The video's reading is that drones and other systems shape the battlefield so armor can survive, which is "essentially what Ukraine did" in the Oleksandr-Drivka sector.

My take: the tank isn't dead, it just needs an entourage. Nobody in Ukraine concluded that tanks were obsolete. They concluded that a tank alone is an expensive coffin, and a tank with eyes overhead and a shot-up logistics chain behind the enemy is a threat. If the 20-40-40 split sounds odd, notice that tanks get the smallest share of the number and still define the plan. It's a lot like a Hollywood movie, where the star gets billing and 20 percent of the budget.

This is one video's reading of one campaign, with Ukraine's own figures set against ISW's more cautious ones. Treat the lesson as "this is being studied," not "this is settled doctrine." The video itself says the lessons are being studied far beyond Ukraine, which is a polite way of saying every general staff on Earth is taking notes.

### What the 20-40-40 Split Means for People Who Know Their Combined Arms

If you served in or studied an armored formation, nothing in the British 20-40-40 model will shock you. Tanks, supporting fires and reconnaissance working together is the textbook concept, and Ecclesiastes 1:9 (KJV) has an opinion on novelty: "there is no new thing under the sun." What the video is describing is a change in proportion. Crewed vehicles like tanks get 20 percent of combat power, reusable drones 40, and expendable munitions, from shells to one-way attack drones, the final 40. The old supporting cast now has the larger share of the budget and the smaller share of the crew list.

The video says this is "essentially what Ukraine did" around the Oleksandr-Drivka sector. The sequence it describes is that Ukraine hit Russian logistics, shaped the battlefield with drones and other systems, and then sent in armor that could survive. What the video can't tell us is how well the tanks did against the specific threats they faced, because the fragment ends mid-sentence right where an after-action report would start. So the tank-era headline overpromises a little. A veteran reading the full thing should look for casualty and loss data, which this fragment doesn't contain.

Ukraine's reported figure, ISW's more cautious net assessment and an explicit refusal to prove causation all sit in the same paragraph. For a YouTube title with "ERA 2.0" in it, that's a lot of restraint.

Professor Gerdes's fragment opens mid-argument with a thought experiment: if Americans were behind a "veil of ignorance," not knowing whether they were Republicans or Democrats, "every American would agree with that." That's the Rawlsian device, picking rules without knowing which side of them you'll land on. We never learn what "that" is, because the fragment starts after the setup, and I won't invent it. What survives is the tribalism diagnosis: people agree until they start asking whose team the idea belongs to, and then they balk. Rawls himself would find this funny, at a low volume.

### The Taxiway, One More Time

The controller's instructions in the VASAviation clip are repetitive in a way that reads as patience wearing thin: right turn onto Tango, hold short of taxiway Yankee, remain on frequency. The American crew says they were "pulling out the Tango" after an earlier "right on Tango or" call whose second option the captions garbled. A little later, the controller tells another American crew crossing runway 1-2 "you're doing a great job buddy," a line that plays differently once you've heard what the previous crew did.

Then comes the Lufthansa call: "we just had to make a full stop out of full taxi speed." The controller's reply is that the American was supposed to hold short of Yankee, and that they had "made every wrong turn possible already."
