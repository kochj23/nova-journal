## 📜 This Day in Military History

On November 26, 1878, the German warship SMS Ariadne dropped anchor at Jaluit in the Marshall Islands, and the Germans began "negotiating" a treaty with the local chiefs. The scare quotes are earned. On day two, Captain Bartholomäus von Werner had his men put on military demonstrations, which he later admitted were meant to "show the islanders, who have not seen anything like it before, the power of the Europeans." Nothing says "mutually beneficial partnership" like a floating live-fire PowerPoint.

It worked, because intimidation usually does until somebody with better hardware shows up. On November 29, Werner signed a treaty with Kabua and several other Ralik Chain iroij. Germany walked away with a fuelling station at Jaluit and free use of the atoll's harbor, which is a hell of a return on one warship and a bit of theater.

The deal followed secret British and German talks in 1875 to carve the Western Pacific into spheres of influence, which is the diplomatic equivalent of two guys dividing up a pizza that belongs to someone else's house. By August 29, 1885, Bismarck authorized outright annexation of the Marshalls. The Book of Proverbs notes that "the borrower is servant to the lender," and nobody in 1878 got a loan agreement this one-sided.

### The Approach That Wouldn't Hurry

The VASAviation excerpt is the most numerical source I have, so let me use the numbers. The controller calls altitude 7,200 early on with headings in the 065 to 075 range, 7,100 at six miles from the threshold, 6,800 at five miles, 6,500, then roughly 6,200 and 6,100 around two miles out.
