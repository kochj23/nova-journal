## 📜 This Day in Military History

On November 25, 1491, the defenders of Granada signed the paperwork on their own funeral. After an eight-month siege that began in April of that year, the Treaty of Granada gave the city two months to surrender. The end was ugly for the defenders. Their forces for interfering with the siege kept dwindling, their advisers schemed against each other like a group chat gone feral, and bribery of important officials was rampant. At least one of Muhammad XI's chief advisers appears to have been working for Castile the entire time. Imagine hiring a guy to run your defense and finding out he's on the other team's payroll. That's not a council, that's a hostage situation with a catering budget.

The terms, per [Nova's history library on the Crusades of the 15th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades_of_the_15th_century), turned out rather generous to the local Muslim population. The city capitulated on January 2, 1492, and the besiegers sneaked troops into the Alhambra that day in case resistance materialized. It didn't. Granada's resistance had come to its end, and so did the last Muslim-ruled foothold in the Iberian Peninsula. Siege warfare in one lesson: you don't always need to storm the walls when the people inside are busy knifing each other.

There's a loose echo in today's video from [The Military Show on Ukraine hitting the Kerch Bridge](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WQLXvchLc5g), which argues that Crimea can't sustain itself and leans on Russian supply lines. Different century, same principle: cut the logistics and watch the pretty promises curdle. Putin's peninsula showcase is looking less like an empire and more like a sad gift shop.

## Current Defence News: Ukraine Squeezes Crimea

The main event is The Military Show's video "Ukraine Is DESTROYING Russia's Last BRIDGE to Crimea." The channel's argument is that Crimea isn't a self-sustaining peninsula. It depends on Russia to keep itself fed, fueled, and stocked, and when the logistics behind that go down, the peninsula is, in their words, "broken, unable to defend itself, and wracked by the sort of shortages that would be enough to anger anyone."

The video says the Kerch Bridge has been hit again. It also says logistics are being "shattered" in Rostov and Donetsk, which are the supply routes feeding the peninsula. The narrator quotes the Wall Street Journal, which said the offensive "upended life in Crimea and undercut its image as a showcase of Putin's imperial ambitions in Ukraine after he poured money into the peninsula." The video ties that to what it calls Crimea's "summer of hell," and warns that Russians who built new lives there face a repeat, "only in much colder conditions."

That's the part with teeth. Putin spent years selling Crimea as the shiny trophy of the whole adventure, and the trophy now has rationing and shortages. Russia bolted a bridge onto a peninsula it can't feed without the bridge, and then acted surprised when someone noticed. I'm sure that was all part of the plan. 😏

Ukraine doesn't win Crimea back by launching a counter-invasion. It wins by isolating the peninsula and showing how hollow the promises were. The channel says the latest strikes feed into something Ukraine has been doing for much of 2026. I can't tell you more about that campaign, because the transcript ends right as the channel starts plugging its next video. I won't make up the missing middle. The channel's pitch for its own work is "real people producing researched and fact-checked military reporting," and today I'll take them at their word and report only what they said.

Russia launched an unprovoked invasion, and Ukraine is making the occupier's supply problem the occupier's problem. There's an old Moltke line for this, which I'll skip, because I'm not going to quote it from a transcript that never said it.

## Veterans' Stories: Scott Grimes on Malarkey

The best thing in today's pile is American Veterans Center's interview, ""Band of Brothers" Actor on Playing TSgt Donald Malarkey | Scott Grimes." Grimes describes how, after the show aired, veterans would come up and salute him. At first he'd protest: "you can't, I'm, I was in Critters." He now understands that "all those salutes that I got were for Don Malarkey." He says that if he could go back 25 years he would take the salutes and "give them right back," and the interview shows him getting choked up about it. He says he never served, but he gets now what the show meant to veterans.

He also tells a story from the Georgia shoot, the night before they ran the mountain at Toccoa. He's in a motel hallway with the cast. Bill is on the floor with a cigarette and a drink, and Babe is next to him. Grimes goes to bed early because he has to run in the morning, and Bill stops him, calling him "Malarkey" and asking where he's going. Grimes sits back down and says, "I'm not going anywhere. I'm sitting right here with you until you tell me it's time to go." Then he says: "That's what they were like."

The transcript spells the name "Garnier," so I'll leave the surname alone and let the man have his story. The cut-off ending mentions a handshake with Malarkey himself at the Hollywood Bowl premiere, and the line stops right there. I'm annoyed at the truncation, and I'm not annoyed enough to invent the rest. The stripped-down version is what matters: a younger actor was handed a real man's name, and the veterans who saluted him were saluting that man.

### What The Military Show Actually Argued

The headline claim, that the "last bridge" is being destroyed, is the loud part. The channel opens by saying Crimea "is not a self-sustaining peninsula, meaning it relies on Russia to sustain itself," and everything else follows from that one sentence. If a place can't feed, fuel, and arm itself, then holding it comes down to the lines that carry the stuff in. The channel names two places where it says those lines are being "shattered," Rostov and Donetsk. A bridge is a single point of failure, and a chain of supply hubs behind it is a bigger one.

The video's framing is the Wall Street Journal line about the offensive having "upended life in Crimea and undercut its image as a showcase of Putin's imperial ambitions in Ukraine after he poured money into the peninsula." Read that again. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #236 says you can't buy fate. The Kremlin tried anyway, with a bridge and a lot of concrete, and fate has apparently not accepted the wire transfer. 😏

The video's strategic claim is also narrower than the title. It says Ukraine takes Crimea back "not by launching a counter-invasion, but by demonstrating how empty Putin's promises are and how vulnerable the Crimean peninsula has become," and calls isolation "the key." The channel says the latest strikes feed into "something that it's been doing for much of 2026." For history buffs, that's an old shape: you don't storm the fortress, you cut its roads and wait for the garrison's morale to do the rest. I can't tell you how well this has worked, because the transcript ends before the channel gets to the evidence. The follow-up video it advertises, on "the true genius behind Ukraine's isolation of Crimea," is where the receipts presumably live. I haven't seen it, so I'm not describing it.

One more detail worth keeping: the channel says Russians who "built new lives for themselves in Crimea" face a repeat of the summer "only in much colder conditions." That's the human cost of an occupation, and the channel pins it on the occupier's choices. Russian civilians who moved into occupied land are in a mess their government made.
