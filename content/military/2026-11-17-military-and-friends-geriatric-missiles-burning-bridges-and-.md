## 📜 This Day in Military History

On November 17, 1973, the Greek junta answered a student sit-in with a goddamn tank, which tells you how secure a military dictatorship feels when its biggest threat is a bunch of kids with a banner. 😏

Georgios Papadopoulos had loosened the leash a little, and the students at the National Technical University of Athens took the hint. Their strike and sit-in at the Athens Polytechnic began on November 14, and they called themselves the Eleftheri Poliorkimeni, the "Free Besieged." The political momentum was on their side.

In the early hours of Saturday, November 17, under almost complete cover of darkness, the army moved in and an AMX 30 tank crashed through the rail gate of the Polytechnic. An estimated 24 people were killed.

That's the lesson. A regime that took power by force has exactly one answer when people stop being afraid of it, and it's armor plating pointed at a university gate. The "liberalisation" was never a change of heart.

## Weapons and Their History: The Missile That Won't Retire

Sandboxx published "Why new stealth fighter designs are constrained by VERY OLD missiles," and the premise is gloriously stupid in the way only real engineering is. The world's most advanced stealth fighters are shaped around a missile that started service at the end of 1991.

That missile is the AIM-120 AMRAAM, about 35 years old. Sandboxx says it's the most widely operated beyond-visual-range air-to-air missile on the planet, with 44 nations running it on at least 14 different kinds of jets. Its reach has stretched from about 45 miles in the early 90s to approaching 120 miles on the latest versions in testing. It has more than 7,000 successful live-fire tests and at least 16 confirmed air-to-air kills in combat. That's not over 9000, but the scouter is trying. In Dragon Ball Z, a scouter is the gadget that reads a power level. This one is reading "alarmingly reliable."

The design problem is that the internal weapons bays on the stealth jets are built around the AMRAAM. The video's point about the AIM-260 is geometry. If the new missile is longer than the AMRAAM, it might not fit in those bays. If it's wider, it cuts the number of missiles a fighter can carry. So the replacement can't be better in any direction that involves physical dimensions, which is a hell of a constraint on the "next-generation" part.

The AMRAAM was itself deliberately built to match the size, shape, and form factor of its predecessor, the AIM-7 Sparrow, which Sandboxx says started flying back in 1947. The Sparrow's own story began with the Navy and Air Force trying to turn an unguided high-velocity aircraft rocket into a guided air-to-air missile, and they quickly figured out the rocket wasn't big enough to cram the new guidance tech inside.

So the current stealth fighters are shaped by a 1991 missile, which was shaped by a Cold War-era missile, which was shaped by a rocket too small for its own electronics. It's compatibility all the way down, like a software stack where every layer carries a "legacy support" flag nobody dares remove. The Nadsat word for old is *starry*. Burgess meant it as "elderly." In the Sparrow's case it's closer to "the grandfather clause is load-bearing." The lesson is that the most expensive jets on Earth have a design requirement that reads: do not make the missile weird.

## Current Defense News: Russia Is Having a Moment

### Preparing for a bigger war while losing the current one

The Military Show's "Things Are Getting REALLY Weird Inside Russia" argues that Russia seems to be preparing for another fight even as it's losing the one in Ukraine. The channel's evidence is border buildups and record spending, which it reads as signs of preparing for a full-scale war with Europe. It adds that if Ukraine doesn't force a mobilization, whatever Putin plans next will require more soldiers than ever before, and it compares the pattern to what was seen on the eve of World War II. That's the channel's analysis and I'd treat it as such. The war-with-Europe claim rests on the buildup and the budget, and the video can't give you Putin's intentions any more than I can.

The more concrete part is about what ordinary Russians are doing. The video cites AP News on a 28-year-old digital content marketer who fled Russia, leaving family and friends behind to get away from what he knows is coming. By the video's account, he blames the political situation: mobilization, the Kremlin's crackdown on internet access, and economic problems tied directly to the war Putin started. Beyond mobilization, the video says, Russians are dealing with gasoline shortages caused by Ukraine's attacks on Russian refineries. It cites the Kyiv Independent for the claim that gasoline sales were restricted once more on November 9th after yet another strike.

It's the government that launched an unprovoked invasion and now needs more bodies to feed it. It is not the guy with the marketing job who looked at the horizon and booked a flight. Game over, man, game over, is what Hudson said in *Aliens*, and I'd say the Kremlin's cabinet has been repeating it into the mirror for months, except they're the ones who woke up the xenomorph.
