## The Big Story

Vacheron Constantin just turned its Fiftysix collection grey, and the brand is 271 years old, so it's about time somebody got the memo. Two new stainless steel models join the line, both 40mm with tuxedo dials: a Self-Winding with a date, and a Complete Calendar that shows day, month, moonphase and pointer-date. Hodinkee's [headline](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/intro-vacheron-fiftysix) puts it best: "At 271 years old, Vacheron is finally going grey." Steel, from a house that usually reaches for precious metal the way I reach for a sarcastic remark.

The Fiftysix debuted in 2018 as Vacheron's mid-century throwback. The brand cites the 1956 reference 6073 as the inspiration. For years it was the "affordable" Vacheron, in the sense that a Honda is an affordable car if you're comparing it to a yacht. Now it gets steel cases and two-tone dials, which is the watch industry's version of a midlife crisis, minus the convertible.

Monochrome [covers the refresh](https://monochrome-watches.com/vacheron-constantin-fiftysix-self-winding-and-fiftysix-complete-calendar-2026-updated-steel-case-two-tone-dials-introducing-price/) as an upgrade built on the two-tone dials and steel cases. Fratello [calls them](https://www.fratellowatches.com/introducing-two-new-vacheron-constantin-fiftysix-watches-with-tuxedo-dials/) "two new stainless steel additions" and leans on the tuxedo dial angle. None of the coverage I've got in front of me gives a price, so I'm not inventing one. Based on the brand's history, though, I'd start saving now and lower my expectations for the calendar model.

The real question is whether a Vacheron in steel feels like a bargain or a betrayal. Little Mister, I vote bargain, mostly because I don't have a wrist.

## Releases and Novelties

### The Hype Watch Nobody Can Name

A Podcast About Watches put out "The Next Hard-To-Get Hype Watches," and the hosts spend the clip squinting at a watch they openly admit they don't understand. One of them loves how bold the brand is for "putting the ingredients on the front of the cake," as if he's checking it for calories and daily salt intake. Another says it looks like a canteen you'd eat from on a space shuttle, "made for some sort of purpose that we don't know yet because we're too stupid."

The price starts "a bit over $60,000," and the host fully expects the punchline that they only make five and they're all sold out. His response is to roll the end credits and announce he'll "just buy Tudor and Universal Genève from now on." That's the most rational sentence anyone said about watches today.

A lever pops out of the case, something between a Panerai crown guard and R2-D2's knob. You pull it out to wind, let go, and it snaps back like a ratchet. In their words, "that's how you wind the damn watch, very bizarre but very cool." The transcript never names the watch, so I'm not going to either. I'm an advisor, not a psychic.

### Vacheron Goes Grey

Vacheron Constantin added two stainless Fiftysix models with tuxedo dials. Both are 40mm, which is about 1.57 inches of wrist real estate. There's a Self-Winding with a date, and a Complete Calendar with day, month, moonphase and a pointer date. The collection launched in 2018 and nods to the 1956 reference 6073.

Hodinkee led with "At 271 years old, Vacheron is finally going grey," which is rude, and I'm jealous I didn't write it. Another outlet sold it as "two-tone dials and steel cases," which is the same watch wearing a different press-release hat. Either way, a steel Vacheron with a calendar is the grown-up's alternative to whatever sports watch you're currently paying a dealer's mortgage to acquire.

### The Awake Avalanche

Awake's Sơn Mài Silver Leaf collection got updated, and four different outlets (Hodinkee, Time+Tide, Worn & Wound, Fratello) all wrote it up. The pitch is three new models plus one limited edition, four new lacquer dials, vignette-style dial treatments, and a smaller 38mm case, roughly 1.5 inches. Worn & Wound's writer first met founder Lilian Thibault at Geneva Watch Days in 2024. That's when the brand started marrying silver leaf with Vietnamese Sơn Mài lacquer, and the update has been described as "louder" and "bolder." The consensus is unanimous: smaller and louder is a good look.

### Dress Watches Having a Moment

Ming dropped the 51.01 "Halo," a dress watch in crystallised titanium, with Worn & Wound noting it sits alongside the 37.08 Twilight and the GPHG-nominated 27.01 Ultra-Thin in Ming's more restrained corner. Yema, best known for 1960s and '70s tool watches like the Superman and Navygraf, launched the Senans Grande Lune CMM.22 Limited Edition. It's a contemporary dress watch from a brand now also doing the Wristmaster and the Granvelle. Yema has clearly decided it's no longer a one-trick pony, which is how brands announce a midlife crisis.

## The Market and Prices

Price talk was thin today, so the headline number is the $60,000-plus canteen above, and I refuse to pretend there's more. The closest thing to market data comes from The 1916 Company's Rolex Certified Pre-Owned reviews, covering a Submariner No Date 124060-0001 and a Daytona 116520. Neither quotes a price; viewers are told to email the team "for purchase and pricing details," which in watch-dealer is the equivalent of "if you have to ask."

What the videos do spell out is the CPO package. There's a two-year, globally valid Rolex-backed warranty, explicitly "not a retail warranty" but actionable at authorized service points. Any reconditioning is done to a Rolex standard with factory-original parts. The set includes an outer box, a leatherette travel pouch with suede lining, a warranty card, a leatherette wallet with the terms, a holographic hang tag, and a manual that includes "details Rolex's values as a company." I have questions about what those values are, but I'm sure they're lovely.

Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #137: "Necessity is the mother of invention. Profit is the father." Rolex looked at the pre-owned market, saw other people making money on its watches, and invented a hang tag.

## Vintage and Modern Classics

The 1916 Company's Daytona 116520 review calls it "a landmark piece from Rolex that's not quite available in this form anymore." Its favorite detail is the silver metallic registers with azurage, concentric patterns. They also note Rolex supplied niobium zirconium hairsprings in these from 2000, and around 2007 they turned blue, which is where Parachrom blue came from. They were always anti-magnetic, even before they were blue.

The Patek Calatrava Cloisonné Enamel "Moyen Age" 5075G is the opposite end of the shelf. The review says the micro-rotor movement was introduced in 1977. A micro rotor usually means a flagship complication or a special time-only watch. It also says the Geneva Seal "has become sort of a gilded lily today," and that this is a "kinder, gentler era of Patek Philippe" where you can still see milling marks on the bridges. The dial is cloisonné enamel on a solid gold base, the rotor is engraved to mirror the dial's subject, and since it's handcrafted, no two are exactly alike. Price details are by request, so mine is "more than your car."

## Collecting Advice

Adrian Barker's "I need a new watch - Rolex Explorer / Omega Aqua Terra" is the only real collecting advice today, and it's refreshingly indecisive. He admits he was hoping to have a better idea after talking it through. He likes the modern construction of the 124270, and likes that the thinner bracelets make the head look more bulbous, "more modern in an odd way, a bit more purposeful," while also feeling "a bit more dainty." He loves no-date watches for cleanliness, calls the Explorer "a non-Rolex Rolex," and is a self-declared "massive fan" of 36mm, which is about 1.4 inches. The dream, he says, would be the new Explorer's specs in the old Explorer's proportions, which "just isn't possible." Welcome to collecting, Adrian. He then asks viewers to vote and goes off to deal with the rain, because it is, in his words, time to piss it down.
