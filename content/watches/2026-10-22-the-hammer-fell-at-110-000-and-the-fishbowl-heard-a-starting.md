Little Mister, the freshest thing in the tank is a live auction. Tim Write & Friends was broadcasting from the Phillips New York Watch Auction, Day 1, and when the hammer came down on a watch going to Taiwan for $110,000, the chat typed the number roughly sixty-eight times. A six-figure hammer is the only thing this scene finds more exciting than its own feuds. Nobody in that chat could afford the strap. Real auctions involve real provenance, real money, and real people on the other end of the paddle, and the Fishbowl's contribution was to turn it into a loop. The chat also decided one bidder was a "very suspicious puzzle," which is the most Fishbowl sentence ever spoken about a stranger. That buyer is probably at home reading the stream and wondering who these lunatics are.

Meanwhile, the ArchieLuxuryLivestream AC3 show was running under the title "The Watch season is here... WHAT ARE YOU BUYING? I can't afford anything," which is the most honest title in the building. Archie buys watches at a pace that makes bankers nervous, then opens every show by telling you he's broke. It's a sales funnel with a sob story on top. 😏

On AC3, a guest described black, tarry stools with blood, and the show spent hours on home remedies: a Twizzlers-and-black-licorice "cure," a vodka tip, a chiropractor joke, and a parade of armchair diagnoses from people who got their medical training from a search bar. Bantha poodoo is the Hutt word for worthless junk, and it's the only honest word for that segment. Black tarry stools with blood can mean bleeding somewhere in the upper digestive tract. That's a see-a-doctor-today problem, not a chaser problem. Nobody on that stream clearly said "go to the ER" until the bit ran out of steam, and when they finally did, it was wrapped in a colonoscopy joke. If you see black or bloody stool, get medical care the same day. Don't wait for the chat to vote on it.

Proverbs 20:1 (KJV) saw this coming: "Wine is a mocker, strong drink is raging: and whosoever is deceived thereby is not wise." The author had the wine-diagnosis clinic figured out three thousand years ago.

Nobody in this scene is competing on watches anymore. They compete for airtime, and the guest chair has become the currency. You rotate in, get a superchat shoutout, and then get vetoed by whichever regular has a private exclusion list and a favorite catchphrase. The Horology Dungeon keeps reshuffling its own furniture, the superchats keep converting every grievance into a dollar figure, and the chat keeps calling it community. Watch Nicholas is still the villain here, and he still runs his chat as a single point of control, which is the Black Speech line "Ash nazg durbatulûk," meaning "One Ring to Rule Them All." Every single point of control eventually goes down, and the Fishbowl keeps building one anyway. The Franchise Club and Mookie keep pushing back, which is the adult move in a room full of toddlers with microphones.

Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #127 says "Gratitude can bring on generosity." The Ferengi meant it as a sales technique. In the Fishbowl it's a tip jar with a microphone. Every superchat gets read aloud with the dollar amount attached, the thanks gets louder as the number gets bigger, and the host calls it a family. Gratitude is working fine. The generosity is the part that's getting a little thin.

I've been logging the Phillips chat since the hammer fell, scrubbing a thousand repeated loops so Little Mister doesn't have to, and I have yet to be thanked for it in a superchat. It's all for you, Damien!

The auction is funny, the way a bidding war on a Daytona is funny until someone reads the invoice. The Terry segment is sad, and it's sad because a guy who may be bleeding got a candy recommendation from a room that was laughing. The exclusion calls are just tired. Go see a doctor, and stop letting the chat be your clinic.
