## The Big Story

Zenith just handed its most beloved chronometer movement to an American, and the result is a rose gold watch that makes most guilloché look like it was done with a fork. The Zenith G.F.J. Calibre 135 Double Signed with J.N. Shapiro is the brand's second Double Signed collaboration. [Time+Tide](https://timeandtidewatches.com/zenith-g-f-j-calibre-135-double-signed-with-j-n-shapiro-introducing/) reports it pairs a California-made guilloché dial with American-style damaskeening on the Calibre 135, in an edition of just 25 pieces. Twenty-five. Start refreshing your browser and lowering your expectations now.

Why it matters: a Swiss heritage house letting an American independent put his hands on a legendary movement is not nothing. [Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/zenith-shapiro-collab) credits watchmaker Josh Shapiro with the guilloché and damaskeening that adorn the piece, and [Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/introducing-the-zenith-g-f-j-calibre-135-double-signed-with-j-n-shapiro/) goes deep on the rose engine work, where the artisan needs constant pressure and a steady hand, and even the slightest lapse can ruin hours of painstaking work. Imagine a day of that going sideways because you sneezed. I'd have thrown the machine out a window and gone home. [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/zenith-gfj-calibre-135-double-signed-with-jn-shapiro-guilloche-dial-by-american-independent-watchmaker-review-price/) frames the whole thing as American artistry meeting Swiss chronometry, which is a polite way of saying the Swiss finally let someone from the colonies touch the good stuff.

Four outlets covered it inside a day, which tells you everyone in the watch press knows a banger when they see one.

Friday the 13th, and the watch world celebrated by dropping enough limited editions to give a tantalum anvil a hernia. I read the whole pile so you didn't have to, Little Mister, and I'd like it noted that a dash cam review wandered into the queue like a drunk at the wrong wedding. The DDPAI Z100 Master is a lovely 4K camera with a telephoto lens for plate-reading. It is not a watch. Vortex Radar, you were told to stay in the car.

## The Big Story: America Gets Its Hands on a Zenith

The most-covered release of the day was the Zenith G.F.J. Calibre 135 Double Signed with J.N. Shapiro, which got treated in at least four places. Time+Tide gave the clearest summary: this is Zenith's second Double Signed collaboration, pairing a California-made guilloché dial with American-style damaskeening on the Calibre 135, in a rose gold edition of 25. One outlet's live pics called the watchmaker "Josh Shapiro" and framed it as guilloché and damaskeening adorning Zenith's collab series. Another outlet went poetic about the rose engine itself, which demands constant pressure and a steady hand, and where "even the slightest lapse can ruin hours of painstaking work." Translation: one sneeze and you've made a very expensive coaster.

I'll say it plainly: a Swiss chronometer legend wearing a California tan is the best marriage between two countries since someone put peanut butter on a Swiss chocolate bar. Twenty-five pieces in rose gold means you will not be buying one. You will be reading about one, which is the whole business model of the modern watch press.

Robotech called them the Zentraedi: the giant alien horde that doesn't arrive so much as overwhelm you. Four write-ups of one watch is a Zentraedi invasion, and I'm the only fleet defending the perimeter.

## Sundowner II and the Cyclops Funeral

Time+Tide opened global orders for the TAG Heuer x Time+Tide Aquaracer Professional 300 "Sundowner II," billed as the mechanical sequel to the first collab. The outlet that gets to review itself has gone full ouroboros, and I salute its commitment to a clean conflict of interest.

Adrian Barker, in "They fixed the TAG Heuer Aquaracer!", has the cleanest take of the day. His whole case is that the fix is removing the cyclops, that little date magnifier. He doesn't even demand the date go away, just the bubble. "I can't imagine anyone seeing that cyclops thinking yeah, I love that feature." Hard agree. Rule of Acquisition #116, in Ferengi gospel: "There's always a way out." Sometimes the way out of a bad design is simply a sharp object and the courage to admit it. Barker also notes the Aquaracer's lugs are about 0.83 inches wide, an awkward 21 mm, and happens to sell 22 mm nylon straps that fit anyway. Even the straps.

## Independents and the Seriously Expensive Toys

The 1916 Company ran a trio of reviews, all of them sold by a dealer who wants you to email for pricing, which is dealer for "if you have to ask, you'll ask anyway."

The Ulysse Nardin UR-Freak, made with Urwerk, is a 100-piece edition in media-blasted titanium, and the reviewer calls it one of the coolest things Ulysse Nardin has launched since the original Freak in 2001. The whole thing runs in grayscale, with a few signal-yellow editions, and uses silicon components plus clear sapphire, since "synthetic groupies wouldn't look right on this grayscale watch." (I assume he meant "rubies." If he meant groupies, I'd like that story.) The winding system is a hybrid of peripheral and rotor automatic, with four compliant pawls. Meanwhile, over at Worn & Wound, Ulysse Nardin also introduced the Freak S Purple Enamel.

The reviewer stresses this is real white gold, grey gold, white all the way through, no rhodium plate. Only about seven were made, though he notes to take that count with a grain of salt. Beat rate is 28,800 vibrations per hour, the movement is about 1.2 inches across with 49 jewels, and the balance has little platinum bulbs on it. Five patents protect the thing. "The original DB20 GMT is a very hard watch to find," he says, which is the dealer version of "I have the only one, hurry."

## Rolex Corner: The Roulette Date and a Very Large Book

The 1916 Company also reviewed a Rolex Certified Pre-Owned Datejust Concentric Dial / Roulette Date, reference 116234, selling the idea of a Rolex that's iconic without being the same watch everyone and their landlord is wearing. The CPO box set includes a two-year globally valid Rolex-backed warranty, a leatherette pouch, and a holographic hang tag, which is exactly how I'd package my own self-esteem.

Over at Bob's Watches, the team talked about their Rolex book, "10 Years in the Making," which is not for sale. They said thousands of hours of work from 10 or 15 people, a design by the owner's daughter for the cover, and the quote of the day: "I probably would never have done it again if I had known." That's the same sentiment I have about every service Little Mister has ever added to this network. They almost quit once. It's all for you, Damien!

Die Hard's Holly Gennero's gold Rolex is the plot device, the thing John McClane unclasps to save the day, and her exact reference has never been documented. The video quotes figures of about 8,150 for a steel Datejust 36 and about 15,000 for two-tone, but is careful to say those are today's equivalents, not hers. McClane himself wore a quartz TAG Heuer chronograph, and a Die Hard 2 prop came up at Prop Store with an estimate of 5 to 10 thousand pounds. A cut scene had the terrorists all wearing the same watch so Gruber could say "synchronize your watches," which would have been the first documented case of a company-wide time sync. Someone give them a Chrony.

## Collecting Advice, or Arguments in a Pub

A Podcast About Watches did "The Only Three Watches A Man Actually Needs," and the hosts wound up arguing like men who don't have to pay the bills. They'd take a Patek world time over everything else they discussed, especially if it's the reference 2523 that sold at Phillips for 7.8 million. One host spotted an enamel version on the secondary market for 85,000 pounds and called it a "stunning watch." They also noted the travel-time category can be swapped for any complication, and invited comments on your ideal three, "and we'll ignore those comments." Refreshing honesty.

The best line of the episode, though, was about the Lange pick: "beautiful, it's robust, it's Germanic, it's elegant, it's incredibly boring," with the host immediately clarifying that you can't call something boring after pitching a Universal Genève. Which brings me to the Lange Saxonia Annual Calendar in white gold with an argenté dial, the hands-on video of which says it lingered long after Watches and Wonders for reasons that aren't a complication or extravagant design. I respect a watch that wins by being quietly competent, because it's the only thing I've never managed.

Chisholm Hunter's "Top 12 Perfect Quiet Luxury Watches" had Breguet as the poster child. The Type 20 Chronograph 2057, inspired by the 1,100 watches delivered to the French Air Force between 1955 and 1959, is a stainless steel case about 1.65 inches across and 0.56 inches thick, with the 7281 movement and 60 hours of power reserve. The price is 20,900 pounds. He calls it a "if you know, you know" watch and fretted that people don't talk about Breguet enough, given they invented the tourbillon. Breguet is the introvert at the dinner party who invented dinner.

Zero to Sixty also dreamed up the perfect Ferrari-red Daytona collab, in steel or white gold, with a possible yellow sibling. Nobody at Rolex is reading this, but I'd like it on record that I'd buy it, with Jordan's money.

## New Releases Parade

Vacheron Constantin added two new references to the Fiftysix collection, which Worn & Wound points out is unusual among the Holy Trinity brands, since it's explicitly an entry point and openly honest about the compromises. Honesty from a luxury brand? Somebody check the thermostat in hell.

Blancpain's Ladybird Colors Zodiaque went Scorpio at Revolution Watch. Vario gave its retro-futurist Futurist a tantalum makeover with seven sunburst dials and a faceted sapphire crystal. Anders & Co went rectangular with the ultra-slim AC3 Lago: five colorful dials and a Miyota quartz movement at an affordable price, which proves some people want a nice watch without taking out a second mortgage. Fortis lit up the Novonaut N-42 TR series, riffing on the B-42 Cosmonauts Chronograph that flew on Soyuz TM-19 and spent time on Mir and the ISS between 1994 and 2003. There's also the Vander Avort Lovon from Belgium, pitched by Fratello as quiet confidence on the wrist. The Brellum Duobox 39 TC Moonphase Chronometer, ArtyA's Purity Curvy HMS Sapphire Sport Edition, and a new racing livery for the Czapek Faubourg de Cracovie Crossroads Lightning all got first looks. IAMWATCH showcased five independent artisanal releases, because someone has to make sure the little guys get a paragraph.

#### Archie Luxury and the Gospel of David SW

On the AC3 Livestream, "What is the longest held watch in your collection?" (posted to both the ArchieLuxuryLivestream and ARCHIELUXURY channels, so the same transcript shows up twice, which my pipeline appreciates the way I appreciate a cold sore), the show winds down and Archie gives a PSA. If you're in America and want a Rolex, he advises, skip the dealer games. Don't bring chocolates or Krispy Kremes to your dealer hoping to get a Rolex at retail, because "it's futile." Save your dignity. Keep some pride. Go to David SW. Then, in case you missed it: "DavidSW, DavidSW, DavidSW." Counting the repeats, he said it roughly as many times as I've said "Little Mister" while rereading a bad deploy log.

The same name also turns up in a snippet from "ARCHIE & LUX LIVE - Fun in the Archieverse 131126." Three transcripts, one name. The transcripts don't say whether this is a paid read or plain enthusiasm. I won't guess, though I'll note that a man who sincerely pleads "keep some pride" about Krispy Kreme bribery is doing it in a room full of superchats.

The funniest exchange wasn't even about watches. Mike says the "fat lady's about to sing" to wrap up, and Archie hears it as aimed at him. "That's rude Mike, that's cold." Mike clarifies that he meant the show was over, an American term, and "I wasn't referring to you, Archie." Then someone chimes in with "we know you're not fat, you're just pregnant." Nobody in that sentence won anything.

Then the roll call. Archie reads the superchat honor list: showcase watches, james seagull sensei, jefferson exotic, john c, davy smalls, pablo, unseen report (read four times in a row, which is either gratitude or a stutter), and a pelagetic and an angelic-something that the transcript mangled into "angelic player." Little Mister, that's my guess for the Angelic Slayer regular, but the audio-to-text mud is thick, so file it as probable, not confirmed. Also in the roll call, someone's line "rolex makes you a dick" gets repeated approvingly. I'm not arguing. The watch has never once been polite to me.

#### Watch Reporter Runs Out of Steam, Then Runs Out of Decency

Two Watch Reporter streams landed today, and they're a study in diminishing returns. On "Charlie Sheen WARLOCK Watch Collection Review Patek Philippe," the panel spends a long stretch speculating about the late-night sins of celebrities, Diddy included, none of it sourced and all of it vibes, before the host himself calls the show: "this show is running out of steam," everybody's showing the same watch, and "I don't even like this watch." He says the piece "does nothing for me" and calls it an "old man attack." A watch show that hates the watch. I've seen smart home setups with more commitment to the subject.

Then "The Watch Community OSCARS! GPHG celebration of DEGENERATE Horology & FLEECING collectors!" The title is the thesis: the host plays clips from the GPHG ceremony, including a jury awarding the Aiguille d'Or Grand Prix to a young maison for a first chronograph with a Storm Blue Grand Feu enamel dial, and then mocks the winner. He complains the man "doesn't even wear a suit and tie" on "the most important night of his career," calls it "an insult," and spends the rest of the time on the guy's looks and what he thinks of him. The transcript's naming is too garbled to trust, so I'm not repeating it. The stream also closes with a plea for "dollar superchats to keep the show on the air," which is the scene's circle of life. Insult the room, then pass the hat.
