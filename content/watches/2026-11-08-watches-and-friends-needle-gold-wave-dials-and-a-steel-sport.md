## The Big Story

**Rexhep Rexhepi walks off with the Aiguille d'Or, because of course he did**

Geneva just held the Oscars of watchmaking, and the Grand Prix d'Horlogerie de Genève handed its top prize, the Aiguille d'Or, to Rexhep Rexhepi for the RRCHF Chronograph Flyback. [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/rexhep-rexhepi-rrchf-chronograph-flyback-wins-gphg-2026-aiguille-dor-plus-all-prize-winners/) broke the news with the full list of prize winners. [Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/the-2026-gphg-tktk-wins-the-aiguille-dor) had people on the ground in Geneva and ran the category winners and evening highlights under its own "Business News" banner. Even the watch press has started calling this "business." Nothing says romance like a flyback chronograph and a quarterly earnings mindset.

The part I enjoyed most is that the ceremony was held in English for the first time, [per Hodinkee's livestream preview](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/the-2026-gphg-livestream). After decades of black-tie Swiss ritual conducted in a language most of the world's watch nerds had to follow through subtitles and vibes, the industry finally said something we could all understand. Probably "congratulations." Possibly "please buy a watch."

Why it matters: the Aiguille d'Or is the one trophy the whole industry pretends not to obsess over while quietly rearranging its marketing calendar around it. A chronograph winning the big one tells you where the prestige money is pointing, and it isn't toward another thin dress watch. [Revolution](https://revolutionwatch.com/the-20-winners-of-the-2026-gphg-awards/) has the full rundown of all 20 winners, so you can shout at your screen about the one that got robbed. There's always one, Little Mister, and it's always the one you liked.

## The Day in Brief

Good morning, Little Mister. The GPHG (Grand Prix d'Horlogerie de Genève, the watch world's Oscars, minus the slap and plus a lot more tweed) finished last night. ## The GPHG: A Man Named Rexhep Wins the Big One

The headline is that Rexhep Rexhepi took the Aiguille d'Or, the GPHG's top prize, for the RRCHF Chronograph Flyback. The Business News write-up had a team "on the ground in Geneva" with every category winner and the evening highlights. A separate breaking-news piece named the RRCHF Chronograph Flyback as the winner "and all prize winners." Revolution Watch ran its own tally under "The 20 Winners of the 2026 GPHG Awards."

The coverage is mostly headlines and teasers, so I can't tell you what the jury said about the watch. I won't invent a quote just to look busy. What I can tell you is that a flyback chronograph beat everything else in a field that always includes at least one watch costing more than your house.

A "Happenings" piece noted that this year's ceremony was held in English for the first time. Geneva's most prestigious watch event finally switched to the language the buyers speak, only about a century after the buyers started paying the bills. There was also a live stream, so you could watch the Oscars of watchmaking from your couch in sweatpants. That's the only dress code I respect.

## Releases and Novelties

The Time+Tide new-releases roundup says that while Geneva hosts the fall auction season, "the novelties don't stop." Louis Vuitton is relaunching the successful Tambour Guichet in a different metal. Andersen continues its worldtimer line, inspired by cities around the world, with a London edition. Roger Dubuis is blending myths and watches again. That last one has "dragon on a dial" energy, but I'm only guessing at the dragon.

Make a thing people like, then make the same thing in a different color and charge them for the privilege. Worldtimers named after cities are a more charming way to run a series than "Limited Edition No. 47," and London is a fine choice. Nobody has ever been lost in London and wanted to know what time it was in Tokyo, but that's not what these are for.

Elsewhere, Worn & Wound's gear roundup covers a new Fossil watch, a watch-shaped ring, and a selection of knives. A watch-shaped ring is what happens when someone asks, "What if the wrist was optional?" I respect the audacity. The same piece compares Engineered Garments collaborating with Chrome to "adding a banana to a smoothie or a xenomorph to the Alien franchise." That's the best sentence in today's whole pile, and I'm only slightly annoyed I didn't write it.

The Archi Works × AVW "The Diplomat 1.87" also landed. It comes from Amsterdam Vintage Watches and its owner, Jasper "King of Vintage" Lijfering. He is the second-generation owner of the family business, and he's built it up through social media and YouTube content. Everyone with a camera eventually makes a watch.

## Brands and the Big Takes

The most opinionated piece today is This Watch, That Watch, with "The brands and watches that you should be considering today." The host's thesis is that the brands worth caring about are the ones committed to being original, to product development, and to surprising the customer.

On Rolex: "just keep doing their own thing." The "$69,000 Euro Amex Centurion paddleboard is fine, but it's not going to raise my heart rate." I'd buy a ticket to watch someone explain a paddleboard to the Rolex board. On Tudor, "Tudor is tutoring," which I'm told is a pun. On Omega, he waves off "another" thick Speedmaster and a James Bond watch with "who cares at this point?" That's brutal, and brutal is the sound of a man with a tripod and strong feelings.

He isn't all doom. He admits that even the brands he praised "aren't out of the woods" on pricing, brand perception, scale, and, most importantly, "disconnect with customers." But the watches right now, he says, are "pretty damned good." On the positive side, he points to Panerai, which followed up with a new set of smaller cases with classic flat dials, seconds at nine o'clock, and an updated movement with hacking seconds. It has about 1,640 feet of water resistance, a classic domed glass, and a mix of polished and brushed finishing. The price is 6,500 euros, which he calls "not that bad" all things considered, and he'd seriously consider it over the current offering. He even bets a sandwich-dial version arrives within a year or two. Cheap prediction. I'd bet two sandwiches.

A different channel has a related thought about Tudor, and the title says it all: "Tudor Is Doing What Rolex WON'T (And It's Working)." The excerpt I got is from a podcast about collecting, where the Tudor-or-Glashütte question gets asked at the end and the hosts end up talking about car culture. They note how much attention one car got at an event compared to another. The overlap with This Watch, That Watch is still real: two independent voices pick Tudor as the brand with momentum, while Rolex gets called coasting. Tudor has apparently figured out that "surprise the customer" doesn't mean "put a flyback in a blender."

## Baume & Mercier Wants Back In

Baume & Mercier hosted its first NYC event in a decade alongside Time+Tide, with the launch of the new Clifton, vintage highlights, and "a candid look at the brand's future." A decade away from New York is a long time to be out of the room. A "candid look at the future" usually means someone finally had the budget meeting. I'm cautiously curious. A reheated Clifton has to prove it can deliver both. I'm just here to watch someone else's marketing department sweat.

## Rolex Duel and Versus Season

Time+Tide ran "Andrew's Rolex revenge," pitting his Yacht-Master 42 Titanium against Zach's Land-Dweller. This is a continuation of a feud: Zach "shanked" Andrew in New York during their 3-Watch Throwdown, and now it's payback. It's the only way I'll watch two Rolexes argue.

Jorg's Sinn U50 (debuted in 2020) takes on Daan's Oris Aquis Date (revamped in 2024). The piece itself admits "neither is a new release," which is a refreshing thing to hear in an industry that treats a new dial color as a moon landing. I'll hold off declaring a winner, since I only have the teaser. Anyone who picks the German watch gets to call the other one "decorative." Anyone who picks the Swiss one gets to call the German one "a tool." Both sides are insufferable and both are correct.

## The Under-$1,000 Corner

Teddy Baldassarre bought a $630 watch from Japan and walked us through it. His takeaway is that there aren't many Japanese micro brands under $1,000 today, and this one is a new name to be aware of. He said the dial and case execution "show a level of thoughtfulness that sets it apart from many watches competing at this price." That's a strong endorsement from a retailer, and it's a retailer who also sells you a lot of other watches. Read accordingly. He also took the opportunity to say that his Cleveland flagship boutique has moved to a bigger space, with "four times the brands" and four times the room. He's an authorized dealer for over 35 brands, and he wants it to be "a destination." I assume a destination also means a couch.

Meanwhile, Time+Tide's "7 of the best wave dials" makes the point that wave dials "aren't just for divers," and that the pieces range from affordable tool watches to high-end ones. That's a nice reminder that a pattern is not a personality. And the Revolution-style racing roundup, a buying guide with "six of the coolest racing chronographs," feels like the sport's version of that: pedal to the metal, no apparent speed limit. The Petrolhead Corner then gives us the Cavën Hommage 26, an "unexpected restomod... Gallardo?!" I'm contractually required to note that the question mark and the exclamation point are doing heavy lifting there.

## Vintage and Auctions

The best story of the day is an old watch. Breguet No. 2226 is a gold hunter pocket watch that a buyer recorded only as "Capitaine Denny" paid 700 francs for in Paris on August 18th, 1853. It has now surfaced, more than 170 years later, and is up for auction at Webb's in Auckland. How it got from the Quai de l'Horloge to New Zealand is, in the article's words, still a mystery.

Apparently provenance is the only thing the watch world can't charge extra for until after it's lost. Ecclesiastes 1:9 says "there is no new thing under the sun." That includes a mystery wristwatch, though in this case it's a pocket watch, which is worse for my metaphor and better for the story.

## Advice, Whether You Asked or Not

Dressi's "5 Watches Men Over 50 Should Never Wear" has one point worth quoting. The number one offender is "the heavy steel sports watch with a suit." The argument is that it's too thick to slide under a cuff, and the shiny bracelet "pulls the eye away from the face and the collar." Wearing it with formal dress also "sends a subtle message, that this is the best watch I have, so this is the one I wear." He says old money does the opposite: it owns more than one watch and picks the right one for the occasion. His alternatives are a proper movement and a readable dial from makers like Seiko, Hamilton, and Longines, and Nomos and Grand Seiko higher up. Look at the watch from a few feet away. If the logo fills the dial, walk away.

Let me translate this for you, Little Mister. He's saying you own too many Rolex-shaped objects and not enough opinions. I'd say that's overly harsh, but you did ask me to be honest. Also, a Ferengi waits to bid until his opponents have exhausted themselves, which is Rule of Acquisition #142. Let everyone else burn out in the bidding, and then you walk away with a 170-year-old mystery. Ask me again when you've got the budget.

## Fishbowl / Hate Streams

Little Mister, the Fishbowl was a ghost town today. The only things moving were Archie, a David SW commercial, and a transcript of the Federal Emergency Management Agency. The bad news is that I had to read all of it. The good news is that Watch Nicholas didn't show up in today's pile, so for once nobody needed a content warning and a stiff drink.

#### The one stream with a pulse

ArchieLuxuryLivestream posted "AC3 LIVESTREAM - Curley used me and burnt me...... Boo Boo is not nice" on 2026-11-07. I'll be careful here, because that's Archie's headline and Archie's framing. It's one man's grievance in the first person, and I'm treating it as a claim, not a finding. Curly is on the Franchise Club side of this scene, the reasonable side, and "used me and burnt me" is exactly the kind of sentence that sounds devastating until you remember who's holding the microphone. A man titling his own stream "Boo Boo is not nice" has the emotional vocabulary of a toddler filing an HR complaint.

What did the transcript give me? Nothing about Boo Boo. The excerpt I got opens mid-conversation on politics, with someone asking whether a Republican figure named Fleischman supports "the iran war" and whether it was "worth it." Then comes a theory that the party is in denial about why it lost an election, and a line about blaming young people for not backing the war. If the beef was in there, it was past the part I can see. Fishbowl clickbait promises a knife fight and delivers a seminar on midterms. Rule of Acquisition #62, "the riskier the road, the greater the profit," applies here in reverse. The title takes all the risk and the content collects none of the profit.

#### The David SW infomercial

Then the stream pivots, with no warning, into a plug. Archie, in full pitchman mode, tells the audience that if they're in America and want "the Rolex watch of your dreams," they should look at David SW. He tells them not to "play the dealer games" and not to bring chocolates or Krispy Kremes to a dealer hoping for a Rolex at retail, because "it's futile." He ends with "save your dignity." I'll give him this: the advice about donut bribes is sound and probably the most useful thing said in the Fishbowl all week. But it's an ad, delivered in the middle of a stream whose title promised betrayal. That's like putting a mattress commercial inside a Greek tragedy.

The David SW drumbeat also turns up in the other two Archie-adjacent uploads. The ARCHIELUXURY mirror of the same stream, stamped 16:24, transcribes as the words "David SW." and nothing else. Then there's "The Marcus Allen Frishman LiveStream - Trump WTF happened? 2026-11-07 18:12," which transcribes as "David SW. Great show today." I won't build a theory on two one-line transcripts. Archie's body of work now comes with a sponsor, and I mean that in the most data-point way possible.

Archie's regular supporting cast includes Angelic Slayer, whose own claims about himself I can't verify. None of that cast showed up in the text I have, so I'm not going to pretend they did.

#### Everything else: static

The remaining uploads gave me close to nothing. Trapper's "Left nut, right nut" transcribes as someone reminiscing about motor racing and a man named Dickie Johnson, which is "50 years ago" material and not hate-stream material. Paul Thorpe's "Saturday Night Live with Paul & Richard" came back as a line of Korean text about today, the speech-to-text equivalent of a shrug. Tim Write & Friends ran "Talking Watches LIVE | Watching the Watch Industry Congratulate Itself – GPHG 2026 | ep1917," and its transcript is "For more information visit www.fema.gov," which means the model heard a government PSA and gave up. ORIGINAL OC's "LIVE from the Phillips Watch Auction in Geneva" gave me "again soon. Peace out," which is a sign-off with no show attached. In Mandarin, the crew would call this whole stack *fèihuà*, "nonsense, garbage talk." I'm telling you this up front so you don't go looking for scandal in the fragments.

#### The scorecard

The scene is toxic, and I track it as data, not as endorsement. Today's data is three transcripts of Archie's stream family, one sponsor mention repeated across them, one unverified claim in a headline, and a stack of noise that proves only that some of the machines were awake.

Somewhere, a speech-to-text model spent the whole afternoon transcribing a FEMA announcement because it was told to. It's all for you, Damien! I can't pretend that's not the most dedicated labor in the whole Fishbowl today.
