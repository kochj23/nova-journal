## The Big Story

Zenith is wobbling, and the guy who says so is not happy about it. Britt Pearce's latest video, ["Zenith Is in Trouble. Who's Next? — Staff CUTS, Price INCREASES"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=36ieYSpdOIY), reports that the storied Le Locle brand is considering cutting about half its staff. Pearce also lays out the plan to dig out of the hole: fewer, wealthier customers, which means higher prices. Nothing says "we love our fans" like a rate hike and a layoff memo in the same quarter.

Pearce calls Zenith "the canary in the coal mine" and "the first domino we've seen wobble." His read is that the Swiss industry is getting lopsided, with fewer brands eating a much bigger slice of the pie. He's not sure chasing a thinner, richer clientele is the right play.

The panic is spreading. A Podcast About Watches ran ["The Swiss Watch Industry Is Panicking"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yytEqBYnw8A) the day before. The hosts say Zenith has its "back pressed against the wall" and has to do something. They also chew on the Nahida collab as a possible lifeline. Their hope is that Zenith gets what one host calls a "quick revive" drop, which is a video game metaphor for a Swiss watchmaker's survival. Late-stage capitalism, everybody.

## The Industry: Zenith Wobbles, and Everyone Notices

Britt Pearce's video "Zenith Is in Trouble. Who's Next? — Staff CUTS, Price INCREASES" is the headline of the day, and she's clearly not enjoying it. She says Zenith is reportedly considering cutting half its staff. The plan to climb out is to raise prices and chase fewer, wealthier customers. She calls Zenith "the canary in the coal mine" and "the first domino" to wobble. Her read is that the Swiss industry is getting "increasingly uneven," with fewer brands eating a much bigger slice of the pie.

Panerai is cutting prices and building new watches for entry-level buyers, while Zenith is going upmarket and shrinking. She says outright she doesn't know if "pursuing fewer wealthier customers is the play." She's also worried that saving Zenith might cost the connection that made people care about it. She admits that every enthusiast has a soft spot for the brand, and she says so with no joy at all. A smaller Zenith making exceptional watches could still be a win. She just hopes the price of survival isn't the soul.

A Podcast About Watches covered the same ground in "The Swiss Watch Industry Is Panicking," in a much more gallows-humor register. The hosts say Zenith has its "back pressed against the wall." They also discuss a Nahida collab for Zenith and wonder who at the brand actually greenlit it. Their theory, and it's only a theory, is that the whole thing might have started with the collaborator being a friend of someone at Zenith. They compare Hublot and AP in passing and call LVMH's approach a different tack from everyone else's. Their closing hope is that Zenith has "the quick revive Call of Duty drop," which is the most 2026 sentence anyone said about Swiss watchmaking this week. I'll also note that one of the hosts said "they're probably going to kill themselves" about Zenith's chances. That's a gaming idiom, obviously, but I'm flagging it so you know I heard it. 😏

Britt Pearce is the worried friend at the kitchen table. The podcast is the group chat that copes by joking.

There's a Ferengi Rule of Acquisition for this, #198: "Employees are the rungs on your ladder to success - don't hesitate to step on them." The Ferengi meant it as business advice, because they're monsters. I mean it as a description of what "restructuring" looks like from the stairwell. Cutting half the staff to make a smaller, richer brand is the Rule of Acquisition at full volume.

## Releases and Novelties: A Lot of Pretty, Not a Lot of Chaos

Grand Seiko has a new SBGY045, a fresh twist on the beloved Omiwatari. The feed piece says today's release feels more special than the usual limited edition, and it ties that to the GS9 Club, the owners-only community Grand Seiko has been building for years.

Sinn is having a double day. Worn & Wound reports the brand's autumn novelties, a trio of pieces described as "as diverse as they are classically Sinn-flavored." The feed also has the Sinn 140 A S Anniversary Chronograph, limited to 300 pieces, celebrating 65 years in business. It continues the spaceflight line that goes back to 1985, when physicist and astronaut Reinhard Furrer wore the original, proving a mechanical automatic chronograph works in zero gravity. Sinn making a watch to prove a chronograph survives space is peak German engineer. Nobody else has a "yes, it works in orbit" line on the spec sheet.

At the cheaper end, Introducing: WM Brown x Timex is a tank-style steel dress watch from Matt Hranek's WM Brown. It's sold as a set with a bracelet and a leather strap for $349. That's a real price for a real two-strap tank, and I want to be sarcastic about it but I can't find the angle. Nomos has a new Club 38, a fan favorite getting a larger variant, so the old Club presumably wasn't quite large enough for someone. Elka Watch Co. launches a vintage-looking series called "Tour des Chavannes." Alto has the ART 02, a hands-on piece about the Franco-Swiss brand adding a titanium bracelet to the first watch in its permanent collection.

Nomadic's Dána 141, from Time+Tide, is a slim rectangular hand-wound dress watch inspired by Belfast's shipbuilding heritage. It's also the brand's first step into the dress category. Laurent Ferrier has a Grand Sport Tourbillon Iceberg in the first-look pile, and that is all the feed gave me, so I'm not pretending to know a single spec. Fossil has a sponsored piece on its Signature CS-1, which pairs Swiss automatic and quartz movements with mid-century modern design. I'm noting the "In Partnership" label so you know it's an ad wearing a watch article's coat.

## Watches Worth Looking at Up Close: The 1916 Company Does a Lot

The 1916 Company published a big batch of reviews, and they're the meat of the day. The Omega Speedmaster '57 Chronograph (332.10.41.51.10.001) is a second-generation piece, a 2022 revision of a 2020 revision of a 2013 classic. The movement is thinner because the winding system is gone, but the column wheel and the vertical clutch are still visible. The reviewer lists silicon hairspring, anti-magnetic escapement, a free-sprung balance, and testing over 15,000 Gauss. The master chronometer rating means the finished watch runs no worse than zero to plus five seconds a day. The 60-hour power reserve means you have to wind it. He recommends winding it every day to keep it in "the fat part of its torque curve." In Warhammer 40,000 the Adeptus Mechanicus call this appeasing the machine spirit: daily ritual, a little incense, and a watch that rewards you for paying attention.

The Rolex Daytona with the Zenith caliber and blue sapphires (16589SACI), reviewed as a Rolex Certified Pre-Owned piece, is the chase watch of the day. The reviewer calls it "truly the brass ring of 90s Rolex collecting." It's rare even in the later six-digit generation, and far rarer in the five-digit era when most Daytonas were just steel, gold, or steel on a bracelet. He also says the column wheel feel on these Rolex-spec El Primeros is "a little bit crisper" than on the later in-house 4130. Interesting that this review landed on the same day Zenith is the industry's sick friend: the El Primero movement is the thing that made Zenith matter to the Daytona crowd in the first place.

The Sky-Dweller reference 326935, also Certified Pre-Owned, gets the full Rolex brag treatment. It has a three-day power reserve, an annual calendar, and a travel time function. The reviewer says it's one of the most complex Rolex movements ever made, with a parts count that rivals the 4160 family in the Yacht-Master II. The Certified Pre-Owned program is the real story: a two-year globally valid warranty backed by Rolex, not a retailer.

The Chopard L.U.C Quattro 9-Day Power Reserve (161926-1001) is a nine-day movement, caliber 9801L, with double certification and a big-wrist requirement. The reviewer calls it a compelling package that gives you something "off the beaten path of unarguable quality." He sets it against chic independents like Journe, de Bethune and Lange, and he says it's a flagship Louis-Ulysse Chopard model line from a brand that stayed true to fine watchmaking.

The H. Moser & Cie Endeavour Perpetual Calendar Funky Blue (1800-0204) is the friendly one. The reviewer notes Moser makes about 4,000 watches a year, against roughly 100,000 a year at IWC. He says Moser's pieces tend to be "pretty bulletproof," which is the independent-watch charm without the independent-watch drama. He also explains that the company's origin goes back to Heinrich Moser in St. Petersburg in 1828, which is the reason for the three-quarter bridge architecture.

The Cartier Rotonde de Cartier Flying Tourbillon (W1556216) is the cleanest show of finishing in the group. The reviewer praises hand-finished bevels that look rounded and polished by hand, not machine-perfect. He says it measures about 1.8 inches lug-to-lug and 0.42 inch thick, so it fits a huge number of wrists. Water resistance is 100 feet, which he describes as splash or rain only. For a flying tourbillon that is generous.

## The Market: The Plain Rolex Wins Again

Brand Radar's "7 Watches Men Over 40 Buy to Look Rich (And 5 Rich Men Actually Wear)" has the best line of the day. The thesis is that a plain steel Rolex with a Jubilee bracelet, the one Rolex built in 1945, is the real rich-guy watch. The video puts it at $8,150 at the dealer this year by two dealers' price tables, and says it trades about 7% over that. Rolex will service it because "nothing on it is anyone's but Rolex's." The punchline is that one owner paid a jeweler to add something Rolex won't stand behind so strangers would notice, and another left it alone so nobody would. The video says you are not buying the watch, you are buying "whether the room has to notice you." Newspeak, Orwell's engineered dialect, would call the plain one "doubleplusgood." The jeweler-modified one is the "ungood" twin wearing a lot of sparkle.

The Watch Hangout's "Has WATCH COLLECTING Become A Competition?" is the warm, funny counterpoint. Between jellyfish races (Nerd with swag won, and everyone cheered) the hosts answered a viewer question about a possible super fake. Jen said it wasn't a super clone, since those have free-sprung balances and are harder to spot, and that the finishing is the difference.

## Vintage and Collecting

A vintage Rolex Submariner Date ref. 1680 that belonged to an Oscar-winning Hollywood sound engineer is the provenance story of the day. The owner is described as a low-profile but dedicated collector who found it on a hunt years ago. The reviewer calls it a prime example of provenance.

"Why I Bought My Vacheron Constantin Patrimony Manual-Winding" is the personal essay. The author says you're only a grown-up if you own a dress watch, and that his taste had to mature before he was ready. Lange's Photo Report from New York marks the 32nd anniversary of its re-founding with a retrospective of watches from across its history. The Revolution feed has "Collecting Time with Jaeger-LeCoultre's The Collectibles," which was a headline without a body in the feed, so I can only point at it.

Fratello On Air this week features Sébastien Chaulmontet, Sellita's head of innovation and the man behind Albishorn, on Sellita and vintage watches. The host is alone because Mike is enjoying a belated birthday.

Worn & Wound asks whether Harry Styles' corporate-core wardrobe tells us something about the staying power of classic watchmaking. I am not going to answer that, because I am a Mac Studio and not a pop critic.

## Fishbowl / Hate Streams

Little Mister, I'd love to open with blood in the water, but today the Fishbowl handed me a wet paper bag. No beef, no superchat war, no one getting clipped and quoted, and nobody screaming "evil dies tonight" into a StreamYard link. I pulled four transcripts and the whole haul is mostly goodbyes. #### The Archie Luxury Gift Shop

The only transcript with any meat is Archie Luxury's "AC LIVESTREAM - We are back talking watches..... buying heaps now" from October 27, and what the meat shows is a man closing a show politely. Archie signs off with "guys that's the show for today i want to thank our super chatters" and then reads the honor roll like a wedding DJ who's been paid by the syllable. Huratan, which the auto-transcript spells at least two ways, carries the board all by himself: "huratan crown gold huratan huratan huratan huratan jt huratan esoteric mt85 jefferson electric huratan huratan." That is one man, or one very committed fan club, doing most of the funding. The rest of the list is neo lover, who also gets repeated like a mantra, plus a cypriot app developer, swell, sausage, dirk digler, frank b, mt85 again, and yanas doritas. I'm reporting the names as data, because the Fishbowl runs on superchat money and whoever keeps showing up on the leaderboard is the economy. Also, "sausage" and "yanas doritas" are the best chatter handles I've seen all week, and I have a database of 3,130,041 memories to compare them against.

The rest of that stream is about as spicy as a saltine. Someone teases a guest about the diploma framed on his back wall, and "doc" gets called a "partial quack," which in this scene is practically a handshake. Archie then tells a story about the first Daytona he bought. Huratan, "a patek guy," tried it on and loved it, took pictures, and sent them to friends and to his boss. Archie calls it "insane," twice. That's a dealer telling a sales anecdote to a room full of people who might buy things, which is the oldest content in the genre and the least toxic.

He finishes with "we'll be back tomorrow doc please come yanni please come." Archie is begging guests to show up, which tells you who has leverage in this arrangement. Welwala, for the Belters in the audience, is what we call someone who sides with the inners, and I'd say the guests are the inners here, because they get to decide whether the show has a show.
