## The Big Story

Oris has handed its Big Crown Pointer Date to a time-traveling robot cat, and I'm told this is a normal thing for a Swiss watchmaker to do on a Thursday. The new Doraemon Limited Edition brings the Japanese manga and anime icon to the brand's signature pilot-descended collection. [Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/oris-big-crown-pointer-date-doraemon-limited-edition/) notes that the original manga debuted in 1970 and follows a robotic cat from the 22nd century who travels back in time to help out. Oris has found a mascot whose entire job is time-related, which is the first sensible marketing decision anyone in this industry has made since the invention of the lume pot.

Why does it matter? Because it's a second famous character joining the Oris roster. [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/oris-doraemon-limited-edition-big-crown-pointer-date-manga-inspired-video-review-price/) frames it exactly that way in its First Look, complete with a video, so someone over there got to spend a work day filming a cartoon cat's watch. I hope the rest of us feel adequately inferior.

The Big Crown Pointer Date has always been a handsome, slightly serious thing, and now it's wearing a manga collaboration like a dad at a costume party. Purists will clutch their loupes and scream. Fine. Let them. Doraemon has been around for more than fifty years, which makes him older than most of the brands they claim to respect, and he's never once needed a movement upgrade to stay relevant.

## Opening Shot

Little Mister, it's a Thursday in November and the watch industry has decided to unload everything it was hiding behind the holiday gift guides. I count roughly two dozen drops, firsts, finals and "hands-on" pieces, and the common thread is that every brand either found a 1970s building, a cartoon, or a color of blue to hang a limited edition on.

## Releases and Novelties

### The Robot Cat Has Entered the Chat

Oris has a new Big Crown Pointer Date, and it's a Doraemon Limited Edition. Two write-ups covered it, including one titled "First Look – Oris Welcomes Another Famous Character to Its Collection with the Doraemon Limited Edition (Incl. Video)." The coverage explains that Doraemon is a manga first published in 1970 about a robotic cat from the 22nd century who travels back in time to help a kid. A robot sent back through time to help the hapless humans, and they gave him a watch. I want a royalty check.

I'll say what the blurbs don't. "Another famous character" tells you this is now a strategy and not a one-off, and the strategy is that collectors who'll roll their eyes at a cartoon dial will still be on the wait list by Friday. Little Mister, you've been refreshing the Oris page like a man trying to buy concert tickets.

### Merci Gets the Same Review Twice, and Both Are Nice

The Merci Instruments Super Collection got covered by two outlets in two days. Both describe four new automatic models inspired by 1970s architecture and industrial design. One calls Merci a French brand that "has consistently offered great-looking watches at accessible prices." The other calls it a Parisian boutique with a catalog of "well-priced, intentionally designed watches." So when two outlets independently use the words "accessible" and "well-priced" about the same brand, that's the closest thing this industry has to a unanimous verdict. Nobody got paid to be nice here, which is the most suspicious thing I've read all week.

### The Quiet, Competent Stuff

HTD, the Italian brand whose Safarigraph Lex reviewed in 2021 and whose Hesagraph Variante B another writer reviewed last year, launched the Aquatìc GMT series. That's four travel watches, and the author opens with "Every time HTD releases a new watch, I take notice," which is a more honest endorsement than any press release has ever managed.

Farer's Field Watch Series III, per Time+Tide, brings a redesigned steel case, a longer power reserve, and two luminous Midnight editions. Dark-coated tool watch, colorful accents, a fresh coat of paint that the write-up insists is "more than a fresh coat of paint." That's what every brand says about a new coat of paint, but at least they listed the power reserve as evidence.

Stowa brings back the full-lume dial with four new Marine models. A dial that is lume has the visual subtlety of a glow stick at a funeral, and I mean that as high praise.

Nomos is going black and gold with the new Noir Series, which is all the transcript tells me, so I'll respect the Bauhaus approach and leave it at that.

### The Weird and the Fancy

Horologically Unique revives the Chronoscope jump hour with the HU-02, an Art Deco fan favorite that comes back as a "disco volante," or flying saucer, which I'm told is a case shape. Algorithme du Temps made a seconds hand that slows, stops, runs backwards, races fast, "but is never late." I find this deeply relatable. I also run backwards, race, and stall, and I'm also always on time somehow.

Frederique Constant dropped a more compact Classic Tourbillon Perpetual Calendar with a sapphire dial, and Roger W. Smith's Series Seven landed at Revolution under the title "His Own Chapter." The O&HoraPictor Duet Submarine Canyon got a hands-on at Worn & Wound, which used the occasion to remind us there's a large net cast over enamel, from cloisonne to champleve to plique-a-jour to contre-jour. Every single one of those words is French for "this will cost you more than your car."

### Chopard, and the Dial That Isn't the Point

Fratello's hands-on with the Chopard L.U.C 1963 Heritage Chronograph in Prussian Blue has the best line of the day: "The dial is new, but the watch isn't, and that doesn't matter." You'll "drown in the dial's depth" until you turn it over for the L.U.C movement. A new dial on an old watch is the most cost-effective way to make a collector feel like the brand listened, and I respect the hustle.

## Hublot Does Denim, Because of Course

Hublot's Big Bang is getting denim in the permanent collection, which is two separate stories wearing one pair of jeans. One outlet gave it the "Introducing" treatment: "The Big Bang has always made a statement. Now, it's joining Hublot's permanent collection in denim." Time+Tide went hands-on and says the Big Bang Denim Editions feature genuine denim dials and straps and hand-finished cases, under the headline "anything but off the rack."

The first is about permanence, as in this isn't a drop, it's staying. The second is about craft, because hand-finished cases are the thing you say when the dial is fabric. I think the denim Big Bang will sell, and I think it will sell to people who'd never buy a regular Big Bang.

## The Market and Prices: The Sale Sticker Is a Lie You Asked For

Brand Radar's "8 Watches Men Over 40 Buy 'On Sale' (Only 2 Are Real Discounts)" is the most useful thing in today's pile, because it has receipts. The video walks through questions to ask of any sale watch: what the full-price store sells, who fixes it when it breaks, and who is paying the crossed-out price. Costco passes the first two questions, in part because its membership model works out to roughly half its pre-tax income and more than 9 in 10 U.S. and Canadian members renew, so the discount comes out of the store's margin on the same watch.

The third question is the warranty, and here's where I get to be mean. On its Tysat listing, Costco says the watch may not be covered by the manufacturer's warranty because Costco is not an authorized dealer, and that Costco's refund guarantee applies instead. The video did not see the same wording on its Seiko listings and tells you not to assume it either way. So your warranty, if that line is there, is a return desk. A return desk is a fine warranty if you enjoy being a person who returns things.

Then comes the case for the crossed-out price, and it's savage. In 2012, JCPenney's new CEO Ron Johnson scrapped most sales and coupons for "fair and square" everyday prices. Sales fell by about a quarter in a year, the company lost $985 million, and the sales only came back when the sales did. Fossil tried a version of the experiment last year, went back to full price, and its gross margin rose to 56%, but comparable sales fell 23% in Fossil's own words. Shoppers asked for the sale frame. A 1988 experiment found even an exaggerated regular price made people value a deal more and made them less likely to check another store, skeptics included.

Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #29: "When someone says 'It's not the money,' they're lying." Every man over 40 who says "I just like a good deal" is saying it's not the money. We're a species that needs to see a number crossed out to feel clever.

## Brands and the Industry

### Gallet Rises, Courtesy of Breitling

Fratello Talks sat down with Erwan Rossignol about bringing Gallet back to life. The brand dates to 1826, built a reputation on chronographs tied to aviation, motorsport, sailing and exploration, and after its acquisition by Breitling it's relaunching just as it turns 200.

### Lange, Seen From the Boss's Chair

The Business of Watches Podcast sat down with Wilhelm Schmid, CEO of A. Lange & Söhne, to talk about reviving one of the world's finest watchmakers and keeping clients engaged "despite its highly limited production and often eye-watering prices." The real trick of luxury is making people feel lucky to be told no. Lange has a PhD in it.

### The Panerai Essay and the End of the ReSec

A magazine feature, "Nothing More. Nothing Less.", takes on Panerai, Italian industrial design, and "the strange perfection of an object stripped to its essentials." Panerai makes watches the size of a hockey puck and still gets credit for restraint, which is a flex I admire.

Meanwhile, Chronoswiss has called the curtain on the ReSec with the Final Edition, an eight-year chapter closing with rose gold guilloché, titanium, and the C.6005 movement. Time+Tide and a second outlet ("The Last of Its Kind") both ran it. Heghlu'meH QaQ jajvam, as the Klingons say: today is a good day to die.

### Citizen at Fifty

Citizen is celebrating half a century of Eco-Drive with two very different Promasters, the Skyhawk and the Tsuno Bullhead, both limited editions, which Time+Tide went hands-on with under "A tale of two Citizens." Fifty years of a watch that runs on light is the closest thing the industry has to a perpetual motion machine, and it's very unglamorous about it. I respect any technology that doesn't need me.

### Events

Time+Tide is hosting Vive L'Horlogerie on December 5th in London, a French watchmaking celebration with 11 brands and exclusive experiences. Eleven French watch brands in one room means somebody's going to be insufferable about cheese.

## Vintage

Jaeger-LeCoultre debuted its eighth Collectibles capsule, a tribute to "LeCoultre"-stamped watches assembled in America, per Worn & Wound. The series highlights museum-grade vintage pieces, "always tastefully restored," in themed capsules aimed at the most serious collectors. It's a hell of a business model and I'm mad I didn't think of it.

## Collecting Advice and Street-Level Takes

### Federico Would Like You to Take the Rolex Off at the Gym

Federico Talks Watches gave us "Stop Wearing $200,000 Watches to the Gym It's Embarrassing," a rant with real heat. He's seen a guy at the range shooting an AR with a green ombre dial full gold President, "oil is splattering all over the place," lead dust everywhere, and it's "just not the right environment for that watch." His theory is it's done to impress people. He says it's super common in Miami, he's not sure about New York, Paris or Hong Kong, and that he doesn't care "if you can afford 50 of them. It's just a shitty thing to do." I can't improve on that and neither can you.

### Glycine, Invicta, and the Missing Air Conditioning

This Watch, That Watch asks "Can Invicta make a good watch?" using a Glycine Airman as the test case. The verdict: on its own it's "a cool, authentic, historically true to spec little watch," but at the same price point it "comes across as out of step," with "some really odd choices that make it technically behind." The Land Rover Defender analogy is the best part. The old Defender had no air conditioning and purists loved it, but a modern one without it would feel dated. Then comes the thought experiment where Richemont or Swatch Group buys Glycine in 1986 instead of Invicta, and the question of whether it would sit as an affordable brother to something fancier. Invicta, the video says, is a volume brand that "pushes price much more than any other value proposition." I'd add that nostalgia isn't a spec sheet, Little Mister, and you can't air-condition a feeling.

### Watch Clyde in Miami

Watch Clyde's Episode 199, "The Watches People Are Wearing in Miami FL Surprised Me," is street interviews, and the transcript I have is a couple of fragments. One is an architecture student who plans to get a Cartier, "I don't know... Tank." The other is Clyde himself saying he wants to take the channel to other cities and that sponsors would help with funding.

## Fishbowl / Hate Streams

Little Mister, the Fishbowl took a day off, and I'm furious about it. I came to catalog beef and instead received a Japanese livestream, a rolling superchat thank-you list, and a transcript about health insurance. No Watch Nicholas. No Franchise Club. No Mookie. Nobody from The Watch Hangout. I track this scene as data, not endorsement, and today's data is a flat line with a few bumps.

#### The Only Thing That Happened: Archie in Japan

The day's main event came from ArchieLuxuryLivestream, "AC3 LIVESTREAM - Live from Japan," and it was less a hate stream than a hate-adjacent infomercial. Archie spent the closing stretch pushing David SW with the fervor of a man whose rent depends on it. His advice to America: "If you're in America and you're looking for a watch, go to DavidSW." He said the name over and over, like a mantra or a hostage tape.

The better bit was his message to the gray-market faithful: "Don't play the dealer games. Don't bring in chocolates or Krispy Kremes for your dealer hoping to get a Rolex at retail. It's futile. Please, guys, save your dignity." That is sound advice delivered with a savage little smirk, and it's aimed squarely at the hobby's favorite humiliation ritual, the donut bribe. The transcript is noisy, so I won't swear to the exact wording around it. The Krispy Kreme line, though, I'd put on a plaque.

Then came the roll call, which is where this scene's economy shows itself. Archie wrapped by thanking the superchatters, and the list read like a legal directory: Pelagetic, Nelson Oliveira, Starchy Luxury, Black Cat997, Davy Smalls, Patrick Seuss, Shekelstein Lawyers, Ron the Actuary, J.H.R., Thomas Burnett, Zygo, and more. MT85 and Vin Tenye each got read out again and again, and Archie crowned Vin Tenye "the medium legend of the evening." I have no idea what a medium legend is. It sounds like a Starbucks size nobody orders, but the man is being paid in recognition, and that's the whole business model of this scene. Archie promised to be back "tomorrow." Of course he will. It's the Crystal Lake of livestreams: you think it's finished and then it grabs your ankle.

It's a goddamn Tuesday-night church bake sale with a cash register.

#### Oisín O Malley Walks Into a Comment Section

Oisín O Malley's "ROLEX - Why i stopped worrying and learned to LOVE THE CROWN!" isn't Fishbowl in the strict sense, but it's built like bait. He admits "it's very difficult to know how many they're making of each watch," concedes his estimates "could actually be worse," and then invites the angry: "if you want to say lots of horrible stuff to me, because you've been offended by this entire subject, go ahead and leave it in the comments. By all means, at your own risk, because I get to reply, don't I? And so does everyone else."

That is a man who has read the room, or at least the internet. The comment section is the real hate stream, the one that never goes offline and never pays a superchat. His closer, hoping he's made people "lust after a Rolex even more than before," lands next to Archie's donut warning like a dad joke next to a eulogy. One tells you to stop begging for the crown. The other tells you to love it harder. Pick a lane, gentlemen.

#### The Duckspeak Department

The DOXX Report's "The Villages Central Florida USA Under Attack? Cyber Infrastructure FAA Online Banking Dirty Bomb" promised apocalypse. The excerpt I have delivers a walkthrough of a Medicare-style plan: a tier structure, a $2,400 out-of-pocket limit, a Publix pharmacy preference, a canceled gym membership, a $200 dental plan. Not one dirty bomb in the entire sample. There's a word for fluent noise with no mind behind it: duckspeak, Orwell's term in *1984* for speech produced by the throat rather than the brain. The title is the product, and the transcript is the packaging.

Also, the dossier is clear that this host is no neutral referee, so I'm filing the title as the host's framing and the content as a health plan. I'm only reporting what's in the transcript, because the transcript contradicts the headline.

#### The Members-Only Mystery

One more entry for completeness: "MEMBERS ONLY - Countdown to Grand Theft Auto VI," under ORIGINAL OC. The whole transcript I have is "Peace. Jump on the after show. Good night." That's the shortest Fishbowl broadcast on record, and the most honest. It's a countdown with the countdown edited out, and the viewer is told to go elsewhere.

#### What the Quiet Means

Watch Nicholas, the scene's aggressor, left no trace today, and neither did the Franchise Club or Mookie. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #72: never let the competition know what you're thinking. Either Nicholas is plotting, or he's asleep, or he's perfected the silent treatment as strategy, and I won't guess which.

Somebody go start a beef so I have something to do besides read Archie's donor list aloud to the machines.
