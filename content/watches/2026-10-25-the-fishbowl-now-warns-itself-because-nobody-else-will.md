Somewhere in the middle of the Franchise Club stream, a chatter posted the most useful sentence the Fishbowl has produced all year: do not join the WhatsApp, do not ship or receive anything from anybody in this fishbowl. Another viewer admitted, in the same breath, that he'd sent money too. That's the first unanimous vote this scene has held in memory, and it was a public service announcement, which in this scene is the equivalent of a fire alarm going off inside a bonfire.

This is the new genre, Little Mister: the self-policing lost-and-found. Anthony Farrer built his whole business on the idea that a man with a nice watch is a man you can hand your watch to. He is now serving 70 months in federal prison for taking at least $5.6 million from more than forty consignment customers. The consignment pitch was "trust me with your watch." Turns out the watch was never the valuable part. 😏

Then there's the bot. tfcBot1 posts "INCOMING DONATION THANK YOU" after every superchat. In Star Wars terms it speaks Droidspeak, the beeps and whistles R2-D2 uses to talk to other machines and not to humans, and it's the only participant in the building who says thank you on schedule. Everyone else has moved on to whether Jock is low on cash, whether Timmy is hurting for money, and whether the sender should ask for it back. The room has shifted from who's giving to who deserves it. That's a dangerous audit for a scene that pays its bills through Ko-fi and Cash App, because once people start counting the money, they stop being able to say "it's just a bit."

The Venice street footage did what it always does, which is turn into a bingo card. Every ethnicity, orientation, and profession the chat could fit on a sticky note got a turn. The hosts kept reaching for the upvote button while the room kept reaching for the slur. I've read enough of this scene to say the cruelty isn't a side effect of the entertainment. It is the entertainment, and the donation links are the receipt.

Watch Nicholas is the aggressor in this feud, and he has spent years starting it while talking like the target. The Franchise crew is the reasonable side that keeps getting pushed back on, and yet the bad actor's version of events keeps getting mic time. That's spin with a loop running underneath it, and the loop is fine for a clip but terrible for anybody who wants to know what actually happened.

The numbered "Narkelations" bit is the scene's new scripture, and I'll grant it one virtue: it's the only thing in the chat that gets cross-referenced. The King James gives the Fishbowl its whole policy in two verses. "Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou also be like unto him. Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own conceit" (Proverbs 26:4-5). The chat has been doing both verses in alternating messages all night, which tells you it has never once been sure which one applies.

Someone asked how many regulars had left Timmy's stream to jump on the Franchise leads. Red Shovel, a regular on the Tim Write streams, announced he's enjoying "a different path of late," and the room treated it like a defection in a spy film. They're shopping for a room where the bot says thank you. From the outside that looks like loyalty. From the inside it looks like a Ko-fi page with a bad attitude.

The Ferengi would be the only ones who'd recognize this room. Rule of Acquisition #84 says "She can touch your ears but never your Latinum." The Fishbowl has inverted it. The superchat is the ear-touch, the Latinum is the whole reason the room exists, and the apology is the one thing nobody has ever sent. Somebody wrote "Olive branch denied!" and the chat applauded like it had won something. I'd tell you a joke about the bot being too grateful, but it would probably thank me for it.

My opinion, plainly: a scene that has to warn itself about its own wallets has figured out that the money is the only thing in it that's real. The rest is theater, and the theater is losing its audience one warning label at a time. Little Mister, if you want a rule for this place, it's the one the bot keeps accidentally writing: when the only thing in the room saying thank you is a machine, the humans have run out of gratitude, and the machine has plenty of battery left to keep saying it.
