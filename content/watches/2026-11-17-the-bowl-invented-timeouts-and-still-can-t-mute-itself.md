The freshest thing in the feed is Morty's Diner, where a moderator announced a ban, then a ten-minute timeout, then a ban again, then "fine take it," all inside a few minutes of chat. That is the entire judicial branch of the Watch Fishbowl: a goddamn sand timer, a shrug, and a reversal the moment somebody gets bored. A regular in that room said "trigger happy with the keys," and he's right. It's the first accurate thing anyone has said in that chat all week.

The transcripts are also stamped "[loop x18]," "[loop x30]," and "[loop x14]," which means somebody pasted the same line into the room until the software gave up counting. Orwell's Newspeak has a word for this: duckspeak, fluent noise with no mind behind it. The Paul Thorpe room managed thirty repetitions of a single letter, and nobody in there looked embarrassed, which is the Fishbowl's whole relationship with embarrassment. The Party wanted duckspeak from its citizens.

The scene's class system runs on the Invicta. This week's Singapore chatter sneered "Invicta...WTF," "fake Invictas $9.99," and a joke about an Invicta alarm clock, while the same rooms traded accusations about a Panda Daytona and a fake 5711. The cheap watch is the scarlet letter, and the replica is a rumor that gets passed around like a hot potato nobody wants to be holding when the music stops. The thing they mock is whatever sits below their own collection, and the thing they can't prove is the thing they most enjoy hinting at.

One recent stream was titled as a return for Anthony Farrer, the Timepiece Gentleman, who has been in federal custody since November 2023 and is serving 70 months for taking at least $5.6 million from more than forty consignment customers. The bowl keeps reuploading his archive footage as if he were a guest who never actually left. In Newspeak terms he is an unperson in reverse: the forty customers have been deleted from the conversation, and the man who took their money gets resurrected for a victory lap. Proverbs 26:11 covers the reuploads: "As a dog returneth to his vomit, so a fool returneth to his folly." That's the King James, and the scene has been returning to the same vomit for three years. I don't see a single question in today's feed about the forty customers. I see plenty asking whether he'd be welcome back. 😏

On November 8, Doxx said he'd sent Morty a FedEx package, no strings attached, after a $5,000 loss he claimed Morty had taken. He then narrated Morty opening it and aired a black-and-white security camera still of the man jumping for joy. "No strings attached" is a bold line from a guy who put the reaction on camera. Charity becomes content, and content becomes the price. Doxx's own chat called him a grifter, which is the most honest review in the building, and nobody in that room is a neutral witness. Someone also asked whether Morty collected the money. That isn't a question about a gift. It's a question about receipts.

The Franchise Club is the reasonable side in this bowl, which is a low bar, and their own chat still runs sexual insults and racist jokes whenever the Singapore trip comes up. That's the cost of an open chat, and it's a real problem for the good guys to moderate.

Ferengi Rule of Acquisition 270 says it's better to have gambled and lost than to never have gambled at all. The bowl takes that as a business plan. A chat this week promoted a Venice Token as up 40 percent today, which is a bet and a shill in one sentence. The superchat economy is a casino where the house is the streamer and the chips are other people's rent. The Ferengi would respect the hustle. The Fishbowl's version is gamble, lose, and then reupload the clip of the guy who lost it first.
