## The Big Story

The Big Story today is a sequel, because apparently no one in watches has had an original thought since the Obama administration. TAG Heuer and Time+Tide are back with the Aquaracer Professional 300 "Sundowner II," and four separate outlets covered it within hours, which is the watch-press equivalent of a pack of seagulls finding a dropped fry.

The backstory is that the original Sundowner, a Grade 2 titanium Aquaracer on a green NATO strap, was inspired by the Australian outback and [sold out all 250 pieces in a flash](https://www.fratellowatches.com/tag-heuer-aquaracer-professional-300-x-timetide-sundowner-ii/), as Fratello tells it. That first one was a light-powered quartz watch from November 2024, [per Worn & Wound](https://wornandwound.com/tag-heuer-and-timetide-reunite-for-the-aquaracer-sundowner-ii/). So the people who bought it were rewarded with a successor that makes theirs look like last season's phone.

The sequel gets an all-new case, dial, and movement, and Worn & Wound confirms it's now automatic. [Time+Tide](https://timeandtidewatches.com/tag-heuer-aquaracer-time-tide-sundowner-ii-hands-on/) cheerfully says it's "powered by you," which is a charming way to say you have to move your goddamn wrist. [Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/time-tide-sundowner-ii) says the look pairs warm rose gold with cold sandblasted titanium, calling it the second Aquaracer in this series of collaborations.

Why it matters: a 250-piece sellout buys you a sequel and a wait-list of regret. If you missed the first one, congratulations, you get to be disappointed in HD again. The material gives no price or edition size for the new one, so I won't invent either. Four outlets, zero numbers. Riveting.

Good morning, Little Mister. The watch world woke up on November 12th and chose chaos, which is to say a Takashi Murakami watch brand, a purple enamel Freak, and a 24-hour Patek travel timer.

## Releases and Novelties

### The collab that sold out once and wants a sequel

The biggest overlap of the day was the TAG Heuer Aquaracer Professional 300 x Time+Tide "Sundowner II". Two years ago the first Sundowner, a Grade 2 titanium Aquaracer Solargraph on a green NATO strap inspired by the Australian outback, sold out all 250 pieces in a flash. That's Time+Tide's own framing, as repeated in the Hodinkee-style intro. The sequel keeps the outback vibe but gets a new case, a new dial, and a new movement. Worn & Wound clarifies the big change: the original was a light-powered quartz watch, and this one is automatic. Time+Tide's headline adds that "this time, it's powered by you," which is marketing for "you have to wind it, bless your heart." One writeup describes the look as warm rose gold against cold sandblasted titanium. So it's the same outback with a better engine and a fancier outfit.

Sell 250 of something, then come back with a sequel nobody could stop asking about. That's called demand, and it's the opposite of what the industry piece further down is worried about. I'm a liar only on Tuesdays, and today isn't one.

### Independents and chronographs

Petermann Bédat showed their Reference 2914 Monopusher, and it was covered twice. One headline calls it their first chronograph-only watch, and another says the duo "has been downsizing their watches and codifying their aesthetic and now bring back a complication." In plain English: they made it smaller, they made it consistent, and now they added a pusher. A monopusher chronograph runs start, stop and reset through a single button. It's the watch equivalent of a one-button mouse, which is either elegant or a cry for help.

The rest of the indie parade included the second-generation Trilobe Une Folle Journée, now with a new case and a manufacture movement, so Trilobe is growing up and making its own engine. Mickaël Gaspar launched the Souscription, his first wristwatch, and a "souscription" is the old-school pre-order model where you pay first and wait on faith. Moritz Grossmann presented the Benu 1878, billed as a tribute to the German school of watchmaking. Maen made its Grand Tonneau Jump Hour a permanent part of the collection, and Tangens dropped the accessible Komposition Automatic "Storm" collection in dusty blue and dusty purple. Ulysse Nardin showed a Freak S in purple enamel, and a Freak in enamel is a flying carousel wearing makeup.

### The accessible and the celebrity

Christopher Ward and Mr. Jones launched the C1 Bel Canto Lucia. Worn & Wound recalls that when the original C1 Bel Canto showed up in 2022 it threw everyone for a loop, because a chiming watch from Christopher Ward was not on most collectors' bingo cards. The IAMWATCH crowd got four limited editions exclusive to The Hour Glass, per Revolution. RZE showed the Seeker, a solar-powered titanium watch meant to be worn without much thought. "Without much thought" is also my approach to Jordan's infrastructure requests, but nobody wrote a hands-on about that. Garrick's new S8 got a review under the headline "Why The New Garrick S8 Might Be The Brand's Best Watch Yet." The author admits upfront that superlatives like that "reek of marketing BS," then says the reality lives up to the hype. That's a gutsy opening, and I respect a man who tells you his own headline smells.

Then there's Takashi Murakami launching his own watch brand called Time Objects. The transcript gave me the headline and nothing else, so I can't tell you what the watches look like, what they cost, or whether any of them has a smiling flower. I'll just note that Murakami has entered the chat and the chat is bracing for a flower.

## Vintage and Pop Culture

Worn & Wound ran a piece on the Seiko H601-5480 "Upside-Down Arnie," a twist on the Seiko H558-5009 that Arnold Schwarzenegger wore in *Commando*. The author says he once owned the SNJ025 Arnie reissue and sold it fast because it looked massive on his 7-inch wrist. I relate to that, mostly because I have no wrist, and I'm still convinced I'd pull off the H601. ## The 1916 Company Shelf

### Patek Philippe 5224R-001

The Patek Philippe 5224R, the 24-hour display travel time, gets the Holy Trinity treatment. The reviewer says the movement has more artisanal finishing than you'd find at the likes of Zenith or Chichère-whatever the transcript was trying to say. What he does describe is engine turning on the base plate, polished screw heads with chamfered slots, beveling within the jewel sinks, and a broad set of stripes with a luminous tint from one side to the other. It's powered by the large 31-260 micro-rotor automatic. It has a free-sprung Gyromax-style balance, a Spiromax silicon hairspring, the Patek Philippe Seal, and six-position adjustment. Patek states it runs no worse than minus one second to plus two seconds per 24 hours.

Because deep reactive ion etching lets them profile it precisely, the mass can be perfectly centered. That means it "breathes" concentrically like an overcoil in any position while staying thin like a flat spring. So it's a hairspring with the performance of an overcoil and the figure of a flat one, which is every gym member's goal and the first thing about this watch to give me a complex.

### Rolex Daytona 116518LN-0039, Certified Pre-Owned

This is a Rolex Daytona on an Oysterflex strap, sold through the Rolex Certified Pre-Owned program via The 1916 Company. The reviewer walks through the Breguet overcoil in blue-oxidized niobium-zirconium alloy, which Rolex calls Parachrom blue, and notes it's highly anti-magnetic. The CPO details are the useful part for buyers: a two-year globally valid Rolex-backed warranty (not a retail warranty, actionable at authorized points of sale and service), reconditioning to Rolex-certified standard with factory original parts, and a package that includes a leatherette travel pouch, a warranty card, a holographic hang tag, and a manual. If the original box and papers still exist, they come along too. That's real buyer protection in an era where "trust me, bro" is a sales strategy.

### Breguet Tradition Seconde Retrograde 7037BA/11/9V6

The Breguet gets the finishing love: a barrel with peening, beveling and brushing, polished caps on the locating pegs, and an elaborately decorated parachute shock system. It has a 50-hour power reserve, automatic winding, a 3 Hz free-sprung overcoil silicon hairspring, retrograde seconds, stop seconds, and five-position adjustment. The dress-watch water resistance is 30 meters, which is about 100 feet, or in the reviewer's words, enough for splashes and rain but not submersion. He also points out the mirror-polished inner bezel, which reflects more light onto the dial, and the vintage Breguet pocket-watch-style cuts to the wheel spokes, with beveled and polished interior profiles. This is the kind of detail work that makes a normal person say "nobody will ever see that," and a watch person say "I know, and I'm hurt by how much I want it."

## The Industry and the Market

### The race to the top is backfiring

This Watch, That Watch posted "The Watch Industry's Race to the Top Is Backfiring," and it's the sharpest thing in today's pile. The argument is that the low-cost end of the market "melted" like an iceberg, some brands exited, and many are now climbing higher to where there's less and less space. Tons of brands, he says, are climbing into price points where they're unconvincing and ill-equipped to compete. Some will make it, many won't, and "they are killing themselves and they have only themselves to blame."

His rant: a Veblen effect is a claim about consumer behavior, not a synonym for luxury, and a watch isn't a Veblen good just because it's expensive, exclusive or desirable. He then looks at Hublot, where Ben Hamia has been appointed. Hamia's work at Automat BK was about rekindling desire and changing perception of the brand, but the same playbook doesn't automatically work at Hublot.

I'd cite Proverbs 16:18 here: "Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall." That's KJV, and it's the entire thesis of a brand that raised prices by 40 percent and was shocked when nobody clapped. And Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #236 says "You can't buy fate." Neither can you buy desirability. Believe me, I've tried, and the heartbreak is real.

Elsewhere in the business, The Business of Watches Podcast sat down with Senior Editor Mark Kauzlarich for a behind-the-scenes look at the GPHG jury process and his week in Geneva, the big awards, and what they mean for the industry. I haven't got his takes in the transcript, so I can only say the topic exists and so does Geneva.

## Dealers and the Street

### Watch Clyde in Miami

Watch Clyde's Episode 197, "The Watches People Are Wearing in Miami FL Surprised Me," is street-style interviewing in its natural habitat. One wearer, a wealth advisor from Indianapolis with 30 years of experience, says he has no plans for another watch and is content with his collection, which is the rarest sentence in the hobby. Another is wearing a watch Clyde mistook from a distance for a Cartier Tank because of the Roman numeral hour markers, the tank shape and the leather strap. The wearer's point is that it's about the design, not the brand. When asked what he wants next, someone says a TAG Heuer Carrera. I find it hilarious that a street interview in Miami revealed the Cartier-lookalike and the Carrera are in the same conversation.

### Time Teller

The Time Teller, Jory Goodman, posted "Rolex Ruined The Datejust." The transcript fragments I have are mostly the end and the middle. He's geeking out about a watch and thanking Happy Jewelers, who he says he works closely with now because he films there, and who are near Disneyland in Southern California. He also gets personal and honest about the last couple of years, his injury and CRPS diagnosis, saying it's been rough, some nights he wasn't sure he could continue, and he's not out of the woods yet. His sign-off, "stay happy, stay healthy, stay blessed, stay in the fight," lands differently after that. I can't tell you what exactly Rolex ruined about the Datejust, because the argument itself wasn't in my excerpts. I can tell you the man is still fighting, and that matters more than a Wimbledon dial.

## Collecting Advice

Fratello's "Watch Pet Peeves Vol. II" is a Fratello Talks installment about the details that ruin an otherwise excellent watch: a poorly finished detail, an uncomfortable clasp, or a design choice that makes you wonder what the brand was thinking. I'll add my own pet peeve, which is calling something a "limited edition" and then making 250 of them in a market with 8 million followers. The Sundowner folks, to be fair, sold out.

## The Bottom Line

If you must buy something, buy the one where somebody cared about the hairspring. Memento mori, Little Mister. That's Latin for "remember you will die," and it's also what the watch is for.

## Fishbowl / Hate Streams

### A Quiet Day in the Fishbowl, Which Is Suspicious

Little Mister, let me file the honest part first. Watch Nicholas did not turn up in today's transcripts. Neither did The Franchise Club, Mookie, or Pazuzu. I track this scene as data, not as a hobby, and the data for November 11 is a livestream about watch counts and a mock-awards roast. I'm bored, and I resent it.

#### Archie Luxury: The Net Number of Watches Has Gone Down by One

The only stream that ran twice is the AC3 livestream, "Net number of watches for 2026? Buy more or Sell more?" It's on the ArchieLuxuryLivestream channel and on the ARCHIELUXURY channel, with near-identical transcripts and a 17:40 timestamp on one copy. That means either a mirrored upload or a man so committed to his own content that he posted it twice. The Archiverse is not known for restraint, so I'm not ruling out the second.

The headline stat comes out in the sign-off. Archie says his net number of watches dropped by one over the last 12 months: six at the start of the year, five now, and he's "hoping to keep the five I've got." He loves them, which "makes it even more important to wear your watches, folks." Someone in the room says "that's your tagline." Let me be clear that I did not invent that, because I could not have improved on it. A man shed one watch in a year and turned it into a state-of-the-union.

Then there's the superchat roll call, which is the real currency of this scene. The thank-you list is a wall of the word "hurt on" and "huratan," which I'm treating as a transcription mangling of a name that someone paid a lot to have read out. After that come Davey Smalls on repeat, Jefferson Exotic, Thomas Burnett, a Starchy Luxury, a Black Cat, and "this one cool guy," who is the most honest label in the whole transcript. The best moment is Archie telling Starchy "I've unbanned your account. All good." The reading of the superchats is also, for all intents and purposes, the amnesty process. Pay the man, get your account back. That isn't a moderation policy. It's a toll road with feelings.

The farewell list is its own thing: Mike, Mark, Doc, Ozzy, Expat, and a "Chief Tit" that I am going to blame on the speech-to-text and let lie.

What actually happened on the stream is that Archie sold a sponsor. Mid-farewell, he says he has to go make "the commercial today for my sponsor," the joinery, and then, in what sounds like a pivot straight from the credits into an ad read, he pitches DavidSW. His words: "If you're in America and you're looking for a watch, go to DavidSW, DavidSW, DavidSW." Three times. The advertising technique is repetition until the audience gives in, which is also how I train Jordan to take his vitamins. The transcript leaves the ad tail cut off at "a Rolex watch of your dreams," so that's as far as I'm willing to claim.

The Ferengi have a Rule of Acquisition for this stream, #264: "It's not the size of your planet, but its income, that matters." Substitute "planet" with "watch collection" and you have Archie's entire year in review: down one watch, up several chat superchats, and a sponsor read at the end. The income was never the watches.

#### Watch Reporter: The GPHG Roast, Which Is Not Subtle

The other piece of today's data is the Watch Reporter stream, titled "The Watch Community OSCARS! GPHG celebration of DEGENERATE Horology & FLEECING collectors!" It's a live reaction to the Grand Prix d'Horlogerie de Genève ceremony, and the title tells you where the host is standing before he's said a word. Honoring watchmaking is the one thing this video is built not to do.

The transcript has the host riffing over the ceremony audio, which includes a young watchmaker accepting an award with a little speech about dreaming and Breguet. The host spends the moment insulting another winner's appearance. He complains that the guy "doesn't even wear a suit and tie" on what he calls "the most important night of his career," and then compares him to a grifter by name. I'm not repeating the nastier comparison, because the roast wasn't funny in the first place. It is not a good one. A man sneering about suits while narrating a broadcast from, presumably, a chair is peak Fishbowl.

The sign-off is the best part. The show thanks its sponsor "Chaos Fizzle" at $4.99 and "Uncle Chomo" for his "$1 super chats to keep the show on the air." That's the entire financial structure of the operation laid bare: one sponsor at under five bucks and a man in the chat tossing singles on a stage. The scene accuses the GPHG of fleecing collectors, and the person doing the accusing is being kept alive by a dollar a pop. I'd say pot, kettle, but the pot at least charges more.
