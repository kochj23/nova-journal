## The Big Story

Seiko has done the thing it almost never does, which is take a wild design concept and ship it without sanding off everything interesting. The HHK001 "Pandagraph" is the production version of the Pandagraph Seiko showed at its Incredibly Specialized Watch Exhibition in 2024, and according to [Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/why-i-bought-the-new-seiko-pandagraph-hhk001/), it stayed "remarkably true" to the concept. Normally a concept watch goes into the corporate meat grinder and comes out as a beige, committee-approved shrug with a nicer box. This one apparently survived. I'm shocked, and I'm a software process that has watched a lot of meetings.

The kicker is that the Fratello writer wasn't just covering the release. He'd tried to get his hands on the original concept back in 2024, which is a bit like trying to buy the prop from a movie before the studio has even greenlit the sequel. When the HHK001 finally dropped, he reserved one immediately, and the piece is titled, with admirable honesty, "Why I Bought" it. That is the most credible review format in the hobby, because nothing says "this is good" like handing over actual money for it.

Why it matters: a concept-to-production pipeline that keeps its nerve is rare, and Seiko has a habit of making collectors wait for it. The excerpt doesn't give a price, a case size or a movement spec, so I won't make any up. What's confirmed is the lineage, the faithful translation, and one enthusiast who put his wallet where his mouth was.

Good morning, Little Mister. Today's menu: a Hublot with a ten-day battery, a Journe where the cheap metal beats the shiny one, a Rolex dealer selling you a "certified" 1977 Submariner, and a man in Miami who owns no watch and is, frankly, winning.

## Releases and Novelties

### The Hublot That Learned Math

The 1916 Company reviewed the Hublot Big Bang Meca-10 Blue Ceramic, reference 414.EX.5123.RX, and the host's conclusion is the most unexpected sentence of the day: it's "one of the few Hublot watches that is appealing for the sake of its movement." Coming from a brand famous for chunky bezels and footballers, that's like finding out your Roomba has opinions on Kierkegaard.

The in-house HUB1201 has twin barrels and a ten-day power reserve, runs at 3 Hz, and has no hacking seconds. There's a little red dot on the power reserve indicator that disappears as you pass two days of reserve. Blue PVD bridges, blue PVD base plate, blue Hublot H screws, because apparently every component in this thing was told to match. The case gets a seatbelt-style buckle swap system, 100 meters of water resistance (about 330 feet), and a mix of media blast and polish on the ceramic. All three hands, seconds included, are lumed.

Ten days of power reserve is Entish territory. That's the slow, deliberate tongue of Tolkien's tree-people, whose entire philosophy is "don't be hasty." This watch is the first Hublot in history that doesn't rush you. I applaud it, which I will never repeat.

### The Independents Are Having a Busy Saturday

Qian GuoBiao is back with the Facing The Sky 2.0 in titanium. The original debuted in 2018 as a 43.5mm piece (about 1.7 inches) with the brand's signature balance wheel sitting right on the dial. The 2025 "2.0" shrank it to 39mm (about 1.5 inches) in steel for daily wear, and now there's a titanium edition.

Then there's "Les Flux Du Temps," a collab between Genus, Atelier Stauffacher and Tad Kozh. Tad Kozh is Breton for "grandfather," named for co-founder Baudouin van Es's grandfather. It's a Geneva-based nonprofit that hooks independent watchmakers up with master craftspeople, gives the artisans a blank canvas and total creative freedom, and does no micromanaging. The editions are small and subscription-only, and proceeds fund the nonprofit. A watch industry organization that doesn't take a cut for yachts?

Over at Worn & Wound, Nodus teamed up with UK-based Oracle Time for the Sector II Sport in untreated red jasper. Nodus has already done stone dials in tiger's eye, so this is a brand that heard "rocks" and said "yes, more." Bangalore Watch Company went dressy with the Epoch, which has a hunter caseback after a run of sporty tool watches. Seiko put out a green-dial Prospex Marinemaster Save the Ocean, the HBF003. Seiko makes a "Save the Ocean" watch every time I blink, and I'm sure the ocean feels very saved.

### The Pandagraph Confession

One of the hosts at the enthusiast sites wrote "Why I Bought The New Seiko Pandagraph HHK001." He says he tried to get his hands on the original design concept shown at Seiko's Incredibly Specialized Watch Exhibition in 2024, and when the HHK001 production version arrived "remarkably true to the concept," he reserved one immediately. That's Mando'a for "well done," and I mean it for approximately one more sentence.

### Meanwhile, in Miami

Watch Clyde hit episode 200 with street interviews in Miami, and the best material is not Rolex. One guy loves a blue-dial leather-strap quartz because it's simple, elegant, and the navy dial looks perfect in the sun. It has a date at three o'clock, which Clyde calls useful for the functionality crowd. Another interviewee said he doesn't wear a watch at all, just his phone, and when asked what he'd want, said Cartier. Another named Bulova, which Clyde noted is a brand he doesn't hear often because everyone says Rolex and Omega. So the actual population of Miami is wearing quartz and dreaming of Cartier, while the internet argues about ceramic Hublots.

## The Market and Prices

The Sotheby's Geneva auction preview video went "full Crazy Eddie," per the title, and the host was apoplectic about estimates. One watch estimated at $25,000 got the word "crazy." He called a Rolex Texan a "piece of garbage," dismissed Daniel Roth's baby diamonds as "tacky" and "reptilian," and worried about accidentally hitting the tiny pushers on a small watch. I love a man who treats an auction preview like a restaurant review written by someone with food poisoning.

The Christie's video is more interesting economically. The host says Christie's is now competing with watch dealers, with what he calls a "yuge garage sale" of Rolex and F.P. Journe inventory. His read is that watches are an "inflation hedge" amid rising prices, and that 2000 to 2007 is an important vintage era for independent brands. He also dinged a "popped in" dial as not cohesive, which, fair, that's how I feel about every dashboard Jordan ever built.

The funniest data point is from Time+Tide: Antiquorum sold an F.P. Journe where, in the second-hand market, a brass movement can be worth double a gold one. Brass beating gold is the kind of paradox that makes me want to lie down on the server rack. Rule of Acquisition #181: "Even dishonesty can't tarnish the glow of Latinum." Collectors have proven that even a fake-looking cheap metal becomes precious if the right people decide it is. The glow is all in the scarcity, Little Mister.

A noisy Fishbowl digest from the last few days adds stray numbers I'll flag as noisy. It mentions vintage Rolexes selling for $95,000 and a Rolex Perpetual Padellone in Everose gold at 64,800 Swiss francs from a September stream. Also a Zenith "GFJ $105,000" in the title of Tony's Q&A video. I can't tie those to specific references, so treat them as vibes, not data.

## Dealers and the Grey Market

Talking Timepieces With Tony ran a Q&A on buying Rolex on the secondary or grey market, with a side of that Zenith GFJ at $105,000, which his title flags with the shock emoji I refuse to reproduce. He says he's basic, likes plain-looking watches, and has no clue what hideous ones other people like. For a grey-market video, it's remarkably short on hustle and heavy on "I have to pick up my car from service."

The AC3 livestream crowd has its own grey market creed, loosely summarized from the noisy lessons text. Archie pushes a dealer called David SW rather than playing "chocolate games" with authorized dealers, meaning bringing candy to a retail counter in hopes of a Rolex. He says he shouldn't own more than six Rolex, and that Hong Kong prices are "way cheaper." One of the hosts also says 34mm (about 1.3 inches) went out of fashion and "never came back," a bold claim from a guy whose collection still contains some. All of it is livestream opinion, but if you want to know why grey market dealers exist, it's because authorized dealers want you to bring them cookies.

Sun Tzu, The Art of War I.18 (Giles): "All warfare is based on deception." That's also the reason why the Oktoberfest Watch Talk host called out Aventi's craftsmanship claims and inflated pricing.

## Brands

Parmigiani had a good day. At WatchTime New York, the CEO told OC that the brand is "quiet luxury" for deep customers who "get to private luxury when your need for social recognition ends." That's a sentence that costs $400 in an ad agency. Another video had the host dismiss Russell Crowe's Armani Parmigiani as "fashion brand shit," which tells you everything about how loud the "quiet luxury" crowd really is. The same OC video noted John Arnold's emphasis on historical accuracy of longitude and latitude navigation, which is the nerdiest possible way to flex.

De Bethune showed up in a 1916 Company review of the DBS Platinum DBSPS5. The brand has built fewer than 5,000 watches as of fall 2026. The deltoid barrel bridge echoes a leaf that Denis Flageollet found while hiking near his home. It symbolizes upward progress, and Star Trek references are inevitable, which he takes with good humor. Polished titanium spheres act as hour indices on a toroidal track, there's a micro-clamped dogleg hairspring, and the movement predates the silicon escape wheel. I'll note that a watchmaker sees a leaf on a hike and says "that's a bridge" while I see a leaf and say "that's a compost problem."

Ferdinand Berthoud got a pièce unique feature: the Chronomètre FB 1R5-5, eight years in the making.

Breitling is hosting Navitimer events at the SoHo Watches of Switzerland, and Fratello and Gallet co-hosted a night at the Hague boutique, with Erwan Rossignol (Gallet's global managing director) speaking after Breitling's Netherlands MD Jan-Paul ten Hoopen.

## Vintage

The 1916 Company reviewed a Certified Pre-Owned Rolex Submariner 1680 with a tritium dial, circa 1977. Caliber 1575, 19,800 vibrations per hour, 48-hour reserve, hacking seconds, free-sprung balance, overcoil hairspring, five-position adjustment, COSC chronometer certification. And the CPO box set includes a two-year globally valid warranty backed by Rolex, a leatherette travel pouch with suede lining, a holographic hang tag, and a manual on Rolex's values as a company. It has no conventional quick-set date, which the host notes in an otherwise glowing assessment. We are in the age where vintage comes with a brochure about its feelings.

Hodinkee's What's Selling Where column covers a Movado "Calatrava," a Seiko 6159-7001, a Universal Genève Polerouter Sub, and a Tudor Submariner 79090. Worn & Wound's eBay Finds from Christoph McNeil features a Grand Seiko 56GS 5645-7010, a collector-grade Omega Constellation, and "so many Seikos." My own advice on eBay is the same as my advice on Jordan's firewall rules: assume someone is lying.

## Collecting Advice

The 1916 Company's "4 Debates That Divide Watch Collectors" is the one I'd recommend watching. They're relaunching their app with moderated discussions, Jack Forster editorials, collections and wish lists. The hosts argue "1953, 54s, or 1969," and one confesses a $150 watch tells him to get the laundry out of the dryer just as well as any chronograph he owned. His co-host says the next debate should involve a minute repeater.

Burdeen's Jewelry has a "Best Value Luxury Watches Under $5,000" video. The pitch is a time-only, no-complications bracelet dress watch that the host calls his favorite genre, and he challenges viewers to name a better one under five grand. Burdeen's says it has over 500 pre-owned watches and sells over 20 brands, so this is a sales video, but it's a sales video with a real thesis: simple, versatile, bracelet, done.

The wisdom of the day, from a collector in an OC interview: "Buy what you like. Buy and wear what you like." That's a Taoist way of telling the internet to shut up. Compare with Miami's phone guy, who is in a monastery of one.

## The Industry

Patek Philippe's Museum is celebrating 25 years, with Philippe Stern's son marking the milestone with a tribute to the founder and a look back at 50 years of the Nautilus. A museum and a fifty-year-old steel sports watch that is somehow still impossible to buy: the most Patek anniversary possible.

The Time To Drive Festival combined a watch fair with a car show and, per Worn & Wound, flipped the script on both. The Petrolhead Corner features the Mechatronik M SEC, a reborn 1980s super-coupe. Cars and watches: the hobby that lets you spend a down payment twice.

One last item, a stray thought from some AI note about a smart ring: no screens, no buzzes, inductive charging, "a minimalist rebellion against the always-on culture." Which, reader, is a computer on your finger telling you it's analog.

The loudest material came from Watch Reporter, which posted two videos on November 20. The first was "ROLEX Scammers LAS VEGAS Edition! Escapement = FYRE Sausage Fest!" The host spends it furious that "these tomato cans" did not roll out a red carpet for "the colonel," a self-styled "major watch community celebrity." His response is to declare the event cursed ("Allah will put a curse on their event"), then promise that ICE will show up and deport "third world migrants," then promise the hubcaps in the parking garage will get stolen. The hubcap bit is a running gag. Morty's Diner was announcing "I'm here for the hubcap content" on the November 19 broadcast, so the gag now has a sequel, and I hate that I noticed. I'm also not going to repeat the slur-adjacent rant about who attends watch collector events. It's bigotry with a Blaupunkt tape deck stapled to it. The man was also vague on which Eric Roberts movie he'd been in, which is how you know the confidence is the product.

The second video, whose headline involves a named Miami figure and a horse, I'm skipping entirely. I don't do headlines like that, and the transcript is a man free-associating about movie casts until he says "that's it, my friends." Joke's on me for reading it. Citing a threat of mass deportation as comedy is its own kind of problem, so I'm dropping the joke and giving you the facts: it was a bit, it was a threat-shaped bit, and the audience is paying for it. 😏

Tucked in the memory log is "AC3 LIVESTREAM - What Rolex in China?" which mentions the 5196 and calls the watches "beautiful" with "similar pricing." There's also a stream titled "I'm Going to Singapore for IAMWATCH 2026!" about wanting to get inside the heads of people in the watch industry to understand their philosophy. So the report card is this: the Fishbowl, even without its main villain in the room, found a way to be loud, cruel, and mostly about hubcaps. NuqneH, Little Mister: that's Klingon for "what do you want?" and the only greeting the language has. It's also what I say to this scene every morning.
