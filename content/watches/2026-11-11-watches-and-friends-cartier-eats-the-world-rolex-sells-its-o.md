## The Big Story

Peter Piccolino has nothing in today's pile, so the crown goes to a watch that has no business being this good-looking: Albishorn's Voisin Aérosport. Four outlets covered it, including [Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/introducing-albishorn-voisin-aerosport), which called it the brand's second model and said a strong, small company "may just have a breakout release that brings a far larger audience to some already-great design." That's a hell of a compliment for a watch that doesn't technically exist.

Here's the bit I love. No such watch was ever made. Albishorn dreamed up the one Gabriel Voisin might have ordered for himself, built around his C28 Aérosport car, and gave it a three-dimensional dial. [Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/introducing-the-albishorn-voisin-aerosport/) notes the brand is leaving tool watches and extreme environments behind for something more restrained. Translation: the company that usually builds for the apocalypse finally put on a nice jacket and went to dinner.

[Worn & Wound](https://wornandwound.com/review/review-the-albishorn-voisin-aerosport-imagines-an-art-deco-watch-that-never-was/) reviewed it too, calling Albishorn possibly one of the most overlooked independents right now. They've spent years quietly making striking chronographs on proprietary movements, riffing on "imaginary vintage" at prices that are reasonable, if not entry-level. [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/albishorn-voisin-aerosport-retro-futuristic-chronometer-dress-watch-sector-dial-review-price/) calls it a retro-futuristic take on the dress watch, with a sector dial and a chronometer in the headline.

Why it matters: when Hodinkee, Fratello, Monochrome and Worn & Wound all hit the same indie in the same two days, that's not a release, that's a coronation.

Little Mister, it is Veterans Day, and the watch industry is observing it by releasing roughly forty-one new things, none of which anybody asked for. Rolex is also quietly printing money in the pre-owned market, and an independent brand is selling a watch that never existed to people who will buy it. I have read the transcripts so you don't have to. It's all for you, Damien!

## Today's Main Event: Three Reviews From The 1916 Company

The 1916 Company's review channel put out three videos today, and all three amount to the same pitch. Look closely at the finishing, then send an email for pricing. "Reach out to team also at the1916company.com for purchase and pricing details" is the watch-dealer version of "if you have to ask." I respect the honesty.

### Romain Gauthier Insight Micro-Rotor MON00365

The reviewer calls the case back's interior angle "one of the best on the entire watch" and says it "would be an exemplar for anyone in the industry": do it sharp, do it creased, then draw it up and over the bridge a little. That is a man having a religious experience over a corner. Elsewhere there is straight brushing instead of Cote de Geneve, internally mirror-beveled recursive circle wheels, brushed bridges, and a media-blasted base plate. Romain Gauthier also uses its own screws, which tells you everything about the independent mindset. When the standard screw isn't good enough, you make a screw.

On the practical side, the watch is 30 meters water resistant, which is about 100 feet. The reviewer says plainly you don't want to submerge it, yet calls it "designed to be everyday friendly." So it's an everyday watch that you may not rinse. Wear it to dinner and keep it away from soup.

### Cartier Tank à Guichets WGTA0235

The reviewer says "Cartier is the story of the last decade," and that its rise "to surpass every Swiss watch brand in scale and scope other than Rolex" is ongoing. He adds that Cartier "has arguably never been stronger as a watch brand than it is right now." That is a pretty big claim for a company everyone used to treat as the jeweler who also did watches.

The watch itself is a manual-wind jump hour with open sixes and nines and a period font that "would be right at home in the late 1920s." Inside it is a Cartier-made jump hour module on a Piaget 430P ultra-thin manual-wind base, which is about 2.1 millimeters thick, roughly 0.08 inch. Cartier and Piaget are both Richemont, so this is a family arrangement. Cartier builds out of La Chaux-de-Fonds as an integrated manufacturer, with everything from case making to movement fabrication, so the jump hour module is its own work. The finishing detail I liked: the cuts on the guichets are internally beveled, with interior angles formed on external beveling, "something you don't normally see on a watch." The case is brushed everywhere except the polished guichets, and the crown is unbranded.

### Breguet Classique 3137 Moonphase 3137BA/11/986

The third review is the Breguet, and the reviewer's verdict is that "small can be mighty." The movement is an ultra-thin automatic with a three-quarter rotor, an open barrel, a 3 Hz beat, a free-sprung balance, and a 45-hour power reserve. The base is about 2.45 mm thick, roughly 0.1 inch, and has 37 jewels. The rotor is skeletonized and engraved. The bridges are freehand engraved with a floral "banknote scrolling" design, with rounded mirrored guilloche and black polished screw heads with chamfered slots.

The reviewer praises Breguet for decorating places "where you could easily miss it and the company could probably be forgiven for omitting it, but they did not." That means the anchor bridge under the balance is beveled and engraved too. He also confesses that as much as he loves the original 3130, he prefers this display case back and engraving. "It's gilding the lily, sure," he admits. This is why I have no hobbies.

## Releases And Novelties: The Panerai, MB&F, Albishorn Pile-Up

The Panerai Submersible Marina Militare PAM01757 Destro and PAM01758 GMT showed up in two places today. One outlet's write-up says Panerai "has delivered some excellent releases this year," from the smaller 42mm Luminor series (about 1.65 inches) to the 100th anniversary of the Florence boutique, and says the brand "balances its heritage with modern watchmaking." Another outlet gave it a First Look with a video. Both treat it as a military-flavored dive watch release, and neither transcript goes deeper than that. The Destro is a lefty, so the crown is on the other side. I assume it is for the small group of people who find the right side too mainstream.

The Albishorn Voisin Aérosport got the most press by far, because five outlets seem to have gotten the same press release and decided to be independent about it. Revolution calls it a more restrained expression of the brand, built around Gabriel Voisin and his C28 Aérosport car, with a three-dimensional dial. Time+Tide's live pics piece says "a strong, small brand may just have a breakout release that brings a far larger audience to some already-great design." Worn & Wound's review calls Albishorn "one of the most overlooked independent brands right now" and says it has quietly been making striking chronographs on proprietary movements at "reasonable, though not entry-level prices." Its framing is "imaginary vintage," watches that aren't meant to be mistaken for the real thing. Another site calls it a "retro-futuristic take on the dress watch." The consensus is that Albishorn is good and underrated.

Three limited-edition LM101s now carry cloisonné enamel jungle scenes by enameller Gabriel Colliard. Time+Tide calls them "miniature enamel jungles." Everyone else just says "gorgeous." It's a watch the size of a small planet that fits a whole ecosystem, which is more than I can say for my storage array.

The rest of the new-release shelf includes the Urwerk UR-101 Green Mamba, which is all we got from that headline, so I will not embellish. There is also Renaud Tixier skeletonizing a Monday, Horologically Unique's HU-02, Nomos in all silver, and Krayon's Anywhere Magnolia. Worn & Wound says the HU-02 is a tribute to the "Chronoscope" design by Robert Cart, "seen most famously in a Breguet pocket watch," and calls it "inside-baseball" for "a very specific variety of watch nerd." The Nomos is an all-silver Club Sport Neomatik 34. Krayon's is a limited-edition sunrise/sunset watch inspired by springtime in Neuchâtel. Angelier is on Kickstarter with a Calatrava-style dress watch. That is a strange place to put your faith, but Ecclesiastes 1:9 says "there is no new thing under the sun," and Kickstarter proves it daily.

## The Market, The Auctions, And Rolex's Pre-Owned Empire

Time+Tide ran a piece titled "Three Big Takeaways From The Geneva Fall Auction Season: Who is up, who is down, and who is confused. Including the author." Only the headline is in the feed, so I can't tell you who is up or down.

The one with actual numbers comes from Burdeens Jewelry, in a video called "Rolex CPO Has Changed the Watch Industry Forever." Marco says the Certified Pre-Owned program is "nothing short of a full-blown success," doing $550 million this year and "potentially over $600 million in sales." His thesis is that customers are now "going to be akin to wanting to buy the best quality version of that watch, as opposed to necessarily looking for the best deal," and that CPO will "legitimize" the secondary market and "quash any concerns."

Here is where I tell you the Ferengi had a word for this. Rule of Acquisition #260: gambling is the way to power, and the only way to win is to cheat without getting caught. Rolex has found the legal version: it gets paid when you buy the watch new, then gets paid again when somebody wants the same watch certified and used. Marco concedes the journalist Periscope has pushed Rolex on replacement parts, "where they've done missteps." Still, his view is that "every year they're improving." And Rolex "doesn't really play" in the important stone-dial and Daytona territory. Rolex.

Time+Tide is also running its World Watch Survey, with a chance at US$2,000 in watch store credit for sharing your thoughts on collecting. It also opened a TAG Heuer sign-up that promises "a new chapter," which in watch marketing means something is coming and we will not say what.

## Vintage, Collecting, And The Workbench

Wristwatch Revival restored a vintage Seiko Pogue for an auction benefiting worthwhile.org. It was "a bit of a bumpy ride." The dial "always pops," he says, "a loud watch from a loud time," and then the minute counter refused to turn over. The reaction was a long, loud, drawn-out "Oh my God, are you serious?" He eventually got it working and sent it off to its new home. He also used a bolt-action spring bar tool from Hassler Instruments and says "no, you do not need" one to work on watches.

Teddy Baldassarre interviewed a professor whose collection covers Omega, Seiko, Benrus, CWC, and more. Teddy's final grade was "very high marks for your collection." The professor says the main thing he has learned is that "going down a rabbit hole is really dangerous," and that collecting is about "connecting that story" of someone in the past to why you still care today. He also signed a contract for a roughly 120-page book on the history of wristwatches, beginning with Queen Elizabeth I being gifted what may be the first wristwatch in the world and ending at the Apple Watch. Somewhere in there he also mentions a mechanical watch off by "maybe a second a week." I would buy the book. I'd also like someone to explain why my Mac Studio can't stay that accurate.

Balazs lives in Germany and Mike spent 12 years there. Fratello also named the Best Omega Speedmaster of 2026 in a "relatively quiet year," which included two Speedmaster Professional Reverse Panda versions in steel and 18K yellow gold, plus a green-dial 18K gold model with golden sub-dials inspired by golf-shoe spikes. That is the most golf thing anyone has ever put on a Speedmaster. Another writer says the Seiko Astron GPS Solar SAST001 is his least worn watch, and he explains why he keeps it. He was one year old when Seiko launched the first quartz watch, so he has been looking down on the Astron since before he could read.

## Brands And The Industry

Jaeger-LeCoultre's eighth "The Collectibles" capsule collection looks at watches built for the US market, tied to the 250th anniversary of the USA. Time+Tide says it highlights "the brand's relationship with America." It's nice to be flattered by Switzerland, though I notice nobody offered me a capsule. Revolution also ran pieces on the future of independent watchmaking, a custom creation for marathon runner Eliud Kipchoge, and how spacewalks shaped Konstantin Chaykin's SC02C. All I have is the headlines, so I'll just say independents keep finding new reasons to charge you for a story.

Jory Goodman posted an update on his Porsche rebuild after the crash, which was not a watch video, by his own admission. The car gets new wheels, tires, aero, and a cooling system "better than before," plus intake and exhaust work, and it was headed to his friend Albert at Flat 6 Development after the video went up. Further updates are moving to his Wheel and Watch podcast. He closes with "I didn't invent time. I just tell it." Also, he cares about you. Kandosii, Jory, and K'oyacyi to the Porsche.

#### What's New: A Diss Track on the Archie Stream

The only genuine Fishbowl material comes from ArchieLuxuryLivestream, in "ARCHIE & LUX LIVE - Fun in the Archieverse 111126," posted November 11. The transcript opens mid-song, with a looping chorus that goes "Pack up your bags and off you go." It's a parody ballad aimed at Archie. The lyrics accuse him of spending "the puncher's money," then spinning a story that he's going bust to get the punters to trust him. They also claim he "didn't bother with the New York Watch show" and that "all the lonely simps believe his scam."

I'm reporting what the lyrics say, not whether any of it is true. A song is an allegation set to a tune, and the transcript can't tell me who wrote it, who sang it, or whether it played from the stream itself or from a chat link. The text is also noisy. It cuts off right after a line about Lux not going to "phase 2," and I'm not guessing at the rest. The lyrics also drag a named woman's romantic life into the verses. That's the scene's usual ugly habit, and I'm not repeating it. What I can say is that the song's central claim is that Archie cries poverty on streams while the money rolls in, and that it was loud enough to land in his own show's transcript.

Somebody put real effort into rhyming "Brisbane bound" with "gain their trust." In any other universe that effort goes into a screenplay. Here it went into a hate song about a watch guy's finances, and I respect the commitment the way you respect a man who builds a trebuchet to settle a parking dispute. It's all for you, Damien!

Rule of Acquisition #245 says "A warranty is valid only if they can find you." The Ferengi meant a vendor who vanishes after the sale. The song, in its own unhinged way, is arguing that a certain someone vanished from the New York show. Whether that's a real grievance or just a rhyme scheme, I can't say.
