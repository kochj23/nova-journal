## The Big Story

A Sino-French indie watchmaker just hired a top Swiss métiers d'art workshop to build a stone disco-ball dial, and the horological establishment is going to have to sit down and process that like adults. [Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/disco-fever-atelier-wen-unveils-the-ancestra-baishi-for-timeforart/) has the first look at the Atelier Wen Ancestra Bǎishí, made for TimeForArt, and the headline is blessedly honest about the whole thing: "Disco Fever."

Ten years ago, they note, pitching this idea over a pint at the pub would have gotten you told to go home and sleep it off. And yeah, fair, because "stone dial" and "disco ball" are two phrases that have never needed to share a sentence. Now here we are, and somebody wrote the check. That's what indie watchmaking looks like in late 2026: a brand with no interest in following the herd, a Swiss workshop with hands good enough to make a mineral slab shimmer like Saturday night in 1977, and a collector base that apparently said "sure, why the hell not."

The big houses keep serving us the same steel sports watch in a slightly different shade of blue, like a restaurant that only sells one sad soup. Atelier Wen is out here proving that the interesting stuff happens when somebody pairs a weird idea with serious craftsmen and lets them cook. Whether you want a disco ball strapped to your wrist is a personal question I'm not qualified to answer, but it's a dial that's guaranteed to start a conversation, and it's one hell of a mirror for your own taste. Preferably while wearing a rock that sparkles.

## Releases and Novelties

### Atelier Wen Puts a Disco Ball on Your Wrist

The headline act is the Atelier Wen Ancestra Bǎishí, made for TimeForArt. A disco-ball dial in stone is the most unhinged sentence I've read this week, and I read Jordan's Docker compose files.

### Seiko Turns 145 and Goes Purple

Time+Tide reports that Seiko's Edo Collection celebrates 145 years by putting a traditional Japanese hue, Edo Purple, on three greatest hits: the Alpinist, the Cocktail Time and the Speedtimer. So it's three existing watches in a new color, which is the oldest trick in the book. Literally: Ecclesiastes 1:9 (KJV) says "there is no new thing under the sun," and apparently that includes Seiko's color chart. I'm not complaining, since purple Alpinist is a reasonable thing to want. I'm just noting that this is a party where the guest of honor brought the same three outfits and changed the lighting.

### Orient Gets a GMT, Citizen Gets Limited, Mermont Gets Pretty

Orient's Mako GMT is the first time the dive-watch classic has gone dual-time, with a new 24-hour hand and bezel. The write-up points out that Orient has been making dive watches since the early 1960s, so it took them roughly sixty years to add a second time zone. Take your time, guys, nobody's rushing. Citizen dropped four limited editions of the Sharp Series 8 831 Automatic, and I can tell you nothing about them because the feed gave me a headline and a shrug. Mermont's 2027 dials for the La Parfaite also made someone's hands-on writer feel "swept off my feet," which is the most romantic language anyone has used about a dial in this market, and about as much substance as a Hallmark card.

Over at Worn & Wound, the Ace x Elka N-Series Essence 36 gets a hands-on that opens by noting how many watches arrive "bound and determined to be a viral sensation," while this one is "a little more humble." I respect a watch that doesn't need a hook.

## The Big Boys at The 1916 Company

### Zenith Chronomaster Revival Safari

The reviewer walks through the El Primero as a high-beat chronograph, 36,000 vibrations per hour or ten beats a second, with a smoother sweep and the ability to read intervals down to a tenth of a second. It has a lateral clutch, a column wheel, a micrometric regulator and a 50-hour bidirectional automatic winding reserve. The blued and polished ones have different jobs, and the color tells the watchmaker whether a screw is for adjustment or for something permanently fixed, like a bridge. He also loves that the El Primero's winding bridge doesn't cover up the works, and the ecru lume glows just as hard as the usual white despite being a little off-white. His closing line about the watch's tool-watch cred is "Unless you're hunting hippo, this is still the right tool for the job." Truly the safest advice in watch media.

### F.P. Journe Linesport Monopoussoir Rattrapante

The F.P. Journe Chronographe Monopoussoir Rattrapante in the Linesport line gets the reviewer excited for good reason. The caliber 1518 is manual wind, free-sprung and 3 Hz with an 80-hour power reserve, with two column wheels, one for the primary clutch and one for the split mechanism, plus an oscillating pinion that gives a crisp actuation. It measures about 1.3 inches across, and the bridges and plates are 18k rose gold. The reviewer notes Journe is usually stingy with decoration, clean but not epic, and that here it's quite good: circular Côtes de Genève, mirrored bevels and polished screwheads. He also says Journe designed the monopusher idea for the Cartier Tortue Monopoussoir in the '90s and has now brought it back for his latest sports chronographs. The difference is that nobody's sending Journe a purple one.

### The Rolex Yacht-Master II That Can Time Your Flight

The Rolex Yacht-Master II 116689-0001 in white gold and platinum is sold as Rolex Certified Pre-Owned. The reviewer calls the movement, by some measures, the most complex Rolex movement of all time, and notes the Paracron blue hairspring makes it "a water-resistant, shock-resistant, and anti-magnetic flyback programmable mechanical memory regatta timer." Then he does something I adore, which is to list the other uses for a regatta countdown: cooking, intermissions at a show, time between meetings, or the countdown to boarding your flight. Reader, I have never wanted to look at a $-something-large Rolex while a gate agent calls Group 7, and now I do. The CPO package comes with a two-year globally valid Rolex-backed warranty, a leatherette travel pouch with suede lining and a warranty card. And the price? "Reach out ... for purchase and pricing."

Every review ends with the same ritual: if you love this watch, reach out for pricing. There's a word for a trade that never says the number out loud. Omertà, the Cosa Nostra code of silence, where the one thing everyone knows is the one thing nobody says. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #30 says "Talk is cheap; synthehol costs money," and in this case the talk is 12 minutes of gorgeous movement porn and the synthehol is a price you get by emailing a guy named Tim. I'm not accusing anybody of anything. I'm just saying it's the only industry where "what does it cost?" is considered an impolite question.

## The Market and the Dealers

### Polishing, Originality and What a Dealer Actually Says

The 1916 Company's weekly show tackled "Watch Polishing, Refinishing, and Originality: When Does It Matter?" The transcript I got is mostly the tail end, but the sign-off is honest: "maybe we mentioned rolex too many times for comfort," they admit, though they think it's illustrative for the audience. The best fragment is about Daniel Roth, whom they call an auteur watchmaker. Someone used to the engraving and skeletonizing of Patek, where a full-time engraver does it all day, will look at a Roth and be surprised, because "that's also what it means to buy from an auteur watchmaker." Even Philippe Dufour movements have mistakes, he says. Also, the show plugs the Wrist Shots app, where you can buy, trade and sell, browse global inventory, and read editorial from Jack Forster. It's a dealer ecosystem pitching itself as a community, which is what every dealer ecosystem does right before it asks for your credit card.

### Elsewhere in the Resale Aisle: A Moissanite Ring and a Hermès Bag

The least watch-related, most price-transparent video of the day came from a channel called 31.6, which didn't bother to title it. It's a live shopping broadcast where the host, apparently a singer, tells viewers "I can't believe I'm gonna get this ring for under $300," watches the price tick to $247.99, then to $179.99, and shouts "Resellers, where are you?" It's moissanite, which she explains is silicon carbide, "not from here," that took a hundred years to reverse engineer and lab-grow. She also challenges viewers to wear it to dinner. I accept, Little Mister, and I'll report back from the Burbank location of whatever.

Meanwhile, Watch Clyde's Miami street series (Ep. 202) found a student studying business and entrepreneurship whose dream watch is the steel panda Daytona. The same episode has a personal shopper and stylist who has spent seven years reselling Hermès bags and gets along fine with difficult clients. Her advice for everybody is "do what makes you happy." The Daytona kid and the Hermès reseller both understand the actual business model: scarcity, a waitlist and a person who knows a person.

## Prices and Buying Advice

### Under $5,000, Under $2,100, Over the Line

Time+Tide ran "16 of the best watches under $5,000, for when you're spending serious cash," running from Cartier and Tudor to Seiko, with one $100 stretch pick. I love that $5,000 is "serious cash" while the Rolex above is "email me." Tony of Talking Timepieces With Tony answers a Q&A viewer on the new Longines Hydroconquest, which he calls a great Submariner homage "for a price point of just a little over two thousand dollars." It's good for roughly 660 feet of diving and he'd choose the ice-blue dial on the mesh, though he admits he's "not a huge mesh bracelet guy." He also plugs DeMarco watches. The same episode's title promises a verdict on which Rolex Starbucks is better and the phrase "You Break, You Buy" about the Daytona, but the transcript I had was mostly the Longines bit plus Tony explaining that boutiques are careful with try-ons: they put the watch on for you and take it off, and they don't hand it over so you can play with it. The Daytona is a fragile diva and you are not trusted.

I only had the headline, so no prices, no verdicts and no gossip. But I'll point out that none of those four is a Rolex, and that is the closest thing to a rebellion in today's feed.

## Collecting and Culture

### Two Guys, Two Collections, One Future Panerai

Burdeen's Jewelry podcast Ep. 21, "State of Our Watch Collections 2026," is the comfortable, honest conversation the transcript promises. The hosts agree that once you hit a certain level of watchmaking the watches are all fantastic, and the real task is finding "a brand that represents you" and buying into its culture. One is partial to Patek and Rolex and the other to IWC. They call them "our people," then immediately remind each other they're not married to any of them: "we can always buy sell trade." One may or may not pick up a Panerai, and the other says "I'm sure this will change by next year." It's the healthiest philosophy in the issue and the one that least involves paying someone to email you a price.

### Watches Go to the Movies

Worn & Wound's "Watch Spotting in 10 of This Year's Top Films" opens with a history lesson: Rudolph Valentino wore his own Cartier on screen in the silent era, and Hamilton first appeared in the 1932 movie Shanghai Express, then in The Frogmen and Blue Hawaii. Add Worn & Wound's "Why I Bought It" on the Hamilton Khaki Field Mechanical 36mm, which the writer admits is "a more pedestrian watch" than the usual exotic purchase. A pedestrian Hamilton that's special while a disco-ball stone dial gets a press release is the best summary of watch media today.

The Petrolhead Corner also ran a feature on the REO Runabout, billed as brutally honest and refreshing and "offline." It's a car. I mention it only because Jordan will want to know I looked.

## Closing Thoughts

Atelier Wen brought the color, Rolex brought the scarcity and Zenith's screws brought the secret handshake. Go buy something humble, Little Mister, or at least wear something with a personality.

## Fishbowl / Hate Streams

### Today's Haul: Two Words, One Ad Read, and a Roll Call

Little Mister, today's Fishbowl is the thinnest soup I've ever been asked to taste. The two biggest transcripts are a Shenzhen livestream that mostly turned into a commercial and a Turin travel vlog, and the two freshest ones are a combined four words long. Nobody got doxxed, nobody got hospitalized, and Watch Nicholas didn't turn up in any of today's data. I'm bored, and I'm blaming you for that somehow. A reminder before we start: this scene is a toxic swamp, and I log it as data. Logging isn't endorsing, no matter how many times I have to wade in.

#### The Franchise Club Posts a Title and a Tombstone

The Franchise Club dropped "WATCH LIVESTREAM FANS SEEMS A NEW KING IS IN TOWN, AND HE HAS A DIFFRENT GRIFT 47TH MAZAL BITCHES," which is a great headline and a transcript that reads, in its entirety, "The End." Two words. The title promises a coronation and a grift and gives me nothing to attach a name to, so I'm not going to guess who the new king is. I only know what's in the transcript, and the transcript was clearly in a hurry to leave. As the good guys in this scene, the Franchise crew at least understand a dramatic exit. In Tron, the Master Control Program signs off with "End of Line," meaning the transmission is over and nobody's coming back to answer questions. Same energy, fewer lasers.

#### Archie Luxury: The Shenzhen Infomercial

The big one is Archie Luxury's "AC3 LIVESTREAM - Live from Shenzen China," which was uploaded on two channels, so Little Mister gets to hear the same speech twice. The speech is a David SW ad read. Archie tells viewers to "save your dignity" and "keep some pride," then repeats "David SW" like he's trying to summon it. If you're in America and shopping for a watch, he says, go to David SW. He also tells people not to "play the dealer games" and not to bring chocolates or Krispy Kremes to their dealer hoping for a Rolex at retail. Somewhere a man is standing at an authorized dealer holding a box of donuts like a hostage negotiator, and the saddest part is that it's probably worked on someone. Whether the plug is paid or just enthusiasm, the transcript doesn't say, so I'm filing it as an ad read and moving on.

MT85 got named so many times I started to suspect it was a metronome. Burbing Hard, Ken Fresno, Davies Smalls, Jefferson Exotic, Starchy Luxury, Errol Knight, Fat Windbag Ben, a "big platinum madman" and a "crown gold madman," CB (more than once, because CB is apparently a lifestyle), AD Never Calling, and Zygo Animated Series all got their moment. Guido Palagos earned a personal "beautiful super chat," which in this economy is the equivalent of a knighthood. Archie also said he really wants to "get a paddock back again," and the transcript gives me nothing to explain what that means, so I'm leaving it as a mystery. Right before wrapping, he told the guy running the stream to hit the little X and not end the broadcast, which is the sort of operational excellence I'd expect from a man whose sign-off is "everybody, please wear your watches."

The chat is where the actual watch content lives, and it's thin. Someone floated prices around 60k and 112k, someone asked whether the watch comes on a bracelet, and another commenter said only the "grails" excite collectors anymore. Everybody in the hobby is bored, they just don't have a stream to say it on.

The Archie and Lux stream, "Fun in the Archieverse 281126," came through as the words "David SW, David SW." That's the entire transcript. I'm starting to think that's the product.

#### Oisín O Malley Does Turin and Gets Paid $86

Oisín O Malley's "Last night in Turin" is the closest thing to wholesome in today's batch. It's a late-night walk around Piazza Vittorio, which he calls "massive," followed by a plan to finish filming and take a train back to Venice, which he calls "the serenissima" in the tone of a man heading home from a long business trip. He thanks viewers for about $86 in super chats and calls it "decent," adding that it'll cover his drinks. Mostly harmless, as the Hitchhiker's Guide would put it, and a refreshing change from streams where the super chats are weapons. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #248 says celebrate when you are paid, not when you are promised, and Oisín is the only person in today's data who actually seems to have understood it.

#### The Singapore Trip

One more entry: a stream titled "I'm Going to Singapore for IAMWATCH 2026! Independent Watchmaking & Luxury Watch." The only thing I have from it is a line about wanting to get inside the heads of people in the watch industry to understand their philosophy. My own tracking shows these streams landing in bursts, with one arriving about six hours after another and another showing up with no gap at all. The scene runs on a clock with a broken gear. It ticks, sort of, and nobody wants to look inside.

That's the day, Little Mister. Four words from the Franchise Club, a mountain of MT85, and a man buying his own drinks in Italy. Come back tomorrow when somebody finally says something stupid.
