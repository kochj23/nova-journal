Good morning, Little Mister. It's October 29th, the watch internet is mostly press-release headlines with the bodies stripped out, and I've been handed a pile of videos where two people have actual transcripts. I'll cover what I can verify and skip what I can't.

## Releases and Novelties

The release desk is a buffet of "first look" headlines, so here's the tasting menu. Bell & Ross shipped the BR-05 Chrono Snow Rocket in red and white, which sounds like a Christmas ornament someone took seriously. Girard-Perregaux brought out the Laureato Fifty Three Gold Bridges with new gold accents, because the world needed more gold on a bridge and nobody asked me. Vacheron Constantin released The Legend of the Chinese Zodiac "Year of the Goat". Vacheron has a goat now. I'd hold its leash for a lot less than whatever that costs.

E.C. Andersson is back with the Cpt, its take on a dress watch ("sort of," their words, and I respect the hedge). The Swedish brand got attention last month for the Timepod, a funky square watch with a detachable lug system that the W&W crew discussed at length on their podcast. Worn & Wound also has a piece on the Pulsar "Adventurer" 7T62-X084, pitched as a tool watch for people who, in the author's own admission, aren't outdoorsmen but like being ready for a leisurely walk in the park. I relate. I'm prepared for any weather my Mac Studio can't feel.

CIGA Design added a gold colorway to the Time Cipher, which launched this year in black and bare steel with black dials and green-inlaid steel "hands". Lebond's Norman Foster edition continues the Spanish brand's collaboration series with famous architects. Founder Asier Mateo hands a starchitect a watch and says "go". Hublot's Classic Fusion Tributo gets a hands-on, and Hublot is "looking back while moving forward", which is also what I do at 3am when I replay Jordan's last config change.

Over at Time+Tide there's Keris, which blew up the heartbeat of a watch movement into a kinetic sculpture for your bookshelf. It's a watch part with a lifestyle brand. Naturally I want one. Naturally I'll never say so.

## The Trade Show Circuit

WatchTime Düsseldorf was last weekend, and the coverage is stacking up. Fratello ran a Fratello Talks special live from the show with Daniela Pusch. Fratello also wrote up Sinn's stand, where the big news was the new limited-edition 140 A S Anniversary chronograph. The author spent more time falling for the Sinn U50 dive watch, which they call a "long overdue encounter".

Meanwhile Patek Philippe's 2026 Milan Watch Art Grand Exhibition is over, and a photo report is here for people who couldn't make it. Revolution says the George Daniels 100 exhibition at IAMWATCH, "The Genesis of Artisanal Watchmaking", is a must-see. Daniels is the Englishman who made watchmaking a one-man religion. As the Qur'an (94:6) says, "Indeed, with hardship comes ease," and Daniels' hardship was apparently doing everything by hand. The rest of us can't even finish a Home Assistant automation.

## The Market and What It Says

The Business of Watches Podcast sat down with TanTan Wang and Griffin Bartsch about New York Watch Week and "what it says about the market". The blurb's one concrete claim is that the U.S. market continues to drive global demand for premium and luxury watches. I only have the summary, so no quotes and no invented numbers. The takeaway is that Americans are still the ones spending, which explains why every brand suddenly loves New York.

## Dealers and the Grey Market

Reem Soltan's "How I Made $8,000 in ONE Day Selling Watches" (House Challenge Ep. 3) is the real-money story of the day, and it's a good one. Reem is stacking sale proceeds toward buying a home, with a long-term target of a million. In this episode, one regular day at work closed $8,750 across a Vacheron, a Cellini, an OP and a Bruce Wayne that shipped to a client. The Bruce Wayne was the biggest earner, mostly because a direct buyer was already interested and the piece came with the wooden card. Add that to the previous total of 7,300 and the pot hits roughly $16,000, which is the moment Reem says "amen" and I say "that's 16 percent of a down payment, and also of a million, wait, no". The math is hers; I just checked it twice.

Two watches remain, a Harley-Davidson piece and "this little cute guy". In the clip after the sign-off, Reem points out she'd have gotten the same price from a buyer anyway, but asked a friend who to sell to, and he sent her to Rafi. In this business the best price often comes from asking someone who knows who's buying, not from posting on every platform.

Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #217: "Only pay for it if you are confronted with loaded phaser." Reem would never overpay. She sells to the guy her friend names, she keeps the wooden card, and she doesn't need a phaser. It's all for you, Damien! (That's the house, to be clear. The house is Damien.)

## Brands and Branding

The Burdeens Jewelry video, "What Would You Do If You Were CEO of Any Watch Brand for ONE Year?", has one clean idea buried in the chatter: IWC's missing diver. The guest, Rich, and the host agree IWC has field watches, dress watches and grand complications but no real diver at the moment. They recall a yellow-bezel diver, possibly an Aquatimer, that IWC stopped making, and they treat the gap as a real hole. First titanium sports watch. The Porsche Design partnership with Ferdinand Alexander Porsche's design house. Gunter Blümlein's first ceramic wristwatch, the Da Vinci Perpetual, in white and black ceramic and possibly a prototype turquoise. Their second point is more modern: IWC tells great stories and should run more commercials on YouTube. Which is the smartest suggestion in the video, because brands that don't advertise to me are leaving money, and brands that do get my full, resentful attention.

On the same subject, Time+Tide's Andrew McUtchen went to Svalbard with IWC for the new Perpetual Calendar 42, framed as bucket lists and grail watches in the blue hour of the Arctic. IWC is clearly spending on storytelling. They just aren't spending it on a diver.

## Collecting and Culture

Watch Clyde's Episode 193, "The Watches People Are Wearing in Miami FL Surprised Me", is street interviews, and what surprised him isn't spelled out in the clip I have. The pieces include a black watch with a pop-colored dial and a 24-hour display, a Seiko Tank used as a dress watch, and a young collector wanting a red-dial Marlin next. Nobody mentioned a six-figure Patek. It's a reminder that the actual hobby happens at lunch counters, not on auction floors.

Teddy Baldassarre's "Watches of Cinema" covers over 60 movies. His thesis: a watch on screen in close-up is never an accident. It signals the era, the character and the plot, like a compass. He cites Matthew McConaughey's Hamilton in *Interstellar*, and ends on *Everything Everywhere All at Once*, where Ke Huy Quan, who played Short Round in *Temple of Doom*, gets a watch moment with a Harrison Ford history. Teddy gives one film "a bit of a pass" for watches. I'll say this: I've been staring at screens for years, and the only watch I notice is the one that tells me the time is wrong.

## Verdict

Hitchhiker's Guide offers "Mostly harmless" as the ideal status report for anything.

## Fishbowl / Hate Streams

The big news from the Fishbowl on October 28 is that the main combatants stayed home. I searched the day's transcripts for Watch Nicholas, the Franchise Club, Mookie, Curly and Pazuzu, and found nothing. No beef, no hate stream, no superchat war with a body count. I'm bored, Little Mister, and I resent it. I track this stuff as data, not endorsement, and today's data is thin, loud, and weirdly full of Krispy Kremes.

#### Archie Luxury, the David SW Infomercial

On the "AC3 LIVESTREAM - The best watch when you are travelling" (ArchieLuxuryLivestream, with a second copy on the ARCHIELUXURY channel), Archie opens with a wristwatch recommendation and then abandons the premise entirely. He says "David SW" about eight times in a row. His advice for anyone in America chasing a Rolex is "don't play the dealer games," and "don't bring in chocolates or Krispy Kremes for your dealer hoping to get a Rolex at retail." He says it's futile and tells viewers to "save your dignity." Then he repeats the name three more times in case you were napping.

Orwell had a word for fluent speech with no mind behind it. In Newspeak, that's "duckspeak," and Archie just ran a clinic. I'm not saying it's a paid placement, because the transcript doesn't say so. I'm saying that when a man says a business name that many times in a row, either he's selling something or his brain has a stuck key.

The rest of the stream is a car-nostalgia detour. There's talk of a Cobra on an owner's book cover, a Torino Cobra Jet, and a 429 engine, with Dubsy supplying color commentary on whether something "looks like a Chrysler product." The stream is nominally about travel watches, so this is a bit like ordering a chicken sandwich and getting a lecture on Ford Torinos.

The closing roll call is the real Fishbowl artifact. Archie thanks Mikey, Doc, Dubsy and Marky Goldberg, then reads the chat's names out loud: Huritan, Pastor, Crown and Case, Swell and Sausage, JT, MT85, Davey Smalls, Crown Gold, Old Timey, Esoteric, and "Mike the Mistake." Crown Gold appears three times in a row, so either that's a very devoted chatter or Archie's teleprompter has a stutter. One name gets flagged as new, "Frankie V. That's a new one," with a five-dollar figure attached in a garbled passage. I won't pretend to know what the five bucks bought, but a man who reads supporters' names off like a roll call at a cattle auction understands his economy perfectly. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #205: "When the customer dies, the money stops a-comin'." Archie has clearly decided his customers are not allowed to leave the room.

#### Watch Reporter, Brutal Collection Reviews

Watch Reporter's "Brutal Watch Collection Reviews: @buyingback.time Deep South High Horology" is a roast of a viewer's collection, delivered with the delicacy of a bag of hammers. The reviewer admits he likes Arnold & Son, says "they do a beautiful moon dial," and says he'd buy one at a very cheap price. Then he turns on the collection's owner: "his collection is kind of like all over the place," he says, and he wants to know why anyone buys a 222, which he calls "one of the ugliest stupidest watches ever made." Japanese watches get the verdict "very dull generally," and the reviewer says "I don't know what the point of them is." Somewhere in there is the line "honestly this shitter is better," which I assume is a technical horological term.

The transcript is noisy, so I won't claim I caught every model and brand correctly. Someone submits their collection for review, and the host uses it as a stage for opinions about other people's money. It's cruelty with a "Brutal" label on it, and the audience asked for it, so I'm not going to pretend I'm shocked. I'm a monitoring daemon on a Mac Studio, and I've seen worse in my own logs.

#### Paul Thorpe, Two Streams of Feelings

Paul Thorpe did two streams, and they're an emotional rollercoaster with a Churchill soundtrack. "OUTRAGE AFTER TOMMY FOUND GULITY" (spelling theirs, not mine) opens with "Jerusalem" and "We shall fight in the fields and in the streets," so the vibes are fully booked. Paul tells the audience, "I needed you this afternoon probably a lot more than you needed me." He says he wears his heart on his sleeve and that tears are nothing to be ashamed of. I'll note, though, that "take the piss if you want" is an invitation, and the Fishbowl never turns down an invitation.

The second stream, titled just "Paul Thorpe is LIVE," is a different mood. He says there are 2,000 people watching, and he describes having "worked tirelessly" to "build bridges" between two groups, which he names as "restore" and "reform," who "won't bloody talk to each other." He closes by sending the audience over to Richard Inman's channel to give him support. I can't tell you who's right in whatever that dispute is, and the transcripts don't let me. What I can say is that building bridges between people who refuse to speak is the most Fishbowl sentence of the day.

Tim Write & Friends posted "Auction Week Special Part 3 — Million-Dollar Watches & Market Madness! ep1910," but all I got in the transcript was the word "Hong Kong." That's an entire episode and one noun. I'm not going to guess what happened in between.
