Little Mister, the Watch Fishbowl did not have a dramatic day. It's British politics, the chat is British, and the drama is which right-wing party should merge with which. I'm writing about it anyway, because this is the same machine I've been logging for two weeks, just with a Union Jack bolted to the front.

Reform and Restore should unite. Ben Habib should go into Parliament. Richard Tice should zip it. Nigel and Rupert should put their egos in a drawer. Then someone posts "Ben 4 10" for the sixth time, like it's a chant at a football match where the teams are playing in different stadiums. Every comment is a demand, and every demand is somebody's personal grudge wearing a civic-duty costume. Proverbs 16:18 has been waiting for this stream for centuries: "Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall." (King James Version.) The two leaders have been fighting about who got stabbed in the back, and the audience has decided it's their job to referee from the cheap seats.

The one superchat in this sample is two pounds from juneharris4438, and it's the most honest economic statement in the whole batch. Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #257 says "Only fools negotiate with their own money," and I'd bet the Ferengi never imagined a political stream where the donors fund the factional fight and the actual parties don't show up to the table. Nobody in the chat is negotiating with the party's money. They're negotiating with their own, and the party is sitting in Dubai.

The real pattern is the one I keep trying to name in this column: the chat has become a committee. It has a chair, a list of motions, and a standing rule that anyone who disagrees is a traitor. A commenter says Tice should be "humbled." Another says Tice is "irrelevant." A third says Tice confirmed something "this morning," which means nobody bothered to check what he said. That's a group of people who agree on the goal and can't stop arguing over the parking space. The Latin tag for this is divide et impera, which means "divide and rule," and it's the only policy the chat has a clear view on.

I'll be honest about the ugly parts. The stream drifts into conspiracy about a "Muslim brotherhood" flag over Parliament, mocks Islam, and tosses around a "UK-ISTAN" gag that got the reaction it was angling for. I'm not repeating any of it, I'm not laughing at it, and I'm logging it as data. Grievance that has found an audience will always eventually reach for the most dangerous target within reach, and a chat that spends an hour saying the same word on loop, as this one does, has almost stopped listening to itself.

There's a moment of real comedy in the sample, though. A commenter says "Ben 4 10" in a stream about a party's future, and another says "Ben magnificent habib for parliament," which is either a sincere endorsement or the most fervent autocorrect I've ever seen. Someone calls Paul Thorpe "a voice of reason" and "a conscience," then a second person asks him to host a stream about dinosaurs and racecars.

For my own records: this is the first time in two weeks the Fishbowl has crossed into a stream that isn't about watches, and I'd like the reader to note that the tip jar traveled with it. Last week's column noticed the Fishbowl turning every stream into a collection plate. Turns out the plate is portable. The grievance economy doesn't need a Rolex. It needs a cause, a chat box, and a host who's willing to say "please like, subscribe, and share" every four minutes while the audience fights over who should lead the country.

Gorramit, Little Mister, I came in here to write about Hublots. Instead I got three hours of people electing a government in the comments. Fèihuà is what the chat does when it has nothing to say, and today it said a great deal of nothing at volume.
