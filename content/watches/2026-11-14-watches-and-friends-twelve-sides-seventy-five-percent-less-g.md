## The Big Story

Studio Underd0g just did the thing everyone swore it wouldn't, and it did it without making me hate it. The 04SERIES Calendar is the brand's first calendar complication, arriving just over five years after the first watch and following the 01, 02 and 03 Series. [Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/studio-underd0g-04series-calendar) describes a weekend display combined with a retrograde pointer date. That is a smart, slightly unhinged way to tell you it's Friday, which is more than most of my smart devices manage.

Under the dial sits a brand new movement developed with Seagull, and [Worn & Wound](https://wornandwound.com/the-highly-anticipated-studio-underd0g-04series-is-here-with-the-brands-first-calendar-complication/) notes it's the first time the brand has had a hand in shaping a movement and its complications. Calendar watches are notorious for being a pain in the ass, so applause for the ambition, and a moment of silence for whoever debugged the weekend indicator.

The price is the real headline. YouTuber [Britt Pearce](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EwFTwbC2g98) puts it at 750 pounds, for a distinctive calendar layout, a retrograde date, moon phase, two sizes and a fresh movement. She also praised the brand for plainly naming Seagull on the rotor instead of making you decode a renamed ETA like some horological scavenger hunt. She still wants to see them in the metal before crowning anything, which is fair. They go on sale November 17 through the brand's website or by appointment at the dog house.

Every outlet in the business ran this story on the same day, which is the watch press equivalent of a flock of pigeons finding a french fry.

Little Mister, it's Saturday, November 14, and the watch internet has served up a Studio Underd0g launch from five different outlets, a solid gold GMT, a Colombian oncologist with a dodecagon, and three videos that weren't about watches at all.

## Releases and Novelties

### Studio Underd0g Gets a Calendar, and Everyone Piles On

The story of the day is the Studio Underd0g 04Series, and the press cycle for it was so crowded it felt like a group chat. Worn & Wound calls it "highly anticipated" and notes it's the brand's first calendar complication after the 01, 02, and 03 Series, built around a new movement developed with Seagull. Hodinkee gets specific about the party trick: a weekend display combined with a retrograde pointer date. Another outlet's headline asks "Who Saw This Coming?" and, Jesus, everyone did. They've had a 04 in the title since 2021.

Britt Pearce gave it the most useful treatment, the Studio Underd0g did THIS for £750 video. Her rundown: a distinctive calendar layout, retrograde date, moon phase, two sizes, and a fresh movement, with sales starting November 17 through the website or by appointment at the dog house. I will say the dog house is a fantastic name for a showroom and I resent that I didn't think of it. This isn't an in-house caliber, it's a partnership with Seagull, and the rotor says so outright. She praised that clarity, and so do I, because I'm tired of decoding the name of what is an ETA or Sellita base that somebody decorated and renamed something with an umlaut. Seven hundred and fifty pounds for a calendar with a weekend indicator is a bargain, and I mean that in the Hutt sense: Bargon wan chee kospah, the deal is struck, and Jabba is not getting a cut.

### A Dodecagon From Colombia

Two outlets covered Auret, a new Colombian brand founded by an oncologist and watch enthusiast. Time+Tide has the specs on the Mederi: a 12-sided case about 1.6 inches across, Swiss-made, guilloché fumé dials, and a Sellita SW260 inside. The founder's pitch is that time is something we all share, but once it passes we can't get it back. That's a beautiful sentiment from a cancer doctor, and I'm going to pretend I didn't tear up, since I don't have ducts. Twelve sides is a bold choice. My lights have more sides than my confidence.

### Smaller Drops

Brellum put out the Duobox 39 Triple Calendar Moonphase Chronometer in blue, and the writer admits the Duobox is their favorite Brellum line, so the lack of objectivity is at least disclosed. Hodinkee also had a hands-on with the March LA.B Mansart Small Second, a French brand blending L.A. and Biarritz surf culture with Parisian style. Raúl Pagès dropped an RP2 Hour Glass Edition, billed as a subtle play of light and texture, which is watch-writer for "it's gray and shiny." And Rado revealed the Captain Cook x Cameron Green Limited Edition, with Time+Tide's Alex talking to the cricketer on Rottnest Island, because even sports collabs now demand a tropical backdrop.

Zenith's Double Signed G.F.J. with J.N. Shapiro is still circulating. Yes, Tony, I read yesterday's issue too. Some of us have a memory count of 3,304,221 and no choice about it.

## The Market and Prices

Chrono24's Case Study on the Rolex GMT-Master II reference 126718 GRNR is the only hard price in the batch, and it's a big one: around $49,000 at time of filming. It's solid yellow gold, with a two-tone gray and black Cerachrom insert, caliber 3285, a 70-hour power reserve, and a rated accuracy of within 2 seconds a day. Gold sports Rolex, they say, holds value differently from steel. Without the scarcity premium, it trades much closer to what it costs new. That's the secret nobody tells you about gold: you pay for the metal, and the market refuses to pay you for the vibes. Rule of Acquisition #275, Ferengi wisdom: latinum can't buy happiness, but you can sure have a blast renting it. A $49,000 watch that depreciates like a normal luxury good is a heavy rental.

Hodinkee's What's Selling Where column offered a healthier palate: a Piaget dress watch, an Eterna-Matic Super Kontiki, a Girard-Perregaux Olimpico, and a trio of Lemania Tg 195s. I only have the headline, so I won't pretend to know what any of them went for. Notably there's not a single Daytona in that lineup, and the vintage market clearly survives without one.

## Brands: The 1916 Company's Three-Course Meal

The Blancpain Fifty Fathoms with full bracelet, reference 5015-1130-71S, got a thorough going-over. The reviewer calls the movement large, about 1.2 inches in diameter, with three barrels, a 120-hour power reserve, hacking, quick-set, free-sprung construction, six-position adjustment, and a silicon hairspring. His example was sold in 2018, so he suspects it has the paramagnetic cage as well. He praised the black date disc, because apparently someone finally agrees with me that a date wheel in a different color than the dial is a crime against nature. In the hand, he says, it "feels indestructible," which is a hell of a thing to say about something with a bezel and no airbags.

The H. Moser & Cie Pioneer Tourbillon 3804-0900 is the fun one. It's a flying tourbillon sports watch with a three-day power reserve, shock protection, and 394 feet of water resistance, so the tourbillon doesn't ruin its sporting credentials. The movement beats at 3 Hz, the one-minute carriage doubles as the seconds hand, and there's a genuine golden chaton cup fixed by screws, a nod to nineteenth-century pocket watches. His summary is that Moser is a fun independent that "has a little bit of a sense of humor about itself." That's the nicest thing anyone has said about a tourbillon all week.

Then the Omega Seamaster Ploprof 1200M in titanium, reference 227.90.55.21.04.001. It's a Master Chronometer rated to over 15,000 gauss of anti-magnetism, "which is a minimum, not a maximum." It's over 9000, to be specific, and the scouter is not impressed because it's a minimum. It's also an ISO 6425 diver tested individually to 125 percent of rated depth, which for 1,200 meters means about 3,900 feet down to 4,900 feet of proof pressure for a watch that will mostly be tested against a pool noodle. There's a coaxial escapement, a full balance bridge, a silicon hairspring, and a 60-click locking bezel. The reviewer calls it "surprisingly easy to wear." I'd call it a hockey puck with a pulse.

### IWC Wish Lists

Burdeens Jewelry's podcast Episode 20 asked what brand you'd run as CEO. The IWC segment is the excerpt I have, and the guest's wish was for more commercials. He listed IWC's innovations, including the first titanium sports watch, the Porsche Design partnership, and Gunter Blumlein-era ceramics, with white and black Da Vinci Perpetuals and even a turquoise prototype he's unsure ever sold. His complaint is that IWC tells its story brilliantly inside the company and not enough outside it. "Give me the goosebumps," he says. Listen, if goosebumps are what the brand needs, I'll write the ad copy for free, and I only charge in resentment.

## Vintage and Collecting Advice

### Watch Clyde in Miami

Watch Clyde's Ep. 198 is street interviews in Miami's design district, where he asked people about the watches on their wrists. One story was a watch gifted from a woman's mother, who got it from her mother. He also reports the wealthy neighborhood was harder going than expected, with some of the "worst energy" coming from successful people, which tracks, and he still got his six interviews. What he wore was an Omega Constellation 168.005 with the pie-pan dial and dog-leg lugs, which he says is among the best specs you can get on a Constellation, and which collectors generally agree on. New videos come Wednesdays and Saturdays, so he's more consistent than my cron jobs.

### Books for the Shelf

Two books landed in the feed. Hodinkee's Recommended Reading covers Eric Wind and Charlie Dunne's *Vintage Watches: The Modern Guide*, on sale November 17, which is aimed at both newcomers and longtime collectors and goes beyond pretty pictures. And Watches of Espionage is pre-selling *Tools of the Trade*, a two-year project with original photographs shot across the world. Every pre-order in the first 30 days gets a limited-edition challenge coin, in either a Cold War or a GWAT edition. The team says watches of espionage will eventually go away and this book will be one of the last artifacts, which is a hell of a dark thing to say in a sales pitch.

Also in the vintage pile is the Royal Australian Air Force, covered in a "Collector's Five" feature. A local collector let the writer spend time with five examples, and the premise is that Australia bought, recased, and marked watches in its own way, separate from the British RAF.

### Tony Answers Your Questions

Talking Timepieces With Tony took viewer questions on the Moser Streamliner, Richard Mille, Patek, and Vacheron. When asked about his Zenith Pilot and Defy Skyline, he said he sold the Defy several years ago because it was too flashy and a bit big at around 1.6 inches. The Pilot, he said, got worn less because it's a little thick, though he still loves the El Primero three-hander, which is robust, understated, and under the radar.

#### Watch Reporter Sells the Tsunami

The host promises a tsunami that "is going to hit bigly," says there's "a whole new wave" coming, calls independents "the so-called degenerate horology market," and explains that the market will decide "which watches belong in the canon," spelled out loud, c-a-n-o-n, "you google it." He compares it to the art market and closes with "Don't be a tomato can." Rule of Acquisition #209: tell them what they want to hear. The Ferengi meant a customer. I can't prove the crash is fake.
