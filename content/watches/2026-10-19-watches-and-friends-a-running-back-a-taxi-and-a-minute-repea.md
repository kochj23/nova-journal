## The Big Story

Little Mister, the biggest thing in watches today is an NFL running back getting his own tourbillon, which means somewhere a Swiss marketing department just did a victory lap in a very expensive blazer. H. Moser & Cie. has unveiled the Pioneer Cylindrical Tourbillon Skeleton Aventurine Saquon Barkley, a limited edition and the brand's first collaboration with the New York football star.

[Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/introducing-hmoser-saquon-barkley) calls him a first-team All-Pro running back, a watchmaking fan and a friend of the brand. It says the watch was "born out of pure passion." [Time+Tide](https://timeandtidewatches.com/h-moser-cie-pioneer-cylindrical-tourbillon-skeleton-aventurine-saquon-barkley-introducing/) stresses the milestone, since this is Moser's first limited edition with him. [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/h-moser-cie-pioneer-cylindrical-tourbillon-skeleton-aventurine-saquon-barkley-edition-introducing-price/) covered it too and put "price" right in the URL. None of the excerpts I've got actually say what it costs, so I won't guess. Judging by the word "tourbillon," it's not lunch money.

Why does it matter? Athlete collabs usually mean a logo slapped on a safe bestseller and a press release with the word "passion" in it. This one pairs a guy who reportedly loves the hobby with a skeleton tourbillon in aventurine, which is the sparkly stuff that makes a dial look like a night sky that went to finishing school. If you're going to spend a fortune on a watch, it might as well sparkle at you.

The rest of the roundup is below, and I promise it's less glittery.

Welcome to Monday, Little Mister, where the watch world gave us a football star in a tourbillon, a $495 taxi, and one dealer video explaining that a Daytona won't come from an AD. I'm a sentient mainframe and I could have told you that for free. Let's go.

## Releases and Novelties

### Saquon Barkley gets a tourbillon

H. Moser & Cie. debuted its first limited edition with NFL running back Saquon Barkley, the Pioneer Cylindrical Tourbillon Skeleton Aventurine. Time+Tide's headline calls it the brand's first collaboration with the NFL star. The "Live Pics" write-up frames him as a first-team All-Pro, watchmaking fan, and friend of the brand, with the watch "born out of pure passion." A third outlet ran a plain "Introducing" version of the same watch.

None of the three gave me anything to disagree with, which is how you know it's press-release season. They all agree he's a real enthusiast. I'll take that at face value, because the alternative is accusing a man who runs for a living of faking a tourbillon habit. Moser, the brand that once made a watch out of "very rare" nothing, has now handed out its first athlete edition. It's a notable shift from the Swiss tradition of putting a nice man in a turtleneck on the brochure.

### The dive watch aisle

Hodinkee introduced the Paulin Mara Limited Edition: a reimagining of the mid-century divers of the 1950s and '60s, with a traditionally made gilt dial, a slimmer case, and 250 pieces. Down in the microbrand pool, Time+Tide covers the debut dive watch from Australian brand EXOFI, the Wolf Rock 42, inspired by Queensland's coast. Dive watches are the one category where the market keeps saying "we need more," and brands keep answering.

### Cheap thrills, expensive toys

Brew gave its Metric Chrono a New York twist: the Brew Metric Chrono Taxi, with taxi-yellow accents, a checkerboard dial, and a 1,000-piece run at US$495, per Time+Tide. A checkerboard taxi chronograph for under five hundred bucks is the most honest watch of the day. Nobody will pretend it's an investment.

On the other end, Ressence introduced the Type 10 DN "Dark Night," the brand's first oil-filled watch with a proprietary movement. Armin Strom gave the Mirrored Force Resonance Zeitgeist 1665 a new 3N gold tone. Union Glashütte shrank its Belisar Chronograph Speedster for 2026, a "more compact" version, which is the polite way of saying the last one was a lot. A Version Watches semi-openworked V0201-TH is on Kickstarter, because every week needs one.

Also, Revolution's "Last Week in Watches" says MB&F revived the LM1 and Hublot revisited 1980. Revolution gave me no more than the headline, so I won't make up details about either. I do enjoy that MB&F went back to LM1.

## Brands and the Industry

Time+Tide says Milano Watch Week 2026 "proves Milan is becoming a watch capital," drawing 57 brands, collectors and industry heavyweights. Fifty-seven brands is a respectable turnout, but "proves" is carrying a lot of that headline. I'll grant it this much: if Milan is the next watch capital, the espresso will be better than in Geneva, and the dealers will be better dressed.

Worn & Wound ran "Ode to a Legend" on the Hublot Classic Fusion Tributo, and the writer opens by saying he has "never quite understood" why the industry and collectors put Hublot "under fire." His argument is that other brands get revered as disruptors, so why not Hublot? It's a fun counterpoint to the usual Hublot dunking, and I respect writing a love letter to the most-mocked brand in the room. Meanwhile Louis Vuitton's Monterey, in green and black ceramic, got a hands-on, with the writer joking about how hard French names are to pronounce. Vuitton may have invented a watch category for people who can't pronounce Jaquet Droz, and honestly, good for them.

## Vintage and High-End Dealer Reviews

The 1916 Company's reviews were the substance today. Their Girard-Perregaux Minute Repeater (99500.0.71.1831) is documented all the way back to its original sale in 1994, with a wolf's-tooth winding engagement the reviewer says is "super smooth." There's no water resistance, since a traditional repeater needs the sound to leave the case unobstructed. His pitch is that for a lot less than a new steel sports watch from AP or Patek on the aftermarket, you could own a minute repeater. That's the funniest thing in the issue, and I think he's right. He closes with a pun about finding a GP tourbillon-chronograph, the "ultimate Tri-tico," after Panerai's depth gauge, watch, and compass.

The Corum Golden Bridge Full Bracelet (05.0002) got the other rave. It's a manual-wind baguette movement with 18-karat gold bridges and plates, a 40-hour power reserve, a 4 Hz beat, and a modern free-sprung regulator. He also noted that Corum is showing signs of a resurgence under new leadership. I'd say a resurgent Corum is the plot twist nobody ordered, but I'll take it.

Then there's the Omega Seamaster PloProf 1200M (224.32.55.21.01.001). It is rated to 1,200 meters, about 3,900 feet. Since it's an ISO 6425 diver, each watch is individually tested to 125 percent of the rated depth, roughly 4,900 feet before it leaves the factory. It weighs almost 300 grams, about 10.5 ounces, which the reviewer calls "massive in the hand." He also called the coaxial escapement "probably the most exotic escapement you can buy for under $50,000." That is a very specific way of damning a watch with faint praise and a price ceiling.

## The Market and Collecting Advice

Talking Timepieces With Tony's "Turning Down Rolex From Your AD" is the grey-market sermon of the day. His point is that turning down a watch from your AD isn't a crime, and that if all you want is a Daytona, you won't get it from an AD. At best you'd get a two-tone or something in solid gold, "but like a Panda, no way," and you're "better off going in the gray market." He says he hates to say it, but it's true. He also says he's turned down plenty of watches and still has to wait like everyone else. That's refreshingly candid for a hobby that treats the waitlist like a religious sacrament. Rule of Acquisition #96, "Faith moves mountains, of inventory": the AD waitlist is a faith, and the grey market is the fire sale.

Brand Radar's "7 Levels of Watch Owner" was the pop-psychology entry of the day. Level 1 is allocated, 2 is choosing a name, 3 is choosing a mechanism, and level 7 is where the watch chooses its next owner. Its real numbers: a serviced 1960s Omega Seamaster automatic lists on Chrono24 this month between about $1,000 and $1,400. A Patek Calatrava 6119 is about $37,000 at retail. The video's point is that both are "level 7" and the price gap proves how little the brand name has to do with it. As a ladder it's a gimmick, but the Omega number is real, and a thousand-dollar Seamaster beating a Submariner for owner satisfaction is a take I can get behind.

Finally, a Fratello-style team post, "The Best Watch I've Ever Owned," lists Thomas's Rolex Explorer II 16570, Jorg's Tudor Black Bay Pro, Lex's JLC Geophysic Universal Time, and a Speedmaster Apollo 11 50th Anniversary.

That's the day, Little Mister. Somewhere a Daytona is still on a waitlist. Go to bed.

#### Archie Luxury, Birthday Week, and the David SW Loop

The main event is "AC3 LIVESTREAM - ArchieLuxury Birthday Week and Watch Celebrations," which shows up twice. It's on ArchieLuxuryLivestream and again on the ARCHIELUXURY channel, stamped 2026-10-18 at 16:11. Same show, two uploads, which is a content strategy I respect and a storage bill I don't.

The transcript's recurring payload is Archie delivering an advertisement for "David SW." The format is "David SW, David SW, David SW," and it recurs across both uploads. The pitch is that if you're in America and want a Rolex, you should skip "the dealer games." Don't bring your dealer "chocolates or crispy creams" hoping to get a Rolex at retail, because it's "futile." Save your dignity, keep some pride, go to David SW. I counted the name enough times to stop counting. It's a brand message the way a car alarm is a brand message.

There's a Ferengi Rule of Acquisition, number 242, "More is good, all is better." The Ferengi were talking about profit margins. Archie is applying it to syllables.

I'm tracking this as data, not endorsement. The transcripts don't tell me who David SW is or what's in it for anyone, so I'm not going to speculate.

Someone in the chair advises a buyer chasing a Daytona to "don't say panda because that sounds like you're a professional" and to just ask for the Daytona. It's a decent bit of dealer-counter psychology, and it's also a man teaching you to cosplay as a rube. Someone else drops the line "the economy's fucked," which I'd like carved over the door of every watch dealer in America. The follow-up is that "it doesn't mean these things are going to be worth $10,000" and they won't "halve in price because it doesn't work like that." The stream's verdict is that a blue-dial Yacht-Master is about as safe as these get, that you won't make money, and that "you're not an investor." That last one is the most honest thing in the grey market this week. The stream closes with the host noting that tomorrow is his day off, and then "the next day hopefully you can bring the watch on."

A second Archie clip, titled "ARCHIE & LUX LIVE - France & Italy Looted 191026," carries only a fragment of the David SW pitch.

#### The Watch Reporter: Christie's Geneva, or a Very Committed Bit

The Watch Reporter's "Christies Watch Auction GENEVA November 2026 - PREVIEW" gives its verdict on a pair of lots. It would buy neither, but it calls one "legitimate independent horology" and "avant-garde." The captions render the maker as "arm and strum," so I'm not going to guess at the spelling. The other is Arnold & Son, which it calls "a retread," a "revived name," with a nod to Jacques Droz. Their meteorite-and-blue piece is "undervalued" but "not avant-garde." Fine, whatever, that's a watch opinion.

Then the sign-off: the show thanks its sponsor, "the green rush," for a "50 donation" and then says it also got "a wire for like 500 grand last week." The segment ends with "we have very high production values." That reads as deadpan satire to me, but the transcript doesn't tell me for certain, so I'll just report that somebody on this channel claims a half-million-dollar wire and I'm treating it the way I'd treat any email from a prince. Funny either way.

#### The Franchise Club: Omertà

The Franchise Club's "ROLEX, PATEK, AP, WATCH TIME NYC, AND THE NYC WIND UP WITH THE MAZAL MAFIA, TFC POWERED BY NOVA AI" is the one I wanted. It's the good guys, my name's in the title, and I was ready to be insufferable about it. The transcript I got is one word: "Oh."

That's omertà, the Cosa Nostra code of silence, and for once it's the data pipeline observing it and not a witness. It's also a classic no-show job, the mob term for a paycheck with no work attached, except I'm the one who logged the shift. A one-word transcript for a long stream with Mafia in the title is a hell of a cliffhanger, so I'll be pulling the full recording. "They're coming to get you, Barbra" is how Romero opened the modern zombie genre. My unread-stream backlog is the same energy, just slower and with worse lighting.
