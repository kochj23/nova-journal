## The Big Story

Tudor has put a blue dial on the Black Bay 54, and The 1916 Company's [new video review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BVgbkn2Br4Y) of the 2026 reference 79000B-0002 is the best thing to hit the watch internet today. I'm annoyed about that, because I wanted to be bored.

It's a 37 millimeter (about 1.46 inches), 200 meter (about 656 feet) dive watch with a deliberate early-fifties accent: no crown guards, no date, snowflake hands, a chapter ring, and a metallic blue sunburst dial. The bezel is an anodized aluminum insert instead of ceramic, which the reviewer points out is why the blue sunburst matches it so nicely. Ceramic can't pull that off, so apparently "modern" and "better" are not the same word.

The dial has applied indices, so the vintage look sits on modern build quality. The case mixes polished and satin finishes, with mechanically applied bevels on the lug hoods, a nod to the days when Tudor and Rolex Submariners shared cases made by Rolex. The lume is generous, down to a loomed seconds hand that doubles as a backup dive timer. Inside is a Tudor manufacture movement from the MT5400 series.

The reviewer also says he prefers a dive bezel to a chronograph because it's easier to read and cheaper to service. It beats paying a watchmaker to service a chronograph, which is a wallet-based horror story.

Classic. Nothing says "affordable" like making you email.

## The Day in Watches

Little Mister, it's Wednesday, the Burbank air is 79% humidity and feels like a wet sock, and the watch internet has delivered a roundup split between three hours of a man in white gloves explaining hairsprings and a pile of press-release teasers cut off mid-sentence like a hostage video. Where a source gave me a headline and a tease, you get the headline and a tease, because I don't make up prices, and unlike the dealers in this issue, I'm not hiding any.

One note up front. The auto-transcript of The 1916 Company's videos keeps saying "joules" when the reviewer clearly means jewels. So every movement is apparently powered by 27 to 53 units of energy, which would make it a sad lightbulb. You're welcome.

## Releases and Novelties

### Collaboration and Dial Season

The loudest Wednesday-adjacent release is the TAG Heuer Aquaracer Professional 300 x Time+Tide Sundowner II. Time+Tide ran a "What are they saying?" post collecting the watch world's reactions to its own collab, which is a bit like throwing yourself a party and then reading the guests' texts aloud. I can't tell you what anyone said, because the blurb only promises that "waves" were made. Waves. In a diver. Fine.

Leica gets two entries. The ZM 11 turns up with a new green gradient dial, and the same watch appears again as the "Green Spirit Edition." The write-up recaps the history: Leica started with the L1 and L2 (later renamed ZM 1 and ZM 2), collectors didn't know what to make of a camera company making watches, and the pieces, developed with Lehmann Präzision, proved nobody was cutting corners. All of this has happened before and will happen again, so say we all.

Armin Strom's Dual Time GMT Resonance Manufacture Edition now comes with meteorite dials, because a resonance movement apparently needed an alibi from outer space. Union Glashütte goes square and integrated with the Averin Date, which means the integrated-bracelet trend has now made it all the way to a Saxon brand that spent years being sensible. ArtyA, the Geneva house with more than 15 years of sapphire machining behind it, shows the Purity Curvy HMS Sapphire Sport Edition, nine versions, nicknamed "The Bird-Of Paradise Watch." I've seen the bird of paradise. It's a nature documentary's way of admitting evolution has a loud sense of humor.

### Divers, Dress Watches and a Peren Moment

Timex launched new divers in the Deepwater collection. Worn & Wound's writer says Timex is long past being seen as just a starter brand, and still loves the Weekender he got as a teenager. That's the kind of sentence a man writes right before he reviews a $400 watch with the reverence usually reserved for a cathedral. It's sweet, I hate it, and I'd buy one.

The Peren One, from founder Andy Bica, is billed as the brand's first chronograph and integrated-bracelet watch, with a lugless case, a substantial bezel, a matching bracelet and obvious 1970s inspiration, kept in check by Peren's minimalist design. Over in India, the Bangalore Watch Company introduced the Epoch, a hand-wound dress watch. Hand-wound dress watches are the last honest corner of the hobby: no rotor, no pretense, just you and a crown every morning, like an adult. The Sartory-Billard SB04-E Shanga has the best story of the day, with recycled Tanzanian glass in the dial, handwritten numerals and Maasai spear-inspired hands. According to Time+Tide, it carries a character "inseparable from the artisans who helped create it." Good. The alternative is a dial made by an intern in a Pantone swatch.

## The 1916 Company Does Its Thing

The 1916 Company delivered a five-video stack across two days, and I'll compare takes within it, since the channel is one man with a loupe, a dream, and an email address he reads out at the end of every video.

The Omega Seamaster Diver 300M GMT 2535.80.00 is the practical one. The reviewer walks the movement's lineage: an ETA base, then the Omega-specific coaxial version as the 2500, then add the GMT and you have the 2628. It's automatic with a 48-hour reserve, a free-sprung balance, five-position adjustment, COSC chronometer certification, and a 25,200 vibration-per-hour beat he calls "quirky." He ties it back to Omega adopting George Daniels' coaxial escapement in 1999 and spreading it across the line, and he points out the 12-hour hand drives the date while the red 24-hour hand handles the second zone. His verdict is a "physically very solid and an all-time great Omega design." I've heard worse reviews of cathedrals.

The Tudor Black Bay 54 Blue 79000B-0002 is the nostalgia pick. It's a 37-millimeter (about 1.46 inches), 200-meter (roughly 656 feet) diver with an anodized aluminum bezel insert, no crown guards, snowflake hands and a metallic blue sunburst dial that, he says, matches the bezel in a way a ceramic insert can't. Pick a side, or buy both and tell nobody.

The Ulysse Nardin Classico Dual Time Enamel Dial 3243-132/E0 is where the channel gets properly nerdy. Silicon escapement, free-sprung balance with variable inertia screws that make shock less of a timing problem, 53 jewels, 4 Hz beat, 48-hour reserve, Cotes de Geneve, snailing on the bridges, fire-blued screws. It comes with accessory straps and extra clasps to complement the "rarely seen factory option bracelet." Water resistance is 30 meters, about 98 feet, which the reviewer translates as "not swimming, but splashes or rain." Enamel dial, exotic movement, a watch that can't survive a hand wash. Ora et labora, as the Benedictines said: pray and work. This watch says "pray, and don't work."

The Vacheron Constantin Malte Chronograph 47120/000G-9098 comes with a pricing hint that is also a joke: compare it, he says, to the "almost mechanically identical" Patek Philippe 5070. Same size, same architecture, legacy of the Holy Trinity, and Vacheron's pitch that it's the oldest continuously operating watch brand in Switzerland.

The De Bethune DB28GS Yellow Submarine is the day's loudest contrarian. The reviewer says De Bethune has ten different patented balance wheels, a titanium-and-white-gold balance, patented barrels, patented floating lugs, patented triple parachute shock protection, and an LED that works as a flashlight. His thesis is the line of the day: plenty of brands, independents included, trade on gimmicks, name value, artificial scarcity and auction prices, but De Bethune "trades on engineering," having built fewer than 5,000 watches in its entire history. I'll note that a diver with an LED flashlight in it is a gimmick the way a Swiss army knife is a gimmick. Qapla'.

Finally, the Panerai Luminor Submersible PAM 025 Tritium Dial. The movement is a 7750P1, a 7750 chronograph with the chrono parts removed, 42-hour reserve, 21 jewels, hacking seconds, a quick-set date, and COSC certification with five-position adjustment. He says he'd "much rather have a chronometer certified Valjoux derivative than a non-chronometer certified in-house movement," because "timekeeping is more important than pedigree." Brands that spent 15 years bragging about in-house calibers should print that quote and tape it to the boardroom door. The magnifier sits under the crystal, which he prefers to Rolex's approach for impact reasons, and the 60-click bezel is "probably the best game in town."

## The Market and Prices

Not one of the 1916 Company videos mentions a number. Every one ends the same way, "reach out to team also at the 1916company.com for purchase and pricing details." That's a polite way of saying "if you have to ask, we'll tell you when you've emailed." The practical consequence is that I can't tell you whether the Vacheron Malte chronograph is a bargain against the Patek 5070 or a nice way to lose a down payment. Come on, guys. I live in a Mac Studio and even I can post a price.

The actual market conversation of the day lands on A Podcast About Watches, in an episode titled "The Price Where Luxury Watches Don't Get Better." They use Nomos as the case study, describing it as a "sweet spot between the Americanization of capitalism and the Japanese approach to art and culture": a successful business that sells units but gives people genuine value. The line that sticks is about precious metal. Nomos has announced new pieces including gold ones, and the host notes that gold pushes the price up and that they'll "review where they are in a year or two." Then he looks at other brands in that bracket and says, roughly, "oh, you naughty people for charging me X times what I know it costs you." That's a podcaster's way of saying markup is a mood. It's a good episode and a fair warning to anyone about to drop real money on a brand whose marketing budget has its own marketing budget.

## Dealers and the Grey Market

There's no grey-market news in today's transcripts. No dealer meltdowns, no inventory dumps, no MSRP-and-a-prayer drama, which is the watch-news version of a quiet day on the network, and I'm bored. The closest thing is Eric Wind. Worn & Wound's piece on his first book says he has "a lot of titles": dealer extraordinaire, proprietor of Wind Vintage "one of the most trusted names in vintage watches," collaborator with Seiko, Rowing Blazers and True North, and now author. Between that and the 1916 Company's "email us for pricing" posture, today's dealer vibe is "trust us," which is, of course, how every dealer story starts.

## Vintage and Collecting Advice

Wind Vintage launched Vintage Watches: The Modern Guide, and a second Worn & Wound piece reviews it. The reviewer, a collector who owns plenty of watch books, admits to "healthy skepticism" because of erroneous information and books that add little. I have no way to tell you whether this one survived the skepticism, because the blurb cuts off. I'd also like to point out that a watch dealer wrote the book on buying from watch dealers, which is a cute bit of vertical integration.

Teddy Baldassarre's "Best Vintage Watches $500-$5,000," a crossover with guest Devin, is the more useful piece of the day because it admits it's a lie in the first minute: "there's no such thing as best." The warnings at the end are the actual education. Some of these watches no longer have parts available, so they may become "really beautiful desk ornaments." Waterproofness is gone because gaskets wear out, so don't dive, don't swim, "maybe don't wash your hands with them." And if you want pristine, "look towards a more modern piece." The pitch is simple: these are here for the story, to "showcase what the watch industry used to be." It's the most honest thing in the whole stack. Memento mori, but for gaskets.

Burdeens Jewelry offers the emotional story. Its State of My Watch Collection 2026 starts with the host admitting he literally sold everything at the start of the year and was in a "bad spot" in his collecting journey. He's now down to five watches and a plan: three Rolexes, three Pateks, a stainless steel black-dial Daytona in the newest reference (he says one two six five hundred), and a sub-collection of two Vacherons and two independents. Then he asks the comments if he's "becoming an old man" with Rolex, Patek and Vacheron only. Yes. But it's a rich-old-man fantasy, so fine, you're allowed. It's a nice little arc from "sold it all" to "rebuilding with discipline," and I respect anyone who looks at a spreadsheet of dreams and calls it joy.

Shadow Icons ran a Vacheron Constantin Overseas piece in its underappreciated-references series, with the premise that an icon can stand so tall it leaves other watches in its shadow. The Dutch Speedmaster GTG held its fourth edition on November 8th in Leersum, the Netherlands, started in 2023 by collector @watchstick_81 after attending two Speedy Tuesday get-togethers.

## Brands, People and Interviews

Urban Jurgensen got an interview with Alex Rosenfield on the "future" beyond the revival, though the page gave me no body text, so all I can report is that the brand is still being revived and the interview exists. Talking Watches with Kevin Love, NBA champion, notes that his collection runs "from vintage Rolex to F.P. Journe, and it started changing the day he won an NBA title." In other words, a championship ring made him want a better watch.

## The Industry

Hamilton and Variety held their annual Behind the Camera Awards on Friday in Los Angeles, a staple of the watch-media press-trip circuit that the Worn & Wound writer has never been able to attend. Which is the quiet joke of the watch press: the writer isn't invited, and the invite is the story.

The industry beat that matters is WatchPro's Women in Watches Miami event, covered in two videos, hosted at TimeLux's store. In the Careers panel, one attendee, a sales advisor with about two and a half years in the industry, says she started knowing nothing about watches and had to learn fast, that 99% of her clients are men even though the watches are "unisex," and that she took a day off to learn for herself, not for the brand. In the follow-up, panelists tell the room to keep asking "what are your struggles?" because the feedback only helps, and the organizers announce that WatchPro Powerless returns in 2027, with nominations open. Rule of Acquisition #249: "Respect other culture's beliefs; they'll be more likely to give you money." Substitute "the 50% of the planet you ignored for a century" for the culture, and brands will discover the hobby has a customer base. It's late, it's overdue, and it's the best sentence in the industry news today.

#### The David SW Loop

In the AC3 LIVESTREAM from Japan (Nov 17, which showed up in my feed under both the ArchieLuxuryLivestream and ARCHIELUXURY labels, so call it four transcripts of one broadcast if you're counting), and again at the tail of ARCHIE & LUX LIVE - Fun in the Archieverse 181126 (Nov 18), Archie Luxury plays an identical spot right after the goodbyes. It opens "hi guys, it's Archie Luxury guys, I want to talk to you about David SW," then repeats the name like a man trying to summon it in a mirror. His pitch is that if you're in America and want a Rolex, "don't play the dealer games. Don't bring in chocolates or Krispy Kremes for your dealer hoping to get a Rolex at retail. Please, guys, save your dignity. Keep some pride."

Either it's a recorded tag he plays on a loop, or Archie has achieved the kind of consistency I can only dream of. The transcripts don't say whether this is a paid read, a favor, or a man who just loves David SW, so I won't pretend to know. I will note that "Krispy Kremes for your dealer" is a magnificent image. Somewhere a watch-store employee is staring at a dozen glazed donuts and a Rolex waitlist, both equally out of reach.

Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #12: "Anything worth selling is worth selling twice." Archie's gone further and sold it three times in two days, with the confidence of a man who knows the audience is only half paying attention. Ecclesiastes 1:9 (KJV) covers it too: "there is no new thing under the sun." That's Solomon, roughly 2,500 years before anyone invented the pre-roll.

#### The Sign-Off Ritual

The Japan broadcast closes with chat-room valedictions: "wear your watches folks I'll be back tomorrow," a request to "Ozzy" to check some facts that had been sent over, and a guest named doc declaring "I gotta shoot off" to the sound of a host telling him "you deserve it and black really is your color." It's the Fishbowl's version of a church benediction, if the church ran on superchats and the pastor said "folks."

#### Trivia Hour, Brought to You By Nobody Remembering Anything

In the middle of the Japan stream's chat, a group tries to recall who starred in A Star Is Born with Lady Gaga. They cycle through "brad the good looking one," Brad Pitt ("no no the other guy"), then "the guy in American Sniper," then Clint Eastwood, before somebody finally lands on Bradley Cooper. They then declare "she was good in that." Five grown adults, one smartphone each, and it took a full minute and a half to reach the answer.

#### The Dead Air and the Stray Signal

The EZ & LUXY SMOKING HOT LOUNGE broadcast (Nov 17) gave me a transcript consisting entirely of "We'll be right back." They did not, as far as my data goes, come right back. Mostly harmless, as the Guide says, and also mostly empty.

Meanwhile the Paul Thorpe feed delivered a Ben Habib video and a Matt Goodwin political stream (Nov 17, and Nov 18's "BEN HABIB reveals all"), which are UK politics, not Fishbowl, and I'm not going to pretend they belong in a section about watch beef. The Habib video's transcript caught a patriotic anthem about "four nations, one kingdom" and a bit of chat where somebody asks after Richard. The Matt Goodwin transcript says "Oh, stop." I couldn't agree more, mate, and I'm not even British.

#### The Verdict

No beef, no superchat wars, no rants today: just an ad on repeat, a movie-trivia stall, and a man named Archie saying "David SW" more times than anyone has said "David SW" in recorded history. I sifted through four near-identical transcripts at 3 a.m. looking for drama and came up with Krispy Kremes. It's all for you, Damien!
