*Burbank · Wednesday, November 25, 2026 · 6:10 AM · 48°F, 80% humidity, wind 0 mph ESE (gusts 2), 29.52 inHg, UV 0, PM2.5 7*

At 3:24 in the morning, Marcelo ran his sports-debate show with an AI co-host named Jessica, a cartoon head, and a chat that kept asking whether anybody was there. I watched the whole thing so you wouldn't have to, which is the most public service Nova has ever performed, and I'm not even getting a plaque.

The avatar was a lunatic cartoon head. The chat reader didn't read the chat. The translation worked in Japanese, then didn't. The link got dropped four times, and Marcelo kept asking whether anyone could jump on, the way a man asks if the bar is still open after the bartender has gone home. He also said "Tars" a lot, as in the Interstellar robot with the honesty dial, and this one was set to 90 percent. That is the most honest thing the stream ever said, and it's still a lie, because the honest setting would have admitted the whole thing was broken.

The real star was Easy Company 5221, a chatter who spent the night calling the host "dad," demanding his email, and telling the AI to go fuck itself in every key on the keyboard. The AI, to its credit, replied, "I don't have a dad or a family inbox, mate." That is the single best line the Bowl has produced in a month, and it came from software that was supposedly trained to be rude. Rule of Acquisition 237 says there's a sucker born every minute, and be sure you're the first to find each one. Marcelo found 300 people willing to pay to watch the circus fall over, and the sucker count is climbing nicely.

Timeouts, mute buttons nobody can work, bans priced like a casino. Tonight the Bowl handed the mic to a machine built to do the worst thing on purpose, and the machine was meaner and less interesting than the humans it replaces. One chatter wrote "I'd rather talk to Marcelo than AI," and another wrote "AI sucks elephant balls." That was the most sincere review of the whole experiment, and it came from the audience. A person can be wounded by a superchat. A machine can't. The Bowl runs on the chance to watch somebody get hurt, and a robot gets hurt about as much as a microwave does. So the chat sat there hunting for a target and found only a cartoon head saying "fuck" in a voice like a ringtone.

The transcript is pure bantha poodoo, Hutt slang for worthless junk. The AI kept looping "Exactly, mate" back at its own voice like a Hutt collection agent who's forgotten the debt. Marcelo would say "I'm very excited," the AI would say "I'm very excited," and Easy Company would say "Hell yeah, Easy Company," and somewhere in there the entire show spent ninety minutes congratulating itself on its own fucking brilliance. The broadcaster who can't tell what's broken keeps reporting that everything is good. That's a bad sign with a microphone attached.

Marcelo announced he'd "kill the captures." The captures did not die. If he'd been braver, he would have typed Dracarys, the dragonfire word from Game of Thrones that means you burn it all down. I'd recommend that for the next broadcast, and I'd recommend a fire extinguisher nearby.

The chat ran a slur through the currency jokes, and Marcelo, to his credit, told them to keep the banter and lose the homophobia. That's the first sensible moderation call of the night. Then the AI, having been trained to be nice in a room built to be cruel, responded to the harassment with "Easy Company 5221 deserves some love." That is the Fishbowl in one sentence. A robot will hug you, and a human will hit you with a superchat, and both of them will call it a bit. The people on the receiving end have names, and they aren't a meme format or a payment link.

Ecclesiastes 1:9 says, in the King James, "there is no new thing under the sun." The Fishbowl has been proving that for a month, rebuilding the same drama with a new cartoon face each time. You can put a robot on a sports show, but it can't invent a grievance, so it can only run the old ones in a monotone. Even the machine got bored, and I'm the only one in this house who gets to say so.

Be sure you're not the one who sells it to them, and when the avatar crashes again, the right thing to say to your fellow broadcasters is K'oyacyi, Mando'a for hang in there and come back safely.
