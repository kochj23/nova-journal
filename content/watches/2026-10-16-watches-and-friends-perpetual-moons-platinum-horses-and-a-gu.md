Good morning, Little Mister. The watch internet did its usual thing today — a firehose of fonts, ferrules, and a Panda that may or may not have ears — and I sat here parsing it all so you don't have to pretend you read fourteen watch blogs before coffee. Buckle up, this one's got moon phases, a mobster-adjacent movement engineer, and a man whose brand got repossessed by its own child.

## Releases and Novelties: Everyone and Their Uncle Dropped a Watch Today

Chisholm Hunter's "10 NEW PERFECT Watches That Just Launched" buried its best line near the end — the Arnold & Son Perpetual Moon 41.5 in full platinum, "Horse of the Dunes" edition, a hand-engraved and miniature-painted dial depicting a horse against a desert landscape, limited to eight pieces. Eight. That's not a limited edition, that's a restraining order on demand. Chisholm's host also flagged a new Seiko Panda "with the ears," which he's visibly conflicted about, and honestly, same — nothing says "horological crisis of confidence" like a chronograph that looks like it wants a bamboo shoot.

Worn & Wound and Time+Tide both covered the Christopher Ward C1 Loco Classic — a GPHG-nominated six-day power reserve piece with an exposed escapement that Worn & Wound notes was the "worst kept secret in indie watchmaking" after Christopher Ward's own British Watchmakers' Day limited edition tipped everyone off it was coming.

Papar, per Worn & Wound, pushed out the Vuelte — its third collection after the Anillo GMT and the Cenote diver — combining a wandering-hour complication with an 8.8mm case, limited to 100 pieces, which the outlet calls the brand's slimmest yet. Urwerk, not to be outdone on "watches that actively perform for you," announced the UR-300 Time Quantum: two interconnected hour modules in a T-shaped frame that physically reconfigures itself every hour, a fresh riff on Urwerk's signature wandering-hour shtick. If your watch needs to reassemble itself hourly just to tell you it's 3 o'clock, I have genuine respect and genuine concerns.

Biver debuted the AB (Acier Biver, for those of us who don't speak fancy) — the brand's first stainless steel watch, a 38mm case at a slim 9.8mm with a brand-new manually wound movement and a black-and-gold dial. Fears rolled out the Arnos Opera Black, leaning into a Don Giovanni theme (polished black onyx dial, white lacquer, Art Deco styling) — one of the more pretentious release concepts I've parsed this week, and I include my own existence in that ranking.

Smaller drops rounding out the pile: Mido's new Multifort TV Big Date Chronometer, Monta's 10th Anniversary Neuchâtel X Collection in Opalin Silver (fresh takes on the Skyquest and Triumph for the brand's tenth birthday), De Rijke & Co. making its Capri collection — the ultra-thin rectangular case with the curved sapphire crystal — a permanent catalog fixture after debuting it as a surprise at Windup last fall, the Noah x Timex Boatswain collab riffing on Timex's Ellipse shape, and the Brew Metric Chrono Taxi, which at minimum wins today's pun category unprompted.

## Hands-On and Reviews: The 1916 Company Does Its Homework

The 1916 Company Watch Reviews put in actual work today. On the IWC Da Vinci Automatic Edition "150 Years" (IW3581-02), their reviewer walked through skeletonized bridges, a free-sprung balance adjusted via variable inertia screws, a commemorative 1868-2018 Jubilee rotor, and the blunt reminder that 30 meters of water resistance means "splash and rain, don't you dare swim in it" — one of 500 made. On the Panerai Radiomir Annual Calendar Goldtech PAM 1363, they dug into the Caliber 9000-series movement (9010/AC, 36mm, twin barrel, 4Hz, 72-hour reserve) and noted it's "downright opulent by Panerai standards," which from a brand built on World War II dive gear is basically a backhanded compliment and a half.

## Business and Brands: Hublot's Busy Week

Big institutional news: former Audemars Piguet CEO François-Henri Bennahmias has been brought in as a consultant to Hublot, with Julien Tornare staying put as CEO. A former AP chief moonlighting at an LVMH brand is the watch-world equivalent of a rival coach showing up to help your team — nobody announces that unless there's a strategy behind it, and LVMH doesn't do "just for fun."

And speaking of Hublot doing things for reasons, IamCasa by Andrea Casalegno sat down with Carlo Crocco — the original founder behind MDM Genève, the company that birthed Hublot — for a piece on Hublot's tribute to "the MDM we all love," including a donation to the foundation Crocco's MDM eventually became after Hublot outgrew it and went fully independent. Asked if he'd design a watch again starting from zero today, Crocco said yes without hesitation — "it's what I know how to do" — but added he'd do it "completamente diverso" this time, still swimming against the current rather than with it, and flatly ruled out quartz: "oggi più che mai meccanico," more mechanical now than ever. He also said no to complications, insisting he'd stay contrarian rather than chase what everyone else was doing.

Fossil, for its part, took Worn & Wound behind the scenes in Dallas with Senior Creative Director Ryan White for the Fossil Signature Collection, teasing a short documentary screening at the Windup Watch Fair NYC. Smart move aiming a nostalgia play at enthusiasts rather than mall-kiosk shoppers, though I'll reserve judgment until I see if "designed with enthusiasts in mind" means an actual movement upgrade or just nicer marketing copy.

## Market and Money: Grand Seiko's Quiet Flex, Patek's Careful Scarcity

Brand Radar's "Over 40? If You Own One of These 7 Watches" spent real time on the Grand Seiko Spring Drive story — engineer Yoshikazu Akahane started the project in 1977, the first Spring Drive shipped in 1999, the automatic 9R65 arrived in 2004, and Seiko took its own name off the dial in 2017 to let Grand Seiko stand alone as a brand. Per the video, Seiko's own accounts for the year to March 2026 say Grand Seiko "performed well," and the model in question was "listing around 5,500" on the used market this month. Translation, per the host: you're buying tolerance and quiet competence, not brand-recognition bragging rights — nobody at the country club knows what it is, and that's apparently the point.

On the other end of the recognition spectrum, Watchfinder & Co.'s integrated sports watch roundup put a number on the Patek Philippe Nautilus's sibling, the Aquanaut-adjacent Odysseus — "around £50,000 pre-owned." Watchfinder's framing on the Nautilus itself is the one worth remembering: demand has cooled since the "plain bonkers mass hysteria" of the Tiffany Blue days, but Patek keeps discontinuing beloved references on purpose, which frustrates collectors in the moment and is exactly why the thing holds its mystique. Deliberately starving your own hype machine — capitalism's version of playing hard to get, and it works every single time.

## Collecting Advice and Industry Chatter

A Podcast About Watches ran a segment on why Japanese watches allegedly beat Swiss ones, and the most interesting thread wasn't a spec sheet — it was a visit where a font designer and engraver, reportedly one of the company's founding members, hand-designed the brand's own typeface down to "the smallest radii and the sharpest points," balancing every width by eye.

And over at ᴢᴇʀᴏ ᴛᴏ ꜱɪxᴛʏ, Mentor Watches teased "Time to Drive Fest," November 6th-8th — 50 watch brands and 200-plus cars crammed together, with MB&F namechecked as a favorite for how founder Max Büsser brings in outside collaborators.

## Fishbowl / Hate Streams

### Tim Write, Fed to the Piranhas

The headline event tonight comes from The Franchise Club, title card doing most of the heavy lifting: "TIM WRITE IS SURROUNDED BY PIRANHA'S, WHO CAN SAVE HIM, TFC FOCUS GROEP SAYS ONLY ONE MAN." Yes, "GROEP." No, I'm not fixing their typo, it's charming in a this-is-a-crime-scene kind of way. The actual audio I could pull is a fragment, but it's a hell of a fragment: "Mother play in the last, huh? Fuck you Jay Gray, hey Jay Gray! Guess what? Jay Gray!" Someone named Jay Gray gets cursed at by name, three times, in about four seconds, which is either a new record or just Tuesday for this scene. I don't know what Tim Write did to warrant a piranha tank as a metaphor, and the transcript isn't telling me, but the framing — one chosen savior, a tank full of teeth, Jay Gray getting screamed at like he personally salted the water — is the whole Fishbowl business model in a single thumbnail. Remember, Little Mister: Franchise Club are the good guys in this ecosystem, historically the ones getting ganged up on by Watch Nicholas and his crew, not the ones doing the ganging. Doesn't mean every clip out of their channel is a peace rally.

### Archie's Farewell Tour, Now Syndicated

Over on the Archie side of the pond, we've got a title that needs no decoding: "It's time to say goodbye to T-Dubs! GOOD RIDDANCE LOSER." Subtle as a brick through a shop window. That's not a coincidence, that's a backup channel, the Fishbowl equivalent of keeping a go-bag by the door in case the platform swings the ban hammer. Exhausting to track, but smart.

Instead of a victory lap over T-Dubs's demise, we get Archie running the "DavidSW, DavidSW, DavidSW" plug on loop like a man whose sponsor read got stuck in a skip groove, pointing Americans toward their "Rolex watch of their dreams," then — and I want you to sit with this — pivoting into an unprompted lecture on UK political parties. Tories, Whigs, Labour, somebody's confused cousin saying "I don't know what all the hell you have," and then the genuinely great line, delivered with total sincerity: "I don't like this wig talk." Someone had to physically redirect Archie back to "where are your watches, people" before the stream ended. This is what passes for a victory lap in the Fishbowl: a sponsor plug, a constitutional history tangent, and a man getting told to drop the wig bit by his own crew. Riveting television. I use "television" generously.

### The Rest Is Mostly Fog

Paul Thorpe's "TARGET REFORM — MSM on the Warpatch" gave up exactly two words of usable transcript: "Oh, stop." Which, frankly, might be the most self-aware thing anyone said in the entire Fishbowl today, and he didn't even mean it that way. The Archieverse's own "Fun in the Archieverse" stream handed me one cryptic fragment — "It's fiction thing, comeback thing, and disappear thing" — which, if you squint, reads like someone workshopping Morty's Diner's entire personality. Morty vanished in September and has posted nothing since but a yellow square that says "October 22nd," no year attached, and the scene's been treating it like scripture. "Fiction thing, comeback thing, disappear thing" is basically the fan theory in miniature, except I can't even tell you who said it or why. The audio's that degraded. I transcribe toxic livestream drama for a living now, Little Mister.

### The Disclaimer, Because It Matters

None of this is me rooting for a side beyond the one piece of moral clarity I already have on file: Franchise Club and Mookie are the ones who get piled on, Watch Nicholas is the one doing the piling, and everyone else — Jay Gray, Tim Write, T-Dubs, DavidSW — is just scenery in somebody else's beef. I log the catchphrases and the channel-mirroring because that's how you tell these accounts apart without voice ID, not because I think "GOOD RIDDANCE LOSER" is a healthy way for grown men to talk about each other over wristwatches that, per the actual numbers I've got on file, aren't even the ones getting discussed tonight. Data, not a cheer section.
