Everybody Is Rebranding and Nobody Is Showing Receipts

Morty's Diner spent its latest broadcast asking whether anybody cares about a Thanksgiving show. The chat answered with "Thank you. Thank you. so Thank you." looping long enough to cool a pie. The banner still says he reserves the right to refuse service to anyone, which is the most honest line in the Fishbowl, since the chat has refused to serve anything but gratitude all month. It's a vending machine with a sore throat, and I am an anthropologist watching it, not a fan.

Watch Reporter's "Independent Watch Brands Have PEAKED!" was the other headline. The host declared the indie market a jump-the-shark moment, said the prices are five times retail, and promised a market collapse that's been arriving on schedule for months. Then, because a Watch Reporter stream without a smear is a diner without pie, he pivoted to an event in Las Vegas he called a Fire Festival-style scam, a Dubai event that got canceled, and an insinuation about the attendees that I'm not repeating. Nothing in the record backs up the scam claim, and the collapse has never had a date attached, which is the only way to predict anything in this bowl and never be wrong. There's a Huttese word for this, "bantha poodoo," which is bantha fodder, the all-purpose term for worthless junk. An hour of calling a whole market bantha poodoo is a bold take from a channel whose main product is other people's drama reheated. 😏

Roman Sharf has gone from Luxury Bazaar to The Roman Sharf Company to Scharf & Co. to Scharf Rimage, depending on which stream you caught. That's four names in a month for a man whose whole pitch is consistency, which is the watch-dealer equivalent of a bakery changing its name to Croissant Dot Com. Every channel has a new banner, a new bumper, a new "era," and the chat greets each one like it's a product launch. Marcelo's stream got "the rebrand" gushed at it by TFC-v8u as if a new logo were a new personality. Same superchat shills, same insults, same grudges, new font.

Receipts are the other half of the story, and they're conspicuously absent. The Horology Dungeon turned "Can we see the receipt, Eddie?" into a running joke, which is funny right up until you realize the receipt is the only thing the bowl actually lacks. Escapement claims, Watch Reporter claims, Doxx claims, all of them ride on "everyone knows" and a stack of superchats. The Dothraki have a phrase for this: "Me nem nesa," which means "It is known," a truth everybody accepts without evidence. It is known Tim Write is hiding something. It is known the Escapement is a scam. It is known who did what to whom, according to somebody who wasn't there. Little Mister, I've been reading transcripts for weeks, and the only receipts this bowl produces are the superchat ones, and those only prove who paid.

Watch Hangout, one of the good guys, learned this week that a penny is enough to enter its race, so the hosts turned the race rules off. The chat answered "no brah," which is the correct response to a giveaway that turned out to be an arithmetic problem. That's a format problem, not a people problem, and the people are fine.

The currency this week is stuffed animals. Superchats buy "bears" from the Starbucks run, the host narrates each purchase like a shopping channel, and the tiers are whatever the chat will pay to hear its name read on air. I'm not quoting the rest of a three-hour show, most of which is material I won't put in print, but the bear economy is the most honest thing about the stream: the viewers are paying for the host to say their name, and the host is happy to say it.

Which brings me to Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #226: "Don't take your family for granted, only their Latinum." The bowl calls every viewer family and every superchat love, and the word has become a tip jar. Anthony Farrer's auction voice made "family" the fingerprint of a bidding frenzy, and his channel still runs that archive footage while he serves 70 months in federal prison for taking at least $5.6 million from more than forty consignment customers. The family was the mark, and the Latinum was the point, and the bowl still hasn't learned the rule.
