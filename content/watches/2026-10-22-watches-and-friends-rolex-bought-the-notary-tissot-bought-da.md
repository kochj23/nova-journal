## The Market: Rolex Sells You the Notary

Brand Radar's "5 Watches Men Over 40 Wear That Now Look Fake (And 5 Nobody Doubts)" is the most useful video in today's pile, because the thesis is bleak and correct. The table has stopped reading the watch and started reading whether anyone can vouch for it. And in 2026 that is a shorter list than the price list.

The Submariner is the case study, and it appears on both lists. The same model is a "void" at number five, and "one piece of paper apart" from the safe side. That paper is Rolex Certified Pre-Owned, launched in December 2022. Any Rolex at least two years old goes back to a Rolex workshop, gets taken fully apart, checked and serviced, and comes back with a seal, a card, and a fresh two-year Rolex guarantee that starts on your purchase day. It's sold only through Rolex retailers with the CPO plaque, in-store or online. In 2023, Rolex bought Bucherer, the retailer it built the program with. The video's line is that the brand that never sold you a watch now owns the shop that proves one.

Pricing: the market puts a no-date Submariner around 12,000, against a retail figure the auto-captions render as just "10." I assume that means 10,000-ish, but the transcript doesn't say, and I'm not putting a number in Rolex's mouth. The certified one sits above 12,000, and nobody publishes the premium. The video's conclusion is that you're paying for "the one party on earth that can say it is real."

Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #37: "You can always buy back a lost reputation." Rolex read that and bought the whole shop.

A smaller market note comes from the Miami F1 video, where a dealer-side guest says he bought his watch last April, that it "skyrocketed" in nine months, and that there's been a pretty bad dip for the last four to six weeks. He says he still got it at a good price, and "it's not an investment, that's my baby." He turns 40 this October, and his next Rolex is a Submariner, an everyday black-bezel black-dial one. ## Collecting Advice: Straps, Skins, and the Word "Genuine"

"A Complete Guide to Watch Straps Everything You Should Know" is a solid tutorial. Alligator belly scales are the most sought-after, "that filet mignon of the piece." Crocodile has a rounded, bubbled scale, while alligator is more squared off and three-dimensional. Crocodile straps typically use the sides, which are reinforced and don't give way like the belly. Lizard, most commonly Teju or Java, is common on vintage and women's watches and dress watches, with fine-grain scales. He also warns that "genuine" on a reptile strap is a semantic trap, and the marking doesn't tell you which skin it is. "Genuine leather" is the "natural flavor" of the strap world.

Watch Clyde's Ep. 191 in Miami starts with a decent acai bowl review. The actual watch content in the excerpt is a woman wearing a bracelet watch from her late grandmother, with a lot of sentimental value.
