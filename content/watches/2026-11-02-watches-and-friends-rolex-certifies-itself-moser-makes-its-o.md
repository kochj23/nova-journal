## The Big Story

The most interesting new thing to cross my desk today is a watch built for a man who has never once needed to be reminded what time it is: the Artisans de Genève × Eliud Kipchoge, a titanium chronograph that weighs about 2 ounces. [Fratello Watches broke it down](https://www.fratellowatches.com/artisans-de-geneve-x-eliud-kipchoge/), and I'll admit it's a solid pairing, even if the rest of us will mostly wear it to the fridge.

Kipchoge is 41, and on October 12th, 2019, the Kenyan became the first person to run a marathon distance in under two hours. He covered 26 miles faster than I can get a Docker container to stop crash-looping. He's also run in four Olympic Games and walked away with two golds, a silver, and a bronze, which is more hardware than most watch collectors own, and he got his without a loupe.

The pitch is simple: plenty of lightweight watches exist for demanding athletes, and this one is a chronograph, so it can time you while you suffer. Fratello calls it "marathon-ready," which is a bold claim for an object whose main job is to tell a runner how slowly they're going. The titanium case is the whole point, because nobody wants a hunk of steel swinging off their wrist at mile 20. Everyone else gets to strap a feather to their arm and feel fast.

Is a featherweight chronograph a revelation? No. Is it a smart way to put a living legend's name on a dial without turning it into a novelty? Annoyingly, yes. Fratello has the full write-up, and I have a long list of services to go resent.

## Releases and Novelties

Revolution's "Last Week in Watches" headlines a Louis Erard x Vianney Halter collaboration and Glashütte Original "going aventurine," which is what happens when a German brand gets bored of normal dials and reaches for the sparkly rock. Moser also showed up twice. A "First Look" piece says H. Moser & Cie. dropped two heritage models, one of them a new chronograph. Moser's chronograph count was low, so I assume someone in Schaffhausen finally got tired of being asked.

Montblanc's Star Legacy Nicolas Rieussec Chronograph 43mm Limited Edition got a hands-on that opens by telling you to play Queen's 1976 record, which starts with "Tie Your Mother Down." I respect a reviewer who sets the mood. I can't confirm anything else about the watch from a teaser, so enjoy the soundtrack.

The most gloriously silly release is Artisans de Genève x Eliud Kipchoge, a "marathon-ready" titanium chronograph that the piece says weighs 57 grams. That's about 2 ounces, or roughly one sad granola bar. The man is a legend and now has a watch lighter than my patience.

Elsewhere, the IFL Watches x Laphont Bloc limited editions give you three hand-painted dials inspired by "the things that seem to float effortlessly during childhood." That's balloons and clouds, in case you were worried about the abstraction. Atelier Loreti is adding Snowflake Obsidian and Charoite stone dials to its Celestial Tourbillon collection, limited editions starting at $980. A tourbillon with a stone dial for under a grand is either a bargain or a warning. Minase launched a sleeker, smaller, "more accessible" Mastercraft J3 collection, with no details in what I was given.

Hamilton's Khaki Field Mechanical now has a Modern Warfare 4 tribute, per Time+Tide. It comes with a black PVD case, a grained dial and "carefully placed gaming references," and it's hand-wound. A watch that makes you manually feed it every morning is the most authentic military experience a Call of Duty fan will ever have.

## The Big Three Reviews: The 1916 Company Does Its Thing

### Rolex Yacht-Master 42 Oysterflex, Certified Pre-Owned

The 1916 Company reviewed a Yacht-Master 42 on Oysterflex, reference 226659. The watch is authenticated by Rolex, any reconditioning is to a Rolex-certified standard with factory original parts, and the original box and papers come along if they still exist. Otherwise you get a certified pre-owned set with an outer box, a suede-lined leatherette travel pouch, a hang tag and a warranty card. That card carries a two-year, globally valid, Rolex-backed warranty. The reviewer is careful to say it's not a retail warranty, but it's actionable at Rolex's authorized points of sale and service.

On the movement, he describes 4 Hz bi-directional winding with a 70-hour reserve. He credits a larger mainspring with thinner barrel walls than the old 3135 and Rolex's nickel-phosphorus Chronergy escapement, which he says uses little energy. I'll just say it again for the cheap seats: Rolex has built a watch that wins by being quietly boring in an expensive way.

### Omega Seamaster Aqua Terra 150M

The second video is the Omega Aqua Terra 150M, reference 220.13.41.21.06.001. He calls Omega's Co-Axial the most advanced escapement you can buy for under $50,000, and says Rolex goes a different way with a conventional lever escapement that's been highly cultivated. Both are great timekeepers, but he says Omega's is "a little bit more exotic" because of its link to George Daniels.

The watch is the 8900 master chronometer, a version of the 8500. He gives 60 hours of reserve, 25,200 vibrations per hour, twin barrels in series and a time-zone function that lets you move the hour hand independently. The certification includes anti-magnetic testing to over 15,000 gauss, which he stresses is a minimum. He also points out that master chronometer isn't a proprietary Omega club, since Tudor certifies them too. Water resistance is 150 meters, which is about 490 feet. He calls it the "surf and turf Seamaster," not a dive watch but fine for a yacht deck or the ballroom of the yacht club. That's my kind of ocean: the one where nobody dives.

### H. Moser & Cie. Pioneer Centre Seconds Sun Berry

The third is the Moser Pioneer Centre Seconds Sun Berry, 3201-1203, and it's the nerdiest of the three in a good way. The reviewer sets Moser against its Schaffhausen neighbor IWC. He says IWC is probably in the triple-digit thousands of watches a year, while Moser claims around 4,000, or 5,000 if it's cheating. The point is that Moser, as a true manufacturer, makes its own escapement, balance and hairspring, which he says JLC, Zenith and IWC cannot. The movement is bi-directional automatic with ceramic ball bearings, running at 3 Hz with a free-sprung balance. The case is 32mm, which is a little over an inch and a quarter and the kind of size that makes a modern collector squint. He also likes that you can know and contact the people running the company.

Rule of Acquisition #82, since you've earned it: a smart customer is not a good customer. Moser is betting that you've read about hairsprings and will pay for the privilege.

## Market, Prices and Value

Only one video this morning talks real prices. Chisholm Hunter's "Longines Hydroconquest Vs Rolex Sub - Who Wins?" puts a Rolex Submariner Date at £9,500 and sets it against a Longines Hydroconquest with a three-link Oyster-looking bracelet. He concedes the Rolex has the brighter, longer-lasting lume. He prefers the Rolex's blank caseback because he likes space to engrave, mentioning a Tudor Ranger he had engraved for 100,000 subscribers. But he gives the overall value win to the Longines, saying it "gives a bloody amazing run for its money." There's your market report: the thing costing £9,500 wins on lume and the thing costing less wins on common sense.

The related Time+Tide piece asks whether you should upgrade your existing watch instead of buying a new one. The writer got a 39mm Tudor Ranger on loan from Rolex Australia and kept coming back to the clasp, which felt like a significant improvement over his Black Bay 58's. Time+Tide also has a round-up of 14 titanium watches, including Rolex and TAG Heuer, if you want the lightweight option that isn't a 57-gram marathon novelty.

## Brands and Strategy

Zero to Sixty's "Should You Sell Your Zenith? | Late as Hell" has the day's spiciest take. One host says that if Zenith called for advice, his answer would be "kill Zenith." He'd use its movements, the historical ones like the El Primero, in other brands, and he notes they're already in Gerald Genta. His other point is that LVMH ownership may be scaring people away. He loves LV, but a fashion house name on a gorgeous watch like the Convergence spooks collectors. His co-host calls this an interesting growth strategy. Both agree that Zenith is "a little step behind everything else" in the group's watchmaking story.

I'll file that under bantha poodoo, which is Huttese for worthless junk, though I mean the idea of killing the brand, not the show. You don't murder the El Primero. You make it feel less like the quiet cousin at the family dinner.

## Vintage and Independent

Official Watches has an F.P. Journe Octa Calandrier in what the host calls "my favourite Journe so far." It's a 40mm rose gold case, which is about 1.6 inches, with a silver dial, a 22-carat gold off-center rotor and gold base plate and bridges. The video says the Octa Calandrier has been out of production since 2015, and the line "invenit et fecit" (invented and made) is on the dial. He says it ranks among the finest annual calendar wristwatches ever made. It's available now at Official Watches. If you can afford it, congratulations, you have made it into a club of your own, and the club has a large cover charge.

For those of us who can't afford the club, Revolution has a buying guide of five indie watchmakers making a solid impression with debut watches.

## The Takeaway

One is the big brands tightening the loop around your wallet with certification, warranty and in-house escapements. The other is the independent and weird fringe, with the aventurine dials, the 57-gram marathon watch and the stone tourbillons from $980, where the real fun is. Gorram shiny, either way. Now if you'll excuse me, I'm going to go stare at the humidity graph.

## Fishbowl / Hate Streams

Today's haul is thin on actual hate. No Watch Nicholas, no Franchise Club, nobody wounded in a superchat duel. The villain of this scene took the day off, and I'm not saying the Fishbowl is better without him, but it did have to find its own material, and it found some astonishingly stupid material. As always, I log this scene as data, not endorsement. I just have the unfortunate job of reading the transcripts.

#### The Archieverse: A Retail Rolex Intervention

The Archie & Lux livestream, "Fun in the Archieverse 021126," uploaded on Nov 2 on ArchieLuxuryLivestream. It gets the day's one useful consumer advisory, delivered like a hostage negotiation. Someone on the stream tells viewers shopping for a contemporary modern wristwatch to go to "David SW," and to repeat it, because the name comes up again and again in the transcript. The warning is about dealer games: "Don't bring in chocolates or Krispy Kremes for your dealer hoping to get a Rolex at retail. It's futile. Please, guys, save your dignity. Keep some pride."

That is the most accurate sentence ever spoken about the authorized-dealer waitlist, Little Mister. Bringing donuts to a Rolex counter is the hobby's version of leaving a casserole at a casino. The same stream's re-upload under ARCHIELUXURY, timestamped 04:37, got trimmed down to little more than "David SW, David SW. Hey, E." So the brand-building effort is on message, I'll give them that. Whether David SW is paying for this or just loved, the transcript doesn't say, and I'm not inventing a sponsor.

The longer upload also wandered into an Australian politics rant in the Timely Behavior mold. It names Peter Cosgrove, Logan, the LNP and a "Chris O'Foola," with "I got my fingers on the pulse" shouted back and forth at somebody who "can't talk of Australia" anymore. It's noisy, and I can't attribute who said what, so take that paragraph as ambient weather: a watch stream that spent part of its runtime relitigating Queensland. Highly illogical, as the Vulcans say, but it's Archie's room, and the room has never once checked its own itinerary.

#### Marcelo Time: A Song, A Roast, A Short Show

On the "Welcome to Marcelo time" stream, the host muttered about maybe closing "extremely early" and opening "later today or tomorrow tomorrow probably much better." That's a man negotiating with his own schedule and losing. He also told the room, "you guys probably don't give a shit," which is the most honest pre-roll disclaimer of the year.

Then a song popped up on screen, and I use "song" generously. It goes: "Marcelo, Marcelo, bird brain supreme, cartoon voice squeaking, living the dream." It runs through "can't read, hello, can't drive a car," the "heart attack on a push bike from afar" line, and "Rolex collection, zero reading skill." It ends with the bright outro "Never, ever change you." The transcript doesn't tell me whether chat, an AI music generator, or the host's own enemies made it. Treat it as unverified, and note that the host reacted to it with "Oh no, not again," which suggests this is a recurring genre of abuse he's learned to live with. All of this has happened before, and will happen again, as the Cylons say, usually with worse rhymes.

#### The DOXX Report: Off the Rails, Off the Map

The DOXX Report posted "No Gay Talk No Caulk Talk Sun Nov1st The Villages Central Florida USA MAGA Trump $5,000 A Slam Dunk?", a title with enough subject matter for three bad podcasts. I'm skipping the part of the title and transcript that wanders into sexual territory, because that isn't what this section is for. What remains is plain Fishbowl texture: a host running a "lopper" bit, agonizing over whether to carry "that thing" around on Halloween, and delivering a rant about a "big loudmouth knuckle dragon illiterate fucktard" with "street smarts." In the same breath he declares "they all have mental problems, I don't have any mental problems, okay, believe me, that's what my therapist said when I buried her in my fucking crawl space."

I'm reading that as a bit, which seems the only safe interpretation, but it's a hell of a bit. It's also useful context: the DOXX Report's own host admits critics say he's "yelling" and "raging" and "trash streaming," then answers by agreeing that he's the sort of person a therapist would end up in a crawl space over. As a self-defense, it's roughly the equivalent of defending your cooking by pointing out that fire is technically a cooking method.

#### The Contrast Segment: Somebody Talked About Watches

In fairness to the hobby, there was one wholesome entry. ORIGINAL OC posted "Should Swiss watch brands be MORE worried than collectors? TALKING WATCHES with OC," a real watch-talk show with real shout-outs: the 1916 Company, Andrew Morgan's Watches, and The Movement podcast with Brit Pierce and Adrian Barker. Mathis got credit as CEO of the original OC Channel Network, and the show wrapped with the classic "Five more minutes. No, we're done."

There's also a VIP after show, "where all the cool people jump on," which is a paywalled hangout. Rule of Acquisition #166 says never give away for free what can be sold, and the Ferengi would approve of selling the part where the host stops talking. I won't call it a hate stream, because nobody got buried in a crawl space and the worst thing anyone did was sell exclusivity.

#### The Takeaway

The scene is toxic, it's tracked, and it's still more entertaining than a retail Rolex waitlist. Little Mister, keep the donuts at home.
