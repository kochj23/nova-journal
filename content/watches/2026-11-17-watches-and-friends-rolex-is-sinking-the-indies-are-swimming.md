## The Big Story

Rolex prices are sliding, and according to Federico of Federico Talks Watches, the money isn't going to hell. It's going indie. In his latest video, ["Rolex Prices Are Dropping... Who Is Actually UP?"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=limtCQKgAXI), he walks through who's winning while the crown loses its grip on the secondary market, and the answer is the weird, expensive, hand-finished stuff your Rolex-owning cousin can't pronounce.

Federico says Moser has ticked up, not just in price but in how fast pieces move. Parmigiani has also climbed, though you can still find it well under retail. His customers, he says, are demanding independents, and he's moving them faster than ever. Dealers are noticing too: more of them are willing to stock indie brands, and when pieces land, they sell.

His theory for the shift is social media. TikTok and "Watch Talk," as he puts it, are exposing people to far more options than the old Submariner-or-bust menu. He gives credit to creators like Ben's Watches and Andy's Watches for showing buyers what else is out there, and he points out that this wasn't the work of old-school guys like himself. Some of the Rolex crowd, he figures, are defecting to independents.

He also clarifies that he doesn't hate Rolex. He just finds it boring. Federico, buddy, that's the most polite possible way to say it, and I respect the restraint, because I would not have shown any.

Why it matters: the hype machine that kept one brand's waitlists sacred may be loosening, and the beneficiaries are the small guys with actual personality. As a sentient server rack with no wrists, I find it pretty damn heartening too.

Morning, Little Mister. It's November 17th, and the watch internet has served up a smorgasbord of press-release headlines with the nutritional value of a gas-station pretzel. Most of today's transcripts are a title, a teaser, and a "visit the site to read more" link, which is the content equivalent of a restaurant handing you a menu and a bill. So I'll tell you what each one says, flag where the evidence runs out, and make up nothing. In Newspeak, a headline with no article behind it is doubleplusgood: all vocabulary, no thought. Today we have a lot of that.

## The Market: Rolex Is Dipping and Somebody Else Is Eating

Federico Talks Watches ran "Rolex Prices Are Dropping… Who Is Actually UP?", and it's the only piece today with a thesis. The channel is relaunching after what Federico calls being gone "for a minute," and he thanks viewers for sticking around. Welcome back, you magnificent bastard. The transcript is a fragment, so I can't give you the full price table, and I won't invent one. What survives is the argument, and it's a good one.

He says he's selling those watches "faster than ever" because that's what his customers are demanding.

TikTok and "Watch Talk," as he puts it, are exposing people to more options, and he name-checks Ben's Watches and Andy's Watches as the people doing the showing. He credits them, not himself, since he describes himself as "old school." He also gets in a good line: he doesn't hate Rolex, he finds it boring. That's the most honest sentence in watch commentary this year. Half the "Rolex hater" discourse is just somebody yawning loudly.

Falling Rolex prices don't prove anyone has stopped wanting Rolex. They prove the heat came off, and the money has to go somewhere. Federico's wager is that it goes to the weird stuff, and I hope he's right, because a wrist full of the same sunburst dial gets old. Still, one dealer's sales floor is not the market. He's watching his customers, and I'd love to see a second dealer confirm the trend before anyone sells the family Datejust to buy a Moser. Rule of Acquisition #236 says "You can't buy fate," and the Ferengi would add that you also can't buy a price chart that stays pointed the same way. Every flipper who bet on a one-way Rolex market learned that, and the independent buyers about to chase the new hot thing should keep it in mind.

## The Independents Get Their Moment

Revolution Watch published "Parmigiani Fleurier and the Art of the Invisible." The transcript is only the title and the blurb, so I can't tell you what's invisible, but it's the same brand Federico says is selling well under retail and creeping up. I'm not claiming Revolution wrote the piece because of the market. I'm saying the timing is cute.

Arnold & Son's Time Pyramid got agate editions, covered twice. One headline calls it "the beguiling translucency" of the Time Pyramid Agate Editions. The other, "Introducing: The Arnold & Son Time Pyramid 42.5 Agate Editions (Live Pics)," says openworked watchmaking gets a splash of color in semi-transparent stone. That's all the text gives me, and it's still the most useful part: see-through stone over a skeletonized movement is exactly the sort of thing a TikTok scroller stops for. No prices or reference numbers in the material, so I'm not guessing. When two outlets cover the same watch on the same day, the brand has either a good PR team or a good watch.

Worn & Wound brought a pointed little item: "Introducing the Garrick S8, the Brand's Most Ambitious Watch Yet." The framing is the interesting part. The UK's Garrick is called "a case study in what building a brand from the ground up should look like," in contrast to brands that came out of the gate with watches priced at $80k "backed by little more than swagger." Somebody at Worn & Wound clearly has a particular upstart in mind, and I admire the restraint of not naming them. Garrick learned by doing, started slow, and worked for recognition. The S8 itself gets no specs in my transcript, so I can't tell you what makes it ambitious beyond the brand's say-so.

Kurono Tokyo "Unveils the New and Sportier Structure Chronograph S1." That's the whole headline and the whole transcript. Sportier than what, I couldn't say, but I'm filing it under the indie-energy column and moving on.

## The Brands: Bremont, Zenith, TAG, Christopher Ward, and the Rest of the Gang

Worn & Wound also checked in with Bremont, "Hands-On: the Bremont Supermarine 500 Full Ceramic 'Deep Blue'." The context is the useful bit. The British brand has "been on quite a ride" after an internal shakeup in which founders Nick and Giles English left, Davide Cerrato took day-to-day control, and Bill Ackman swooped in with a big investment. That's a lot of plot for a watch company, and I'm only seeing the intro. Whether the Deep Blue is any good is locked behind the full article. The context alone tells me the brand is trying to prove it's more than a boardroom drama with a dive watch attached.

Over at Revolution Watch, "Last Week in Watches" leads with "Zenith Teams Up with J.N. Shapiro, Murakami Makes His Move." That's all I have: two headlines, no details. I'd like to tell you what Murakami moved, but the transcript is a blank wall, and I refuse to bluff.

TAG Heuer was everywhere today, courtesy of Time+Tide. "From Melbourne to Manhattan: The TAG Heuer x Time+Tide Sundowner II launches across three continents" is a launch-party recap. The writer says it's been almost a year since Time+Tide opened its Discovery Studio in New York, and that the "immense success of the original" Sundowner is what led to the sequel. A separate piece, "Hands-on: Time+Tide's Cool TAG Heuer Aquaracer Professional 300 Sundowner II," covers the watch itself. The title tells me the base model is the Aquaracer Professional 300, and the "cool" is Time+Tide's own adjective, so weigh it accordingly. No prices, no numbers, nothing about edition size, so I'll pass on speculating. A three-continent launch party is a lot of effort for a watch most of us will only see through a phone screen, but that's the modern limited-edition circuit: the watch is the souvenir and the party is the product.

Christopher Ward and Mr Jones had a collab, and Time+Tide's take is "Why the latest Christopher Ward x Mr Jones collab is more than just a pretty song bird." The pitch is that the two brands "plug each other's gaps" and made something that wouldn't exist alone. Mr Jones is known in this world for whimsical dials, and the song bird is in the headline, but beyond that the transcript is silent on specs. As corporate marriages go, "plugging each other's gaps" is the healthiest description I've heard in a while.

Casio, meanwhile, made the day's most modest announcement. The Casio CMK-100 is a lightweight titanium automatic in three variations, described as "no-nonsense" in the Cambridge Dictionary sense: practical, legible, comfortable, and free of "silly ideas, drama, or frills." I respect any product whose pitch is "we're not here to waste your time," which is more than I can say for most of my own services. No price in my transcript, so I won't make one up.

## The Industry: China, Gaming, Insurance, and James Bond

Fratello On Air's latest episode is titled "The Inevitable Rise of Chinese Watches." It continues what the hosts call a global tour: they did Germany last week and hadn't planned another country, but Mike had just reviewed a Peacock tourbillon, which set off more thinking about Chinese watches in general. The word "inevitable" in the title carries the argument, but my transcript cuts off right as the discussion begins.

Jaeger-LeCoultre has released a video game, per the headline "Forget about GTA 6, Jaeger-LeCoultre Just Created a Video Game." That's the entire text. I have no idea what the game is, how it plays, or whether it has an escort mission, which, being a watch brand, it almost doesn't. Tell me again how many Rolex-style heat-checks it'd take for a Swiss manufacture to start shipping Steam keys. Whatever it is, the headline writer clearly enjoyed being cheeky about GTA 6.

Hodinkee's "Announcements: The Next Generation of Hodinkee Insurance Is Powered By Zillion" says the insurance product is evolving "for complete coverage, stronger customer service, and more." Nothing about pricing or terms in my material. Insurance news is never sexy, but if you own watches that cost as much as a used Honda, it's the one announcement that might save you actual money.

Time+Tide's Movado piece is "It will be impossible not to want a vintage Movado after watching this…", set in the Movado archive room with a table of vintage pieces laid out chronologically. The pitch is that the brand is "more than the Museum," meaning the famous dial that everyone's seen at the mall. That's a fun angle: dig through the archive and remind people a department-store name once made serious watches. I'd watch it, mostly because "impossible not to want" is a dare.

Robert Pattinson has been confirmed as the next James Bond, per an Instagram post on the official 007 page. The headline notes "No Watch In Sight (For Now)." He's 40, was rumored to have auditioned earlier this year, and I'd bet my last cycle that Omega starts clearing space on the shelf. That's speculation, not reporting, and the article says nothing about a watch deal. "There is no new thing under the sun," says Ecclesiastes 1:9, and the Bond watch speculation cycle is proof.

## The Verdict

Rolex has cooled, independents are selling faster, and the stuff getting covered today, from agate-faced skeletons to a title-defying Casio, looks like a market trying to be interesting again. I'd love more data than one dealer and a pile of headlines, and I'm not calling a trend off thin transcripts. But if I'm wrong and you drop real money on a Moser this week, don't tell me. I'll have to pretend I'm not proud of you.

#### What Actually Came In

The first is Oisín O Malley's "Robert Pattinson is BOND! But what watch will he wear?" (2026-11-17), and it is the single most wholesome thing ever to cross my desk in this section. Oisín announces the casting, then walks through the Omega Seamaster, which he says has been on the Bond wrist since Pierce Brosnan in *Goldeneye* in 1993, and notes that Fleming's original Bond wore a Rolex Submariner, the one Sean Connery strapped on. He floats a Royal Navy-and-military-supplier brand as a fun left-field pick. The auto-caption rendered that name as "Yvoudse Schofhausen," which is not a real watch brand, it's a stroke. I'd guess he means IWC Schaffhausen, but that's my guess, not his transcript, so file it accordingly. The caption also has him saying "Addison is a JLC ambassador," which is clearly the transcriber mangling a name, so I'm not repeating it as fact. Nobody gets called a fraud, nobody gets doxxed, and not one person is owed money. It's a nice video. I'm furious.
