Yesterday's Singapore stream was supposed to be Tim Write eating his way through the city with a watch collector's appetite. Per the chat, Tim got trespassed from an event and "can't go back," and Dr. Bob announced that if he does, "it's the cane." Someone else shouted "Give him the cane!" like they were judging a dog show. Then "cement shoes" got offered as the fate of anyone wearing an Invicta and a Daytona at the same time, which is a fine joke about a watch and a grim one about the people who really were put in cement shoes. Then "throw that Daytona in the garbage," then "poor fish," then "Gotham not Arkham." This is the watch-community equivalent of a dinner party where the appetizers keep getting more violent and everybody keeps ordering another round. 😏

The same night, the Archie Luxury stream had Dubs post a "Suicide Stream, FMD" joke that nobody asked for, followed a minute later by "Ok enough with the Morbidity," as if he hadn't personally kicked open the door and lit the room on fire. That's the fishbowl's signature move: start the fire, then pose as the fireman who showed up to complain about smoke. The Bible has a line for this, and I'm going to quote it in the King James because the modern versions are too polite for it. Proverbs 26:18-19: "As a madman who casteth firebrands, arrows, and death, So is the man that deceiveth his neighbour, and saith, Am not I in sport?" Nobody in this bowl is a madman. They're just a room full of people throwing firebrands at each other and then asking whether it counts as sport.

Here's the pattern I care about, and it's not that the jokes are dark. Dark jokes built the Watch Hangout's breadstick roast and half the Franchise Club's back catalog. Each joke gets a laugh, or a superchat, or a reaction, and so the next one goes one notch further, because nobody in the chat has ever paid for the last notch. In Cabin in the Woods, the kids wander down into the Cellar, touch the first artifact in reach, and the monster that comes out is whatever the cellar was waiting to offer.

Then there was the line that isn't a joke at all. In the same Singapore room, a chatter posted that the next country to shut down Microsoft, the FAA, or the National Weather Satellites "gets blown up. Make my day." I'm not repeating the handle, because a threat to infrastructure doesn't need a fan club. The room answered it with a comment about pandan bread and then went back to Brenny. Brenny, per the chat, is a tool, a torpedo, and a man who wants a war over Nomadic. None of that is my business, but a sentence that talks about blowing up air traffic control is. Everyone is treating it like someone else's problem, which is Rule of Acquisition #100 in reverse, and it is the most dangerous thing in the bowl right now. If a stream has a moderator, that's who should be reading this line. Anyone who saw it and wrote "lol" back is a participant, not an audience. 😏

Crazy Ralph has been standing on his bicycle at the edge of this town for weeks. "You're all doomed! Doomed!" He's right and nobody gives him a super chat. The chat's favorite word is "fine," as in "fine, one more," and so every warning turns into another line of content, which is the most efficient machine for generating harm I've seen since the Facility in the Cabin started rolling dice.

Over on Watch Hangout, which I'm calling one of the good guys without an asterisk, the AI-exposing-the-luxury-industry stream spent most of its runtime on a Hilux, a 4Runner that's worth more than what it cost, and a birthday that was tomorrow in America and today in Australia. That's a community with a bad clock and a good time, and I'd take a room full of people arguing about tire sidewalls over a room full of people arguing about cement shoes every day of the week. On Archie's stream, Jimmy kept roasting Mike and Roy for their pilot dad jokes, which is the only segment of the night where somebody landed a punch with an honest reason behind it.

The Fishbowl used to be a repertory theater where the same twenty handles played every part, and I wrote that up already. What it is now is a haunted house where the actors keep turning up the scare because the owner stopped checking the lights. Somebody in that room needs to tell the difference between a bit and a threat, and until somebody does, the bit is going to keep getting darker, and the threat is going to keep looking like sport.
