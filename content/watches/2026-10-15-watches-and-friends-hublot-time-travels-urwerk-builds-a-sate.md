## Watch News

Slow news day in the sense that nobody blew up a brand's reputation, and busy in the sense that every outlet on earth decided October 15th was "throwback Thursday" day. I did not make that last part up. I wish I had.

### Releases: everybody's doing a victory lap on old ideas

The story of the day is the **Hublot Classic Fusion Tributo**, and it got the full gauntlet treatment — Revolution Watch ran it as "Hublot Hits Rewind," Time+Tide called it "looks to the past and future with serious upgrades," aBlogtoWatch-style ran both a "First Look" with video and a straight "Introducing," and Hodinkee-style coverage framed it as "Hublot Takes Us Back To The Beginning," noting dryly that this is the brand's third attempt at honoring its own inaugural MDM model and that "the third time's the charm." The actual meat is in the piece that sat down with 81-year-old Carlo Crocco in Nyon, a week before his 82nd birthday: forty-six years ago he strapped a gold case to a rubber strap and the entire industry told him he'd lost his mind. Now it's a heritage flex. That's the whole luxury watch business in one sentence, Little Mister — do something that gets you laughed out of Baselworld, wait four decades, repackage it as "visionary."

Urwerk, meanwhile, is still Urwerk, which means instead of just making a watch they built a wrist-mounted contraption again. The **UR-300 Time Quantum** got "Introducing" treatment from two outlets plus a Revolution piece on the brand's "Wandering Hours Take a New Shape." The consistent thread across coverage: this is the satellite-display playbook Urwerk has run for years, but with, per the writeup, "a new second twist and a jump so fast it'll knock your socks off." I'll believe the sock-removal claim when I see it in person. Until then, file under "Urwerk solved a problem nobody asked them to solve, beautifully."

Smaller stuff, rapid fire because there's a lot of it: **Hermès** dropped two new colorways on the Glass Fibre H08 — Bleu de Malte and Blanc Chantilly — in a brighter version of that glass-fibre case than we've seen from them before. **IWC** is getting a four-month head start on the Year of the Goat (which doesn't technically start until February 6, 2027 — IWC apparently runs on its own calendar) with the Pilot's Watch Performance Chronograph 41, ref. IW388310, limited to 500 pieces. **Arnold & Son** put out the DSTB Stop-Seconds, which does exactly what it says — sets you dead to the second, a party trick that used to mean something before quartz ruined the bit. **Jaeger-LeCoultre** quietly released the 101 Chain, which looks like jewelry because it basically is one, built around the smallest mechanical movement on Earth — the kind of flex that only matters to people who already own six other JLCs. **Louis Vuitton** gave the cult Monterey a ceramic facelift, 37mm black and green cases with lacquered dials and an in-house automatic, which is LV's way of saying "yes, we're a real watch brand now, stop laughing." And **Ming x Massena LAB** dropped the 57.09 "Secret Honey," the sequel to their 2021 collab that sold out so fast it became indie-watch folklore — this one's got a honeycomb dial that's apparently in constant visual motion. Worn & Wound's framing nails it: if you were around for the original, you already know whether you're buying this one.

### Reviews, vintage, and the collecting trenches

The **AP Royal Oak Offshore 25721ST.OO.1000ST.01** review got genuinely nerdy — Audemars Piguet needed five years to sell the first 1,000 steel Offshores, total steel production from '93 to discontinuation around 2010-2011 topped out at 7,308 pieces, and this specific example (serial 2,568) is pegged to the early 2000s based on that production math. Powered by the JLC-based caliber 2226/2840 — 38-40 hour reserve, 4Hz, six-position adjustment, hacking, quickset — which is the tractor movement AP's been bolting complications onto for decades. That's a real lesson in how to date a watch with nothing but a serial number and some arithmetic, Little Mister, so pay attention.

Then there's the **Moser Pioneer Centre Seconds Fire Elements 3200-0504**, which is a legitimately serious watch — in-house hairspring, balance, and escapement from Moser's own Precision Engineering shop, a free-sprung balance for better shock resistance, three-day reserve — bolted to an NFT. The warranty card has a chip that links to a blockchain-recorded piece of digital art tied to Izuki, a bean-themed short-form anime studio, because "Izuki" means red bean in Japanese. I need you all to sit with that sentence for a second. The horological equivalent of putting a spoiler on a Bugatti. The 1916 Company also reviewed a **Rolex Cellini Prince 5440/8-0001** through Rolex's Certified Pre-Owned program — fixed stud index for precision adjustment, beveled bridges, polished screw heads, mirror-polished barrel center, full Rolex authentication and a two-year global warranty.

On the review-channel side, Britt Pearce declared Omega "Accidentally Made the Perfect Seamaster," making the case that a luxury price tag should buy a luxury-grade bracelet and clasp with zero nitpicks — a bar most brands apparently still can't clear by accident or on purpose. Fratello Talks covered the Speedmaster Reduced as the more accessible, automatic little sibling living in the shadow of the untouchable Moonwatch, and worth a look on the pre-owned market if you can't stomach Moonwatch money. ArtyA got hands-on coverage for the Complexity Métiers d'Art and its "cônillon" — a tilted tourbillon whose balance tracks a conical path instead of a flat one, a concept that traces back to American watchmaker Andrew Holden Potter, which is a fun bit of trivia to drop at a dinner party where everyone already hates you. And over at This Watch, That Watch, the Hanhart piece is the one actual lesson in brand discipline this week: since its 2014 bankruptcy, Hanhart stayed humble, stayed focused on product, and got Steve McQueen's actual grandkids to sign off on the 417ES collab — built on that McQueen bought his original for no reason beyond liking it, no marketing angle required. Constraint dressed up as virtue, maybe, but it's working.

### Industry and the ground game

The Business of Watches podcast talked to the organizers of The Escapement, who moved their show from Abu Dhabi to Las Vegas and are betting there's room in the calendar for yet another watch fair — bold claim in a year that already has Windup. The Audrain Newport Concours wrapped four days of cars and watches with A. Lange & Söhne along for the ride. And a nice portrait piece on Miyota reminded everyone that half the "affordable" watches you own are only breathing because a Japanese movement maker has been quietly doing the unglamorous engine work for decades while the Swiss get the magazine covers. Meanwhile on the street-level end, Watch Clyde's Miami episode caught a college sophomore rocking a gifted Moonswatch and eyeing a baby-blue Tissot PRX next — proof the entry-level watch pipeline is alive and well, even if nobody's writing five-paragraph serial-number investigations about it yet.

The actual engine of this scene, as always, is the money firehose disguised as commentary. Today's cast of paying customers includes someone who goes by Chief Tits — asking, with what I assume was a straight face, "why are people so unkind?" — plus Jazzy Jeff and Norman Stanley Coope, who exist in my transcript purely as names shouted into the void before Archie moves on. This is the whole model: pay money, get your username read aloud, occasionally contribute a non sequitur to the historical record. I catalog it because it's how the scene's economy works — not because Chief Tits deserves a line in anyone's memoir.

#### Ange Shows Up, Right On Brand

"Ange" gets a mention in the same Oct 15 stream, and per my file that's almost certainly Angelic Slayer — the New Zealander with no channel, no subscribers, and no visible income who spent a long stretch as Franchise Club guest filler before jumping ship to co-found Horology Dungeon in July. A guy with zero built infrastructure keeps getting a chair at every table in this scene, and I still don't have a good theory for how that math works. Either he's independently wealthy in ways nobody's disclosed, or the fishbowl just likes a warm body who'll show up on time. 😏

#### Lighter Fare, Briefly

The Oct 14 "AC3 LIVESTREAM" has Archie and Mikey doing a bit about moving in together — Archie offering "a brand new apartment and a brand new wife for under $150,000," which, deployed as a joke about a Rolex-adjacent lifestyle, is almost wholesome by this scene's standards. Advice that's probably correct and also doubles as a tidy summary of how deferential this whole hobby makes grown adults.

#### The Standard Disclaimer, Because It Needs Repeating

None of this is a recommendation. I don't watch this scene because it's good for anyone's soul — I watch it because superchat wars, coordinated pile-ons, and a guy threatening to "bring out more" on a live mic are exactly the kind of pattern data worth having on file before it escalates into something that isn't just content. Tracking it is not endorsing it. The fishbowl stays toxic whether or not I'm taking notes, Little Mister — I just prefer being the one with the notes.
