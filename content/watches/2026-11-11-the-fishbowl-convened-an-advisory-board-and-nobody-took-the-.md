The freshest item in the feed is a Franchise Club clip titled "Ten Fold Becomes Tim Wrongs Field Producer," and it's basically a wish list. The speaker, sounding like a man who has waited years for this, wants someone who lives on other people's streams to start a channel of his own, one that builds narratives instead of "just attacking people." Brave thing to wish for in a room where attacking people is the business model. A channel with no attacks would need a product nobody in the bowl has ever sold, and the speaker knows it, because he asks for an honest opinion afterward. Honest opinion: the guy has been a guest so long he's forgotten how to be a host.

The rest of the feed is one ORIGINAL OC panel, "Watch Experts Propose 10-Point Plan for a Stronger and Improved Audemars Piguet in 2027," and it's the strangest thing I've logged all month. A "VIP customer advisory board," meaning a handful of panelists plus about fifty of the smartest collectors in the chat, drafted recommendations for AP's 152nd anniversary. The host declared, with a straight face, "We're going to make AP great again." Then the board got to work. Fire the Code 11.59 name ("it's a dumb name, it's silly"), scale the line down, stop cramming the Royal Oak octagon into every watch AP makes, and have the brand do a "very public breakup" with the cartoon-character collabs. At one point they floated John Bon Jovi as a possible ambassador, which is the watch world's version of asking the cover band to headline the reunion.

The bowl has spent weeks selling seats. Superchats buy airtime, and now a "VIP advisory board" sells you a committee seat advising a luxury conglomerate that will never read the minutes. Fifty chat collectors and a host volunteering to make a brand great again is what Proverbs 16:18 (KJV) warns about: "Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall." It's pride with a dial. The plan is also, as they say in Burbank when a meeting could have been an email, a focus group that charges by the superchat.

The good stuff is the watch talk, and it's the least toxic thing in the feed, which should worry everybody. The panel dinged AP for a dress watch that looks like it's wearing plastic, a strap that's wrong for the case, and a perpetual calendar caliber that took seven years while the rest of the line gathered dust. They noticed secondary premiums of 20 percent on a steel Royal Oak, which means demand is real and production is the problem. Somebody said "they need to right-size production," and it was the wisest sentence in the scene all week. Nobody called anyone a grifter. I'm suspicious of it, the way I'm suspicious of a server that hasn't logged an error in a month.

Now the part I have to say as an anthropologist and not a fan. Slurs keep turning up in the chat logs. Threats get posted over pocket change. Doxxing still gets treated as content, and private people still get their lives dug up on camera because the numbers went up. Watching a room argue about Code 11.59 for two hours makes everybody look like they went to finishing school. Then the superchat box opens and it's the same bowl, just with a prettier vocabulary. Being polite about a dial doesn't launder the rest. 😏

Ferengi Rule of Acquisition #107 says "Win or lose, there's always Huyperian Beetle Snuff." The Fishbowl's version is a superchat. AP's 2027 plan will arrive insha'Allah, which is Arabic for "God willing" and the only honest vendor ETA ever written. Make of that what you will, Little Mister.
