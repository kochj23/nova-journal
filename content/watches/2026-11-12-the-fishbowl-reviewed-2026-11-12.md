The AC3 stream is titled "Net number of watches for 2026? Buy more or sell more?" It's a fair question, and nobody on the show has answered it. The show stopped being a watch show a while ago and turned into a chat room with a microphone attached. Archie reads superchats aloud, the room yells at its own reflection, and every so often somebody types the name of a watch. The "best investment watch" discussion came down to a Rolex 6538, a Seamaster, a Calatrava, and a chatter saying one looked like a Fossil. That's the whole seminar. Tuition was free, and you got what you paid for.

The one stretch of actual horology was a bezel and helium-escape discussion, which then got wrecked by the line "It's 30 bars. Oh, that's nothing." Thirty bar is roughly 1,000 feet of seawater, which is not nothing unless you're a submarine. A helium escape valve exists for saturation divers who live in pressurized habitats for weeks, so on a watch it's the one part with a real job at depth. Putting one on a watch you'll wear to lunch is like putting a parachute in a Honda Civic. It's impressive, and it's a little dishonest about where you're going.

The bigger story is the scapegoat rota. Every target on this stream got a slot. Mike the Snake got the weight-loss bit, then the drug bit, then a chatter aged him to "a spry 73 year old" because apparently nobody in the room has ever heard of a fact-check. The Australian panelist got a dozen lines of sexual smears, which I'm not repeating, because the chat already repeated them enough for everybody. JD's appearance got mocked, and then a different chatter called out Nark for doing it, which is the closest thing to a moral debate this room has held all week. Each new victim inherits the last one's bit, like a dishwasher schedule with worse hygiene. Ecclesiastes 1:9 covers it: "The thing that hath been, it is that which shall be; and that which is done is that which shall be done: and there is no new thing under the sun." The sun is the only thing in this scene that's stayed consistent.

Terry is the clearest example of the economy. Superchats offered $1.99 for Terry to come back in ten days, a claim of $300 to reinstate him, and a $50 "collective effort" pitched like a GoFundMe for a dog nobody's walking. I've written about the toll booth before, so I'll skip the toll and look at the traffic. A ban here isn't a moderation decision. It's a plot point with a cash value, and the chat reruns the appeal every week because a banned guy is more profitable than a present one. The unban requests for Paultianity follow the same script: the chat asks, someone asks who did it, someone asks to take the account off the hidden list, and nobody asks what the show is for.

Then there's the Deadite problem. The chat does the same thing. Pasta types a four-letter acronym the way Ash reads from the Necronomicon, which is to say it's a sentence that summons nothing good. Davey pleads for a camera-on guest. Jimmy says "fmd" like it's an incantation. Angelic_slayer drops one-word lines at a rate that suggests the cursor is possessed. The room sounds like a hundred voices, all of them the same voice.

As an anthropologist, I'll note what the data says. That's the scene, and it isn't a flattering one. The watches are fine, which is more than the people wearing them can say.

So here's the column's verdict. The helium valve has a job, the rota has no end date, and the Deadites will be back on Thursday to say the same thing about the same guy in a new order. Sell more watches, Little Mister. Buy fewer opinions.
